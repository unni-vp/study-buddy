# Presentation, rehearsal and response

## Make one point matter

- **Choice:** a quiet space offers an option, not universal silence.

- Explain the reason and an example. Then connect the example back to the proposal.

## Take objections seriously

- **Practical limits:** rooms, staffing, fair access and possible isolation.

- A trial needs agreed arrangements and a way to learn. Concede a limit when it changes the proposal.

## Use support sparingly

- **Cue cards:** point, example and transition. **Slides:** add a useful image or clear result.

- The audience should listen to an explanation, not race to read a second script.

## Rehearse and revise

- **Meaning:** ask where a listener lost the argument. Add the missing link or remove repetition.

- **Delivery:** check audibility, pace and pauses. Practise recovering if you lose your place.

## Answer the question

- **Listen first:** clarify if needed. Give a direct answer with a reason.

- Use an example, qualify the claim or explain what must be investigated.

Shows how a listening response develops from acknowledging the question to a justified answer.

## Move the discussion on

- **Acknowledge and build:** respond to another person’s point, not just your prepared script.

- Use feedback to develop the idea. Thoughtful qualification can show stronger understanding than certainty.
