# Synthesis, comparison and judgement

## Find the shared focus

- **Waste:** both writers value remaining usefulness, but differ in flexibility.

- Choose a shared issue before collecting quotations from each source.

Links a shared issue to separate evidence and a meaningful comparison.

## Select the right evidence

- **Child:** “Nothing” and “every member” create firm expectations.

- **Modern article:** “moral purity” is rejected, alongside limits of safety, cost and time.

## Synthesise the reasons

- **Information:** Child links thrift to useful objects and household needs.

- The modern article links repair to replacement costs and materials that retain value.

## Connect the comparison

- **Both … but …:** identify the relationship, then support each side.

- Child’s commands sound stricter. The modern concessions create a more accommodating argument.

## Evaluate the strength

- **Weigh the evidence:** useful planning can coexist with an inflexible demand.

- Explain agreement or doubt through the text. Do not replace analysis with an unrelated personal story.

## Check the response

- **Accurate source:** each quotation belongs to the writer named. Address the exact focus.

- Analyse effects in context. Longer responses need developed understanding across the required range.
