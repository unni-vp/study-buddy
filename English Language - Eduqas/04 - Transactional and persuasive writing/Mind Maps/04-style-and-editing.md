# Style & editing

## Precision

- “The fee excludes students on tight budgets” is clearer than “It is really, really bad”.
- Use words accurately. Qualify claims when needed: can, may, some. Avoid unsupported “everyone” or “always”.

## Controlled persuasion

- Vary pace: longer sentences explain; short sentences emphasise. “Neither sounds spectacular. Both matter.”
- Use emotion proportionately. A brief example can be more convincing than a dramatic exaggeration.

## Sentence boundaries

- Comma splice: “It is free, everyone can try it.”
- Repair with a full stop, a semicolon between complete clauses, or a conjunction: “It is free, so everyone can try it.”

## Punctuation & grammar

- Colon after a complete clause introduces explanation. Apostrophe marks possession or omitted letters.
- Its = possession; it’s = it is/has. Their = possession; they’re = they are. Check agreement and tense.

## Meaning first

- Does every paragraph add a distinct idea? Have you explained how or why with a concrete detail?
- Does the order make sense? Does the ending fulfil the task? Keep the tone suitable when disagreeing.

## Accuracy pass

- Check sentence boundaries, spelling, apostrophes, tense and agreement. Use spellings printed in the task correctly.
- Replace vague repetition with an example. Keep checking time for both responses; do not perfect only the first.
