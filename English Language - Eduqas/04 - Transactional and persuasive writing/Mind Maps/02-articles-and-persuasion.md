# Articles & persuasion

## Headline & opening

- Use a headline that reveals the angle. Begin with a relevant moment, contrast or focused question.
- Reach the subject quickly. A long fictional story can take over an article.

## Write for these readers

- School magazine: familiar situations and a lively, respectful voice. Formal publication: adapt the register.
- Direct address can involve readers. Avoid blaming them or assuming everyone has the same life.

## Develop paragraphs

- One main idea → concrete example → why it matters. Change paragraph when the job or focus changes.
- Connect naturally: “This small change ...”, “Another difficulty ...”, “That benefit ...”.

## Use devices deliberately

- A question sharpens an issue; a contrast clarifies a choice; a three-part pattern gives rhythm.
- Reasons support persuasion. Techniques express them. Do not force every device into every piece.

## Handle a real objection

- **Concede → respond → justify.** Accept a valid concern and give a workable answer.
- “After-school travel is difficult; a lunchtime trial offers another route.” Avoid dismissing opponents.

## A purposeful close

- Bring the message together. Return to an opening image or give a practical next step.
- “Choose one room, one lunchtime and one chance.” Do not introduce a major new argument at the end.
