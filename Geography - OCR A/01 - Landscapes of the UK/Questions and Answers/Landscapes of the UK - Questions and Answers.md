# Landscapes of the UK questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** What is weathering?

**Answer:** The breakdown of rock **in place**.

**2.** Name the process in which pebbles collide and become smaller and rounder.

**Answer:** Attrition.

**3.** Name the river-transport process in which large stones roll along the bed.

**Answer:** Traction.

**4.** What is deposited on a floodplain during a flood?

**Answer:** Alluvium.

**5.** Name the process that moves beach sediment along a coast in a zigzag.

**Answer:** Longshore drift.

**6.** Which type of wave usually builds a beach?

**Answer:** Constructive wave.

## 2 marks

**7.** Give two differences between typical upland and lowland landscapes.

**Answer:**

- Uplands have **higher relief and steeper slopes**.
- Lowlands are generally **lower and flatter**, with wider valleys.

**8.** Explain how abrasion erodes a river channel.

**Answer:**

- The river carries rock fragments.
- These scrape against the bed and banks, **wearing them away**.

**9.** Explain why deposition occurs when a river enters flatter land.

**Answer:**

- The gradient and water velocity decrease.
- The river loses energy and can no longer carry as much sediment, so it **deposits its load**.

**10.** Explain how a river cliff and slip-off slope form on a meander.

**Answer:**

- Faster flow erodes the outside bend, forming a **river cliff**.
- Slower flow deposits sediment on the inside bend, forming a **slip-off slope**.

**11.** Explain how geology helps create headlands and bays.

**Answer:**

- Softer rock is eroded rapidly to form bays.
- More resistant rock erodes slowly and remains as headlands.

**12.** Give two possible effects of building groynes.

**Answer:**

- They trap sediment and build a wider beach locally.
- Less sediment may reach places further along the coast, increasing erosion there.

## 3 marks

**13.** Explain the formation of a V-shaped valley.

**Answer:**

- A river uses vertical erosion to cut down into its bed.
- Weathering and mass movement weaken and lower the valley sides.
- The river removes fallen material, leaving steep sides shaped like a V.

**14.** Explain how a waterfall retreats to form a gorge.

**Answer:**

- Softer rock beneath resistant rock is eroded faster and a plunge pool forms.
- The resistant overhang collapses because it is unsupported.
- Repetition moves the waterfall upstream, leaving a narrow, steep-sided gorge.

**15.** Explain how an oxbow lake forms.

**Answer:**

- Erosion narrows the neck of a meander.
- During high flow, the river cuts through the neck and takes a straighter route.
- Deposition seals the ends of the old bend, leaving an oxbow lake.

**16.** Explain how a spit forms.

**Answer:**

- Longshore drift transports sediment along the coast.
- Where the coastline changes direction, material continues into open water and is deposited as energy falls.
- The ridge grows outward; its end may curve if wave direction changes.

## 4 marks

**17.** Explain how climate and geology can influence a UK river landscape.

**Answer:**

- Heavy or frequent rainfall raises discharge, giving a river more power to erode and transport material.
- Storms can also increase flooding and floodplain deposition.
- Resistant rock erodes slowly and can produce steeper features.
- Softer or less resistant rock erodes more quickly, helping valleys widen.

**18.** Explain how erosion and deposition are connected along a coast.

**Answer:**

- Waves erode cliffs and release sediment.
- Longshore drift transports that sediment along the shoreline.
- Where wave energy falls, the sediment is deposited.
- This can build beaches or spits, so erosion in one place supplies landforms elsewhere.

**19.** Explain how people and natural processes combine to shape the River Wye landscape.

**Answer:**

- Rainfall, gradient and river discharge control erosion, transport and deposition.
- Geology influences the resistance and permeability of the valley rocks.
- Farming and building can change vegetation and surface runoff.
- Flood management and recreation alter parts of the valley and affect how people use it.

**20.** Explain why managing one part of the North Norfolk coast can affect another part.

**Answer:**

- Coastal places are linked by sediment moved through longshore drift.
- Groynes trap material and can widen the beach they protect.
- This may reduce the supply reaching places further along the coast.
- Those places may then have narrower beaches and greater exposure to wave erosion.

## 6 marks

**21.** Assess the importance of geology in shaping a UK coastal landscape you have studied.

**Answer:**

On the **North Norfolk coast**, geology is very important because much of the coast contains soft material that waves can erode readily. Differences in resistance influence the rate and form of cliff retreat. The eroded material also supplies sediment that longshore drift transports and deposits as beaches and spits. However, geology does not act alone. Storm conditions and wave energy alter erosion rates, while sea walls, groynes and managed realignment change sediment movement and the places protected. Geology creates the underlying vulnerability, but marine processes, weather and human management determine how quickly and where the landscape changes.

**22.** Evaluate the use of hard engineering to manage a coastal landscape.

**Answer:**

Hard engineering such as sea walls and groynes can protect homes, roads and businesses in high-value places. A sea wall resists wave attack, while groynes build a beach that absorbs wave energy. These defences can therefore reduce risk quickly. However, they are expensive, need maintenance and can look intrusive. Groynes may interrupt longshore drift and increase erosion elsewhere. A strong judgement is that hard engineering is most suitable where the benefits justify the cost and impacts on neighbouring coastlines have been considered; lower-value or environmentally sensitive areas may suit managed realignment better.

**23.** Examine how river processes create contrasting landforms from upstream to downstream.

**Answer:**

In the steep upper course, turbulent water and vertical erosion cut V-shaped valleys. Where resistant rock overlies softer rock, differential erosion can produce waterfalls and gorges. Further downstream, greater discharge and lateral erosion help meanders grow. Erosion occurs on outer bends while deposition occurs on inner bends; a cut-off can form an oxbow lake. Across the lower valley, repeated flooding deposits alluvium to build a floodplain and coarser material beside the channel to form levees. Changing gradient, discharge and energy therefore produce contrasting landforms along the river.

**24.** Assess how natural processes and people have shaped the River Wye landscape.

**Answer:**

The Wye's relief, rainfall and geology guide river processes. Steeper upper reaches encourage runoff and erosion, while gentler downstream sections allow meanders, floodplain development and deposition. Weathering and mass movement also supply material to the river. People modify these natural patterns through farming, settlements, transport routes, recreation and flood management. Removing vegetation or creating impermeable surfaces can increase runoff, while defences may protect selected places and alter local flow. Natural processes created the main valley and landforms, but human activity increasingly influences their rate of change and the risks people experience.
