# Rainforest processes and the Peruvian Amazon

## Interdependent components

- **Biotic:** organisms. **Abiotic:** climate, light, water and soil. A change in one affects the others.

- Heat and rain support growth, while vegetation changes habitats, water movement and soil protection.

## Cycles and adaptations

- **Nutrients:** biomass → litter → soil → root uptake. Warm, moist conditions speed decay.

- **Water:** interception and transpiration help recycle moisture. Drip tips shed rain and buttress roots support trees.

## Why Peru’s forest matters

- **East of the Andes:** biodiversity, carbon storage, water regulation and Indigenous livelihoods.

- Timber, food, tourism and mineral or energy resources also have economic value.

## Threats and their chains

- **Clearance:** habitat and biomass loss expose soil to leaching and erosion, while reducing water recycling.

- Farming, logging, mining and access routes can reinforce one another’s impacts.

## Named choices

- **Camisea:** energy and revenue alongside environmental and community concerns. Operating since 2004.

- **Manu and Purús–Manu:** protected and connected habitats complement community management.

## Judge sustainability

- **Secure land rights, enforcement and sustainable income:** make conservation practical.

- Evaluate who gains and whether forest functions remain. Protection on paper alone is insufficient.
