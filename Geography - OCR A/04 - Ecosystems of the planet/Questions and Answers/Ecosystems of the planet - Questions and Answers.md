# Ecosystems of the planet questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** What is an ecosystem?

**Answer:** A community of organisms interacting with each other and the non-living environment.

**2.** Give one biotic component of an ecosystem.

**Answer:** Plants.

**3.** Name the process by which plants release water vapour.

**Answer:** Transpiration.

**4.** What is coral bleaching?

**Answer:** Coral turning white after expelling its symbiotic algae.

**5.** Name the rainforest case study used in this course.

**Answer:** The Peruvian Amazon.

**6.** Name the coral reef case study used in this course.

**Answer:** Andros Barrier Reef, The Bahamas.

## 2 marks

**7.** Explain why tropical rainforests have rapid nutrient cycling.

**Answer:**

- Heat and moisture cause dead material to decompose quickly.
- Dense roots rapidly absorb the released nutrients.

**8.** Give two ways in which coral reefs are valuable to people.

**Answer:**

- They support fishing and tourism income.
- They absorb wave energy and protect coasts.

**9.** Explain why rainforest soils may lose fertility after trees are cleared.

**Answer:**

- The nutrient store in vegetation is removed.
- Heavy rain then leaches nutrients from exposed soil.

**10.** Give two threats to the Peruvian Amazon.

**Answer:**

- Farming or cattle ranching.
- Mining, road building or logging.

**11.** Explain why warm-water coral reefs are found in shallow, clear seas.

**Answer:**

- The algae living in coral need sunlight for photosynthesis.
- Clear, shallow water allows enough light to reach them.

**12.** Give two local strategies used to protect Andros Barrier Reef.

**Answer:**

- Marine protected areas.
- Sustainable fishing, monitoring or eco-tourism.

**13.** Why is coral classed as a biotic component rather than an abiotic structure?

**Answer:**

Living coral consists of animals called polyps. They interact with other organisms and build the reef’s calcium-carbonate structure.

## 3 marks

**14.** Explain one example of interdependence in a tropical rainforest.

**Answer:**

- Trees provide food and habitats for animals.
- Animals may pollinate flowers or spread seeds.
- This helps trees reproduce, so each group supports the other.

**15.** Explain how deforestation can change the rainforest water cycle.

**Answer:**

- Fewer leaves intercept rainfall.
- Less water returns to the atmosphere through transpiration.
- Surface runoff rises and local rainfall recycling may fall.

**16.** Explain how climate controls the distribution of hot deserts.

**Answer:**

- Many occur around 20–30° north and south.
- Descending air creates high pressure and prevents cloud formation.
- Rainfall is therefore very low, producing sparse vegetation.

**17.** Explain how a marine protected area may improve reef health.

**Answer:**

- Damaging fishing or development is restricted.
- Fish populations and habitats have time to recover.
- A healthier food web can make the reef more resilient.

## 4 marks

**18.** Explain two adaptations of tropical rainforest plants.

**Answer:**

- **Drip-tip leaves** shed heavy rain quickly, reducing water damage and fungal growth.
- **Buttress roots** spread near the surface and support tall trees where soil is shallow and nutrient-poor.

**19.** Explain two ways the Peruvian Amazon is valuable.

**Answer:**

- It stores carbon and recycles water, helping regulate climate and rainfall.
- It supplies habitats, food, materials and livelihoods for Indigenous communities and businesses.

**20.** Explain two threats to Andros Barrier Reef.

**Answer:**

- High sea temperatures cause bleaching because coral expels its algae and loses a major food source.
- Pollution or sediment reduces water quality and blocks sunlight needed by the algae.

**21.** Explain why clearing rainforest may create short-term gains but long-term costs.

**Answer:**

- Farming, mining and timber can create jobs, exports and tax income.
- However, habitat and stored carbon are lost, soils erode and water cycling is disrupted, weakening future livelihoods.

**22.** Explain why nutrients can be lost quickly after rainforest clearance.

**Answer:**

Trees store many nutrients in living biomass, so clearance removes a major store. Less litter is available for decomposition and fewer roots take nutrients up. Heavy rain can leach dissolved nutrients and wash away exposed soil. The disrupted cycle makes continued fertility harder to maintain.

**23.** Explain how damage to Andros mangroves could affect reef-related fishing.

**Answer:**

Mangrove creeks provide nursery habitat and shelter for young fish. Loss of this habitat can reduce survival and the number moving into other habitats. Fish populations supporting local fishing may then decline. Protecting reef alone may therefore be insufficient if connected nursery areas are damaged.

## 6 marks

**24.** Assess the success of strategies used to manage the Peruvian Amazon sustainably.

**Answer:**

- Community land rights address the problem of open, insecure land. From 2015–2021, 58 native communities gained titles covering about 230,000 hectares.
- Deforestation-free coffee and cocoa can provide income without clearing more forest; the FOLUR project targets 17 provinces.
- Restoration and monitoring can repair damage and identify illegal activity.
- However, profitable mining, farming and logging continue, and enforcement is difficult in remote areas.
- Overall, management is most likely to succeed when secure land rights are combined with lasting local income and enforcement.

**25.** Assess how effectively Andros Barrier Reef is being protected.

**Answer:**

- North and South Marine Parks protect about 8,500 acres in total, restricting some local pressures.
- Fishing controls and eco-tourism can conserve breeding stocks while maintaining incomes.
- Monitoring allows managers to respond to disease, bleaching and species change.
- Yet pollution, dredging and overfishing can cross boundaries, while local parks cannot stop global ocean warming.
- Protection is valuable, but its success depends on enforcement, community support and wider action on climate change.

**26.** Compare the nutrient cycles of tropical rainforests and coral reefs.

**Answer:**

- Both recycle nutrients quickly and depend on close interdependence between organisms.
- In rainforest, most nutrients are stored in biomass; decomposition releases them into thin soil before roots absorb them.
- On reefs, nutrients are scarce in the water and are rapidly exchanged among coral, algae, fish and decomposers.
- Removing trees breaks the rainforest cycle; losing coral or key fish species disrupts the reef cycle.
- Both systems are productive but can be damaged rapidly when their tight recycling system is broken.

**27.** Assess whether development can be compatible with conservation in the Peruvian Amazon.

**Answer:**

Development can provide energy, jobs and income, as illustrated by the Camisea gas project. However, extraction and infrastructure create risks for forest habitats and Indigenous communities. Protected areas such as Manu, connected habitats in the Purús–Manu corridor and secure community land rights can conserve biodiversity while supporting appropriate livelihoods. Compatibility depends on safeguards, consultation, enforcement and whether local people share benefits. Development is not automatically sustainable: large economic gains cannot by themselves compensate for irreversible habitat or cultural losses.

**28.** Evaluate sustainable management of a named coral reef ecosystem.

**Answer:**

At Andros, marine protection can reduce fishing pressure and damage to reef habitats. West Side National Park protects linked mangrove creeks and nursery areas, while the Crab Replenishment Reserve protects land-crab habitat. Sustainable fishing and careful tourism can support income while giving local people a reason to conserve habitats. Success requires enforcement, community participation and good water quality outside park boundaries. Local measures improve resilience but cannot stop rising sea temperatures, so wider climate action remains necessary.
