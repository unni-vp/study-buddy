# Liverpool urban fieldwork: school account

**Course:** OCR A J383 | **Exam year:** 2027 | **Specification:** Geographical Skills, fieldwork
**Source:** Student account supplied on 4 October 2026. Findings below are reported observations; raw data has not been supplied.

## Enquiry question

**How far does environmental quality change across Liverpool town centre?**

In plain English: were some parts cleaner, better maintained and more attractive than others? Think of comparing different rooms in a building using the same checklist.

## What was measured

- **Environmental quality:** litter, graffiti, noise, traffic, building condition and attractiveness. An **environmental quality survey** was completed at each site. Individual scores were added to produce a **total score**.
- **Pedestrian flow:** people passing a **fixed point** were counted during a **set time**.
- **Land use:** **field sketches** and **land-use maps** recorded shops, services and other buildings.

## Sampling

**Systematic sampling** meant collecting data at **regular points along a route**. Several sites in busier and quieter parts of the centre allowed comparisons.

## Reported findings

- Main shopping and central areas had the **highest environmental quality scores**.
- These areas also had the **highest pedestrian counts** and the greatest concentration of **retail and services**.
- Quieter streets away from the busiest central area had **lower environmental quality scores** and **fewer pedestrians**.

## Conclusion

Environmental quality **varied across the sampled sites**. The busiest central areas were usually the best maintained and most attractive, according to the supplied account.

## Use evidence carefully

- State the observed pattern. Do not claim pedestrian numbers caused better environmental quality: the account shows an association.
- Do not invent site names, scores, pedestrian totals, sample sizes or a statistical correlation.
- Traffic counts and questionnaires were mentioned earlier for the Liverpool setting. Their role in this specific enquiry has not been described.
