# Climate, circulation and Australian drought

## Climate changes through time

- **Quaternary:** glacial and interglacial periods. More recent evidence includes records, ice cores and tree rings.

- Natural factors explain past changes, while human greenhouse gases dominate rapid modern warming.

## Circulation creates patterns

- **Equator and 60°:** air generally rises, giving low pressure. **30° and poles:** air sinks, giving high pressure.

- Unequal heating and Earth’s rotation shape climate belts and prevailing winds.

## Hazards and evidence

- **Tropical storms:** warm sea, moist air, rotation and low wind shear. **Drought:** prolonged water deficit.

- Compare like-for-like records. A reported disaster count also reflects exposure and vulnerability.

## ENSO is a risk factor

- **El Niño:** weaker Pacific trade winds, warmer water farther east and shifted rainfall.

- Parts of Australia can be drier. La Niña often shifts conditions towards a wetter western Pacific.

## Australia: 2002–2009

- **Repeated dry phases:** rainfall deficits and heat depleted stores. This was not one continuous El Niño.

- Farms, towns and ecosystems competed for water, particularly in the Murray–Darling Basin.

## Evaluate adaptation

- **Restrictions, irrigation changes, recycling and desalination:** reduce demand or diversify supply.

- Compare cost, energy, fairness and environmental flows. Adaptation improves resilience but does not create rainfall.
