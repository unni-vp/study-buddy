# People of the planet questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** What does HDI stand for?

**Answer:** Human Development Index.

**2.** What is urbanisation?

**Answer:** An increase in the proportion of people living in urban areas.

**3.** How many people must a city have to be called a megacity?

**Answer:** More than 10 million.

**4.** Name Ethiopia's major export crop.

**Answer:** Coffee.

**5.** On which river is Rosario located?

**Answer:** The Paraná River.

**6.** Name the goat-sharing system used in the Oxfam project.

**Answer:** Hirbaa-dabaree.

## 2 marks

**7.** Give two components of HDI.

**Answer:**

- Life expectancy.
- Education or income per person.

**8.** Explain one limitation of GNI per person.

**Answer:**

- It is a national average.
- It can hide large differences between rich and poor people.

**9.** Give one push and one pull factor for rural-to-urban migration.

**Answer:**

- Push: few rural jobs or drought.
- Pull: urban employment, education or healthcare.

**10.** Give two reasons why Ethiopia's development is difficult.

**Answer:**

- It is landlocked, increasing trade costs.
- Drought, conflict, debt or weak infrastructure restrict progress.

**11.** Explain how goat aid can create an income.

**Answer:**

- Goats reproduce and create a growing asset.
- Families can sell offspring or goat products.

**12.** Give two important economic roles of Rosario.

**Answer:**

- Paraná River port and export centre.
- Food processing, manufacturing, logistics or regional services.

## 3 marks

**13.** Explain how colonialism can continue to affect development.

**Answer:**

- Colonial economies often focused on exporting raw materials.
- Infrastructure linked resources to ports rather than joining communities.
- Dependence on low-value exports may therefore continue after independence.

**14.** Explain how foreign direct investment may support Ethiopia's development.

**Answer:**

- Foreign firms may build factories and infrastructure.
- They create jobs, training, exports and tax income.
- These benefits can raise incomes and fund services if they remain in Ethiopia.

**15.** Explain why rapid urban growth may cause informal settlements.

**Answer:**

- Migration and natural increase raise housing demand quickly.
- Formal homes may be too few or too expensive.
- Lower-income families build on unplanned land without full services.

**16.** Explain one way Rosario's urban agriculture programme is sustainable.

**Answer:**

- Unused urban land is turned into productive gardens.
- Local growers earn income and supply nearby food.
- Shorter supply chains and extra green space can reduce environmental pressure.

## 4 marks

**17.** Explain one advantage and one limitation of using HDI.

**Answer:**

- Advantage: it combines health, education and income, so it is broader than a single economic measure.
- Limitation: it is still a national average, so regional and social inequalities may be hidden.

**18.** Explain two ways rapid urbanisation can benefit a city.

**Answer:**

- A larger labour force attracts firms and supports business growth.
- A bigger population creates demand that can justify better transport, education and healthcare.

**19.** Explain two limitations of the Oxfam goat-aid project.

**Answer:**

- Goats remain vulnerable to severe drought, disease and shortage of grazing or water.
- A small livestock project cannot by itself solve poor infrastructure, education or healthcare.

**20.** Explain how Rosario Hábitat improved informal settlements.

**Answer:**

- It combined secure land ownership and basic infrastructure with community participation.
- Employment support and selective relocation addressed social needs as well as the physical condition of housing.

## 6 marks

**21.** Assess the importance of different factors affecting Ethiopia's development.

**Answer:**

- Landlocked location raises transport costs because exports must cross another country to reach a port.
- Drought and reliance on rain-fed farming make food supply and rural incomes unreliable.
- Historical conflict and political instability have diverted money and discouraged investment.
- Trade and foreign investment create jobs and exports, while infrastructure spending improves electricity and water access.
- Poverty fell from 39% to 24% between 2004 and 2016, showing progress, but conflict and low incomes remain serious.
- Environmental and political factors interact, so no single factor fully explains Ethiopia's level of development.

**22.** Assess how successful Oxfam's goat-aid project was as a development strategy.

**Answer:**

- The scheme targeted 220 households, giving seven goats to each first-round family.
- Milk improved nutrition, while selling offspring created income and assets.
- Passing kids to another household multiplied the benefit and used a familiar local tradition.
- It encouraged ownership rather than repeated emergency handouts.
- However, drought, disease and lack of water could destroy the herds, and the project was small compared with national need.
- It was a useful bottom-up strategy, especially for livelihood recovery, but needed water, veterinary and wider development support.

**23.** Assess the sustainability of Rosario's response to urban challenges.

**Answer:**

- Rosario Hábitat improved land security, services and employment for about 5,200 families, but historic informal-settlement need was much larger.
- Waste rules make major businesses responsible for part of the waste stream, though enforcement and recycling markets matter.
- Public transport and active travel can reduce congestion and emissions; transport still produced 34.3% of city emissions in 2018.
- Urban agriculture used about 25 hectares and supported 155 growers by 2021, joining food, income and green-space goals.
- The city uses integrated strategies, which is a strength, but their scale and long-term funding limit how far they solve citywide inequality.
