# People of the UK questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** What is an import?

**Answer:** A good or service bought from another country.

**2.** What does a population pyramid show?

**Answer:** The age and sex structure of a population.

**3.** Define immigration.

**Answer:** Movement into a country to live.

**4.** What is suburbanisation?

**Answer:** Movement of people or activities from the inner city to the urban edge.

**5.** Name one indicator of development within the UK.

**Answer:** Income.

**6.** Which city is the major UK city case study in this course?

**Answer:** Leeds.

## 2 marks

**7.** Explain why using income alone may give a misleading picture of development.

**Answer:**

- Income does not show health, education or access to services.
- An average can hide large differences between people or neighbourhoods.

**8.** Give two reasons why an area with good infrastructure may attract businesses.

**Answer:**

- Transport links make workers, customers and goods easier to reach.
- Reliable broadband helps firms communicate and provide digital services.

**9.** Explain why the UK population is ageing.

**Answer:**

- People live longer because death rates have fallen.
- Lower birth rates mean young people form a smaller share of the population.

**10.** Give one social and one economic benefit of immigration.

**Answer:**

- Social: migrants can add cultural diversity.
- Economic: migrants can fill skills gaps, work and pay taxes.

**11.** Explain one possible consequence of counter-urbanisation for a rural settlement.

**Answer:**

- Housing demand may rise as people move in.
- This can increase prices and make homes less affordable for existing residents.

**12.** Give two challenges created by rapid city growth.

**Answer:**

- Increased demand for affordable housing.
- More congestion and pressure on transport.

## 3 marks

**13.** Explain one reason why development varies across the UK.

**Answer:**

- Areas with poor transport or digital connections are less accessible.
- Firms may invest elsewhere because costs or travel times are lower.
- This reduces job opportunities and income in the less connected area.

**14.** Explain one challenge caused by an ageing population.

**Answer:**

- A larger older population increases demand for healthcare and social care.
- These services require more workers and public spending.
- With fewer workers per dependent person, funding them can become harder.

**15.** Explain how migration can change the character of a city.

**Answer:**

- Migrants bring languages, food, beliefs and traditions.
- They may establish businesses and community services.
- These changes make neighbourhoods and the city's workforce more diverse.

**16.** Explain how the decline of the docks affected Salford Quays.

**Answer:**

- Shipping and industrial change reduced dock activity.
- Jobs were lost in the docks and linked businesses.
- Land and buildings became derelict, damaging the area's environment and image.

## 4 marks

**17.** Explain how government policy and infrastructure can reduce uneven development.

**Answer:**

- Government can fund regeneration, education or business support in weaker areas.
- These policies can create jobs and improve skills.
- Better roads, rail and broadband connect people and firms to markets.
- Improved accessibility can attract investment and raise incomes.

**18.** Explain two possible impacts of re-urbanisation.

**Answer:**

- Reusing brownfield land can bring homes, jobs and improved public spaces back to an inner city.
- Rising demand may increase rents and house prices, so some lower-income residents may be displaced or excluded.

**19.** Explain how regeneration changed Salford Quays.

**Answer:**

- Derelict dockland was reused for offices, homes, culture and leisure.
- New transport links and public spaces improved accessibility and image.
- Investment created employment and attracted visitors and businesses.
- However, high costs and skills requirements mean the benefits may not be shared equally.

**20.** Explain why Leeds is important at more than one scale.

**Answer:**

- Regionally, Leeds provides jobs, shops, education, culture and services.
- National transport and business links connect it to other UK cities.
- Universities and firms attract people and investment from abroad.
- Its decisions and growth therefore influence local, regional and wider networks.

## 6 marks

**21.** Assess the view that immigration has mainly positive effects on the UK.

**Answer:**

Immigration can fill labour and skills shortages, increase tax revenue and support businesses and public services. Migrants may start firms and add cultural diversity. A younger working population can also reduce some pressure from population ageing. However, rapid growth can increase demand for housing, schools, healthcare and transport, especially where investment does not keep pace. Some workers may face greater competition, although impacts vary by occupation and place. Overall, immigration can bring strong social and economic benefits, but the result depends on planning, service capacity and how fairly opportunities are shared.

**22.** Evaluate the success of regeneration at Salford Quays.

**Answer:**

Regeneration transformed derelict dockland into a mixed area of employment, housing, culture and leisure. Improved transport and public spaces attracted investment and changed the area's image. This shows environmental and economic success. However, success should also include existing communities. New homes may be expensive and some jobs may require skills local residents do not have. Benefits can spread beyond the regenerated site, but inequality may remain. Salford Quays is therefore successful in physical and economic renewal, while its social success depends on affordable housing, accessible jobs and links to surrounding neighbourhoods.

**23.** Examine the challenges created by an ageing UK population and possible responses.

**Answer:**

Longer lives and lower birth rates increase the proportion of older people. This raises demand for pensions, healthcare, accessible housing and social care. A smaller working-age share may have to fund these services. Older people also contribute experience, spending, childcare and volunteering, so ageing is not only a cost. Responses include later retirement, workplace pensions, preventative healthcare, adapted homes and technology that supports independent living. Working-age migration may also help. The best response combines funding and care reforms with measures that allow older people to remain healthy and involved.

**24.** Assess how successfully a major UK city can become more sustainable.

**Answer:**

In Leeds, better public transport, walking and cycling can reduce congestion and pollution. Brownfield redevelopment and energy-efficient housing can limit outward growth, while recycling and green spaces improve environmental quality. These measures can also make neighbourhoods healthier and more attractive. Challenges include cost, disruption, rising population and unequal access. A new development is not fully sustainable if housing is unaffordable or residents cannot reach jobs and services. Leeds can become more sustainable through connected long-term strategies, but success must be measured across environmental, social and economic outcomes.
