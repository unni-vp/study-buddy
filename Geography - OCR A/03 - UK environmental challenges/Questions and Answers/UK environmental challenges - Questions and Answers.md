# UK environmental challenges questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** What is an air mass?

**Answer:** A large body of air with similar temperature and moisture.

**2.** Which air mass usually brings mild, wet weather from the Atlantic?

**Answer:** Tropical maritime.

**3.** What is continentality?

**Answer:** The effect of distance from the sea on climate.

**4.** Give one renewable energy source.

**Answer:** Wind power.

**5.** What gas makes biogas useful as a fuel?

**Answer:** Methane.

**6.** Name the UK flood case study used in this course.

**Answer:** Somerset Levels.

## 2 marks

**7.** Explain how the North Atlantic Drift affects the UK climate.

**Answer:**

- It transfers warm ocean water towards north-west Europe.
- This helps make UK winters milder than places at a similar latitude.

**8.** Explain why western uplands are generally wetter than eastern lowlands.

**Answer:**

- Moist Atlantic air is forced to rise over upland relief.
- It cools, condenses and produces rainfall before descending towards the east.

**9.** Give two effects of flooding on people.

**Answer:**

- Homes or businesses may be damaged.
- Roads may be cut off, disrupting travel and services.

**10.** Give one advantage and one disadvantage of a reservoir.

**Answer:**

- Advantage: it stores water for periods of high demand or low rainfall.
- Disadvantage: it floods land and can damage habitats or communities.

**11.** Explain why wind power needs support from storage or other energy sources.

**Answer:**

- Wind speed varies, so output is intermittent.
- Supply must still meet demand when turbines generate little electricity.

**12.** Give two products of anaerobic digestion.

**Answer:**

- Biogas.
- Digestate.

## 3 marks

**13.** Explain how prolonged rainfall can cause river flooding.

**Answer:**

- Rain fills soil stores until the ground becomes saturated.
- More water then flows over the surface or rapidly through the soil into rivers.
- Discharge rises above channel capacity and water spills onto the floodplain.

**14.** Explain why the Somerset Levels are vulnerable to flooding.

**Answer:**

- Much of the landscape is low-lying and very flat.
- Water drains slowly, especially when the ground is saturated and rivers are high.
- Tidal conditions and limited channel capacity can make it difficult to remove floodwater.

**15.** Explain one environmental problem caused by intensive food production.

**Answer:**

- Large fertiliser inputs can be washed into rivers.
- Nutrients encourage excessive algal growth.
- Decomposition then reduces oxygen, harming aquatic life.

**16.** Explain how anaerobic digestion produces useful energy.

**Answer:**

- Microorganisms break down organic material in a sealed tank without oxygen.
- This releases methane-rich biogas.
- The gas is burned in suitable equipment to produce heat or electricity.

## 4 marks

**17.** Explain how air masses and continentality create contrasting UK weather.

**Answer:**

- Tropical maritime air crosses the Atlantic and brings mild, moist conditions.
- Polar continental air can bring cold, dry conditions from eastern Europe.
- Coastal places are moderated by the sea because water heats and cools slowly.
- Inland areas have a greater temperature range because land changes temperature faster.

**18.** Explain how two long-term strategies could reduce flood risk on the Somerset Levels.

**Answer:**

- Maintaining or dredging selected channels can increase the amount of water carried away, although effects downstream must be considered.
- Floodplain or wetland storage holds water back and lowers the peak discharge reaching vulnerable settlements.

**19.** Explain why the UK's energy mix has changed since 1950.

**Answer:**

- Domestic coal declined as mines closed and pollution and carbon concerns grew.
- Oil and gas became important for transport, heating and electricity.
- Nuclear provided dependable low-operational-carbon electricity.
- Falling costs, technology and climate policy encouraged growth in renewables.

**20.** Explain two disadvantages of Hinkley Point C.

**Answer:**

- Its construction is expensive and lengthy, so benefits take a long time to arrive.
- Nuclear waste remains radioactive and requires secure long-term management.

## 6 marks

**21.** Assess the success of flood management on the Somerset Levels.

**Answer:**

Emergency warnings, evacuation and pumping can reduce danger during a flood. Longer-term channel maintenance, drainage improvements, raised infrastructure and floodwater storage can reduce the frequency or severity of disruption. However, no method removes risk from a low-lying landscape during prolonged extreme rainfall. Dredging and faster drainage may transfer water downstream, while hard defences cost money and need maintenance. A successful strategy therefore combines warning and protection with space to store water, and is judged over several events rather than by whether flooding ever occurs again.

**22.** Evaluate Hinkley Point C as a sustainable response to UK energy needs.

**Answer:**

Hinkley Point C can provide large quantities of dependable electricity with low operational carbon emissions. This supports energy security and climate goals, while construction and operation create employment. However, the project has high financial costs, a long construction period and local environmental and transport impacts. Radioactive waste also requires secure management far into the future. It is more sustainable when judged as part of a varied low-carbon mix rather than a complete solution. Its national reliability benefits must be weighed against cost, construction impacts and long-term waste responsibility.

**23.** Assess anaerobic digestion at Dryholme Farm as a local energy strategy.

**Answer:**

Anaerobic digestion converts organic material into biogas that can supply controllable heat and electricity. The remaining digestate may replace some manufactured fertiliser, so material is reused rather than simply discarded. This can lower fossil-fuel use and provide income. However, tanks and generators cost money, feedstock transport may add traffic and emissions, and poor management could cause odour or methane leakage. It is a strong local strategy where suitable material is available nearby and the plant is carefully managed, but its output is smaller than that of national-scale projects.

**24.** Evaluate whether reducing demand is better than increasing resource supply.

**Answer:**

Reducing leakage, waste and energy use can cut bills and environmental damage without flooding land for reservoirs or building new power stations. Efficiency measures can act across many homes and businesses. However, they require investment and behaviour change, and demand cannot be reduced to zero. Population, industry and extreme weather may still require secure additional supplies. New reservoirs or power generation can improve reliability but create cost and environmental impacts. The most resilient approach combines demand reduction first with carefully chosen, diverse supplies for the remaining need.
