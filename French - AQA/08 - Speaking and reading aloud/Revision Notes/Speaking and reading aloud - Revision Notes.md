# Speaking and reading aloud

## The speaking tasks

Speaking is a conversation, not a recital. Build answers like a small bridge: **answer the question, add a reason, then give a relevant detail**.

The test lasts **10–12 minutes**, with **15 minutes of supervised preparation**. The three tasks total 50 marks:

| Task | What you do | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Role-play | Complete five short tasks, including asking a question | 10 |
| Reading aloud | Read a prepared short text, then answer four unprepared questions | 15 |
| Photo card | Describe two photos, then take part in an unprepared conversation | 25 |

In preparation, you can make written notes and use them during the test. **Do not read the text aloud during preparation.** Dictionaries are not allowed. Prepare for all three themes; the questions in the unprepared parts are not given in advance.

## Role-play: communicate exactly

Each role-play task is worth **2 marks**. Follow its English instruction and give the requested information. A clear, accurate sentence usually works better than an unrelated memorised speech.

| Need | Useful French |
|---|---|
| request | Je voudrais un billet pour Paris. |
| explain a problem | Il y a un problème avec ma chambre. |
| give a preference | Je préfère le train parce que c'est confortable. |
| ask a price | Combien coûte le billet ? |
| ask a time | À quelle heure part le train ? |
| ask a location | Où est la gare ? |

**Question words:** où = where; quand = when; pourquoi = why; comment = how; combien = how much/how many; quel/quelle = which/what.

If asked about yesterday, use a past form. If asked to request something, **je voudrais + noun/infinitive** is useful. Include all details in the prompt: a room **for two people** needs **pour deux personnes**.

## Reading aloud with meaning

The Higher text has **at least 50 words**. Pronunciation is worth **5 marks**. The following four compulsory questions form a conversation marked **as a whole for 10 marks**, rather than separate identical marks per reply.

During silent preparation:

- Break the text into meaningful phrases at punctuation.
- Identify **u/ou**, nasal vowels, silent endings and liaison.
- Notice infinitive -er and participle -é, normally pronounced /e/.
- Work out the meaning, so your phrasing supports it.

Read at a steady pace. If you stumble, correct the word briefly and continue. Do not race to hide a difficult sound.

### Short reading practice

> Dans ma ville, il y a un grand parc où je retrouve mes amis après les cours. Nous aimons jouer au football, mais parfois nous préférons discuter. La semaine dernière, nous avons organisé un pique-nique. C'était une journée agréable parce qu'il faisait beau. À l'avenir, j'aimerais participer à un projet pour protéger les espaces verts près de chez moi.

Notice **où**, the silent plural endings in amis/cours, **j'aimerais**, and the vowel groups in journée/beau. Follow the punctuation; the text moves from normal activities to a past event and a future wish.

The unprepared questions ask for **your responses**, not just a translation of the paragraph. Listen to the question's verb and time phrase.

## Describing both photos

Talk about **both images**. Roughly 1½ minutes is recommended for the description; coverage need not be exactly equal. At least one relevant statement about each photo is necessary, but two minimal statements do not by themselves make a developed response.

| Purpose | Phrase | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| introduce | Sur la première photo, je vois... | In the first photo, I see... |
| locate | au premier plan / à l'arrière-plan | in the foreground / background |
| describe an action | les personnes jouent / mangent / travaillent | the people play / eat / work |
| add an observation | il fait beau; les personnes sourient | the weather is nice; the people are smiling |
| qualify an inference | ils ont l'air contents | they look happy |
| move to second image | Sur la deuxième photo... | In the second photo... |

Use visible details first: people, setting, actions and objects. **Ils ont l'air...** marks an impression; do not present an invented name, age or relationship as a visible fact.

Describe what is there. If you do not know one object, choose a different accurate detail. A comparison can help when it is supported, but is not a compulsory formula.

## Developing the conversation

After the photos, the unprepared conversation lasts about **4½–5½ minutes** and can use any of the three topics within the photo card's theme. The description has 5 marks; the conversation has **15 for communication and 5 for language**.

Answer the actual question, then develop it:

**J'aime mon quartier parce qu'il est calme. Il y a un parc où je retrouve mes amis. Cependant, il n'y a pas assez de bus. L'année prochaine, j'aimerais participer à un projet pour améliorer le quartier.**

I like my neighbourhood because it is quiet. There is a park where I meet my friends. However, there are not enough buses. Next year, I would like to join a project to improve the neighbourhood.

The **reason**, **relative clause**, **contrast** and **future wish** add meaningful detail. They are not a checklist to force into every short answer.

Useful time frames:

- **Normalement, je...** — normally, I...
- **La semaine dernière, j'ai...** — last week, I...
- **Quand j'étais petit(e), je...** — when I was young, I...
- **Demain, je vais...** — tomorrow, I am going to...
- **À l'avenir, j'aimerais...** — in future, I would like...

## Listening and recovering

Question clues: **Qu'est-ce que tu as fait ?** asks what you did; **Qu'est-ce que tu vas faire ?** asks what you are going to do; **Pourquoi ?** needs a reason.

**Vous pouvez répéter, s'il vous plaît ?** — Could you repeat, please? A brief request can help when you miss a question. The role-play examiner must follow its task script, so do not depend on being coached towards an answer.

If a word disappears from memory, simplify: **c'est utile**, **je peux aider**, **j'aime travailler avec les autres**. Keep communicating rather than switching to a long unrelated paragraph.

### Common mistakes

- Preparing only one theme or assuming the conversation questions are preselected for you.
- Describing only one of the two photos.
- Giving an answer when instructed to **ask** a question.
- Using yesterday with a present-tense account throughout.
- Reading the text aloud during the supervised preparation period.
- Speaking so quickly that otherwise correct sounds become unclear.
