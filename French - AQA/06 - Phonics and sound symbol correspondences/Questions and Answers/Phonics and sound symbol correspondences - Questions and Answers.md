# Phonics and sound symbol correspondences questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** Which letter group gives /u/ in sous?

**Answer:** ou.

**2.** Which vowel is different from ou in dur?

**Answer:** u.

**3.** What sound does qu usually make?

**Answer:** /k/.

**4.** What sound does French th make?

**Answer:** /t/.

**5.** What sound does ç make?

**Answer:** /s/.

**6.** Which final verb letters are silent in ils parlent?

**Answer:** -ent.

**7.** Does the h in homme make an English h sound?

**Answer:** No.

**8.** What linking sound can final s make in les amis?

**Answer:** /z/.

**9.** What linking sound can final x make in deux amis?

**Answer:** /z/.

**10.** What linking sound is heard in petit ami?

**Answer:** /t/.

**11.** What consonant links in un ami?

**Answer:** /n/.

**12.** Which sound group appears at the end of travail?

**Answer:** -ail, /aj/.

## 2 marks

**13.** Explain how your mouth position differs for u and ou.

**Answer:**

- U: tongue forward, lips rounded.
- Ou: tongue further back, lips rounded.

**14.** Explain what a nasal vowel means for grand.

**Answer:** Some air passes through the nose. Do not add a separate strong n after its /ɑ̃/ vowel.

**15.** Give two spellings that can represent the /o/ sound in beau.

**Answer:** Au and eau. Closed o/ô can also represent it.

**16.** Explain the pronunciation of infinitive parler and vous parlez endings.

**Answer:** Both endings normally give /e/. Their different written endings still show different grammatical forms.

**17.** Why is ville an important exception when learning -ille?

**Answer:** Ville has an /l/ sound, while words such as famille commonly have /j/. Not every -ille word follows one rule.

**18.** Explain why a single s differs in maison and chanson.

**Answer:** In maison it lies between vowel sounds and is usually /z/. In chanson it is followed by a consonant and is /s/.

**19.** Explain the difference between bon and bonne.

**Answer:** Bon has a nasal vowel; bonne has a pronounced n and a non-nasal vowel. The doubled consonant changes the sound pattern.

**20.** Explain why et amis should not be read with a linking t.

**Answer:** There is no liaison after **et**, even though the next word starts with a vowel.

**21.** What changes between les hommes and les héros?

**Answer:** Les hommes allows /z/ liaison. Héros has an h aspiré, which blocks liaison; neither h is pronounced like English h.

**22.** Compare French ch and j.

**Answer:** Ch is /ʃ/ as in chose; j is voiced /ʒ/ as in jeunesse.

## 3 marks

**23.** Identify the sound groups in beau, matin and campagne.

**Answer:**

- Beau: eau, /o/.
- Matin: in, /ɛ̃/.
- Campagne: gn, /ɲ/.

**24.** Identify the sound groups in cœur, porte and application.

**Answer:**

- Cœur: open œu, /œ/.
- Porte: open o, /ɔ/.
- Application: -tion, /sjɔ̃/.

**25.** Explain three pronunciation checks for Les petits enfants jouent.

**Answer:**

- No liaison between les and petits, because petits starts with a consonant.
- Petits enfants can link with /z/.
- The verb ending -ent in jouent is silent.

**26.** Why can je parle, tu parles and ils parlent sound similar, but require different spellings?

**Answer:**

- Regular -e/-es/-ent endings can be silent.
- The subject identifies the person and number.
- Written agreement is still required even when it is not audible.

## 4 marks

**27.** A learner says every final consonant is silent and every vowel-starting next word causes liaison. Correct both claims with examples.

**Answer:**

- Many final consonants are silent, but **avec** has a final /k/.
- Some final r sounds are heard, as in **partir**.
- Liaison depends on the word combination: **les amis** allows it.
- **Et amis** has no liaison after et.

**28.** Explain four checks when writing a sentence from dictation.

**Answer:**

- Match letter groups to the heard vowel and consonant sounds.
- Separate liaison sounds from the next word's spelling.
- Use the subject to recover silent verb endings.
- Check noun/adjective number and gender, including silent endings.
