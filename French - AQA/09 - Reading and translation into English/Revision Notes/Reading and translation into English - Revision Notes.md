# Reading and translation into English

## Reading for the question

Reading is like looking through a map: first locate the relevant area, then check the precise detail. Do not translate a whole passage before looking at its questions.

The paper lasts **one hour** and has **50 marks**: comprehension is 40 and French-to-English translation is 10. Comprehension answers are in English or use the requested non-verbal response. Dictionaries are not allowed.

1. Read the question and identify **who, what, where, when or why**.
2. Find the matching part of the text.
3. Check the subject, verb, time phrase, negative and qualifying words.
4. Answer the exact request, including both details if it asks for two.

**Léa aime le cinéma, mais son frère préfère le sport.** — Léa likes cinema, but her brother prefers sport. A question about the brother needs his preference, not Léa's.

## Opinions, changes and reference

**Avant, je trouvais les maths difficiles. Maintenant, j'aime cette matière parce que le professeur explique bien.**

Before, I found maths difficult. Now, I like this subject because the teacher explains well. The opinion changes: difficult is the **earlier** view; a positive view and its reason are **current**.

| Clue | Meaning to preserve |
|---|---|
| ne...jamais | never |
| ne...plus | no longer |
| ne...que | only |
| pas encore | not yet |
| sauf | except |
| malgré | despite |
| moins cher | cheaper |
| trop cher | too expensive |

Track pronouns: **Ma sœur aime la lecture. Elle lit souvent.** Elle refers to the sister. **Les livres sont chers, mais je les achète.** Les replaces the books.

Distinguish fact from opinion: **il y a trois magasins** states a number; **les magasins sont agréables** expresses a judgement. For a mixed view, preserve both sides.

## Unfamiliar words and inference

Use several clues rather than the first guess:

- **Context:** what situation is being described?
- **Grammar:** is the word a person, action, description or manner?
- **Word family:** does its base or ending help?
- **Logic:** does the proposed meaning fit the complete sentence?

**Cette solution est impossible.** Knowing possible and the opposite prefix im- helps you infer impossible.

**Elle répond rapidement.** Rapide means quick; -ment gives the adverb **quickly**.

**Les consommateurs achètent moins de produits.** Consommer means consume; consommateur refers to a **consumer**. The surrounding achats help check the inference.

A **cognate** looks like an English word and shares its meaning, such as important. A **false friend** looks familiar but differs: **actuellement** means currently; **une librairie** is a bookshop. Familiar spelling is a clue, not proof.

Use any glossary supplied beside a text. If one word remains unclear, continue with the information you can establish; do not invent a whole interpretation from it.

## Translating meaning into English

The translation contains **at least 50 French words**. Translate the whole passage into natural English, keeping its meaning. You do not need to preserve French word order where English works differently.

Break long sentences into meaning units. Check:

| French construction | Natural English |
|---|---|
| j'ai quinze ans | I am fifteen |
| j'ai faim | I am hungry |
| il y a / il y avait | there is/are / there was/were |
| il faut + infinitive | it is necessary to / must / need to |
| j'habite ici depuis deux ans | I have lived here for two years |
| je viens de finir | I have just finished |
| je voudrais | I would like |
| je ne regarde plus | I no longer watch |

**Je suis allé(e)** means I went / have gone, not I am going. **J'allais** can mean I used to go / was going. **Je vais aller** means I am going to go. Choose the English that fits the time frame and context.

### Keep the small words that change the message

| French | Accurate meaning | Meaning lost in a weak answer |
|---|---|---|
| Elle ne travaille plus ici. | She no longer works here. | She does not work loses the change over time. |
| Il n'a que deux euros. | He only has two euros. | He has two euros loses the restriction. |
| Nous n'avons pas encore fini. | We have not finished yet. | We have finished reverses the meaning. |
| Je dois partir. | I must leave. | I want to leave changes obligation into desire. |

Check the message in small units, then read the English as a whole. A natural sentence can change the French word order, but it must not discard a negative, comparison, time reference or degree word.

## Worked translation

**La semaine dernière, je suis allée au cinéma avec ma sœur. Le film était intéressant, mais les billets étaient trop chers. D'habitude, je préfère regarder des films à la maison parce que c'est plus calme. À l'avenir, j'aimerais aller au cinéma moins souvent et économiser de l'argent pour mes prochaines vacances.**

Last week, I went to the cinema with my sister. The film was interesting, but the tickets were too expensive. Usually, I prefer watching films at home because it is quieter. In future, I would like to go to the cinema less often and save money for my next holidays.

- **La semaine dernière + suis allée:** completed past.
- **Étaient trop chers:** past description with the important too.
- **D'habitude:** a usual preference, not just one visit.
- **Plus calme:** quieter, preserving the comparison.
- **J'aimerais:** would like, not will definitely.
- **Moins souvent:** less often, not never.

### Work through an unfamiliar sentence

**Mes amis veulent visiter le musée, mais je préfère rester chez moi parce que j'ai trop de devoirs.**

1. **Mes amis veulent visiter le musée:** My friends want to visit the museum. Veulent supplies the wish; visiter is the following infinitive.
2. **Mais je préfère rester chez moi:** but I prefer to stay at home. The subject changes from my friends to I.
3. **Parce que j'ai trop de devoirs:** because I have too much homework. Trop is essential to the reason.

**Complete translation:** My friends want to visit the museum, but I prefer to stay at home because I have too much homework.

Check each part against the French instead of stopping once you have recognised the general topic of leisure.

## Checking an answer

Read your English version as a complete message. Then compare it against the French phrase by phrase.

- Have you translated **every sentence**?
- Is each subject correct: I, we, they or someone else?
- Have past, present, future and conditional meanings survived?
- Have you preserved negatives, comparisons and degree words?
- Do possessives and object pronouns refer to the correct people/things?
- Are dates, times and numbers accurate?
- Have you added anything the French does not say?

**Je travaille dans une librairie** is I work in a bookshop, not I work in a library. **J'aime apprendre** is I like learning, not I like learn. Natural English and precise meaning work together.

For comprehension, a concise answer is usually enough. For translation, leave no phrase untranslated just because you already conveyed the general topic.
