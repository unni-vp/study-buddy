# Working scientifically and required practicals questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** What is an independent variable?

**Answer:** The variable deliberately changed or selected by the investigator.

**2.** What is instrument resolution?

**Answer:** The smallest change the instrument can distinguish.

**3.** What does accuracy mean?

**Answer:** Closeness to the true value.

**4.** What type of error makes every reading consistently too high by the same amount?

**Answer:** Systematic error.

**5.** Which meter is connected in series to measure current?

**Answer:** An ammeter.

**6.** What must be subtracted from loaded spring length to calculate extension?

**Answer:** The unloaded length.

**7.** What is the independent variable when comparing the effect of insulation thickness?

**Answer:** Insulation thickness.

## 2 marks

**8.** Distinguish repeatability from reproducibility.

**Answer:**

- **Repeatability:** the same investigator and conditions give similar repeated results.
- **Reproducibility:** different investigators or equipment give similar results.

**9.** Why does taking a mean of repeats help with random error but not with an unchanged zero offset?

**Answer:**

- Random fluctuations can partly cancel, reducing their effect on the **mean**.
- A zero offset shifts all readings systematically, so the mean **retains the offset** unless corrected.

**10.** Why should an anomalous reading not be discarded automatically?

**Answer:**

- It may be real, or indicate a cause that needs investigation.
- **Repeat and check the method**; exclude it only for a justified measurement failure, recording the reason.

**11.** A student tests longer wires but also uses thicker wire for the longest lengths. Why is this not a valid length-only comparison?

**Answer:**

- **Cross-sectional area also changes resistance**.
- The result cannot isolate length; keep material, area and temperature constant.

**12.** Why is a floating object unsuitable for a simple displacement-volume method unless the method is adapted?

**Answer:**

- It is not **fully submerged**, so it displaces less than its full volume.
- Submerge it using a suitable adapted method and account for any extra object used.

**13.** Why should a resistance-wire circuit be switched off between measurements?

**Answer:**

- Current **heats the wire**.
- Temperature changes resistance, so cooling helps keep a **control variable constant** and reduces burn risk.

**14.** In an insulation comparison, give two important controls besides the insulation itself.

**Answer:**

- Same **water mass and starting temperature**.
- Same **container, lid and room conditions**, with matched measurement intervals.

**15.** Explain why resistance is calculated from both voltage and current rather than current alone.

**Answer:**

- **R = V/I**.
- Current also depends on applied voltage, so comparing I alone at different V would not isolate resistance.

**16.** Why does measuring several wavelengths improve a ripple-tank wavelength estimate?

**Answer:**

- The same absolute ruler uncertainty is a **smaller fraction of a longer measured distance**.
- Divide that distance by the number of **complete wavelengths** to find λ.

**17.** For a fixed resistor, a current–voltage graph has current on y and voltage on x. What does its straight-line gradient represent?

**Answer:**

- Gradient = **I/V**.
- It is **1/R**, not R, for a constant-temperature ohmic resistor.

**18.** Give one suitable way to reduce parallax in the spring practical and explain it.

**Answer:**

- Read the pointer and ruler **at eye level**.
- This reduces apparent displacement from viewing at an angle.

**19.** Why is a microphone/data-logger method more suitable than a handheld stopwatch for a sound travel-time delay of 0.003 s?

**Answer:**

- Human reaction time is much larger than the short delay.
- Electronic timing can **resolve the delay** with much smaller timing error.

**20.** What is the difference between a hazard and risk?

**Answer:**

- A **hazard** is a potential source of harm.
- **Risk** considers the likelihood and seriousness of harm in the actual situation.

**21.** Explain why a strong correlation alone does not prove causation.

**Answer:**

- Another uncontrolled variable or biased sample could produce the relationship.
- Further **controlled evidence and a plausible mechanism** are needed before attributing a cause.

## 3 marks

**22.** Three repeated times are 1.8, 2.0 and 1.9 s. Calculate the mean, range and half-range uncertainty estimate.

**Answer:**

- Mean = (1.8 + 2.0 + 1.9)/3 = **1.9 s**.
- Range = 2.0 − 1.8 = **0.2 s**.
- Half-range estimate = **±0.1 s**, with other sources of uncertainty considered separately.

**23.** An empty cylinder has mass 80 g. With 50 cm³ of liquid it has mass 120 g. Calculate liquid density.

**Answer:**

- Liquid mass = 120 − 80 = **40 g**.
- ρ = m/V = 40/50 = **0.80 g/cm³**.
- This equals **800 kg/m³**.

**24.** A spring measures 12.0 cm unloaded and 15.0 cm under a 6.0 N load. Calculate extension and spring constant within the proportional region.

**Answer:**

- Extension = 15.0 − 12.0 = **3.0 cm = 0.030 m**.
- k = F/e = 6.0/0.030.
- **k = 200 N/m**.

**25.** A heater supplies 8,000 J to a 0.40 kg block, raising its temperature by 20°C. Calculate specific heat capacity using the measured input.

**Answer:**

- **c = E/(mΔθ)**.
- c = 8,000/(0.40 × 20).
- **1,000 J/kg°C**, assuming the input energy heats the block.

**26.** Explain why heat loss to the room generally makes a metal-block specific-heat result too high when all electrical input is used in the calculation.

**Answer:**

- Not all supplied energy increases the **block’s thermal store**.
- The measured temperature rise is smaller than the ideal rise.
- In **c = E/(mΔθ)**, using all input E with that smaller Δθ overestimates c. Insulation reduces the error.

**27.** Explain how to vary force while keeping total moving mass fixed in a trolley-and-hanger acceleration experiment.

**Answer:**

- Transfer masses **from trolley to hanger** rather than adding new mass to the system.
- Total moving mass stays the same, while hanging weight increases.
- Measure acceleration for each driving force, keeping track and friction conditions comparable.

**28.** A student compares IR readings from two cube faces, but the detector is closer to the second face. Evaluate the comparison.

**Answer:**

- Detector reading depends on **distance**, not just surface finish.
- The change makes the finish comparison invalid.
- Use the **same distance and angle**, matched surface temperature, and repeat readings.

**29.** Describe how to check whether repeated reflection measurements support the reflection law.

**Answer:**

- Measure incidence and reflection angles **from the normal**.
- Repeat reflection readings and calculate the mean at each incidence angle.
- Compare **mean r with i**, considering measurement uncertainty and protractor resolution.

**30.** Why does a filament lamp’s I–V graph curve even when its circuit is connected correctly?

**Answer:**

- Increasing current heats the filament.
- Higher temperature increases **resistance**.
- Current then rises less rapidly with voltage, so the I–V gradient decreases.

**31.** Explain why peer review and reproduction are useful before a scientific claim is widely accepted.

**Answer:**

- Specialists check **methods, evidence and reasoning** during peer review.
- Independent investigations test whether results are **reproducible**.
- These steps can expose weaknesses, although neither guarantees a claim is permanently correct.

## 4 marks

**32.** Describe how to measure the density of an irregular solid that sinks and does not dissolve in water.

**Answer:**

- Measure mass with a balance, checking its zero.
- Record an initial water volume in a measuring cylinder or use a displacement can.
- Fully submerge the solid, removing trapped bubbles, and measure **displaced volume**.
- Calculate **ρ = m/V** in matching units; read the meniscus at eye level and repeat suitably.

**33.** Describe how to investigate the I–V characteristic of a resistor.

**Answer:**

- Connect ammeter in **series**, voltmeter **across the resistor** and an adjustable low-voltage supply.
- Change voltage through a suitable range, recording paired V and I readings.
- Obtain negative readings by reversing polarity where appropriate and repeat. Limit heating to keep temperature approximately constant.
- Plot **I against V** and assess the best-fit line; a constant-temperature ohmic resistor gives a straight line through the origin.

**34.** Explain how to measure wave speed on a stretched string using a stationary pattern.

**Answer:**

- Use a vibration generator and signal generator with fixed string type and tension.
- Adjust frequency until a clear stationary pattern forms; record **f**.
- Measure several loop lengths. Each loop is **λ/2**, so find λ from the total distance.
- Calculate **v = fλ**, repeat and keep hanging loads secure.

**35.** A student says, “My readings repeat closely, so they must be accurate.” Evaluate this claim and suggest a check.

**Answer:**

- Closely clustered readings show **precision and repeatability** under the same conditions.
- They could all contain the same **systematic error**, such as a zero offset.
- Check zero and calibration against a reference, or use an independent appropriate instrument.
- Compare with the expected or reference value and its uncertainty before concluding accuracy.

## 6 marks

**36.** Plan an investigation of how insulation thickness affects cooling of water. Include controls, measurements and a useful conclusion.

**Answer:**

- Use identical containers with different thicknesses of the **same insulation material**. Match the exposed area and use comparable lids.
- Put the same mass of water in each, at the **same starting temperature**, and keep room conditions and draught exposure comparable.
- Record temperature at fixed equal intervals for the same total time, using suitable thermometers in consistent positions.
- Repeat each thickness and calculate means. Compare temperature drops over the same time or plot comparable cooling curves.
- The thickness with the smaller drop reduces cooling more under those conditions. Use actual data to judge the trend and allow for uncertainty.
- Handle hot water safely on heatproof surfaces. A valid comparison controls material and initial conditions rather than changing them alongside thickness.

**37.** Describe a complete investigation of how wire length affects resistance. Include safety, controls and analysis.

**Answer:**

- Connect a low-voltage supply and ammeter in series with a test wire. Connect a voltmeter across the selected length.
- Use a ruler and movable contact to test several measured lengths, including repeated readings.
- Keep **wire material, cross-sectional area and temperature** constant. Limit current and switch off between measurements to reduce heating.
- At each length, measure **V and I**, then calculate **R = V/I**. Do not compare current alone if voltage varies.
- Plot mean R against length with quantities and units on the axes. Assess whether the best-fit line supports a proportional relationship, considering uncertainty and contact resistance.
- Check connections before switching on and avoid short circuits or hot wire. Give the observed trend with data rather than assuming every result must lie exactly on a line.

**38.** Plan a trolley investigation of acceleration at different masses while keeping driving force approximately constant. Explain how the data test the relationship.

**Answer:**

- Use a low-friction track, trolley, pulley, string and a fixed hanging mass. Secure the pulley and arrange a safe trolley stop.
- Add mass to the trolley while keeping the **hanging mass fixed**. Record the total moving mass, including the hanger.
- Use light gates or a motion sensor to measure velocities and time, then find **a = Δv/Δt** before the hanger reaches the floor.
- Repeat each mass and calculate mean acceleration. Keep track, string and friction conditions comparable, measuring or allowing for friction where possible.
- Plot acceleration against **1/total moving mass**. An approximately straight line supports inverse proportionality at fixed driving force.
- Explain the approximation: driving weight acts on the whole moving system; string tension on the trolley alone is not equal to hanging weight during acceleration.
