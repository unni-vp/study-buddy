# Electricity questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** Define electric current.

**Answer:** The rate of flow of electric charge.

**2.** What is the SI unit of resistance?

**Answer:** Ohm (Ω).

**3.** Which wire in a mains cable is brown?

**Answer:** Live.

**4.** What happens to the resistance of an LDR as light intensity increases?

**Answer:** It decreases.

## 2 marks

**5.** Explain the difference between direct and alternating potential difference.

**Answer:**

- Direct pd has **one polarity**.
- Alternating pd **repeatedly reverses polarity**, causing current to reverse direction.

**6.** A current of 0.25 A flows for 80 s. Calculate the charge transferred.

**Answer:** **Q = It** = 0.25 × 80 = **20 C**.

**7.** A component has 9.0 V across it and carries 0.30 A. Calculate its resistance.

**Answer:** **R = V/I** = 9.0/0.30 = **30 Ω**.

**8.** Where should an ammeter and a voltmeter be connected to measure a lamp’s resistance?

**Answer:**

- Ammeter **in series** with the lamp.
- Voltmeter **in parallel across** the lamp.

**9.** State the potential difference and frequency of the UK mains supply.

**Answer:** About **230 V**, at **50 Hz**.

**10.** Two parallel branches carry 0.40 A and 0.65 A from a 12 V supply. State the total current and the pd across each branch.

**Answer:**

- Total current = 0.40 + 0.65 = **1.05 A**.
- Each branch has **12 V** across it.

**11.** Explain why a rod becomes negative when rubbed with a cloth.

**Answer:**

- **Electrons transfer onto the rod** from the cloth.
- It has an excess of negative charge; protons do not transfer.

**12.** How does a thermistor’s resistance change when heated, and give one application.

**Answer:**

- Its resistance **decreases**.
- It can be a temperature sensor in a **thermostat**.

## 3 marks

**13.** A 5 Ω resistor and a 10 Ω resistor are in series across 6.0 V. Calculate the total resistance, the current and the pd across the 10 Ω resistor.

**Answer:**

- Rtotal = 5 + 10 = **15 Ω**.
- I = 6.0/15 = **0.40 A**.
- V across 10 Ω = 0.40 × 10 = **4.0 V**.

**14.** Explain why a filament lamp’s I–V graph becomes less steep at larger positive potential differences.

**Answer:**

- Increasing current **heats the filament**.
- Ions vibrate more, increasing opposition to electron flow, so **resistance increases**.
- Current then increases less rapidly with pd; the graph flattens.

**15.** An appliance receives 230 V and carries 4.0 A for 60 s. Calculate the energy transferred.

**Answer:**

- **P = VI** = 230 × 4.0 = **920 W**.
- **E = Pt** = 920 × 60.
- **55,200 J**.

**16.** Why does adding a resistor in parallel reduce total resistance when the supply pd is fixed?

**Answer:**

- It provides an **additional path** for charge.
- Total current from the supply **increases**.
- With the same V, **R = V/I decreases**.

**17.** Explain why transmitting a fixed power at high potential difference reduces losses in National Grid cables.

**Answer:**

- **P = VI** means higher V gives lower I for the same power.
- Cable heating power is **I²R**.
- Lower current therefore reduces unwanted heating and improves transmission efficiency.

**18.** Explain how an earth wire and fuse protect someone if a live wire touches a metal appliance case.

**Answer:**

- The earth wire provides a low-resistance path from the case to earth.
- A large **fault current** flows.
- The fuse **melts and breaks the live circuit**, disconnecting the supply.

**19.** Explain how a spark can form between a charged object and a nearby conductor.

**Answer:**

- The charge creates a **strong electric field** in the gap.
- The field **ionises air**, allowing it to conduct.
- Charge flows through the air as a spark, reducing the charge imbalance.

## 4 marks

**20.** A cable has resistance 0.80 Ω and carries 50 A. Calculate its heating power and the heating power if current is halved. State the factor change.

**Answer:**

- **P = I²R**.
- Initial P = 50² × 0.80 = **2,000 W**.
- New P = 25² × 0.80 = **500 W**.
- Heating falls to **one quarter**, because current is squared.

**21.** A component gives these readings: 2.0 V at 0.40 A, and 6.0 V at 0.80 A. Calculate both resistances and explain whether it is ohmic across this range.

**Answer:**

- First R = 2.0/0.40 = **5.0 Ω**.
- Second R = 6.0/0.80 = **7.5 Ω**.
- Resistance increases, so current is **not directly proportional** to pd.
- It is **not ohmic** across this range; a heating filament is one possible explanation.

## 6 marks

**22.** Describe how to investigate the I–V characteristic of a filament lamp. You may use this circuit as a starting point, replacing the test resistor with the lamp.

![Measurement circuit: ammeter in series and voltmeter across the test component.](../Diagrams/resistance-circuit.svg)

**Answer:**

- Connect a **low-voltage supply, ammeter and lamp in series**, with a voltmeter across the lamp. Add a variable resistor or use an adjustable supply.
- Vary pd over a suitable range below the lamp’s rating; record paired **V and I** readings at each setting.
- Reverse the supply and record negative V and I readings, using the meter correctly.
- Repeat measurements and investigate anomalies before calculating means. Avoid touching the hot lamp.
- Plot current vertically against pd horizontally and draw a smooth best-fit curve.
- The curve is approximately symmetric but becomes less steep as |V| increases because heating increases the filament’s resistance.

**23.** Plan an investigation of how wire length affects resistance. Include controls and how to process the results.

**Answer:**

- Place a uniform wire beside a ruler and use a movable contact to select measured lengths.
- Connect a low-voltage supply and ammeter in series; a voltmeter measures pd across the selected length.
- Record **V and I** for several lengths and calculate **R = V/I** each time.
- Keep material and cross-sectional area fixed. Use low current and switch off between measurements to keep **temperature** approximately constant.
- Repeat, find mean resistances and plot **R against length**. A straight line through the origin supports direct proportionality.
- Longer wire provides more interactions opposing electron flow. Check contact quality and avoid hot wire; heating would increase R for a second reason.
