# Atomic structure questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** Which particle has no electric charge and is found in the nucleus?

**Answer:** Neutron.

**2.** What is the atomic number?

**Answer:** The number of protons in a nucleus.

**3.** What is the SI unit of activity?

**Answer:** Becquerel (Bq).

**4.** What is an alpha particle?

**Answer:** A helium nucleus: two protons and two neutrons.

**5.** Which emission changes neither the mass number nor the atomic number of a nucleus? Choose one: A alpha; B beta-minus; C gamma; D neutron.

**Answer:** C: gamma.

## 2 marks

**6.** Explain what is meant by isotopes of an element.

**Answer:** Atoms with the **same number of protons** but **different numbers of neutrons**.

**7.** An atom has atomic number 11 and mass number 23. State its neutron number and the number of electrons in its singly positive ion.

**Answer:** - Neutrons = 23 − 11 = **12**.
- The ion has lost one electron: 11 − 1 = **10 electrons**.

**8.** Describe what happens when an electron absorbs radiation and then returns to its original energy level.

**Answer:** - It moves to a **higher energy level** after absorption.
- It **emits electromagnetic radiation** when returning to a lower level.

**9.** State how the mass number and atomic number change during beta-minus decay.

**Answer:** - Mass number **does not change**.
- Atomic number **increases by 1** because a neutron becomes a proton.

**10.** How does irradiation differ from contamination?

**Answer:** - **Irradiation** is exposure to radiation from a source.
- **Contamination** is radioactive material being present where it is unwanted.

**11.** Give one natural source and one human-made source of background radiation.

**Answer:** - Natural: **rocks/radon gas**.
- Human-made: **fallout from nuclear weapons tests**.

**12.** Why is radioactive decay described as random even though a sample has a predictable half-life?

**Answer:** - The decay time of an **individual nucleus cannot be predicted**.
- Large numbers follow a predictable overall halving pattern.

## 3 marks

**13.** An isotope has activity 960 Bq and half-life 4 hours. Calculate its activity after 12 hours.

**Answer:** - 12/4 = **3 half-lives**.
- 960 → 480 → 240 → 120.
- Activity = **120 Bq**.

**14.** An isotope undergoes four half-lives. State the fraction remaining, the fraction decayed and the ratio of decline to initial activity.

**Answer:** - Remaining = (½)⁴ = **1/16**.
- Decayed = 1 − 1/16 = **15/16**.
- Decline : initial = **15 : 16**.

**15.** A detector reads 110 counts/s with a source present and 10 counts/s without it. What total reading is expected after two half-lives?

**Answer:** - Initial source count rate = 110 − 10 = **100 counts/s**.
- After two half-lives, source rate = 100/4 = **25 counts/s**.
- Add unchanged background: **35 counts/s**.

**16.** A nucleus with mass number 226 and atomic number 88 emits an alpha particle. Find both numbers for the daughter nucleus and explain the change.

**Answer:** - Daughter mass number = 226 − 4 = **222**.
- Daughter atomic number = 88 − 2 = **86**.
- An alpha particle removes **two protons and two neutrons**.

**17.** Explain why most alpha particles pass through thin metal foil but very few are deflected backwards.

**Answer:** - Most of the atom is **empty space**.
- Positive alpha particles near the **positive nucleus** are repelled.
- Very large deflections are rare because the dense nucleus occupies a **tiny fraction of the atom**.

**18.** Explain why a gamma-emitting isotope with a suitable short half-life can be useful as a medical tracer.

**Answer:** - Gamma can **leave the body** and reach an external detector.
- The source lasts long enough to complete the investigation.
- A suitably short half-life reduces continuing activity and exposure afterwards.

**19.** Complete the alpha-decay equation by giving the mass number A and atomic number Z of thorium. Explain how both numbers balance.

²³⁸₉₂U → ᴬ_ZTh + ⁴₂He

**Answer:** **A = 234** and **Z = 90**, giving **²³⁸₉₂U → ²³⁴₉₀Th + ⁴₂He**. Mass numbers balance because 234 + 4 = 238. Atomic numbers balance because 90 + 2 = 92.

## 4 marks

**20.** Explain why an alpha-emitting contaminant can be much more dangerous inside the body than outside it.

**Answer:** - Alpha has **low penetrating power** and is largely stopped by outer skin.
- Inside the body it is close to living cells without that protection.
- It is **highly ionising**, depositing energy over a short distance.
- This can damage cells and DNA, increasing the risk of mutations and cancer.

**21.** Compare nuclear fission and nuclear fusion.

**Answer:** - Fission **splits a large unstable nucleus**, usually after neutron absorption.
- It releases smaller nuclei, neutrons, gamma radiation and energy; neutrons can continue a chain reaction.
- Fusion **joins light nuclei** into a heavier one.
- Fusion also releases energy as some mass is converted; it needs conditions allowing positive nuclei to overcome repulsion.

**22.** A detector measures a source for 60 s and records 3,600 counts. A background test records 300 counts in 60 s. Calculate the corrected source count rate and give two ways to improve the measurement.

**Answer:** - Background rate = 300/60 = **5 counts/s**.
- Corrected rate = 3,600/60 − 5 = **55 counts/s**.
- Count for longer or repeat to reduce relative random variation.
- Keep distance and shielding fixed so geometry does not change the detection rate.

## 5 marks

**23.** A detector has a constant background count rate of 4 counts/s. The readings below include the background. Determine the source's initial corrected count rate and half-life. Calculate the fraction of the initial source activity that has decayed after 15 minutes and predict the total detector reading at 20 minutes.

| Time (minutes) | Total count rate (counts/s) |
|---|---|
| 0 | 84 |
| 5 | 44 |
| 10 | 24 |
| 15 | 14 |

**Answer:** - Subtract background. Corrected source rates are **80, 40, 20 and 10 counts/s**.
- The corrected rate halves every **5 minutes**, so this is the half-life.
- Fifteen minutes is three half-lives. The fraction remaining is **1/8**, so the fraction decayed is **7/8**.
- At 20 minutes, four half-lives have passed. Source rate = 80 ÷ 16 = **5 counts/s**.
- Add background back: total expected reading = 5 + 4 = **9 counts/s**. Actual counts fluctuate randomly around the expected value.

## 6 marks

**24.** Explain how neutron absorption can lead to a chain reaction and how controlling the chain differs from an uncontrolled reaction.

**Answer:** - A large unstable nucleus **absorbs a neutron**.
- It splits into two smaller nuclei, releasing energy, gamma and **two or three neutrons**.
- The products carry kinetic energy, which can transfer to thermal stores through collisions.
- Released neutrons can be absorbed by other unstable nuclei, causing more fissions.
- In a reactor, neutron absorption by control rods helps keep the chain and energy release at a controlled rate.
- An uncontrolled multiplying chain allows very rapid energy release. It is the nuclei that split, not ordinary chemical bonds.

**25.** Two gamma tracers have half-lives of 10 minutes and 10 days. An organ investigation lasts 30 minutes. Explain how a scientist should choose between them; do not assume the shorter half-life is automatically suitable.

**Answer:** - Gamma is useful because it can leave the body and be detected externally.
- The 10-minute isotope undergoes **three half-lives**, leaving only **1/8** of its initial activity after 30 minutes.
- It may be suitable if enough signal remains; otherwise useful images could be lost or a larger initial activity might be needed.
- The 10-day isotope changes little during the test but may expose the patient for longer unless removed quickly from the body.
- Compare starting activity, biological removal, detector sensitivity and total dose, as well as half-life.
- Choose a source that provides an adequate signal with the smallest justified dose, balancing the medical benefit and exposure rather than choosing on half-life alone.
