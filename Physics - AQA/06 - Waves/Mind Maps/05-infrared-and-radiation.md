# Infrared and radiation balance

## Emission

All objects emit and absorb **infrared**.

Higher temperature means greater emitted power.

Emission spectrum shifts towards shorter wavelengths when hotter.

Visible glowing is not needed to emit IR.

## Black bodies

A perfect black body absorbs all incident radiation.

It reflects and transmits none.

It is the best emitter at a given temperature.

Matt black surfaces are useful approximations, not perfect in all circumstances.

## Power balance

**Absorbed = emitted:** temperature constant.

**Absorbed > emitted:** warms.

**Emitted > absorbed:** cools.

Include conduction or convection if they also transfer energy.

## Earth

Some sunlight is reflected. The rest can be absorbed.

Earth emits IR. Greenhouse gases absorb and emit IR.

Changing the balance changes temperature until a new balance is reached.

More reflected sunlight generally means less absorbed energy.

## Compare emission

Hot Leslie cube: differently finished faces share a temperature.

Keep detector distance, angle and surroundings constant.

Repeat readings. Matt black usually emits most, shiny metal less.

Do not confuse temperature effects with finish effects.

## Compare absorption

Use matched samples, equal mass and starting temperature.

Same IR lamp, distance, angle and exposure time.

Compare temperature rises. Repeat and average.

Keep electrics dry and avoid hot lamps or water.
