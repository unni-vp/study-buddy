# Heating & gas pressure

## Internal energy

- Total kinetic + potential energy of all particles. Temperature is related to average kinetic energy.
- **ΔE = mcΔθ** when temperature changes
- use kg, J/kg°C and a temperature difference.

## Latent heat

- **E = mL**
- L in J/kg.
- Change state without changing temperature.
- Melting/boiling: potential energy rises. Freezing/condensing releases energy. c and L describe different changes.

## Heating graphs

- Slopes: temperature rises. Plateaus: state changes while energy still enters.
- Constant-power heating: longer time means more input energy. Cooling gives the reverse changes.

## Combine stages

- 0.10 kg ice at 0°C → water at 20°C: mL + mcΔθ.
- With L = 334,000 and c = 4,200
- total = **41,800 J**.
- At 100 W, ideal time = 418 s
- losses add time.

## Fixed-volume heating

- Fixed mass: hotter → higher average kinetic energy → faster particles.
- More frequent wall collisions and larger momentum changes → greater force per area → greater pressure.

## Volume & compression

- Fixed mass, constant T: **pV constant**.
- 100 kPa × 60 cm³ = 150 kPa × 40 cm³.
- Rapid compression does work and may heat gas, so constant-T rule may not apply.
