# Orbits and satellites

## Satellite categories

**Planet:** orbits a star.

**Natural satellite:** a moon orbiting a planet.

**Artificial satellite:** constructed and placed in orbit.

All rely on gravity for orbital motion.

## Force versus velocity

For a circular orbit, gravity points towards the central body.

Velocity points along the tangent.

## Speed and acceleration

Speed can stay constant while direction changes.

Velocity includes direction, so velocity changes.

A velocity change is **acceleration**.

Zero acceleration is not a correct circular-orbit explanation.

## Stable radius

Compare circular orbits around the same central body.

Smaller radius has greater orbital speed.

Larger radius has lower orbital speed.

If speed changes, a stable circular orbital radius must change.

## Orbital calculations

**Distance in one orbit:** 2πr.

**Speed:** 2πr/T. Use metres and seconds.

7.0 × 10⁶ m, 6,000 s gives about **7.3 × 10³ m/s**.

Radius is from the central body’s centre.

## Read evidence carefully

Height above surface is not the orbital radius.

Add planet radius when required.

Gravity is an inward resultant, not cancelled by orbital motion.

A stable circular orbit comparison does not describe every rocket manoeuvre.
