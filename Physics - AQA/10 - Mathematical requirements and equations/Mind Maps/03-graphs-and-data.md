# Graphs and data

## Linear relationships

**y = mx + c:** gradient m, intercept c.

**Gradient = Δy/Δx.**

Use a large triangle on best-fit line.

F against extension gives k. I against V gives 1/R.

Axis order changes the gradient’s meaning.

## Curved graph

A tangent gives the rate at a chosen point.

On distance–time, its gradient gives instantaneous speed.

## Area and displacement

Velocity–time signed area gives displacement.

Area magnitudes add for distance.

## Area methods

Rectangle: base × height. Triangle: ½ × base × height.

Count squares for curved or irregular areas, allowing for each square’s units.

F–extension area gives work to spring.

Distance–time area does not give distance.

## Statistical summaries

Mean = sum/count.

Frequency tables need weighted sum.

Median: middle after sorting. Mode: most frequent.

Range: maximum − minimum.

Percentage change = change/original ×100.

## Chart interpretation

Bar charts compare categories. Scatter graphs show correlation.

Histogram frequency is area

density = frequency/interval width.

Correlation does not establish cause.

Use units, uncertainties and actual data in conclusions.
