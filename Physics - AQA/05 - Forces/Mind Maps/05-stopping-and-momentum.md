# Stopping & momentum

## Thinking & braking

- Stopping distance = thinking + braking.
- Thinking = speed × reaction time.
- Typical reaction time 0.2–0.9 s.
- Tiredness, drugs/alcohol and distractions can delay reaction. Ruler-drop tests: repeat, control setup, consider anticipation.

## Braking factors

- Wet/icy roads, worn tyres/brakes reduce effective braking. Friction transfers kinetic energy to thermal stores.
- Same mass/force: double speed → four times braking distance. Thinking only doubles at fixed reaction time. Large deceleration risks overheating/loss of control.

## Momentum is a vector

- **p = mv**, kg m/s.
- Choose a positive direction and use signed velocities.
- Closed system with zero resultant external force: total momentum before = after.

## Collisions & recoil

- 2 kg at 3 m/s joins stationary 1 kg: 6 kg m/s / 3 kg = **2 m/s**.
- Recoil from rest: equal opposite momenta, lighter mass moves faster. Kinetic energy need not be conserved.

## Force changes momentum

- **F = Δp/Δt**
- **F = m(v − u)/Δt**.
- Gives average force for the interval.
- 60 kg at 10 m/s → rest: |Δp|=600 kg m/s.
- Over 0.20 s → 3,000 N
- over 0.50 s → 1,200 N.

## Safety features

- Airbags, belts, helmets, mats and soft surfaces increase stopping time for the same momentum change.
- Average force is lower. They do not stop momentum changing or destroy energy.
