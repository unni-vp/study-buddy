# Rate and extent of chemical change questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** What is activation energy?

**Answer:** The minimum energy particles must have for a successful reaction.

**2.** Which rate unit applies when measuring moles consumed per second?

**Answer:** mol s⁻¹.

**3.** What does a horizontal product–time graph show?

**Answer:** No further measured product formation.

**4.** What does ⇌ represent?

**Answer:** A reversible reaction.

**5.** What must be equal at dynamic equilibrium?

**Answer:** Forward and reverse reaction rates.

**6.** What is meant by a closed reaction system?

**Answer:** Reactants and products cannot escape.

**7.** What solid makes thiosulfate/acid mixtures cloudy?

**Answer:** Sulfur.

**8.** At dynamic equilibrium, which statement must be true? Choose one: A reactant and product amounts are equal; B both reactions have stopped; C forward and reverse rates are equal; D all reactants have become products.

**Answer:** C: forward and reverse rates are equal.

## 2 marks

**9.** Calculate mean rate when 72 cm³ gas forms in 90 s.

**Answer:** - Mean rate = 72/90.
- **0.80 cm³ s⁻¹**.

**10.** Calculate molar rate if 0.020 mol reactant is used in 40 s.

**Answer:** - Rate = 0.020/40.
- **5.0 ×10⁻⁴ mol s⁻¹**.

**11.** Explain why higher concentration usually speeds a solution reaction.

**Answer:** - More reactant particles occupy each unit volume.
- There are **more frequent successful collisions**.

**12.** Explain why increased gas pressure at the same temperature can increase rate.

**Answer:** - Reacting gas particles are closer together.
- Collisions occur **more frequently**, increasing successful collisions per second.

**13.** Explain why a powder reacts faster than the same mass of large pieces.

**Answer:** - Powder has greater **exposed surface area**.
- More particles are available for collisions at the solid surface.

**14.** Explain how a catalyst increases reaction rate.

**Answer:** - It provides a **different pathway**.
- This pathway has **lower activation energy**.

**15.** Why must a stopper be fitted quickly during gas collection?

**Answer:** - Gas starts forming as soon as reactants mix.
- A delay lets gas escape, underestimating collected volume or altering measured endpoint time.

**16.** Explain why reaction rate commonly decreases as a batch reaction proceeds.

**Answer:** - Reactants are used up, reducing their concentration.
- Successful collisions become **less frequent**.

**17.** Explain why equilibrium does not mean equal amounts of reactant and product.

**Answer:** - Equilibrium requires **equal rates**, not equal quantities.
- Constant concentrations can be unequal while both reactions continue.

**18.** An exothermic forward reaction releases 80 kJ for a stated quantity. What is the reverse energy change?

**Answer:** - The reverse reaction is **endothermic**.
- It absorbs **80 kJ** for the corresponding quantity.

**19.** For A+B ⇌ C, predict the change after adding A and explain it.

**Answer:** - Equilibrium shifts **towards C**.
- More added A is used, opposing the concentration increase.

**20.** For A+B ⇌ C, predict the change after removing C.

**Answer:** - Equilibrium shifts **towards products**.
- More C forms, opposing its removal.

**21.** What is a catalyst’s effect on equilibrium yield and time to equilibrium?

**Answer:** - **No change in equilibrium yield/position**.
- Equilibrium is reached faster because both directions are accelerated.

## 3 marks

**22.** Explain the effect of higher temperature using collision theory.

**Answer:** - Particles gain kinetic energy and move faster.
- Collisions occur more frequently.
- A greater proportion has energy **at least equal to activation energy**, so more successful collisions occur per second.

**23.** A tangent has points (20 s, 18 cm³) and (60 s, 46 cm³). Calculate instantaneous rate.

**Answer:** - Volume change = 46−18 = **28 cm³**.
- Time change = 60−20 = **40 s**.
- Rate = 28/40 = **0.70 cm³ s⁻¹**.

**24.** The same turbidity endpoint is reached in 80 s and 50 s. Calculate the rate proxies and their ratio.

**Answer:** - Rates = 1/80 = **0.0125 s⁻¹** and 1/50 = **0.020 s⁻¹**.
- Ratio = 0.020/0.0125 = **1.6**.
- The 50 s condition is **1.6 times as fast** for this endpoint.

**25.** Explain why reducing temperature can increase equilibrium yield but slow production in an exothermic reaction.

**Answer:** - Cooling favours the **exothermic direction**, increasing equilibrium product proportion.
- Particles move more slowly and collide less frequently.
- Fewer collisions exceed activation energy, reducing rate.

**26.** For N₂(g)+3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), explain the effect of increased pressure on ammonia yield.

**Answer:** - Reactant side has **four gas moles**, product side two.
- Increased pressure favours the side with **fewer gas moles**.
- Equilibrium shifts towards ammonia, increasing its yield.

**27.** For H₂(g)+I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g), explain why pressure does not shift equilibrium position.

**Answer:** - Each side has **two gas moles**.
- Neither side reduces the gas-molecule total relative to the other.
- Pressure changes can affect reaction rates while leaving equilibrium position unchanged.

**28.** Describe the reversible changes in hydrated copper sulfate when heated and then treated with water.

**Answer:** - Heating removes water and changes **blue hydrated** copper sulfate to **white anhydrous** copper sulfate.
- Adding water restores the blue hydrated form.
- Dehydration absorbs energy; reverse hydration releases energy.

**29.** Why might two concentrations give different final gas volumes even with the same marble-chip mass?

**Answer:** - Changing concentration at fixed acid volume changes the amount of acid.
- At low concentration, acid may be the **limiting reactant**.
- If less carbonate reacts completely, less total gas forms; this is a yield difference as well as a rate difference.

## 4 marks

**30.** Describe how to calculate rate at a chosen point on a curved product–time graph.

**Answer:** - Draw a tangent touching the curve at the chosen time.
- Choose two well-separated points on the tangent, using actual axis scales.
- Calculate Δproduct/Δtime from a large triangle.
- State correct units; a chord gives an average over an interval instead.

**31.** Explain a catalyst’s effects on a reaction energy profile and on equilibrium.

**Answer:** - Alternative pathway has lower **activation-energy peak**.
- Reactant/product energy levels and overall energy transfer stay unchanged.
- Forward and reverse reactions both become faster.
- Equilibrium is reached sooner, with **unchanged equilibrium position**.

**32.** Explain why the disappearing-cross experiment needs controlled total volume and viewing conditions.

**Answer:** - Volume and flask shape affect solution depth.
- Different depth changes how much cloudiness hides the cross.
- Lighting, cross and observer affect endpoint judgement.
- Keeping these constant makes time comparisons better reflect concentration-dependent rate.

**33.** An exothermic equilibrium is 2A(g) ⇌ B(g). Explain the effects of cooling and reducing pressure separately.

**Answer:** - Cooling favours the **exothermic forward direction**.
- This increases B’s equilibrium proportion.
- Reducing pressure favours the side with **more gas moles**, two on the left versus one on the right.
- This decreases B’s equilibrium proportion; the two changes oppose each other, so no combined amount can be calculated without more data.

## 5 marks

**34.** Two reactions use the same mass of marble chips and hydrochloric acid in excess, at different concentrations. Use the table to calculate the mean gas-production rate over the first 40 s for A and B, and the ratio rate A : rate B. Explain why their final volumes can be the same despite their different rates.

| Time (s) | Gas from A (cm³) | Gas from B (cm³) |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | 0 | 0 |
| 20 | 30 | 18 |
| 40 | 48 | 30 |
| 80 | 60 | 60 |
| 100 | 60 | 60 |

**Answer:** - Mean rate A = (48 − 0) ÷ 40 = **1.2 cm³/s**.
- Mean rate B = (30 − 0) ÷ 40 = **0.75 cm³/s**.
- Rate A : rate B = 1.2 : 0.75 = **1.6 : 1**.
- Both stop at **60 cm³** because the same amount of marble chips is the **limiting reactant** and acid is in excess.
- Concentration changes collision frequency and therefore **how quickly** gas forms. It does not change the maximum gas amount when the limiting amount stays the same.

## 6 marks

**35.** Plan a valid concentration investigation using gas collection.

**Answer:** - Use a range of known acid concentrations with fixed total volume and temperature.
- React each with the same mass/surface area of a suitable carbonate and collect gas in a connected syringe.
- Mix/seal promptly and record volume at fixed intervals, checking for leaks.
- Repeat conditions and compare mean curves or common-interval rates.
- Keep the limiting-reactant situation clear if interpreting final volumes.
- Use dilute acid, eye protection and a syringe able to move freely; evaluate start-up loss and collection resolution.

**36.** Plan a concentration investigation using a change in turbidity.

**Answer:** - Prepare thiosulfate concentrations by dilution while keeping total solution volume constant.
- Add fixed acid amount in the same flask over the same cross and start timing.
- Control temperature, solution depth and viewing/endpoint conditions.
- Repeat, compare mean times and 1/time proxies for a common endpoint.
- Use suitable ventilation, small quantities and eye protection because acid and sulfur dioxide present hazards.
- Evaluate subjective judgement and consider a fixed light-sensor threshold as an improvement.

**37.** Evaluate choosing high temperature, high pressure and a catalyst for an exothermic industrial reaction with fewer product gas moles.

**Answer:** - Higher temperature increases rate but shifts equilibrium towards reactants, reducing equilibrium product yield.
- A lower temperature improves equilibrium yield but can make production too slow.
- Higher pressure favours fewer product gas moles and can increase rate, but compression costs energy and requires strong safe equipment.
- A catalyst lowers activation energy, increasing rate without changing equilibrium yield.
- Compare production per unit time, operating/plant costs and risks.
- A justified compromise seeks economical safe output, rather than simply maximum theoretical yield.
