# Glass, ceramics, polymers and composites

## Glass and clay

Soda-lime: sand, sodium carbonate, limestone.

Borosilicate: sand/boron trioxide, heat-resistant.

Clay ceramics: shape wet clay, furnace heat.

Hard/durable but often brittle.

## LDPE and HDPE

Both use ethene monomers.

Different conditions/catalysts change branching.

LDPE branches pack less closely.

HDPE closer packing gives higher density/rigidity.

## Heating and chain structure

Covalent cross-links versus separate chains.

## Thermal behaviour

Thermosoftening: chains move when attractions overcome.

Thermosetting: cross-links prevent melting.

Very strong heat can decompose material.

Do not confuse weak attractions with backbone bonds.

## Composites

Matrix/binder holds reinforcement.

Fibres/fragments supply useful properties.

Fibreglass, carbon-fibre, reinforced concrete.

Combined functions suit specialised uses.

## Select from data

Compare strength, density and temperature limits.

Consider corrosion, cost and lifetime.

Use equivalent functions, not arbitrary sample masses.

Link each needed property to the application.
