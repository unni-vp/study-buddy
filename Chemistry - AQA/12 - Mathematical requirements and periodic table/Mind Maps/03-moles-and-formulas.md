# Moles, stoichiometry and formulas

## Counting quantity

One mole: 6.02×10²³ particles.

n=m/Mr with grams and molar mass.

Mass=n×Mr. Particles=n×constant.

Identify molecules, atoms or ions explicitly.

## Equation ratios

Balanced coefficients give mole ratios.

2Mg+O₂ →2MgO.

0.10 mol Mg needs 0.050 mol O₂.

Ratios are not equal mass relationships.

## Reacting masses

Known mass →moles.

Apply coefficient ratio.

Desired moles →mass.

4.8 g Mg produces 8.0 g MgO if oxygen sufficient.

## Limiting reagent

Compare moles divided by coefficient.

Smaller reaction capacity limits.

Use limiting amount for maximum product.

Calculate excess left by subtraction.

## Empirical formula

Element mass/Ar gives mole amount.

Divide all by smallest.

Obtain simple whole-number ratio.

1:1.5 becomes 2:3, not 1:2.

## Molecular formula

Empirical mass from simplest formula.

Molecular Mr/empirical mass gives multiplier.

CH₂ mass 14, Mr 42: multiplier 3.

Molecular formula C₃H₆.
