# Bonding, structure and properties of matter questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** What type of bond forms when non-metal atoms share electrons?

**Answer:** Covalent.

**2.** State the charge on an oxide ion.

**Answer:** 2−.

**3.** What does the state symbol (aq) mean?

**Answer:** Dissolved in water.

**4.** Name the carbon structure that is a single layer of graphite.

**Answer:** Graphene.

**5.** State the size range of nanoparticles.

**Answer:** 1–100 nm.

## 2 marks

**6.** Explain what an ionic bond is.

**Answer:** It is a **strong electrostatic attraction** between **oppositely charged ions**.

**7.** Explain why magnesium chloride has the formula MgCl₂.

**Answer:**

- Magnesium forms **Mg²⁺** and chlorine forms **Cl⁻**.
- Two chloride ions balance one magnesium ion, making the compound **electrically neutral**.

**8.** Explain why a metal conducts electricity.

**Answer:**

- It has **delocalised electrons that can move** through the structure.
- These electrons **carry charge**.

**9.** Why does solid sodium chloride not conduct electricity?

**Answer:**

- Its ions are **held in fixed positions** in the lattice.
- There are no **mobile charged particles** to carry current through it.

**10.** Explain why oxygen has a low boiling point.

**Answer:**

- There are **weak intermolecular forces** between oxygen molecules.
- Little energy is needed to overcome them; the covalent bonds inside the molecules remain intact.

**11.** Explain why graphite can be used as a lubricant.

**Answer:**

- There are **weak forces between its layers**.
- The layers **slide over one another** easily.

**12.** A substance melts at −20 °C and boils at 60 °C. Give its state at 25 °C and explain your choice.

**Answer:**

- Liquid.
- 25 °C is **above its melting point but below its boiling point**.

**13.** Give two limitations of a ball-and-stick molecular model.

**Answer:**

- Atoms and gaps may **not be to scale**.
- Bonds are represented by rods, but real bonds are **attractions involving electrons and nuclei**, not physical sticks.

**14.** Why might a nanoparticle catalyst work effectively using less material than a larger-particle catalyst?

**Answer:**

- Nanoparticles have a **larger surface area to volume ratio**.
- A larger fraction of the material is exposed at the surface where reaction occurs.

## 3 marks

**15.** Explain why sodium chloride has a high melting point but conducts electricity when molten.

**Answer:**

- A **giant ionic lattice** contains strong electrostatic attractions between opposite ions.
- Much energy is needed to **overcome these attractions**.
- When molten, the **ions can move and carry charge**.

**16.** Explain why an alloy is often harder than the pure metal.

**Answer:**

- Atoms of the added elements are **different sizes**.
- They **distort the regular layers** of metal atoms.
- The layers **cannot slide as easily**, so the alloy resists deformation.

**17.** Explain why diamond is hard and does not conduct electricity.

**Answer:**

- Each carbon forms **four strong covalent bonds** in a rigid giant structure.
- These bonds resist deformation, making it **very hard**.
- It has **no delocalised electrons or mobile ions** to carry charge.

**18.** Explain why graphite has a high melting point and conducts electricity.

**Answer:**

- Each carbon forms **three strong covalent bonds** within a giant layered structure.
- Much energy is needed to break these bonds.
- Each carbon contributes a **delocalised electron**, which can move and carry charge.

**19.** Explain why a polymer can be solid at room temperature while a small molecule such as methane is a gas.

**Answer:**

- Polymers consist of **very large, long molecules**.
- Forces **between polymer molecules are stronger** than those between small methane molecules.
- More energy is needed to overcome those forces; strong covalent bonds within either molecule are not broken in melting or boiling.

**20.** A cubic nanoparticle has side length 20 nm. Calculate its surface area, volume and surface area to volume ratio.

**Answer:**

- Surface area = 6 × 20² = **2,400 nm²**.
- Volume = 20³ = **8,000 nm³**.
- SA/V = 2,400 ÷ 8,000 = **0.30 nm⁻¹**.

**21.** Describe three ways the particle model differs from real substances.

**Answer:**

- Particles are not all identical hard spheres: their sizes and structures differ.
- Simple drawings often omit **attractive forces** between particles.
- A still drawing does not show **continuous particle movement**.

## 4 marks

**22.** Describe how magnesium and oxygen form magnesium oxide, including the electron changes and the resulting bonding.

**Answer:**

- A magnesium atom **loses two electrons**, becoming Mg²⁺.
- An oxygen atom **gains two electrons**, becoming O²⁻.
- Both ions have **full outer shells**.
- **Electrostatic attraction between opposite ions** holds a giant ionic lattice together.

**23.** Describe the outer-shell electrons in a dot-and-cross diagram of hydrogen chloride. Explain what holds the atoms together.

**Answer:**

- Show **one shared pair**, containing one electron from hydrogen and one from chlorine.
- Chlorine also has **three lone pairs**.
- Hydrogen has two electrons in its shell; chlorine has eight in its outer shell when the shared pair is counted.
- Both positive nuclei **attract the shared negative electrons**, making a covalent bond.

**24.** Compare particle arrangement and movement in a solid and a gas, and explain what changes on heating.

**Answer:**

- In a solid, particles are **close together in fixed positions**.
- They **vibrate**; in a gas, particles are far apart and move randomly in all directions.
- Heating transfers energy to the substance.
- During a change of state, energy overcomes attractions and changes particle arrangement; the particles themselves do not expand or turn into different substances.

**25.** Give two uses of fullerenes or nanotubes and explain the useful property for each.

**Answer:**

- A hollow fullerene cage can **carry drug molecules**, making targeted drug delivery possible.
- Carbon nanotubes have **high tensile strength**, so adding them can strengthen composite materials.
- Their useful properties arise from their carbon structures, not simply from being small.

## 6 marks

**26.** Compare diamond, sodium chloride and methane in terms of structure, melting point and electrical conductivity.

**Answer:**

- Diamond is **giant covalent**; many strong covalent bonds require much energy to break, giving a high melting point.
- It lacks mobile ions and delocalised electrons, so it does not conduct.
- Sodium chloride has a **giant ionic lattice**; strong attractions between opposite ions give a high melting point.
- It conducts when molten or dissolved because ions can move, but not when solid.
- Methane consists of **small molecules**; weak intermolecular forces need little energy to overcome, so its melting point is low.
- Methane has no mobile ions or delocalised electrons, so it does not conduct.

**27.** A company plans to use silver nanoparticles in an antibacterial fabric. Evaluate the decision and the evidence it should obtain.

**Answer:**

- A **large surface area to volume ratio** can make a small amount effective at contacting bacteria.
- An antibacterial fabric could reduce bacterial growth and odours, but the effectiveness must be measured rather than assumed.
- Compare treated and untreated fabric under the same conditions, including after washing.
- Investigate whether nanoparticles are released during use or washing and whether people or organisms are exposed.
- Assess toxicity at realistic **doses and exposure routes**, including longer-term effects.
- Use the fabric if evidence supports a useful benefit with acceptable exposure and environmental risks; uncertain safety cannot be settled just by noting that bulk silver is familiar.
