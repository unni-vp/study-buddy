# Structures & states

## Solid → liquid → gas

- Solid: fixed arrangement, vibration. Liquid: close particles move past one another. Gas: widely spaced, rapid random motion.
- Melting/freezing; boiling/condensing. Heating transfers energy; changes of state are physical.

## Read melting & boiling data

- Below melting point → solid. Between melting and boiling → liquid. Above boiling → gas.
- State symbols: (s), (l), (g), (aq). Aqueous means dissolved in water.
- Particles are not hard identical spheres: models omit forces, size differences and continuous movement.

## Metals conduct & bend

- Strong attraction between positive ions and delocalised electrons → giant metallic structure.
- Electrons move and carry charge and transfer energy. Metal layers slide while metallic bonding remains.

## Alloys resist bending

- An alloy mixes a metal with other elements. Different-sized atoms distort regular layers.
- Layers slide less easily → alloy is harder. Use a labelled particle drawing to explain, not just “stronger”.

## Polymer chains

- Many atoms joined by strong covalent bonds in long molecules. Forces act between chains.
- Intermolecular forces are usually sufficient for polymers to be solid at room temperature.
- A repeating unit in brackets with n represents many linked units.

## Recognise giant structures

- Diamond, graphite and silicon dioxide are giant covalent. Many strong bonds need much energy to break → high melting.
- Contrast small molecules: melting/boiling does not break their internal covalent bonds.
- Use diagrams, formulae and melting/conductivity data together to identify structure.
