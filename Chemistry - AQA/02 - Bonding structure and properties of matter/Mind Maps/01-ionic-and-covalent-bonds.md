# Ionic & covalent bonds

## Electron transfer

- Metal atoms lose electrons → positive ions. Non-metals gain → negative ions. Protons stay unchanged.
- Group 1/2: +1/+2 ions; Group 7/6: −1/−2 ions. Electron arrangements match a noble gas.

## Charge → formula

- Balance total charges: Mg²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ → MgCl₂; 2Na⁺ + O²⁻ → Na₂O.
- Dot-and-cross ionic diagrams: brackets, ion charge and full outer shells; distinguish electron origins.

## Ionic lattice

- A giant, repeating 3D arrangement of opposite ions, with strong electrostatic attractions in all directions.
- Many attractions need much energy to overcome → high melting/boiling. Not separate molecules.

## Conductivity follows movement

- Solid: ions held in fixed positions → cannot carry charge through it.
- Molten/dissolved: ions free to move → conducts. Electrons do not carry current through the ionic liquid.

## Covalent pairs

- A single bond shares one pair; double shares two; triple shares three.
- H₂, Cl₂, HCl: one single bond. O₂: double. N₂: triple. H₂O: two single; NH₃: three; CH₄: four.
- Show lone pairs too: O has two in H₂O; N has one in NH₃; Cl has three.

## Molecules & models

- Strong bonds inside a molecule; weaker intermolecular forces between molecules. Boiling overcomes those forces.
- Bigger molecules generally have stronger intermolecular forces. No mobile ions/electrons → no conduction.
- 2D formulae omit 3D shape; ball-and-stick bonds are not literal rods.
