# Planning, measurement and evidence

## Hypotheses and variables

Prediction with chemical explanation.

Independent variable changed/selected.

Dependent measured, controls kept consistent.

Control experiment checks alternative cause.

## Measurements

Suitable apparatus, range and resolution.

Read scales at eye level and record units.

Calibrate/check zero.

Repeat across suitable range and intervals.

## Error types

Random scatter: repeats and mean.

Systematic bias: correct/recalibrate source.

Accuracy close to true, precision clustered.

Investigate anomalies before exclusion.

## Graphs and conclusions

Suitable bars, scatter or best-fit curve.

Label axes with quantity and unit.

Describe values, then chemical mechanism.

Correlation alone does not prove causation.

## Uncertainty and models

Consider instrument resolution and repeat spread.

Models simplify and have limits.

New evidence can revise explanations.

Avoid unsupported certainty from small samples.

## Judgement and communication

Peer review checks methods/claims.

Reproducibility compares independent work.

Risk: likelihood and consequence of harm.

Evaluate social, economic, environmental and ethical issues.
