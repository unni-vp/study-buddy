# Working scientifically and required practicals questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** What is the dependent variable in a concentration experiment measuring gas volume per second?

**Answer:** Gas-production rate.

**2.** What does a concordant titre mean?

**Answer:** A titre closely agreeing with other careful repeat titres.

**3.** At which electrode does reduction occur?

**Answer:** Cathode.

**4.** Which electrical supply is used for electrolysis?

**Answer:** DC.

**5.** What is the liquid passing through filter paper called?

**Answer:** Filtrate.

**6.** Why use a pipette filler?

**Answer:** To transfer measured liquid safely without mouth pipetting.

**7.** What is a systematic error?

**Answer:** A consistent measurement bias or offset.

**8.** Which gas turns limewater milky?

**Answer:** Carbon dioxide.

## 2 marks

**9.** Distinguish a control variable from a control experiment.

**Answer:**

- A control variable is a factor kept consistent across trials.
- A control experiment is a comparison used to check the cause of an effect.

**10.** Why repeat measurements and calculate a mean?

**Answer:**

- Repeats reveal scatter and possible anomalies.
- A mean reduces the influence of **random variation**, but does not fix a consistent bias.

**11.** Explain the difference between accuracy and precision.

**Answer:**

- Accuracy is closeness to the true value.
- Precision means repeated results cluster closely; they may still all be biased.

**12.** What should happen before deciding to exclude an anomalous result?

**Answer:**

- Check for a procedural or measurement problem and repeat if possible.
- Exclude only with a **justified reason**, since a real difference can also be unusual.

**13.** Why add excess insoluble copper oxide to acid in salt preparation?

**Answer:**

- It ensures the acid is used up.
- Unreacted insoluble oxide can be removed by **filtration**.

**14.** Why cool a concentrated salt solution instead of evaporating it completely dry?

**Answer:**

- Cooling reduces solubility and allows crystals to form.
- Strong heating to dryness can cause decomposition or unwanted damage/impurities.

**15.** A burette reads 1.20 cm³ initially and 21.45 cm³ finally. Calculate titre.

**Answer:**

- Titre = final−initial = 21.45−1.20.
- **20.25 cm³**.

**16.** Why add acid dropwise near the titration endpoint?

**Answer:**

- It reduces the chance of overshooting the colour-change endpoint.
- The reacting volume can be measured more accurately.

**17.** Why does distilled water in the conical flask not change the required acid moles?

**Answer:**

- It changes alkali concentration but not the **moles initially pipetted**.
- The same stoichiometric acid amount is therefore still required.

**18.** Why rinse a burette with the solution it will contain?

**Answer:**

- Residual water could dilute that solution.
- This would create a systematic concentration error in the titration.

**19.** State the products from aqueous copper sulfate using inert electrodes.

**Answer:**

- Cathode: **copper**.
- Anode: **oxygen**.

**20.** Where do ions and electrons move in an electrolysis setup?

**Answer:**

- **Ions** move through the liquid electrolyte.
- **Electrons** move through the external wires.

**21.** Why use insulation and a lid in a temperature-change investigation?

**Answer:**

- They reduce energy exchange with the surrounding room.
- Measured ΔT then better reflects the reaction’s energy transfer.

**22.** Why compare temperature changes rather than final temperatures alone?

**Answer:**

- Starting temperatures may differ.
- Subtracting each initial value gives the actual rise/fall for that trial.

**23.** Why does a gas-collection leak reduce measured output?

**Answer:**

- Some formed gas escapes instead of entering the collector.
- Recorded volume/rate is therefore underestimated.

**24.** Why keep total volume constant in the disappearing-cross investigation?

**Answer:**

- It keeps solution depth comparable in the same flask.
- Different depths could hide the cross at different amounts of sulfur, changing endpoint time.

**25.** Why must both chromatography distances be measured from the baseline?

**Answer:**

- Both numerator and denominator must describe travel from the same starting position.
- Measuring one from the paper edge would give a false Rf ratio.

**26.** Why use a fresh sample portion for each unknown-ion test?

**Answer:**

- Earlier reagents could introduce interfering ions or products.
- Separate portions reduce contamination and false identification.

**27.** Why is nitric acid used before silver nitrate in a halide test?

**Answer:**

- Acid removes interfering carbonate.
- Nitric acid avoids introducing chloride, which HCl would add.

**28.** Why cool an evaporating basin before weighing its residue?

**Answer:**

- Hot apparatus can give unstable/inaccurate balance readings and damage equipment.
- Comparable cooled readings give a more reliable residue mass.

**29.** Why does distillation require an open receiver?

**Answer:**

- Vapour must be able to pass and condense without sealing the whole system.
- A sealed receiver can create unsafe pressure buildup.

## 3 marks

**30.** Describe how to separate and dry crystals after cooling a salt solution.

**Answer:**

- Filter/separate the crystals from remaining solution.
- Wash with a little **cold distilled water** to remove surface solution while limiting dissolution.
- Dry between filter papers or by a suitable gentle method.

**31.** A 25.0 cm³ sodium-hydroxide sample reacts with 20.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ HCl. Calculate NaOH concentration.

**Answer:**

- HCl moles = 0.100 ×0.0200 = **0.00200 mol**.
- HCl:NaOH ratio is 1:1, so alkali moles = 0.00200.
- Concentration = 0.00200/0.0250 = **0.0800 mol dm⁻³**.

**32.** Convert 0.0800 mol dm⁻³ NaOH into g dm⁻³, showing Mr using Na=23, O=16, H=1.

**Answer:**

- Mr = 23+16+1 = **40**.
- Mass concentration = molar concentration ×Mr.
- 0.0800 ×40 = **3.20 g dm⁻³**.

**33.** Explain why temperature rise can decrease when one reactant is reduced.

**Answer:**

- Less reactant may become limiting, so less reaction occurs.
- Less energy is transferred to the solution.
- For the same total mass and heat capacity, the temperature rise is smaller.

**34.** A graph tangent passes through (10 s, 12 cm³) and (50 s, 36 cm³). Calculate gas rate.

**Answer:**

- Volume rise = **24 cm³**.
- Time interval = **40 s**.
- Rate = 24/40 = **0.60 cm³ s⁻¹**.

**35.** A cross disappears in 60 s, then in 30 s at another concentration. Compare reciprocal-time rates.

**Answer:**

- 1/60 ≈ **0.0167 s⁻¹**.
- 1/30 ≈ **0.0333 s⁻¹**.
- The second rate proxy is **twice** the first for the same endpoint.

**36.** A chromatography spot travels 24 mm and solvent 40 mm. Calculate Rf and state a comparison condition.

**Answer:**

- Rf = 24/40 = **0.60**.
- It has **no unit**.
- Compare references using the **same solvent and stationary phase/conditions**.

**37.** A 100 cm³ water sample leaves 0.20 g residue. Calculate residue concentration in g dm⁻³ and state a limit of the result.

**Answer:**

- Volume = **0.100 dm³**.
- Concentration = 0.20/0.100 = **2.0 g dm⁻³**.
- The result alone does not identify harmful chemicals or microbes, so it cannot prove potable quality.

**38.** Explain how peer review and reproducibility help assess a chemical investigation.

**Answer:**

- Reviewers check methods, analysis and whether conclusions follow evidence.
- Independent investigators test whether similar results occur.
- Agreement increases confidence, while errors or limitations can still remain.

## 4 marks

**39.** Describe a controlled investigation comparing temperature rise at different metal masses in a displacement reaction.

**Answer:**

- Use measured equal solution volumes/concentrations at comparable starting temperatures in insulated cups.
- Add a range of known metal masses with controlled piece size and mixing.
- Record temperatures consistently to find each maximum/change, and repeat.
- Compare ΔT, considering limiting reagent, heat loss and total solution mass; handle chemicals safely.

**40.** Explain why a correlation between acid concentration and rate needs a controlled method to support causation.

**Answer:**

- Temperature, solid surface area or volume may also change between trials.
- Each can affect collisions or the measured endpoint.
- Keep these consistent while changing only concentration and repeat.
- Then rate differences are better attributed to concentration, with uncertainty still considered.

**41.** Describe a safe aqueous electrolysis investigation with inert electrodes.

**Answer:**

- Place the solution in a suitable vessel and separate inert electrodes.
- Connect to low-voltage DC with named cathode− and anode+.
- Observe/collect small product samples and use suitable gas/metal tests.
- Keep conditions controlled, avoid short circuits and use ventilation/eye protection without inhaling gases.

## 6 marks

**42.** Plan preparation of a pure dry copper-sulfate sample from copper oxide.

**Answer:**

- Warm dilute sulfuric acid and add copper oxide in small portions with stirring.
- Continue until some insoluble oxide remains, showing acid exhaustion.
- Filter excess oxide, retaining copper-sulfate filtrate.
- Evaporate some water gently to concentrate, then cool for crystals.
- Separate crystals, wash with a little cold distilled water and dry appropriately.
- Use eye protection and safe heating/acid handling, avoiding evaporation to complete dryness.

**43.** Plan a titration determining strong acid concentration from a known alkali.

**Answer:**

- Use a rinsed pipette/filler to transfer a known alkali volume into a flask, adding suitable indicator.
- Fill/rinse the burette with acid, fill the tip, remove funnel and read the initial meniscus at eye level.
- Add acid while swirling, then dropwise near the endpoint over a white tile.
- Record titre from final−initial; repeat beyond a rough run to obtain concordant readings.
- Use known alkali concentration/volume, equation mole ratio and acid volume in dm³ to calculate concentration.
- Average appropriate concordant readings, evaluate overshoot/dilution errors and handle corrosive solutions safely.

**44.** Plan concentration-rate investigations using both gas collection and turbidity.

**Answer:**

- For gas collection, use known acid concentrations with the same solid mass/surface area, total volume and temperature.
- Seal promptly and record gas volume over time, checking leaks and free syringe movement.
- For turbidity, dilute thiosulfate at fixed total volume, add the same acid and time a common disappearing-cross endpoint.
- Keep depth, viewing conditions and temperature consistent; calculate 1/time for comparable endpoints.
- Repeat each condition, compare means/curves and relate trends to successful collision frequency.
- Use appropriate acid/gas precautions, ventilation and specific limitations for each method.

**45.** Plan analysis and purification of water from different sources.

**Answer:**

- Collect labelled representative samples and measure pH consistently with indicator/calibrated probe.
- Evaporate known equal volumes in weighed basins, cool and weigh residue to consistent mass.
- Subtract empty-basin mass and compare concentrations in matching units.
- Distil another portion through a cooled condenser into an open receiver.
- Compare original/distilled samples, considering balance resolution, contamination and substances not identified by these measurements.
- Use safe heating/eye protection, avoid distilling dry and never infer safe drinking or taste samples solely from appearance/pH.
