# Working scientifically and required practicals questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** What is the dependent variable in a concentration experiment measuring gas volume per second?

**Answer:** Gas-production rate.

**2.** What does a concordant titre mean?

**Answer:** A titre closely agreeing with other careful repeat titres.

**3.** At which electrode does reduction occur?

**Answer:** Cathode.

**4.** Which electrical supply is used for electrolysis?

**Answer:** DC.

**5.** What is the liquid passing through filter paper called?

**Answer:** Filtrate.

**6.** Why use a pipette filler?

**Answer:** To transfer measured liquid safely without mouth pipetting.

**7.** What is a systematic error?

**Answer:** A consistent measurement bias or offset.

**8.** Which gas turns limewater milky?

**Answer:** Carbon dioxide.

**9.** Which action addresses a systematic balance offset? Choose one: A take more readings without changing anything; B use a mean alone; C check and correct the zero before measuring; D remove the highest reading.

**Answer:** C: check and correct the zero before measuring.

## 2 marks

**10.** Distinguish a control variable from a control experiment.

**Answer:** - A control variable is a factor kept consistent across trials.
- A control experiment is a comparison used to check the cause of an effect.

**11.** Why repeat measurements and calculate a mean?

**Answer:** - Repeats reveal scatter and possible anomalies.
- A mean reduces the influence of **random variation**, but does not fix a consistent bias.

**12.** Explain the difference between accuracy and precision.

**Answer:** - Accuracy is closeness to the true value.
- Precision means repeated results cluster closely; they may still all be biased.

**13.** What should happen before deciding to exclude an anomalous result?

**Answer:** - Check for a procedural or measurement problem and repeat if possible.
- Exclude only with a **justified reason**, since a real difference can also be unusual.

**14.** Why add excess insoluble copper oxide to acid in salt preparation?

**Answer:** - It ensures the acid is used up.
- Unreacted insoluble oxide can be removed by **filtration**.

**15.** Why cool a concentrated salt solution instead of evaporating it completely dry?

**Answer:** - Cooling reduces solubility and allows crystals to form.
- Strong heating to dryness can cause decomposition or unwanted damage/impurities.

**16.** A burette reads 1.20 cm³ initially and 21.45 cm³ finally. Calculate titre.

**Answer:** - Titre = final−initial = 21.45−1.20.
- **20.25 cm³**.

**17.** Why add acid dropwise near the titration endpoint?

**Answer:** - It reduces the chance of overshooting the colour-change endpoint.
- The reacting volume can be measured more accurately.

**18.** Why does distilled water in the conical flask not change the required acid moles?

**Answer:** - It changes alkali concentration but not the **moles initially pipetted**.
- The same stoichiometric acid amount is therefore still required.

**19.** Why rinse a burette with the solution it will contain?

**Answer:** - Residual water could dilute that solution.
- This would create a systematic concentration error in the titration.

**20.** State the products from aqueous copper sulfate using inert electrodes.

**Answer:** - Cathode: **copper**.
- Anode: **oxygen**.

**21.** Where do ions and electrons move in an electrolysis setup?

**Answer:** - **Ions** move through the liquid electrolyte.
- **Electrons** move through the external wires.

**22.** Why use insulation and a lid in a temperature-change investigation?

**Answer:** - They reduce energy exchange with the surrounding room.
- Measured ΔT then better reflects the reaction’s energy transfer.

**23.** Why compare temperature changes rather than final temperatures alone?

**Answer:** - Starting temperatures may differ.
- Subtracting each initial value gives the actual rise/fall for that trial.

**24.** Why does a gas-collection leak reduce measured output?

**Answer:** - Some formed gas escapes instead of entering the collector.
- Recorded volume/rate is therefore underestimated.

**25.** Why keep total volume constant in the disappearing-cross investigation?

**Answer:** - It keeps solution depth comparable in the same flask.
- Different depths could hide the cross at different amounts of sulfur, changing endpoint time.

**26.** Why must both chromatography distances be measured from the baseline?

**Answer:** - Both numerator and denominator must describe travel from the same starting position.
- Measuring one from the paper edge would give a false Rf ratio.

**27.** Why use a fresh sample portion for each unknown-ion test?

**Answer:** - Earlier reagents could introduce interfering ions or products.
- Separate portions reduce contamination and false identification.

**28.** Why is nitric acid used before silver nitrate in a halide test?

**Answer:** - Acid removes interfering carbonate.
- Nitric acid avoids introducing chloride, which HCl would add.

**29.** Why cool an evaporating basin before weighing its residue?

**Answer:** - Hot apparatus can give unstable/inaccurate balance readings and damage equipment.
- Comparable cooled readings give a more reliable residue mass.

**30.** Why does distillation require an open receiver?

**Answer:** - Vapour must be able to pass and condense without sealing the whole system.
- A sealed receiver can create unsafe pressure buildup.

## 3 marks

**31.** Describe how to separate and dry crystals after cooling a salt solution.

**Answer:** - Filter/separate the crystals from remaining solution.
- Wash with a little **cold distilled water** to remove surface solution while limiting dissolution.
- Dry between filter papers or by a suitable gentle method.

**32.** A 25.0 cm³ sodium-hydroxide sample reacts with 20.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ HCl. Calculate NaOH concentration.

**Answer:** - HCl moles = 0.100 ×0.0200 = **0.00200 mol**.
- HCl:NaOH ratio is 1:1, so alkali moles = 0.00200.
- Concentration = 0.00200/0.0250 = **0.0800 mol dm⁻³**.

**33.** Convert 0.0800 mol dm⁻³ NaOH into g dm⁻³, showing Mr using Na=23, O=16, H=1.

**Answer:** - Mr = 23+16+1 = **40**.
- Mass concentration = molar concentration ×Mr.
- 0.0800 ×40 = **3.20 g dm⁻³**.

**34.** Explain why temperature rise can decrease when one reactant is reduced.

**Answer:** - Less reactant may become limiting, so less reaction occurs.
- Less energy is transferred to the solution.
- For the same total mass and heat capacity, the temperature rise is smaller.

**35.** A graph tangent passes through (10 s, 12 cm³) and (50 s, 36 cm³). Calculate gas rate.

**Answer:** - Volume rise = **24 cm³**.
- Time interval = **40 s**.
- Rate = 24/40 = **0.60 cm³ s⁻¹**.

**36.** A cross disappears in 60 s, then in 30 s at another concentration. Compare reciprocal-time rates.

**Answer:** - 1/60 ≈ **0.0167 s⁻¹**.
- 1/30 ≈ **0.0333 s⁻¹**.
- The second rate proxy is **twice** the first for the same endpoint.

**37.** A chromatography spot travels 24 mm and solvent 40 mm. Calculate Rf and state a comparison condition.

**Answer:** - Rf = 24/40 = **0.60**.
- It has **no unit**.
- Compare references using the **same solvent and stationary phase/conditions**.

**38.** A 100 cm³ water sample leaves 0.20 g residue. Calculate residue concentration in g dm⁻³ and state a limit of the result.

**Answer:** - Volume = **0.100 dm³**.
- Concentration = 0.20/0.100 = **2.0 g dm⁻³**.
- The result alone does not identify harmful chemicals or microbes, so it cannot prove potable quality.

**39.** Explain how peer review and reproducibility help assess a chemical investigation.

**Answer:** - Reviewers check methods, analysis and whether conclusions follow evidence.
- Independent investigators test whether similar results occur.
- Agreement increases confidence, while errors or limitations can still remain.

## 4 marks

**40.** Describe a controlled investigation comparing temperature rise at different metal masses in a displacement reaction.

**Answer:** - Use measured equal solution volumes/concentrations at comparable starting temperatures in insulated cups.
- Add a range of known metal masses with controlled piece size and mixing.
- Record temperatures consistently to find each maximum/change, and repeat.
- Compare ΔT, considering limiting reagent, heat loss and total solution mass; handle chemicals safely.

**41.** Explain why a correlation between acid concentration and rate needs a controlled method to support causation.

**Answer:** - Temperature, solid surface area or volume may also change between trials.
- Each can affect collisions or the measured endpoint.
- Keep these consistent while changing only concentration and repeat.
- Then rate differences are better attributed to concentration, with uncertainty still considered.

**42.** Describe a safe aqueous electrolysis investigation with inert electrodes.

**Answer:** - Place the solution in a suitable vessel and separate inert electrodes.
- Connect to low-voltage DC with named cathode− and anode+.
- Observe/collect small product samples and use suitable gas/metal tests.
- Keep conditions controlled, avoid short circuits and use ventilation/eye protection without inhaling gases.

## 5 marks

**43.** A titration starts at 1.20 cm³ and ends at 21.20 cm³ on the burette. Each reading has uncertainty ±0.05 cm³. For this question, add the two absolute uncertainties. Calculate the titre, its absolute uncertainty and its percentage uncertainty. Explain how overshooting the endpoint affects the titre and how to reduce that error.

**Answer:** - Titre = 21.20 − 1.20 = **20.00 cm³**.
- Combined absolute uncertainty = 0.05 + 0.05 = **±0.10 cm³**, using the rule given.
- Percentage uncertainty = 0.10 ÷ 20.00 × 100 = **0.50%**.
- Overshooting adds acid after the endpoint, making the measured titre **too large**.
- After a rough trial, add acid **drop by drop near the endpoint while swirling**. This reduces the chance of passing the first permanent expected indicator change.

## 6 marks

**44.** Plan preparation of a pure dry copper-sulfate sample from copper oxide.

**Answer:** - Warm dilute sulfuric acid and add copper oxide in small portions with stirring.
- Continue until some insoluble oxide remains, showing acid exhaustion.
- Filter excess oxide, retaining copper-sulfate filtrate.
- Evaporate some water gently to concentrate, then cool for crystals.
- Separate crystals, wash with a little cold distilled water and dry appropriately.
- Use eye protection and safe heating/acid handling, avoiding evaporation to complete dryness.

**45.** Plan a titration determining strong acid concentration from a known alkali.

**Answer:** - Use a rinsed pipette/filler to transfer a known alkali volume into a flask, adding suitable indicator.
- Fill/rinse the burette with acid, fill the tip, remove funnel and read the initial meniscus at eye level.
- Add acid while swirling, then dropwise near the endpoint over a white tile.
- Record titre from final−initial; repeat beyond a rough run to obtain concordant readings.
- Use known alkali concentration/volume, equation mole ratio and acid volume in dm³ to calculate concentration.
- Average appropriate concordant readings, evaluate overshoot/dilution errors and handle corrosive solutions safely.

**46.** Plan concentration-rate investigations using both gas collection and turbidity.

**Answer:** - For gas collection, use known acid concentrations with the same solid mass/surface area, total volume and temperature.
- Seal promptly and record gas volume over time, checking leaks and free syringe movement.
- For turbidity, dilute thiosulfate at fixed total volume, add the same acid and time a common disappearing-cross endpoint.
- Keep depth, viewing conditions and temperature consistent; calculate 1/time for comparable endpoints.
- Repeat each condition, compare means/curves and relate trends to successful collision frequency.
- Use appropriate acid/gas precautions, ventilation and specific limitations for each method.

**47.** Plan analysis and purification of water from different sources.

**Answer:** - Collect labelled representative samples and measure pH consistently with indicator/calibrated probe.
- Evaporate known equal volumes in weighed basins, cool and weigh residue to consistent mass.
- Subtract empty-basin mass and compare concentrations in matching units.
- Distil another portion through a cooled condenser into an open receiver.
- Compare original/distilled samples, considering balance resolution, contamination and substances not identified by these measurements.
- Use safe heating/eye protection, avoid distilling dry and never infer safe drinking or taste samples solely from appearance/pH.
