# Chemistry of the atmosphere questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** What gas makes up most of today’s atmosphere?

**Answer:** Nitrogen.

**2.** Approximately what percentage of dry air is oxygen?

**Answer:** 21%.

**3.** What process increased atmospheric oxygen?

**Answer:** Photosynthesis.

**4.** Name a greenhouse gas other than carbon dioxide or methane.

**Answer:** Water vapour.

**5.** Which pollutant is toxic, colourless and odourless?

**Answer:** Carbon monoxide.

**6.** Which kind of radiation is emitted by Earth’s warm surface?

**Answer:** Long-wavelength infrared.

**7.** What is global dimming?

**Answer:** A reduction in sunlight reaching Earth’s surface caused by atmospheric particulates.

**8.** Which process causes global dimming? Choose one: A particles reducing sunlight reaching the surface; B greenhouse gases absorbing outgoing infrared; C ozone being formed; D carbon monoxide carrying oxygen in the blood.

**Answer:** A: particles reducing sunlight reaching the surface.

## 2 marks

**9.** Describe two likely features of the early atmosphere.

**Answer:** - Much **carbon dioxide**.
- **Little or no oxygen**.

**10.** How did Earth’s oceans form in the volcanic-outgassing model?

**Answer:** - Volcanoes released **water vapour**.
- As Earth cooled, vapour **condensed** into liquid water.

**11.** Give two processes that reduced atmospheric carbon dioxide over long timescales.

**Answer:** - **Photosynthesis** incorporated carbon into organisms.
- Dissolution/carbonate sediment formation and fossil-fuel formation stored carbon outside the atmosphere.

**12.** Give two human activities that increase methane emissions.

**Answer:** - Cattle farming or flooded rice cultivation.
- Anaerobic decomposition of organic waste in landfill.

**13.** Explain how burning sulfur-containing fuel can harm the environment.

**Answer:** - Sulfur burns to form **sulfur dioxide**.
- This can cause **acid rain**, damaging ecosystems/buildings.

**14.** Why can nitrogen oxides form from a nitrogen-free fuel?

**Answer:** - Air contains nitrogen and oxygen.
- They react at the **high combustion temperature**.

**15.** Explain why increasing oxygen supply reduces carbon monoxide formation.

**Answer:** - Carbon monoxide forms during **incomplete combustion**.
- More oxygen supports complete oxidation to carbon dioxide.

**16.** Why does deforestation increase atmospheric carbon dioxide?

**Answer:** - Burning/decay releases carbon dioxide from biomass.
- Fewer trees remove it through **photosynthesis**.

**17.** Give two possible effects of climate change.

**Answer:** - **Sea-level rise** can increase coastal flood risk.
- **Habitat/rainfall changes** can affect species or food production.

**18.** Why is one cold winter insufficient evidence against a long-term warming trend?

**Answer:** - Weather varies by location and year.
- Climate trends need **long-term, broad measurements**, not one local event.

**19.** Define the carbon footprint of a product.

**Answer:** - Its total **carbon dioxide and other greenhouse-gas emissions**.
- Across the **full life cycle**, including production, use and disposal.

**20.** A product’s emissions are 30, 12, 60 and 8 kg CO₂e at four life-cycle stages. Calculate total footprint.

**Answer:** - Total = 30+12+60+8.
- **110 kg CO₂e**.

## 3 marks

**21.** Explain the greenhouse effect using radiation wavelengths.

**Answer:** - Short-wavelength sunlight passes through and warms Earth’s surface.
- The surface emits **long-wavelength infrared**.
- Greenhouse gases **absorb and re-emit some infrared**, including downwards, reducing net energy loss at a given temperature.

**22.** Explain how crude oil and natural gas deposits formed.

**Answer:** - Ancient marine biomass such as plankton died and was buried in sediment.
- Oxygen-poor conditions limited complete decay.
- Heat and pressure changed it over **millions of years** into fossil-fuel deposits.

**23.** Explain how limestone formation can store carbon.

**Answer:** - Carbon dioxide dissolves in oceans and contributes to carbonate formation.
- Carbonate sediments and shells/skeletons accumulate.
- They compact into limestone, storing carbon outside the atmosphere.

**24.** Explain the formation of coal.

**Answer:** - Dead vegetation accumulates in conditions limiting decay.
- It becomes buried under sediments.
- Heat and pressure over long timescales transform it into a carbon-rich fuel.

**25.** Why is evidence for the early atmosphere uncertain?

**Answer:** - Events occurred billions of years ago, with no direct measurements.
- Ancient rocks/materials have been altered or destroyed.
- Scientists compare surviving evidence and models, revising explanations as new data arise.

**26.** Explain how a growing population can raise carbon-dioxide emissions.

**Answer:** - Greater demand for energy and transport increases fossil-fuel burning.
- Burning carbon-containing fuels releases carbon dioxide.
- Extra farmland/development can also cause deforestation and reduced carbon uptake.

**27.** A total footprint is 135 kg CO₂e, including 80 kg from use. Use emissions fall by 25%. Calculate new footprint and whole-footprint reduction.

**Answer:** - Saving = 0.25 ×80 = **20 kg**.
- New footprint = 135−20 = **115 kg CO₂e**.
- Whole reduction = 20/135 ×100 ≈ **14.8%**.

**28.** Explain why carbon monoxide is dangerous.

**Answer:** - It is **colourless and odourless**, making exposure difficult to notice.
- It binds strongly to haemoglobin.
- This reduces blood’s **oxygen-carrying ability**.

**29.** Explain how particulates affect sunlight and human health.

**Answer:** - Particles scatter/reflect incoming sunlight.
- Less sunlight reaches the surface, causing **global dimming**.
- Inhaled particles can damage respiratory health.

## 4 marks

**30.** Compare global warming with acid rain.

**Answer:** - Greenhouse gases absorb/re-emit outgoing infrared, enhancing warming.
- This affects average temperature and climate.
- Sulfur dioxide/nitrogen oxides form acidic substances in atmospheric moisture.
- Acid rain damages plants, aquatic systems and buildings; it is a different mechanism.

**31.** Give four potential consequences of climate change with a brief link to each.

**Answer:** - Warmer oceans and melting land ice raise sea level, increasing coastal flooding.
- Changed rainfall can increase drought and limit freshwater.
- Habitat conditions shift, changing species distributions.
- Heat/rainfall changes can alter crop yields and food security.

**32.** Explain why full-life-cycle evidence is needed to compare two transport technologies.

**Answer:** - Manufacturing and raw materials can emit greenhouse gases before either is used.
- Fuel/electricity production affects use-related emissions.
- Disposal/recycling and lifetime change the total.
- Compare equal transport service, not just exhaust emissions from one trip.

## 5 marks

**33.** Two products provide the same service for the same lifetime. Use the data to calculate each total carbon footprint and the percentage reduction from choosing B instead of A. Explain why using only emissions during use would give an incomplete comparison.

| Stage | A (kg CO₂e) | B (kg CO₂e) |
|---|---|---|
| Manufacture | 30 | 60 |
| Transport | 5 | 10 |
| Use | 80 | 30 |
| Disposal | 5 | 5 |

**Answer:** - A's total footprint = 30 + 5 + 80 + 5 = **120 kg CO₂e**.
- B's total footprint = 60 + 10 + 30 + 5 = **105 kg CO₂e**.
- Choosing B saves 120 − 105 = **15 kg CO₂e**.
- Percentage reduction = 15 ÷ 120 × 100 = **12.5%**.
- B has lower use emissions but higher manufacture and transport emissions. A valid comparison includes the **whole life cycle**, using equivalent lifetimes and services.

## 6 marks

**34.** Evaluate a news claim about climate change based on a short temperature record from one city.

**Answer:** - A short local record may reflect weather variability and local factors rather than global climate.
- Examine measurement quality, coverage, missing data and whether start/end dates were selectively chosen.
- Compare longer records from many locations and independent methods.
- Consider physical greenhouse mechanisms and other influences, not correlation alone.
- Peer-reviewed analysis and independently tested models increase confidence, while exact local predictions remain uncertain.
- A justified conclusion states what these limited data show and why they cannot alone establish or overturn a global trend.

**35.** Evaluate a factory plan to reduce sulfur dioxide and its carbon footprint.

**Answer:** - Removing sulfur from fuel or capturing sulfur dioxide can reduce acid rain and respiratory damage.
- Efficiency improvements reduce fuel use and associated carbon dioxide.
- Lower-carbon energy may further reduce emissions, but manufacturing/supply impacts must be included.
- Compare costs, reliability, removal efficiency and maintenance using measured data.
- A sulfur-dioxide treatment alone does not necessarily reduce carbon dioxide; extra treatment energy can add emissions.
- Choose a justified combination based on full-life-cycle greenhouse emissions and local pollutant benefits.
