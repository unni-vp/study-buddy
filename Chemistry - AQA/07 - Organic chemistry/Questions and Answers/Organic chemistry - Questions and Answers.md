# Organic chemistry questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** What is a hydrocarbon?

**Answer:** A compound containing carbon and hydrogen only.

**2.** State the general formula of alkanes.

**Answer:** CₙH₂ₙ₊₂.

**3.** Name the alkane C₃H₈.

**Answer:** Propane.

**4.** State the functional group of alkenes.

**Answer:** C=C.

**5.** Name the alkene C₂H₄.

**Answer:** Ethene.

**6.** What colour change identifies an alkene using bromine water?

**Answer:** Orange to colourless.

**7.** State the functional group of alcohols.

**Answer:** –OH.

**8.** State the functional group of carboxylic acids.

**Answer:** –COOH.

**9.** Name the ester made from ethanol and ethanoic acid.

**Answer:** Ethyl ethanoate.

**10.** What is a monomer?

**Answer:** A small molecule that can join others to form a polymer.

**11.** Which small molecule is usually produced during condensation polymerisation?

**Answer:** Water.

**12.** What are DNA’s monomers?

**Answer:** Nucleotides.

**13.** What are protein’s monomers?

**Answer:** Amino acids.

**14.** Which formula represents a hydrocarbon? Choose one: A C₂H₅OH; B CH₃COOH; C C₃H₈; D H₂O.

**Answer:** C: C₃H₈.

## 2 marks

**15.** Why is crude oil a finite resource?

**Answer:** - It forms over **millions of years** from buried ancient biomass.
- We use it much faster than it is replaced, so reserves are limited on human timescales.

**16.** Why is a crude-oil fraction usually not pure?

**Answer:** - It contains **several hydrocarbons**.
- These have similar chain lengths and boiling ranges, rather than being one compound.

**17.** State how boiling point and viscosity change as hydrocarbon chain length increases.

**Answer:** - **Boiling point increases**.
- **Viscosity increases**, so liquids flow less easily.

**18.** Give two useful product types from cracking.

**Answer:** - Smaller **alkanes** used as fuels.
- **Alkenes** used as polymer monomers or chemical feedstocks.

**19.** Complete and balance C₁₂H₂₆ → C₈H₁₈ + _____.

**Answer:** - Remaining atoms: 12−8 = 4 carbons; 26−18 = 8 hydrogens.
- **C₁₂H₂₆ → C₈H₁₈ + C₄H₈**.

**20.** Explain why an alkene is unsaturated.

**Answer:** - It contains a **carbon–carbon double bond**.
- Atoms can add across it, giving greater hydrogen content than the starting alkene.

**21.** Describe hydrogen addition to ethene.

**Answer:** - React with hydrogen using a **heated nickel catalyst**.
- C=C becomes C–C, producing **ethane**.

**22.** Describe water addition to ethene.

**Answer:** - React with **steam**, using suitable acid catalyst, elevated temperature and pressure.
- Product is **ethanol**.

**23.** State the structural change when bromine adds to ethene.

**Answer:** - C=C becomes a **single C–C bond**.
- One bromine attaches to each carbon: **CH₂BrCH₂Br**.

**24.** Describe the reaction of ethanol with sodium.

**Answer:** - Sodium reacts to form the corresponding sodium compound.
- **Hydrogen gas** is released.

**25.** How can ethanol be changed into ethanoic acid?

**Answer:** - React it with a suitable **oxidising agent**.
- Oxidation changes the primary alcohol into the **carboxylic acid**.

**26.** What products form when a carboxylic acid reacts with a carbonate?

**Answer:** - A **salt**.
- **Water and carbon dioxide**.

**27.** Distinguish a weak acid from a dilute acid.

**Answer:** - Weak means **partially ionised in water**.
- Dilute means a low amount of dissolved acid per volume; these are different properties.

**28.** Why does addition polymerisation produce no small by-product?

**Answer:** - Alkene monomers join by opening their double bonds.
- All monomer atoms remain in the repeat units rather than being eliminated.

**29.** What two functional groups are present in an amino acid?

**Answer:** An amino group, –NH₂, and a carboxyl group, –COOH.

**30.** State DNA’s main shape and why it is important.

**Answer:** - Most DNA has two polymer chains in a **double helix**.
- It carries **genetic instructions** for development and functioning.

**31.** Name the monomer of starch and cellulose and explain why their properties can differ.

**Answer:** - Both use **glucose** monomers.
- Different arrangements/linking produce different structures and properties.

## 3 marks

**32.** Explain fractional distillation of crude oil.

**Answer:** - Heat oil so hydrocarbons **evaporate**.
- Vapours rise through a column cooler at the top.
- They **condense at different heights** according to boiling ranges, separating fractions.

**33.** Explain why longer hydrocarbons have higher boiling points.

**Answer:** - Their molecules generally have stronger **intermolecular attractions**.
- More energy is needed to separate the molecules.
- Boiling overcomes these attractions rather than breaking covalent bonds inside molecules.

**34.** Describe catalytic cracking and explain why it is useful.

**Answer:** - Vaporise a large hydrocarbon.
- Pass vapour over a **hot catalyst** to break molecules into smaller ones.
- Products supply high-demand smaller fuels and reactive alkene feedstocks.

**35.** Balance complete combustion of butane, C₄H₁₀.

**Answer:** - Match carbon and hydrogen: C₄H₁₀ → 4CO₂ +5H₂O.
- This needs 6½O₂; double all coefficients.
- **2C₄H₁₀ +13O₂ → 8CO₂ +10H₂O**.

**36.** Balance complete combustion of ethanol, C₂H₅OH.

**Answer:** - Two carbons give 2CO₂; six hydrogens give 3H₂O.
- Products contain seven oxygen atoms, one already supplied by ethanol.
- **C₂H₅OH +3O₂ → 2CO₂ +3H₂O**.

**37.** Describe fermentation conditions and products.

**Answer:** - Use **yeast and sugar solution**.
- Keep conditions **warm and anaerobic**, avoiding temperatures that denature enzymes.
- Products are **ethanol and carbon dioxide**.

**38.** Explain why equal-concentration ethanoic acid has a higher pH than hydrochloric acid.

**Answer:** - Ethanoic acid is **weak**, so it only partially ionises.
- Hydrochloric acid ionises fully.
- Ethanoic acid therefore has fewer hydrogen ions per volume and a higher pH.

**39.** Describe ester formation from ethanol and ethanoic acid.

**Answer:** - React the alcohol and carboxylic acid, commonly with an acid catalyst.
- They form **ethyl ethanoate and water**.
- This is **condensation**, with water eliminated; the process can be reversible.

**40.** Describe how to obtain the monomer from a displayed addition-polymer repeat unit.

**Answer:** - Identify the two carbon atoms in the repeating backbone.
- Restore a **C=C double bond** between them and remove outward polymer links/brackets.
- Keep every side group attached to its original carbon.

**41.** Explain how a polyester forms from a diol and a dicarboxylic acid.

**Answer:** - Each monomer has **two reactive functional groups**, allowing chain growth.
- –OH and –COOH groups form **ester links**.
- **Water is eliminated** as the monomers join.

**42.** Explain how amino acids form a polypeptide.

**Answer:** - Amino acids each contain **amino and carboxyl groups**.
- These join by **condensation**, eliminating water.
- Repeated joining forms a chain; different amino acids can form a protein.

## 4 marks

**43.** Compare fractional distillation and cracking.

**Answer:** - Distillation is **physical separation** based on boiling ranges.
- It uses evaporation and condensation without breaking hydrocarbon molecules.
- Cracking is a **chemical reaction** breaking larger molecules into smaller ones.
- High temperature with catalyst or steam produces useful smaller alkanes and alkenes.

**44.** Draw the poly(ethene) repeating-unit diagram and explain the essential features.

**Answer:** ![Ethene opens its double bond to form a poly(ethene) unit.](../Diagrams/addition-polymer.svg)

- A **single C–C backbone bond** replaces C=C.
- Each carbon retains two hydrogens.
- Outward single bonds pass through both brackets.
- **n** outside the brackets shows repetition; no atoms are lost.

**45.** A polymer has repeat unit –CH₂–CH(CH₃)–. Identify its monomer and explain your reasoning.

**Answer:** - It is **propene**, CH₂=CHCH₃.
- Restore a double bond between the two backbone carbons.
- Retain the methyl side group on the second carbon.
- The repeat unit contains all three carbons and six hydrogens from the monomer.

**46.** Compare addition and condensation polymerisation.

**Answer:** - Addition generally uses **alkene monomers** with double bonds.
- All atoms remain in the polymer, with **no small by-product**.
- Condensation uses monomers with **two functional groups**.
- Groups join while a small molecule, usually **water**, is eliminated.

## 5 marks

**47.** Ethene can be used to make ethane or poly(ethene). Explain the structural changes in each reaction. State suitable conditions for making ethane and show the poly(ethene) repeat unit, including the brackets and n.

**Answer:** - To make **ethane**, add **hydrogen with a heated nickel catalyst**. The C=C opens to a C–C bond, and each carbon gains a hydrogen: **CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ → CH₃–CH₃**.
- To make **poly(ethene)**, many ethene molecules undergo **addition polymerisation**. Their double bonds open and join the molecules into a long carbon backbone.
- The repeat unit is **[–CH₂–CH₂–]ₙ**, with one chain bond extending through each end of the brackets. Each carbon keeps its two attached hydrogens.
- No small by-product is released. The atoms in each repeat unit are the same as those in an ethene monomer.

## 6 marks

**48.** Evaluate making ethanol by fermentation compared with hydration of ethene.

**Answer:** - Fermentation can use renewable crop sugars with yeast under warm anaerobic conditions.
- It is slower/batch-based and gives dilute ethanol needing separation; crop land can compete with food supply.
- Hydration reacts ethene with steam under high temperature/pressure and a suitable acid catalyst.
- It can be continuous and rapid, but usually uses finite petroleum feedstock and energy-intensive equipment.
- Compare measured energy, purity, yield, feedstock costs and land impacts, including separation energy.
- Choose a justified method for the stated situation rather than assuming renewable automatically means impact-free.

**49.** Explain how carbon compounds provide fuels and materials, using separation and chemical change.

**Answer:** - Crude oil contains many hydrocarbons and is separated into similar-boiling fractions by fractional distillation.
- Smaller, less-viscous, more-flammable molecules are useful fuels.
- Complete combustion releases energy and produces carbon dioxide and water.
- Cracking converts less-demanded large hydrocarbons into smaller fuels and alkenes.
- Alkene addition reactions and polymerisation produce many useful materials; petrochemical feedstocks also support solvents, lubricants and detergents.
- Benefits must be balanced against finite supply, emissions and waste, using the context rather than treating all products as identical.
