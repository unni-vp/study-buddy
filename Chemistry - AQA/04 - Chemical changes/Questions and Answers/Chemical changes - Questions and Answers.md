# Chemical changes questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** Which ion makes an aqueous solution acidic?

**Answer:** H⁺.

**2.** Give the formula of the ion responsible for alkalinity.

**Answer:** OH⁻.

**3.** What type of salt is formed using nitric acid?

**Answer:** Nitrate.

**4.** At which electrode does reduction take place during electrolysis?

**Answer:** Cathode.

**5.** What is meant by a weak acid?

**Answer:** An acid that is only partially ionised in aqueous solution.

**6.** Which gas forms when magnesium reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid?

**Answer:** Hydrogen.

## 2 marks

**7.** Explain why copper does not react with dilute hydrochloric acid.

**Answer:**

- Copper is **less reactive than hydrogen**.
- It cannot **displace hydrogen** from this dilute acid.

**8.** Write a balanced equation for magnesium reacting with hydrochloric acid.

**Answer:** **Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂**. The two hydrogen atoms form one H₂ molecule.

**9.** Write the ionic equation for neutralisation, including state symbols.

**Answer:** **H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l)**.

**10.** Zinc is placed in copper sulfate solution. State two expected observations.

**Answer:**

- A **brown copper deposit** forms.
- The **blue colour fades** as copper ions leave the solution.

**11.** Explain why carbon can extract copper from copper oxide but cannot extract aluminium from aluminium oxide.

**Answer:**

- Carbon is **more reactive than copper**, so it can remove oxygen from copper oxide.
- Aluminium is **more reactive than carbon**, so its oxide cannot be reduced by carbon in this way.

**12.** Why is excess copper oxide added when preparing copper sulfate from sulfuric acid?

**Answer:**

- It ensures **all the acid reacts**.
- Any unreacted insoluble oxide can then be **removed by filtration**.

**13.** Name a suitable indicator for adding acid to sodium hydroxide in a titration, and state its endpoint colour change.

**Answer:**

- Phenolphthalein.
- **Pink to colourless**.

**14.** Why does solid sodium chloride not undergo electrolysis, whereas molten sodium chloride does?

**Answer:**

- In the solid, ions are **fixed in a lattice**.
- When molten, ions are **free to move to electrodes and carry charge**.

**15.** Write the half-equation for aluminium forming from aluminium ions. State whether this is oxidation or reduction.

**Answer:**

- **Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al**.
- **Reduction**, because electrons are gained.

**16.** Explain why aluminium oxide is mixed with cryolite for electrolysis.

**Answer:**

- The mixture has a **lower melting/operating temperature** than pure aluminium oxide.
- This reduces the **energy needed for heating**.

**17.** How does hydrogen ion concentration change when pH changes from 5 to 2?

**Answer:**

- It **increases**.
- By **10³ = 1,000 times**, because each decrease of one pH unit gives a tenfold increase.

## 3 marks

**18.** Metal A displaces B from its salt. Metal C displaces A. B does not displace A or C. Deduce the reactivity order and explain what greater reactivity means in terms of ions.

**Answer:**

- Order: **C > A > B**.
- A metal displaces a **less reactive** metal from its compound.
- More reactive metals have a greater tendency to **lose electrons and form positive ions**.

**19.** Write the ionic equation for zinc displacing copper and identify which species is oxidised and which is reduced.

**Answer:**

- **Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu**.
- Zinc is **oxidised**, losing two electrons.
- Copper ions are **reduced**, gaining two electrons.

**20.** Write a balanced equation for copper carbonate reacting with sulfuric acid, and name the gas test.

**Answer:**

- **CuCO₃ + H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + H₂O + CO₂**.
- Bubble the gas through **limewater**.
- It becomes **cloudy**, identifying carbon dioxide.

**21.** Explain why the carbon anodes must be replaced during aluminium extraction.

**Answer:**

- **Oxygen forms at the positive electrode**.
- It **reacts with the carbon**, forming carbon dioxide.
- The carbon anodes are consumed and wear away.

**22.** Predict both products when molten lead bromide is electrolysed using inert electrodes. Write the anode half-equation.

**Answer:**

- Lead forms at the **negative cathode**.
- Bromine forms at the **positive anode**.
- **2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻**.

**23.** Predict the products from aqueous sodium sulfate with inert electrodes and explain your choices.

**Answer:**

- **Hydrogen at the cathode**: sodium is more reactive than hydrogen.
- **Oxygen at the anode**: the solution contains no halide ions.
- Water supplies hydrogen and hydroxide ions, which compete with the dissolved salt ions.

**24.** Why should a titration burette be rinsed with acid, but the conical flask may be rinsed with distilled water?

**Answer:**

- Water left in the burette would **dilute the acid** and change its concentration.
- Rinsing with acid avoids that dilution.
- Water in the conical flask does **not change the moles of alkali**, so it does not change the reacting acid amount.

**25.** A student obtains titres 24.60 cm³ (rough), 24.20 cm³, 24.10 cm³ and 24.15 cm³. Using a concordance tolerance of 0.10 cm³, calculate the mean titre and explain the selection.

**Answer:**

- Exclude the **rough trial**.
- The three accurate titres are within 0.10 cm³ of each other.
- Mean = (24.20 + 24.10 + 24.15)/3 = **24.15 cm³**.

## 4 marks

**26.** Compare a dilute strong acid and a concentrated weak acid. Explain why strength alone is insufficient to decide which sample has the lower pH.

**Answer:**

- Strong means **fully ionised**; weak means **partly ionised** in water.
- Dilute means little acid per volume; concentrated means much acid per volume.
- pH depends on the resulting **H⁺ concentration**, which depends on both properties.
- Without concentrations and ionisation information, strength alone cannot determine the lower pH.

**27.** Copper chloride solution is electrolysed in the apparatus below using inert electrodes. Name the products and write both electrode half-equations.

![Electrolysis setup with a negative cathode and positive anode.](../Diagrams/electrolysis.svg)

**Answer:**

- Cathode product: **copper**.
- **Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu**.
- Anode product: **chlorine**.
- **2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻**.

**28.** Compare the products of molten and aqueous sodium chloride electrolysis, using inert electrodes. Explain the difference at the cathode.

**Answer:**

- Molten: **sodium at cathode, chlorine at anode**.
- Aqueous: **hydrogen at cathode, chlorine at anode**.
- The aqueous solution also contains ions from **water**.
- Sodium is more reactive than hydrogen, so hydrogen is produced instead of sodium in the aqueous solution.

**29.** Write the anode half-equation for oxygen production from hydroxide ions. Show that charge and atoms balance.

**Answer:**

- **4OH⁻ → O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻**.
- Both sides contain **four oxygen atoms and four hydrogen atoms**.
- Left total charge is −4; right total charge is also −4 from the four electrons.
- Electrons are lost, so this is **oxidation**.

## 5 marks

**30.** 25.0 cm³ NaOH is neutralised by 18.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ H₂SO₄. Calculate NaOH concentration in mol/dm³ and g/dm³. H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O. Mᵣ: NaOH = 40.

**Answer:**

- Acid volume = **0.0180 dm³**.
- Acid moles = 0.100 × 0.0180 = **0.00180 mol**.
- NaOH moles = 2 × 0.00180 = **0.00360 mol**.
- NaOH concentration = 0.00360/0.0250 = **0.144 mol/dm³**.
- In g/dm³: 0.144 × 40 = **5.76 g/dm³**.

## 6 marks

**31.** Describe how to prepare a pure, dry sample of copper sulfate crystals using copper oxide and dilute sulfuric acid. Explain the main stages.

**Answer:**

- Wear goggles and **gently warm dilute sulfuric acid**, then remove the burner.
- Add copper oxide in small portions while stirring until **excess solid remains**, ensuring all acid has reacted.
- **Filter** to remove the excess insoluble oxide; keep the copper sulfate filtrate.
- Use a water bath or electric heater to **evaporate some water**, concentrating the solution without strongly heating it to dryness.
- Leave to **cool and crystallise**.
- Filter crystals, rinse with a little cold distilled water and dry between filter papers.

**32.** Describe how to find accurate reacting volumes for hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide by titration.

**Answer:**

- Rinse a volumetric pipette with alkali and use a filler to transfer a fixed volume to a conical flask. Add a few drops of phenolphthalein; use a white tile and goggles.
- Rinse the burette with acid, fill it, remove the funnel and ensure the jet has no air bubble.
- Record the **initial meniscus reading at eye level**.
- Add acid while swirling; add **drop by drop near the endpoint** until the first permanent pink-to-colourless change.
- Record the final reading; **titre = final − initial**.
- After a rough trial, repeat to obtain **concordant accurate titres** and calculate their mean, excluding the rough trial.

**33.** Plan an investigation to test whether changing an aqueous salt changes electrolysis products. Include control variables and product identification.

**Answer:**

- Predict products for two solutions, such as copper chloride and sodium sulfate, using the ions present and reactivity rules.
- Use a low-voltage DC supply and **inert electrodes**, keeping them apart. Identify each electrode polarity.
- Keep concentration, volume, voltage, electrode area/separation and reaction time constant. Use clean electrodes to prevent contamination.
- Observe each electrode separately and collect any gases: hydrogen gives a pop; oxygen relights a glowing splint; chlorine bleaches damp litmus. Look for a copper deposit as well.
- Repeat each trial and compare observations with predictions; check the circuit before treating lack of bubbles as evidence of no product.
- Wear goggles, use the instructed ventilation for chlorine and switch off before adjusting equipment.
