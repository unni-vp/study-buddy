# Periodic patterns

## Modern & historical tables

- Modern order: **atomic number**. Groups link outer electrons and reactions; periods link occupied shells.
- Mendeleev used mass/properties, left gaps and predicted missing elements. Discoveries supported predictions; isotopes explain mass-order anomalies.

## Group 0

- Full outer shell: 8 electrons except **helium has 2**. Very unreactive, monatomic gases.
- Boiling point increases with atomic mass down the group. Use the trend to predict values.

## Group 1

- One outer electron → **+1 ions**. Li, Na, K: water → hydroxide + H₂; oxygen → oxides; chlorine → chlorides.
- **2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂.** Alkaline solution; reactions get more vigorous Li → Na → K.
- Down group: more distance/shielding → outer electron lost more easily → reactivity rises.

## Group 7

- Seven outer electrons; gain one → **−1 ions**. Diatomic Cl₂, Br₂, I₂. Metal halides ionic; non-metal compounds covalent.
- Cl₂ green gas; Br₂ red-brown liquid; I₂ grey-black solid. Melting/boiling points rise down group.
- More distance/shielding → weaker attraction for incoming electron → reactivity falls.

## Halogen displacement

- More reactive halogen displaces less reactive halogen from its aqueous salt.
- **Cl₂ + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br₂.** Ionic: Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂.
- Iodine cannot displace chlorine from chloride. Use group position to predict.

## Transition metals

- Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu: generally stronger/harder/denser, higher melting and less reactive than Group 1.
- Many have variable ion charges, coloured compounds and catalytic uses.
- Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺; blue copper sulfate; iron/nickel catalysts. Trends have exceptions.
