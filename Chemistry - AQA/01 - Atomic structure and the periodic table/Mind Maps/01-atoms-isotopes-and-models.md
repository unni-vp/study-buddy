# Atoms, isotopes & models

## Nucleus & charge

- Protons +1 and neutrons 0 each have relative mass 1. Electrons −1 have tiny mass.
- Atomic number = protons. Neutrons = mass number − atomic number.
- Na-23: 11p, 12n, 11e. Na⁺: 11p, 12n, 10e.

## Size & isotope averages

- Atom radius ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ m; nucleus ≈ 10⁻¹⁴ m or less. Almost all mass is nuclear. **1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m.**
- Isotopes: same protons, different neutrons. Aᵣ weights each isotope by abundance.
- 75% mass 35 + 25% mass 37 → **35.5**.

## Shells & position

- Lowest shells fill first: **2,8,8,2** for first 20 elements. O: 2,6; Na: 2,8,1; Ca: 2,8,8,2.
- Period = occupied shells. Same main group = same outer electrons → similar reactions.
- Metals generally lose electrons; non-metals gain/share.

## Atoms → ions

- Losing negative electrons produces a **positive ion**; gaining them produces a negative ion.
- The number of protons stays unchanged: it remains the same element.
- Full outer shells help explain the stability of noble gases.

## Scattering conclusions

- Most alpha particles pass → atom mostly empty space.
- Some deflect → nucleus is positive and repels positive alpha particles.
- Very few return → tiny, dense nucleus holds most mass; contradicts spread-out positive charge.

## Changing models

- Indivisible spheres → discovery of electron → plum pudding. Scattering → nuclear model.
- Bohr: specific electron shells. Later work: protons. Chadwick: neutrons.
- New experimental evidence can replace a scientific model.
