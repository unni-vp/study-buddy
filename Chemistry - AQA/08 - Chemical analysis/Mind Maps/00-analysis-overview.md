# Chemical analysis

## Purity and formulations

Pure: one element or compound.

Sharp melting/boiling point at stated conditions.

Formulation: designed mixture, measured proportions.

Natural does not automatically mean chemically pure.

## Chromatography

Stationary paper, mobile solvent.

Different phase attraction gives separation.

Rf = spot distance/solvent-front distance.

Same baseline, units and comparison conditions.

## Gas identification

Hydrogen: burning splint, pop.

Oxygen: glowing splint relights.

Carbon dioxide: limewater milky.

Chlorine: damp litmus bleached white.

## Metal-ion tests

Flame colours identify some cations.

Sodium hydroxide forms characteristic precipitates.

Aluminium white dissolves in excess alkali.

White alone does not distinguish all cations.

## Anion tests

Carbonate +acid: carbon dioxide.

Halide: nitric acid then silver nitrate.

Sulfate: hydrochloric acid then barium chloride.

Use fresh sample portions and clean reagents.

## Instruments and evidence

Flame emission: reference line patterns identify ions.

Calibrated signals measure concentration.

Rapid, sensitive and accurate.

Record test and observation before conclusion.
