# Chemical analysis questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** What is a chemically pure substance?

**Answer:** A single element or compound, not mixed with other substances.

**2.** What is a formulation?

**Answer:** A mixture designed to have useful properties.

**3.** Name the mobile phase in paper chromatography.

**Answer:** The solvent.

**4.** Which gas relights a glowing splint?

**Answer:** Oxygen.

**5.** Which gas bleaches damp litmus white?

**Answer:** Chlorine.

**6.** Which metal ion gives a lilac flame?

**Answer:** Potassium.

**7.** Which metal ion gives a crimson flame?

**Answer:** Lithium.

**8.** Which metal ion gives a yellow flame?

**Answer:** Sodium.

**9.** What colour is iron(III) hydroxide precipitate?

**Answer:** Brown.

**10.** Which instrumental technique identifies metal ions from emitted spectral lines?

**Answer:** Flame emission spectroscopy.

**11.** Which reagent sequence tests for chloride ions without adding chloride first? Choose one: A hydrochloric acid then silver nitrate; B nitric acid then silver nitrate; C sulfuric acid then barium chloride; D sodium hydroxide then limewater.

**Answer:** B: nitric acid then silver nitrate.

## 2 marks

**12.** Why can “pure milk” still be chemically impure?

**Answer:** - Everyday “pure” can mean nothing was added.
- Milk contains several substances, so it is a **mixture**, not one element or compound.

**13.** How can melting-point data help identify an impure solid?

**Answer:** - Compare with a known pure reference at comparable conditions.
- Impurity commonly gives a **lower melting point and a melting range**, rather than a sharp reference temperature.

**14.** Why are formulation ingredients carefully measured?

**Answer:** - Each component has a purpose.
- Their proportions control the product’s required properties and consistency.

**15.** Why use pencil for a chromatography baseline?

**Answer:** - Graphite does not dissolve in the usual solvent.
- Ink could dissolve and separate, contaminating the chromatogram.

**16.** Why keep the starting spots above solvent level?

**Answer:** - Submerged samples could dissolve into the solvent reservoir.
- They would not begin a controlled separation along the paper from the baseline.

**17.** Why mark the solvent front promptly?

**Answer:** - Solvent evaporates after the paper is removed.
- Marking it preserves the travelled distance needed for a valid **Rf** calculation.

**18.** A spot moves 4.2 cm and the solvent front 7.0 cm from the baseline. Calculate Rf.

**Answer:** - Rf = 4.2/7.0.
- **0.60**, with no unit.

**19.** Explain why a compound can have different Rf values on two papers in the same solvent.

**Answer:** - Its attraction to the stationary phases can differ.
- More time associated with a paper means less movement with solvent and a lower Rf.

**20.** Describe the hydrogen test and positive result.

**Answer:** - Hold a **burning splint** at the open end of a tube containing the gas.
- Hydrogen burns with a **squeaky pop**.

**21.** Describe the carbon-dioxide test and result.

**Answer:** - Bubble the gas through **limewater**.
- It turns **milky/cloudy**.

**22.** State copper’s flame-test colour and sodium-hydroxide precipitate colour.

**Answer:** - Flame: **green**.
- Hydroxide precipitate: **blue**.

**23.** How can aluminium ions be distinguished from calcium or magnesium ions using sodium hydroxide?

**Answer:** - All initially give **white precipitates**.
- Only aluminium’s precipitate **dissolves in excess sodium hydroxide** among these three.

**24.** State the initial hydroxide-precipitate colours of iron(II) and iron(III).

**Answer:** - Iron(II): **green**.
- Iron(III): **brown**.

**25.** Which metal ion gives an orange-red flame, and why is a clean loop needed?

**Answer:** - **Calcium**.
- A clean loop prevents previous samples contaminating the colour.

**26.** State two advantages of instrumental analysis over simple chemical tests.

**Answer:** - It can be **more sensitive**, detecting smaller quantities.
- It can be **rapid and accurate**, with quantitative measurements when calibrated.

## 3 marks

**27.** Describe the test for chloride ions.

**Answer:** - Acidify a fresh dissolved sample with **dilute nitric acid**.
- Add **silver nitrate solution**.
- A **white precipitate** indicates silver chloride.

**28.** Describe the test for bromide ions.

**Answer:** - Add **dilute nitric acid** to a fresh solution sample.
- Add silver nitrate solution.
- A **cream precipitate** indicates bromide.

**29.** Describe the test for iodide ions.

**Answer:** - Acidify with dilute nitric acid.
- Add silver nitrate solution.
- A **yellow precipitate** indicates iodide.

**30.** Describe the test for sulfate ions.

**Answer:** - Acidify the sample with **dilute hydrochloric acid**.
- Add **barium chloride solution**.
- A **white barium sulfate precipitate** indicates sulfate.

**31.** Describe the test for carbonate ions.

**Answer:** - Add a suitable **dilute acid** to the sample.
- Collect/bubble evolved gas into limewater.
- Limewater turns **milky**, identifying carbon dioxide and supporting carbonate.

**32.** Why must hydrochloric acid not be used to acidify a halide test?

**Answer:** - It introduces **chloride ions**.
- Silver nitrate would then form white silver chloride precipitate from the reagent itself.
- Dilute **nitric acid** avoids this false chloride result.

**33.** Explain why one chromatography spot does not always prove purity.

**Answer:** - Different compounds may overlap under those conditions.
- Some components may not dissolve or travel clearly.
- A different solvent or independent test can help separate/detect them.

**34.** Write a balanced ionic equation for copper(II) hydroxide formation.

**Answer:** - Copper ion: **Cu²⁺**; hydroxide: **OH⁻**.
- Two hydroxide ions balance charge and give the correct formula.
- **Cu²⁺(aq)+2OH⁻(aq) →Cu(OH)₂(s)**.

**35.** Explain how flame emission spectroscopy identifies ions and measures concentration.

**Answer:** - Put the sample in a flame and pass emitted light through a spectroscope.
- Match line positions/patterns against reference spectra to identify ions.
- Compare a suitable signal with **calibrated concentration data** for quantity.

## 4 marks

**36.** Plan a flame test to check for potassium ions.

**Answer:** - Clean a suitable metal wire loop to remove contaminating colour.
- Put a small sample on the loop.
- Hold it in a **blue non-luminous flame** and observe.
- A **lilac flame** supports potassium; use suitable hot-wire/acid precautions.

**37.** An unknown gives a green flame and a white precipitate after hydrochloric acid/barium chloride. Identify the ions and a possible formula.

**Answer:** - Green flame supports **copper ions**.
- Acidified barium chloride white precipitate supports **sulfate ions**.
- In this usual single-compound context the cation is Cu²⁺ and anion SO₄²⁻.
- Formula: **CuSO₄**, with balanced charge.

**38.** An unknown gives a white sodium-hydroxide precipitate that dissolves in excess, and a yellow acidified silver-nitrate precipitate. Identify both ions and a formula.

**Answer:** - White precipitate dissolving in excess supports **Al³⁺**.
- Yellow silver-nitrate precipitate supports **I⁻**.
- Three iodides balance one aluminium ion.
- Formula: **AlI₃**.

**39.** Explain how to compare an unknown dye with references fairly using chromatography.

**Answer:** - Spot the unknown and references on the same baseline/paper or use matched conditions.
- Use the same solvent and keep conditions consistent.
- Compare spot positions and Rf values measured from the baseline.
- Matching values support identity but coincident spots can occur, so use another solvent/test if uncertain.

## 5 marks

**40.** On a chromatogram, the pencil baseline is 1.0 cm above the bottom of the paper. The solvent front is at 9.0 cm. An unknown has spot centres at 3.0 cm and 7.0 cm. Calculate both Rf values. A reference dye has Rf = 0.25 in the same conditions. What do the results show about the unknown, and what limit should be placed on identifying its components?

**Answer:** - Solvent distance from the baseline = 9.0 − 1.0 = **8.0 cm**.
- First spot distance = 3.0 − 1.0 = **2.0 cm**, so **Rf = 2.0/8.0 = 0.25**.
- Second spot distance = 7.0 − 1.0 = **6.0 cm**, so **Rf = 6.0/8.0 = 0.75**. Rf has no unit.
- Two separated spots show that the unknown is a **mixture**. Its first spot matches the reference dye's Rf.
- This match **supports but does not prove** the dye's identity because different compounds can coincide. Another solvent or suitable technique can provide further evidence.

## 6 marks

**41.** Plan a chromatography investigation to separate and compare coloured samples.

**Answer:** - Draw a pencil baseline and apply small dried sample/reference spots.
- Place paper in a covered vessel with solvent below the baseline.
- Allow solvent to rise, then remove before it reaches the top and mark the front promptly.
- Measure baseline-to-spot-centre and baseline-to-front distances, calculating dimensionless Rf.
- Compare matched conditions, repeat and try another solvent if components overlap.
- Use appropriate solvent safety and evaluate spot spreading/measurement uncertainty.

**42.** Plan tests identifying potassium bromide in an unknown single-compound sample.

**Answer:** - Use clean equipment and dissolve a small amount in distilled water where required.
- Perform a flame test on a clean loop in a blue flame; **lilac** supports potassium.
- Use a fresh solution portion for the anion test.
- Add dilute **nitric acid**, then silver nitrate solution.
- A **cream precipitate** supports bromide; combine evidence as KBr.
- Repeat uncertain observations and use eye protection, small quantities and safe hot-wire/reagent handling.
