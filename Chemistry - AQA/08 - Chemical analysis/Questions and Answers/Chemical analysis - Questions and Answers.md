# Chemical analysis questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** What is a chemically pure substance?

**Answer:** A single element or compound, not mixed with other substances.

**2.** What is a formulation?

**Answer:** A mixture designed to have useful properties.

**3.** Name the mobile phase in paper chromatography.

**Answer:** The solvent.

**4.** Which gas relights a glowing splint?

**Answer:** Oxygen.

**5.** Which gas bleaches damp litmus white?

**Answer:** Chlorine.

**6.** Which metal ion gives a lilac flame?

**Answer:** Potassium.

**7.** Which metal ion gives a crimson flame?

**Answer:** Lithium.

**8.** Which metal ion gives a yellow flame?

**Answer:** Sodium.

**9.** What colour is iron(III) hydroxide precipitate?

**Answer:** Brown.

**10.** Which instrumental technique identifies metal ions from emitted spectral lines?

**Answer:** Flame emission spectroscopy.

## 2 marks

**11.** Why can “pure milk” still be chemically impure?

**Answer:**

- Everyday “pure” can mean nothing was added.
- Milk contains several substances, so it is a **mixture**, not one element or compound.

**12.** How can melting-point data help identify an impure solid?

**Answer:**

- Compare with a known pure reference at comparable conditions.
- Impurity commonly gives a **lower melting point and a melting range**, rather than a sharp reference temperature.

**13.** Why are formulation ingredients carefully measured?

**Answer:**

- Each component has a purpose.
- Their proportions control the product’s required properties and consistency.

**14.** Why use pencil for a chromatography baseline?

**Answer:**

- Graphite does not dissolve in the usual solvent.
- Ink could dissolve and separate, contaminating the chromatogram.

**15.** Why keep the starting spots above solvent level?

**Answer:**

- Submerged samples could dissolve into the solvent reservoir.
- They would not begin a controlled separation along the paper from the baseline.

**16.** Why mark the solvent front promptly?

**Answer:**

- Solvent evaporates after the paper is removed.
- Marking it preserves the travelled distance needed for a valid **Rf** calculation.

**17.** A spot moves 4.2 cm and the solvent front 7.0 cm from the baseline. Calculate Rf.

**Answer:**

- Rf = 4.2/7.0.
- **0.60**, with no unit.

**18.** Explain why a compound can have different Rf values on two papers in the same solvent.

**Answer:**

- Its attraction to the stationary phases can differ.
- More time associated with a paper means less movement with solvent and a lower Rf.

**19.** Describe the hydrogen test and positive result.

**Answer:**

- Hold a **burning splint** at the open end of a tube containing the gas.
- Hydrogen burns with a **squeaky pop**.

**20.** Describe the carbon-dioxide test and result.

**Answer:**

- Bubble the gas through **limewater**.
- It turns **milky/cloudy**.

**21.** State copper’s flame-test colour and sodium-hydroxide precipitate colour.

**Answer:**

- Flame: **green**.
- Hydroxide precipitate: **blue**.

**22.** How can aluminium ions be distinguished from calcium or magnesium ions using sodium hydroxide?

**Answer:**

- All initially give **white precipitates**.
- Only aluminium’s precipitate **dissolves in excess sodium hydroxide** among these three.

**23.** State the initial hydroxide-precipitate colours of iron(II) and iron(III).

**Answer:**

- Iron(II): **green**.
- Iron(III): **brown**.

**24.** Which metal ion gives an orange-red flame, and why is a clean loop needed?

**Answer:**

- **Calcium**.
- A clean loop prevents previous samples contaminating the colour.

**25.** State two advantages of instrumental analysis over simple chemical tests.

**Answer:**

- It can be **more sensitive**, detecting smaller quantities.
- It can be **rapid and accurate**, with quantitative measurements when calibrated.

## 3 marks

**26.** Describe the test for chloride ions.

**Answer:**

- Acidify a fresh dissolved sample with **dilute nitric acid**.
- Add **silver nitrate solution**.
- A **white precipitate** indicates silver chloride.

**27.** Describe the test for bromide ions.

**Answer:**

- Add **dilute nitric acid** to a fresh solution sample.
- Add silver nitrate solution.
- A **cream precipitate** indicates bromide.

**28.** Describe the test for iodide ions.

**Answer:**

- Acidify with dilute nitric acid.
- Add silver nitrate solution.
- A **yellow precipitate** indicates iodide.

**29.** Describe the test for sulfate ions.

**Answer:**

- Acidify the sample with **dilute hydrochloric acid**.
- Add **barium chloride solution**.
- A **white barium sulfate precipitate** indicates sulfate.

**30.** Describe the test for carbonate ions.

**Answer:**

- Add a suitable **dilute acid** to the sample.
- Collect/bubble evolved gas into limewater.
- Limewater turns **milky**, identifying carbon dioxide and supporting carbonate.

**31.** Why must hydrochloric acid not be used to acidify a halide test?

**Answer:**

- It introduces **chloride ions**.
- Silver nitrate would then form white silver chloride precipitate from the reagent itself.
- Dilute **nitric acid** avoids this false chloride result.

**32.** Explain why one chromatography spot does not always prove purity.

**Answer:**

- Different compounds may overlap under those conditions.
- Some components may not dissolve or travel clearly.
- A different solvent or independent test can help separate/detect them.

**33.** Write a balanced ionic equation for copper(II) hydroxide formation.

**Answer:**

- Copper ion: **Cu²⁺**; hydroxide: **OH⁻**.
- Two hydroxide ions balance charge and give the correct formula.
- **Cu²⁺(aq)+2OH⁻(aq) →Cu(OH)₂(s)**.

**34.** Explain how flame emission spectroscopy identifies ions and measures concentration.

**Answer:**

- Put the sample in a flame and pass emitted light through a spectroscope.
- Match line positions/patterns against reference spectra to identify ions.
- Compare a suitable signal with **calibrated concentration data** for quantity.

## 4 marks

**35.** Plan a flame test to check for potassium ions.

**Answer:**

- Clean a suitable metal wire loop to remove contaminating colour.
- Put a small sample on the loop.
- Hold it in a **blue non-luminous flame** and observe.
- A **lilac flame** supports potassium; use suitable hot-wire/acid precautions.

**36.** An unknown gives a green flame and a white precipitate after hydrochloric acid/barium chloride. Identify the ions and a possible formula.

**Answer:**

- Green flame supports **copper ions**.
- Acidified barium chloride white precipitate supports **sulfate ions**.
- In this usual single-compound context the cation is Cu²⁺ and anion SO₄²⁻.
- Formula: **CuSO₄**, with balanced charge.

**37.** An unknown gives a white sodium-hydroxide precipitate that dissolves in excess, and a yellow acidified silver-nitrate precipitate. Identify both ions and a formula.

**Answer:**

- White precipitate dissolving in excess supports **Al³⁺**.
- Yellow silver-nitrate precipitate supports **I⁻**.
- Three iodides balance one aluminium ion.
- Formula: **AlI₃**.

**38.** Explain how to compare an unknown dye with references fairly using chromatography.

**Answer:**

- Spot the unknown and references on the same baseline/paper or use matched conditions.
- Use the same solvent and keep conditions consistent.
- Compare spot positions and Rf values measured from the baseline.
- Matching values support identity but coincident spots can occur, so use another solvent/test if uncertain.

## 6 marks

**39.** Plan a chromatography investigation to separate and compare coloured samples.

**Answer:**

- Draw a pencil baseline and apply small dried sample/reference spots.
- Place paper in a covered vessel with solvent below the baseline.
- Allow solvent to rise, then remove before it reaches the top and mark the front promptly.
- Measure baseline-to-spot-centre and baseline-to-front distances, calculating dimensionless Rf.
- Compare matched conditions, repeat and try another solvent if components overlap.
- Use appropriate solvent safety and evaluate spot spreading/measurement uncertainty.

**40.** Plan tests identifying potassium bromide in an unknown single-compound sample.

**Answer:**

- Use clean equipment and dissolve a small amount in distilled water where required.
- Perform a flame test on a clean loop in a blue flame; **lilac** supports potassium.
- Use a fresh solution portion for the anion test.
- Add dilute **nitric acid**, then silver nitrate solution.
- A **cream precipitate** supports bromide; combine evidence as KBr.
- Repeat uncertain observations and use eye protection, small quantities and safe hot-wire/reagent handling.
