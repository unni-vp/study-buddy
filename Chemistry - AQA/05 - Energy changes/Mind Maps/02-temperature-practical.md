# Temperature practical

## Choose the variable

- Example hypothesis: raising acid concentration increases the temperature rise during neutralisation until alkali limits reaction.
- Independent: acid concentration. Dependent: temperature change.
- Keep acid/alkali volumes and alkali concentration fixed.

## Prepare & control

- Polystyrene cup supported in beaker; lid and thermometer. Use fresh measured solutions at the same starting temperature.
- Control apparatus, initial temperature and stirring/recording method. Goggles; handle chemicals as instructed.

## Mix & record

- Record starting temperature. Mix, replace lid, stir consistently; record at regular intervals.
- Exothermic: find maximum. Endothermic: find minimum.
- Change = extreme − initial. 20.5 → 28.0 °C gives **+7.5 °C**.

## Repeat & plot

- Repeat each condition, investigate anomalies and calculate a mean.
- Plot concentration on x-axis, temperature change on y-axis.
- A plateau may occur when alkali becomes limiting. More added acid cannot react after alkali is used up.

## Explain improvements

- Insulation/lid reduce energy transfer to surroundings. Stirring evens out mixture temperature.
- Prompt readings avoid missing peak. Repeats reduce random error effects, not thermometer calibration error.
- If given time data, extrapolate appropriate trends to mixing time to estimate change before heat loss.

## Apply the method

- Other reactions: displacement; acid + metal; acid + carbonate. Decide appropriate variable and controls.
- Allow gases to escape safely; keep flames away from hydrogen.
- Compare applications using measured temperature change, duration, cost and safe operating temperature.
