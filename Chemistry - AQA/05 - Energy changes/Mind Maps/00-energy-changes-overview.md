# Energy changes

## Exothermic vs endothermic

- Exothermic: energy to surroundings; temperature rises; products lower in energy. Combustion, many oxidations, neutralisation.
- Endothermic: energy from surroundings; temperature falls; products higher. Thermal decomposition is an example.

## Reaction profiles

- Vertical axis: energy. Horizontal: reaction progress, not time. Activation energy = minimum energy for a successful collision.
- Draw a curved peak. Activation energy: reactant level → peak. Overall change: reactant → product level.

## Bonds: break minus make

- Breaking bonds takes energy; forming bonds releases energy. Overall = total broken − total formed.
- Negative → exothermic; positive → endothermic. Count every bond and equation coefficient; use supplied values.

## Temperature practical

- Insulated cup/lid, measured volumes, same starting temperature. Mix/stir; record maximum/minimum. Change = extreme − initial.
- Vary one factor; control others, repeat and average. Heat loss can reduce measured rise; use time-graph extrapolation when requested.

## Cells & batteries

- Different electrodes + electrolyte → electricity. Voltage depends on both; compare under controlled conditions.
- Non-rechargeable stops when a reactant runs out. External current reverses reactions in rechargeable cells. Series cell voltages add.

## Hydrogen fuel cells

- Continuous H₂ and O₂ supply; hydrogen oxidised, oxygen reduced. Overall: **2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O**.
- Electrons go through external load; ions through electrolyte.
- Compare refuelling/recharging, storage, cost, lifetime and whole-process emissions, not only water at the outlet.
