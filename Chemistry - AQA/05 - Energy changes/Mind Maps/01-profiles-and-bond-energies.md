# Profiles & bond energies

## Energy is conserved

- Exothermic pays energy out to surroundings → surroundings warm; products have less energy.
- Endothermic takes energy from surroundings → surroundings cool; products have more energy.
- A starting spark does not make an overall reaction endothermic.

## Examples & applications

- Exothermic: combustion, many oxidation reactions, neutralisation → hand warmers/self-heating cans.
- Endothermic: thermal decomposition; citric acid + sodium hydrogencarbonate → cooling applications.
- Judge temperature, duration, safety, cost and disposal.

## Profile axes & peak

- Energy vertically; reaction progress horizontally. Reactants and products at flat levels joined by curved peak.
- Activation energy: minimum collision energy; measure reactant level to peak. Overall change is between reactant and product levels.

## Products decide the sign

- Products lower → energy transferred out, negative overall change. Products higher → energy taken in, positive.
- Activation energy is not measured from the baseline; it is not the overall change.
- Both exothermic and endothermic reactions need activation energy.

## Count bonds correctly

- **Overall = broken − formed.** Breaking takes in energy; making releases it. Include equation coefficients.
- H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl. Given 436, 243, 431 kJ/mol: break 436 + 243 = 679; form 2 × 431 = 862.
- 679 − 862 = **−183 kJ** for molar quantities in equation: exothermic.

## Unknown bond energy

- If H–Cl energy is x and overall change is −183: **679 − 2x = −183**.
- 2x = 862 → x = **431 kJ/mol**. Use the data supplied.
- CH₄ has four C–H bonds; 2H₂O contains four O–H bonds. Do not confuse bonds with molecules.
