# Organisation questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** What is a tissue?

**Answer:** A group of similar cells working together to perform a particular function.

**2.** Name the reagent used to test for protein.

**Answer:** Biuret reagent.

**3.** Name the enzyme that digests starch.

**Answer:** Amylase.

**4.** Which blood vessels supply the heart muscle itself with oxygen?

**Answer:** Coronary arteries.

**5.** Name the cells that control the opening and closing of stomata.

**Answer:** Guard cells.

**6.** Name the movement of dissolved sugars through phloem.

**Answer:** Translocation.

**7.** Name the plant tissue containing actively dividing cells at growing tips.

**Answer:** Meristem tissue.

## 2 marks

**8.** Explain why large food molecules need to be digested before absorption.

**Answer:**

- They are **large and insoluble**, so cannot pass through the intestinal wall into the blood.
- Digestion breaks them into **small, soluble molecules** that can be absorbed.

**9.** State the products of protein digestion and lipid digestion.

**Answer:**

- Protein → **amino acids**.
- Lipid → **glycerol and fatty acids**.

**10.** Explain why an enzyme normally acts on only one type of substrate.

**Answer:**

- Its **active site has a specific shape**.
- Only a substrate with a **complementary shape** can bind effectively.

**11.** Explain why the left ventricle has a thicker muscular wall than the right ventricle.

**Answer:**

- It must pump blood around the **whole body**, rather than only to the lungs.
- Its thicker muscle generates a **higher pressure**.

**12.** Explain how a stent can improve the oxygen supply to heart muscle.

**Answer:**

- It holds a narrowed coronary artery **open**.
- This increases blood flow, delivering more **oxygen** to the heart muscle.

**13.** Give two differences between benign and malignant tumours.

**Answer:**

- **Benign** tumours remain within one area; **malignant** tumours invade nearby tissues.
- Malignant cells can spread in the blood and form **secondary tumours**; benign tumours do not spread this way.

**14.** Give two differences between the substances and tissues involved in transpiration and translocation.

**Answer:**

- The transpiration stream carries **water and mineral ions in xylem**.
- Translocation carries **dissolved sugars in phloem**.

**15.** Why does high humidity reduce transpiration, if other conditions stay the same?

**Answer:**

- The outside air contains more water vapour.
- This makes the **water-vapour concentration gradient** between the leaf and air smaller, so diffusion is slower.

**16.** An amylase reaction takes 80 seconds to remove all the starch. Calculate its relative rate, giving a unit.

**Answer:** **Rate = 1 ÷ time = 1 ÷ 80 = 0.0125 s⁻¹.**

## 3 marks

**17.** Explain why an enzyme reaction speeds up as temperature rises towards the optimum, but slows rapidly above it.

**Answer:**

- Below the optimum, particles gain kinetic energy, causing more **successful collisions**.
- Above the optimum, the enzyme becomes **denatured**: its active site changes shape.
- The substrate no longer fits, so fewer enzyme–substrate complexes form.

**18.** Explain three ways in which alveoli are adapted for rapid gas exchange.

**Answer:**

- Many alveoli give a **large surface area** for diffusion.
- Alveolar and capillary walls are **one cell thick**, giving a short diffusion distance.
- Ventilation and a good blood supply maintain **steep concentration gradients**.

**19.** Describe the roles of red blood cells, platelets and plasma.

**Answer:**

- Red blood cells carry **oxygen using haemoglobin**.
- Platelets help blood **clot**, reducing blood loss.
- Plasma transports blood cells and dissolved substances such as carbon dioxide, urea and glucose.

**20.** A person has a heart rate of 72 beats per minute and a stroke volume of 75 cm³. Calculate cardiac output in dm³ per minute.

**Answer:**

- Output = heart rate × stroke volume.
- 72 × 75 = **5,400 cm³/min**.
- 5,400 ÷ 1,000 = **5.4 dm³/min**.

**21.** Explain how smoking can increase the risk of coronary heart disease and why an association in a small survey is not enough to prove causation.

**Answer:**

- Smoking can damage artery linings and increase the risk of **fatty deposits/narrowing**.
- Reduced coronary blood flow can reduce the **oxygen supply** to heart muscle.
- Other variables, such as diet and age, may differ between groups; these **confounding factors** must be considered.

**22.** Explain why wind usually increases the rate of water loss from a leaf.

**Answer:**

- Wind removes **humid air** from around the leaf.
- This maintains a **steeper water-vapour concentration gradient**.
- Water vapour therefore diffuses out through stomata faster.

**23.** Describe three leaf-tissue adaptations and link each to its function.

**Answer:**

- Palisade cells contain many **chloroplasts**, absorbing light for photosynthesis.
- Spongy mesophyll has **air spaces**, allowing gases to diffuse through the leaf.
- The waxy cuticle reduces **evaporation**, limiting water loss.

**24.** In a potometer, an air bubble moves 36 mm in 3 minutes. Calculate the movement rate and explain why this is not an exact measurement of transpiration.

**Answer:**

- Rate = 36 ÷ 3 = **12 mm/min**.
- The potometer measures **water uptake**, rather than water vapour lost.
- Some absorbed water is used in photosynthesis or retained by the plant.

## 4 marks

**25.** Explain how bile helps lipid digestion in the small intestine.

**Answer:**

- Bile is **alkaline**, so it neutralises acid arriving from the stomach.
- This provides suitable pH conditions for intestinal enzymes.
- It **emulsifies** lipids into smaller droplets.
- This increases the **surface area** for lipase to act on; bile itself is not an enzyme.

**26.** Describe the route of blood from the body, through the heart and lungs, and back to the body.

**Answer:**

- Body → **vena cava → right atrium → right ventricle**.
- Right ventricle → **pulmonary artery → lungs**.
- Lungs → **pulmonary vein → left atrium → left ventricle**.
- Left ventricle → **aorta → body**. Valves prevent backflow.

**27.** Compare arteries, veins and capillaries, linking their structures to their functions.

**Answer:**

- Arteries carry blood **away from the heart**; thick muscular and elastic walls cope with high pressure.
- Veins return blood at lower pressure; their **wide lumen** reduces resistance.
- Veins have **valves to prevent backflow**.
- Capillaries have **one-cell-thick walls**, giving a short diffusion distance for exchange with tissues.

**28.** Explain how water moves from the soil into a root hair and then leaves a leaf.

**Answer:**

- Water enters root hairs by **osmosis** through partially permeable membranes.
- It moves into the **xylem**, which carries water up the plant.
- Water **evaporates** from mesophyll cell surfaces into air spaces.
- Water vapour **diffuses out through stomata**; water loss helps pull the transpiration stream upwards.

**29.** The mean amylase reaction times are 180 s at pH 4, 60 s at pH 7 and 150 s at pH 9. Identify the fastest condition, calculate its relative rate, and explain the effect of pH.

**Answer:**

- **pH 7** is fastest because it has the shortest time.
- Rate = 1 ÷ 60 = **0.0167 s⁻¹** (3 significant figures).
- Changing pH can change the shape of the **active site**.
- At unsuitable pH, fewer substrate molecules bind; only the three tested pH values can be compared, so the exact optimum needs more measurements.

**30.** A survey finds 30 cases of a disease among 600 smokers and 20 among 1,000 non-smokers. Calculate the percentage in each group and evaluate what the comparison shows.

**Answer:**

- Smokers: 30 ÷ 600 × 100 = **5%**.
- Non-smokers: 20 ÷ 1,000 × 100 = **2%**.
- The proportion is 2.5 times as high in the smoker group, showing an **association**.
- It does not alone prove causation: age, diet or other factors may differ, and the sample may not represent the population.

## 6 marks

**31.** Describe a safe method to test a crushed food sample for starch, reducing sugar, protein and lipid. Include the positive results.

**Answer:**

- Mix the crushed food with distilled water; use **separate samples** and clean equipment for each test.
- Add **iodine**: a **blue-black** colour shows starch.
- Add **Benedict’s reagent**, then heat in a hot water bath: a green, yellow, orange or brick-red precipitate shows reducing sugar.
- Add **Biuret reagent**: **purple** shows protein.
- Add **Sudan III**, shake gently and allow layers to settle: a **red-stained oil layer** shows lipid.
- Wear goggles; handle the hot water bath and tubes carefully. Use water as a negative control and known nutrients as positive controls so results can be checked.

**32.** Describe how to investigate the effect of pH on the rate at which amylase breaks down starch. Explain how to make the comparison valid.

**Answer:**

- Put drops of iodine into separate wells of a spotting tile. Use **buffer solutions** to set several different pH values.
- Equilibrate starch, amylase and buffer to the same temperature in a **water bath**. Keep enzyme/starch concentrations and volumes constant.
- Mix measured volumes of starch, buffer and amylase; **start the timer** immediately.
- Every **30 seconds**, transfer a drop to fresh iodine. Record the first time iodine stays orange-brown: no starch is detected.
- Use **1/time** to compare relative rates because the starting amount of starch is the same. Repeat at each pH and calculate a mean.
- Keep sampling intervals identical; shorter intervals improve endpoint resolution. Wear eye protection and avoid contamination between samples.

**33.** Explain how a blocked coronary artery can cause a heart attack, then compare a stent with statins as treatments for coronary heart disease.

**Answer:**

- Fatty deposits **narrow coronary arteries**, reducing blood flow. A blockage can stop oxygen reaching part of the heart muscle.
- Less **aerobic respiration** means less energy for contraction; prolonged oxygen loss can kill muscle cells and cause a heart attack.
- A **stent** opens the narrowed artery and can restore blood flow quickly, but insertion is invasive and carries clotting/infection risks.
- **Statins lower blood cholesterol**, slowing further fatty-deposit build-up and reducing risk; they do not physically reopen a blocked artery.
- Statins need regular use and can cause side effects. A stent treats a particular narrowing but does not remove the underlying risk factors.
- Treatment depends on urgency, severity and the patient’s risks; the two may be used together alongside lifestyle changes.

**34.** Explain how a large reduction in pancreatic enzyme secretion could cause poor digestion and difficulty gaining body mass.

**Answer:**

- Less **amylase** means less starch is broken into sugars.
- Less **protease** means fewer amino acids are released from proteins.
- Less **lipase** means less lipid becomes fatty acids and glycerol.
- Fewer **small, soluble molecules** are available for absorption in the small intestine.
- Fewer amino acids can be used to build new proteins for growth and repair; less lipid is available for storage.
- Less absorbed glucose is available for respiration, so less energy is released for building cells and tissues. These linked effects make gaining mass harder.

**35.** Describe how to use a potometer to investigate the effect of wind speed on water uptake by a leafy shoot. Include one limitation.

**Answer:**

- Cut the shoot **under water** and fit it into a water-filled potometer without unwanted air; make all connections **airtight**. Dry the leaves.
- Introduce an air bubble into the capillary tube and mark its starting position. Allow the shoot to acclimatise.
- Use a fan to provide several measured wind speeds, keeping **temperature, light, humidity and leaf area** as constant as possible.
- Measure bubble distance over a fixed time; calculate **distance/time**. With a known bore radius, volume uptake = **πr² × distance**.
- Reset the bubble, repeat each condition and calculate a mean; check for leaks and allow adjustment between conditions.
- The instrument measures **water uptake**, not exact transpiration: some water is used or retained in the plant.

**36.** Explain how damage to roots and their transport tissues can reduce plant growth.

**Answer:**

- Fewer functioning root hairs reduce **water uptake**; water is a reactant in photosynthesis.
- Reduced **nitrate uptake** limits amino acid and protein production, reducing growth.
- Reduced **magnesium uptake** limits chlorophyll production, reducing light absorption.
- Damaged **xylem** carries less water and fewer mineral ions to the leaves.
- Damaged **phloem** can reduce sugar transport to growing tissues and roots.
- Less photosynthesis means less glucose for respiration and for making cellulose and other new cell materials, so growth slows.
