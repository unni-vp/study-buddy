# Medicines & antibodies

## Antibiotics & symptoms

- Antibiotics such as penicillin treat bacteria; choose an effective one. **Resistance** makes infections harder to treat.
- Antibiotics do not kill viruses. Viruses reproduce inside cells, making safe targeting difficult.
- Painkillers relieve symptoms without killing pathogens.

## Drug origins

- **Digitalis ← foxglove. Aspirin ← willow.**
- **Penicillin:** Fleming’s discovery from Penicillium mould.
- Most new drugs are synthesised; natural chemicals may provide starting points.

## Testing sequence

- Check **toxicity, efficacy and dose**. Preclinical: cells, tissues, live animals.
- Clinical: low doses in healthy volunteers, then patients to test effectiveness and optimum dose.
- Results undergo **peer review** before publication.

## Fair comparisons

- Placebo: no active drug. **Double-blind:** patients and assessors do not know allocations.
- Random allocation, comparable groups and large samples reduce misleading effects.
- Compare recovery rates and side effects, not raw counts alone.

## Make monoclonal antibodies

- Antigen stimulates mouse lymphocytes. Fuse an antibody-producing lymphocyte with a tumour cell → **hybridoma**.
- Select and clone one hybridoma; collect/purify antibodies. Clone makes antibodies with **one specific binding site**.

## Uses & limits

- Specific binding: pregnancy tests, hormone measurement, pathogen detection; fluorescent tags locate molecules.
- Attach toxic drugs/radioactive substances/growth blockers to target cancer cells.
- Targeting reduces unwanted damage but side effects and unsuitable targets can limit benefit.
