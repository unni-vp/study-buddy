# Milk decay and pH: practical

## Measured mechanism

Lipase breaks down fat to fatty acids.

pH falls to a defined indicator endpoint.

Models a component of decay.

Not a direct census of microorganisms.

## Change temperature

Use controlled water baths.

Equilibrate milk and enzyme before mixing.

Test several suitable temperatures.

Start timing when enzyme is added.

## Keep fair

Same milk, enzyme volume/concentration.

Same carbonate, indicator and initial pH.

Same mixing and endpoint rule.

No-enzyme control checks cause of change.

## Repeat and compare

Repeat each temperature.

Calculate comparable mean times.

Rate proxy = 1/time.

Use same endpoint for valid comparison.

## Interpret rates

100 s: 0.010 s⁻¹.

50 s: 0.020 s⁻¹, twice as fast.

Warmer speeds activity up to optimum.

Excess heat reduces enzyme activity.

## Accuracy and safety

pH probe can give continuous readings.

Calibrate and define a common pH interval.

Eye protection for alkaline solutions.

Handle hot water safely. Do not taste mixtures.
