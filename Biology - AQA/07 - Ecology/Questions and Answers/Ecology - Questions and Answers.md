# Ecology questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** What is a population?

**Answer:** All organisms of one species in an area.

**2.** What is biodiversity?

**Answer:** The variety of species in an ecosystem or on Earth.

**3.** What is an abiotic factor?

**Answer:** A non-living environmental factor.

**4.** What is a producer?

**Answer:** An organism that synthesises organic molecules, usually by photosynthesis.

**5.** What does a food-chain arrow represent?

**Answer:** Transfer of material and energy from the organism eaten to its consumer.

**6.** What is an extremophile?

**Answer:** An organism adapted to an extreme environment.

**7.** Name the gas collected from anaerobic biogas generators.

**Answer:** Methane.

**8.** What is food security?

**Answer:** Having enough food to feed a population.

**9.** Which fungus is grown to produce mycoprotein?

**Answer:** Fusarium.

**10.** State the trophic level of a primary consumer.

**Answer:** Level 2.

**11.** Which statement about an ecosystem is correct? Choose one: A energy is recycled into sunlight; B materials cycle while energy transfers through the system and to the surroundings; C decomposers create carbon atoms; D food-chain arrows point from predator to prey.

**Answer:** B: materials cycle while energy transfers through the system and to the surroundings.

## 2 marks

**12.** Give two resources for which plants compete.

**Answer:** Light and water.

**13.** Give two biotic factors that could reduce a rabbit population.

**Answer:** - A new **predator** can increase deaths.
- A new **pathogen** can cause disease and reduce survival.

**14.** Give two abiotic factors affecting plant distribution.

**Answer:** Light intensity and soil moisture.

**15.** Explain why removing pollinating insects can affect a whole community.

**Answer:** - Fewer flowers are pollinated, reducing seed/fruit production.
- Animals relying on those fruits or seeds may lose food: species are **interdependent**.

**16.** Explain how small ears can help an animal in a cold environment.

**Answer:** - They reduce exposed **surface area**.
- Less energy is transferred to the surroundings, conserving body heat.

**17.** Distinguish a structural adaptation from a behavioural adaptation.

**Answer:** - Structural: a physical feature, such as thick fur.
- Behavioural: an action pattern, such as nocturnal activity avoiding daytime heat.

**18.** Why use random coordinates when placing quadrats?

**Answer:** - They reduce **selection bias** towards convenient or obvious locations.
- Samples are more likely to represent the habitat if sufficiently numerous and spread out.

**19.** When is a transect more useful than random quadrats alone?

**Answer:** - When investigating how abundance changes **across an environmental gradient**.
- Regularly spaced quadrats along the line link distribution to distance or measured conditions.

**20.** Why do fish numbers fall if dissolved oxygen decreases greatly?

**Answer:** - Fish need oxygen for **aerobic respiration**.
- Less oxygen limits energy release for essential functions, reducing survival.

**21.** Why does a predator peak often occur after the prey peak?

**Answer:** - More prey provides more food, improving predator survival and reproduction.
- The predator population takes time to increase, so its rise **lags behind** the prey increase.

**22.** Explain why dry compost decays slowly.

**Answer:** - Microorganisms need **water** to grow and function.
- Lack of water reduces enzyme-controlled decomposition.

**23.** Why does turning a compost heap speed aerobic decay?

**Answer:** - It supplies **oxygen**.
- This supports aerobic microbial respiration and activity, increasing decomposition under suitable conditions.

**24.** How does photosynthesis contribute to the carbon cycle?

**Answer:** - Plants take up **carbon dioxide** from air.
- They incorporate its carbon into organic molecules such as glucose.

**25.** Explain why peat-free compost can reduce environmental damage.

**Answer:** - Less peat extraction protects specialist **habitats and biodiversity**.
- It preserves stored carbon that could otherwise be released as carbon dioxide through decay or burning.

**26.** State two ways to conserve fish stocks.

**Answer:** - **Catch quotas** limit numbers taken.
- Appropriate **mesh size** lets small fish escape and reach breeding age.

**27.** How can controlling the temperature around livestock improve growth efficiency?

**Answer:** - Less energy is needed to maintain body temperature.
- More energy/material from food is available for **growth** rather than respiration supporting temperature regulation.

**28.** Give two threats to food security other than population growth.

**Answer:** - New **pests or pathogens** reduce crop yields.
- Failed rainfall or conflict can reduce food production or access.

## 3 marks

**29.** Five 0.5 m² quadrats contain 4, 6, 3, 7 and 5 plants. Estimate population in a 300 m² habitat.

**Answer:** - Mean = (4+6+3+7+5)/5 = **5 plants per quadrat**.
- Density = 5/0.5 = **10 plants m⁻²**.
- Estimate = 10 ×300 = **3000 plants**, assuming samples are representative.

**30.** Calculate the median, mode and range for counts 2, 3, 3, 7, 10.

**Answer:** - Median: middle value = **3**.
- Mode: most frequent value = **3**.
- Range = 10 −2 = **8**.

**31.** Milk reaches the same pH endpoint in 80 s and 40 s. Compare relative decay rates.

**Answer:** - Rates = 1/80 = **0.0125 s⁻¹**, and 1/40 = **0.025 s⁻¹**.
- The second rate is **twice** the first.
- The shorter time represents faster change for the same endpoint.

**32.** A primary-consumer population gains 60 kg from 750 kg of producer biomass. Calculate efficiency and give one cause of loss.

**Answer:** - Efficiency = 60/750 ×100 = **8%**.
- Much producer material may not be eaten or absorbed.
- Absorbed molecules may also be used in respiration, releasing carbon dioxide/water rather than becoming new biomass.

**33.** Explain why very high temperature can slow milk-fat breakdown despite faster particle movement.

**Answer:** - Enzyme structure changes at excessive temperatures.
- The **active site is denatured** and substrate binds less effectively.
- Fewer successful enzyme reactions occur, so breakdown slows or stops.

**34.** Explain how decomposers recycle material.

**Answer:** - They secrete **enzymes** that digest dead matter into soluble molecules outside cells.
- Products are absorbed and used; microbial **respiration releases carbon dioxide**.
- Mineral ions return to soil for uptake by plants.

**35.** Describe how water returns from the sea to land and then back to sea.

**Answer:** - Water **evaporates**, then condenses to form clouds.
- **Precipitation** supplies water to land.
- Runoff and groundwater flow return water through rivers to sea.

**36.** Explain how deforestation can increase atmospheric carbon dioxide.

**Answer:** - Burning wood releases carbon dioxide.
- Decay of remaining biomass also releases it through respiration.
- Fewer trees remove carbon dioxide by **photosynthesis**.

**37.** Explain why higher biodiversity can improve ecosystem stability.

**Answer:** - Species have more alternative sources of food or shelter.
- Losing one species is less likely to remove every supporting resource.
- Interdependent populations may therefore be less vulnerable to a single change.

**38.** Describe conditions used to produce mycoprotein.

**Answer:** - Grow **Fusarium** on glucose syrup with suitable nutrients and temperature.
- Supply **oxygen** for aerobic conditions.
- Harvest and purify the fungal biomass as protein-rich food.

## 4 marks

**39.** Describe a valid investigation of plant abundance from a shady hedge into open grassland.

**Answer:** - Lay a transect across the gradient and place equal-sized quadrats at regular distances.
- Count the species consistently and measure light intensity at each site.
- Repeat along several comparable transects; record soil moisture or other possible influences.
- Plot abundance against light/distance and compare patterns without assuming correlation alone proves causation.

**40.** Describe how to compare milk-fat breakdown at different temperatures.

**Answer:** - Equilibrate equal milk, indicator and alkaline starting mixtures in water baths.
- Add equal volume/concentration of lipase and time a defined pH endpoint.
- Keep starting pH, volumes and mixing consistent, repeat each temperature and use a no-enzyme control.
- Compare mean endpoint times or 1/time rates; use eye protection and safe handling of hot water.

**41.** Explain why biomass normally decreases up a food chain.

**Answer:** - Some organisms or parts are never eaten.
- Some ingested material is **egested** without absorption.
- Respiration uses organic molecules and releases carbon dioxide and water.
- Other absorbed material is excreted in urine, leaving less to build new biomass at the next level.

**42.** Explain how fertiliser pollution can harm aquatic animals.

**Answer:** - Extra mineral ions support rapid algal growth.
- Shading and later algal death provide more material for decomposition.
- Microorganisms use dissolved **oxygen in respiration**.
- Aquatic animals receive insufficient oxygen and can die, reducing biodiversity.

**43.** Explain how a warmer, drier climate could change distribution of a moisture-dependent plant.

**Answer:** - Some existing sites become too dry or hot for survival/reproduction.
- The plant may decline there, while wetter or cooler sites become more suitable.
- Competitors, pollinators and seed dispersal also affect whether new sites are occupied.
- Compare actual time-series and habitat data; suitable climate alone does not guarantee successful colonisation.

## 5 marks

**44.** Five 0.25 m² quadrats contain 2, 3, 5, 4 and 6 plants. The meadow is 100 m². Calculate the mean count and estimated population. The quadrats were all placed beside a heavily trampled path, where plant density is known to be lower than elsewhere. Explain the likely bias and improve the sampling method.

**Answer:** - Mean count = (2 + 3 + 5 + 4 + 6) ÷ 5 = **4 plants per quadrat**.
- The meadow contains 100 ÷ 0.25 = **400 quadrat-sized areas**.
- Estimated population = 4 × 400 = **1600 plants**.
- Sampling only beside the path is unrepresentative and likely **underestimates** the meadow population because density is lower there.
- Use **random coordinates across the whole meadow**, with more well-spread quadrats and the same counting rule. This reduces site-selection bias and better represents the habitat.

## 6 marks

**45.** Plan a valid estimate of a plant population in a meadow and evaluate its reliability.

**Answer:** - Measure habitat area and choose a quadrat of known area.
- Generate random coordinates across the meadow and apply a consistent counting rule.
- Use many quadrats, count the target species and calculate mean abundance.
- Estimate population as mean × habitat area/quadrat area.
- Repeat or increase samples and consider variability, habitat patchiness and identification errors.
- State that representativeness limits the estimate; avoid claiming random sampling guarantees an exact total.

**46.** Evaluate planting hedgerows and field margins around a monoculture farm.

**Answer:** - They provide food, shelter and breeding sites for additional species.
- Habitat connections can support movement, pollinators and natural predators of crop pests.
- This may increase biodiversity and stability.
- They occupy land that could grow crops, and maintenance costs time and money.
- Monitor species richness, crop yield and pest levels before and after, using comparable farms or areas.
- A reasoned judgement balances measured ecological benefits with food production and economic feasibility.

**47.** Evaluate intensive farming that keeps animals warm, restricts movement and provides high-protein feed.

**Answer:** - Warm suitable surroundings reduce energy expenditure on maintaining temperature.
- Less movement reduces respiration linked to activity, and protein supports growth.
- More food can become useful biomass, improving output per animal.
- Crowding or restriction can impair welfare and increase disease transmission; heating/feed have costs and impacts.
- Compare measured growth, feed use, health and living conditions, not production alone.
- A justified decision depends on whether efficiency gains are achieved with acceptable welfare and environmental effects.

**48.** Evaluate quotas and larger mesh sizes for restoring a declining fish stock.

**Answer:** - Quotas reduce the overall catch and can leave more breeding adults.
- Larger suitable meshes allow smaller fish to escape and reach maturity.
- Together they can improve future recruitment, provided suitable habitat and food remain.
- Fishers may lose short-term income; inappropriate mesh or poorly chosen quotas may fail.
- Stock monitoring, compliance and enforcement are essential.
- Use evidence of population recovery and sustainable catches to judge effectiveness rather than assuming any fixed quota works forever.
