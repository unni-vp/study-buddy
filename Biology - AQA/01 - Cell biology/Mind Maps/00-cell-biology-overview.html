Cell biology overview

Cell structure

  • Animal cells: nucleus → DNA; cytoplasm → reactions; membrane → controls entry and exit.
  • Mitochondria → aerobic respiration; ribosomes → protein synthesis.
  • Plant cells: cellulose wall → strength; permanent vacuole → cell sap; photosynthetic cells have chloroplasts → photosynthesis.
  • Bacteria: smaller, prokaryotic; no nucleus. Main DNA loop; some have plasmids. Membrane, cytoplasm and wall.

Specialised cells

  • Structure → effect → function. Apply this link to unfamiliar cells too.
  • Sperm: tail → swimming; mitochondria → energy; acrosome enzymes → penetrate egg's outer layers.
  • Nerve: long fibre and branches → carry impulses and connect cells. Muscle: contractile proteins and mitochondria → contraction.
  • Root hair: long extension → large absorption area; no chloroplasts normally.
  • Xylem: hollow, lignified tubes → water transport and support. Phloem: sieve tubes and companion cells → sugar transport.

Differentiation & stem cells

  • Differentiation: cells become specialised. Most animal cells do this early; many plant cells can throughout life. Adult division mainly repairs and replaces cells.
  • Stem cells: undifferentiated; divide and can differentiate. Embryonic → most human cell types; bone marrow → limited range, including blood cells; meristems → any plant cell type.
  • Potential treatments: diabetes and paralysis. Therapeutic cloning → patient's genes → matched tissue avoids rejection.
  • Weigh benefits against viral infection and ethical or religious objections. Plant clones → conserve rare species and grow useful crops.

Microscopy

  • Magnification = image enlargement. Resolution = distinguish nearby points as separate. Electron microscopes have more of both → finer detail.
  • Magnification = image size ÷ actual size. Use the same units; rearrange for image or actual size.
  • 1 cm = 10 mm; 1 mm = 1,000 µm; 1 µm = 1,000 nm. Use standard form; a tenfold size difference is one order of magnitude.
  • Practical: thin sample, stain, coverslip angled; low power first, then fine focus. Observe, draw and label plant and animal cells; include a magnification scale or scale bar.

Culturing microorganisms

  • Binary fission: bacteria double with nutrients and suitable temperature. Grow in nutrient broth or as colonies on agar.
  • Aseptic technique: sterilise medium, dishes and loop; cool loop. Tape lid at edges; invert plate; incubate at maximum 25°C in school.
  • Practical: compare antibiotic or antiseptic discs; solvent control; keep conditions equal; repeat. Larger zone of inhibition → greater inhibition under these conditions.
  • Zone area = πr²; radius = diameter ÷ 2. Use squared units. Population = starting number × 2ⁿ; n = time ÷ division time. Use standard form when appropriate.

Chromosomes & mitosis

  • Chromosomes are DNA, carry many genes and normally occur in pairs in body cells.
  • 1. Grow and copy: more sub-cellular structures; DNA replicates into two joined identical copies of each chromosome.
  • 2. Mitosis: copies separate; one complete set moves to each end; nucleus divides.
  • 3. Divide: cytoplasm and membrane divide → two genetically identical daughter cells, with the same chromosome number as the parent.
  • For growth, development, repair and replacement. DNA copying happens before mitosis.

Diffusion & exchange

  • Diffusion: net movement of gas or dissolved particles from high to low concentration. No energy from respiration. Examples: oxygen, carbon dioxide and urea.
  • Faster with a steeper gradient, higher temperature and larger membrane area.
  • Large organisms have lower surface area to volume ratio → need exchange surfaces and transport systems. Cube: SA = 6a²; volume = a³.
  • Large area; thin barrier; good blood supply; ventilation → efficient exchange in animals. Alveoli, villi and gills have large areas. Root hairs increase absorption area; thin leaves and air spaces shorten gas diffusion paths.

Osmosis & active transport

  • Osmosis: net water movement from dilute to concentrated solution through a partially permeable membrane; no respiration energy needed.
  • Water gain → plant cell turgid; water loss → flaccid. Animal cells can swell or shrink.
  • Practical: equal-size plant samples in salt/sugar solutions; initial and final masses; blot, control conditions and repeat. % change = (final − initial) ÷ initial × 100. Rate = mass change ÷ time; graph's 0% crossing → no net water movement.
  • Active transport: low → high concentration, against gradient; energy from respiration. Root mineral uptake and sugar absorption from gut into blood.
