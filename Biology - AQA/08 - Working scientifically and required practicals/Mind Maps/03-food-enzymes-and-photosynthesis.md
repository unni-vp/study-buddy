# Food tests, enzymes and photosynthesis

## Food reagents

Benedict’s + heat: reducing sugar precipitate.

Iodine: starch blue-black.

Biuret: protein lilac/purple.

Sudan III: red oil layer. Ethanol test: milky emulsion.

## Food-test safety

Clean separate samples and comparison controls.

Warm Benedict’s in water bath.

Eye protection for reagents.

Ethanol flammable: keep away from flames.

## Amylase method

Buffers change pH. Same enzyme/starch amounts.

Water bath controls temperature.

Sample onto fresh iodine every 30 s.

Orange-brown endpoint: no starch detected.

## Amylase interpretation

Repeat comparable endpoints.

Rate proxy = 1/time.

Extreme pH changes active-site shape.

Shorter sampling intervals improve timing precision.

Do not contaminate main reaction with iodine.

## Pondweed method

Measured light intensity or lamp distance.

Collect oxygen volume per fixed time.

Same plant and carbon dioxide supply.

Equilibrate and control lamp heating.

## Light and limitations

Intensity ∝ 1/distance² in suitable model.

Double distance gives quarter intensity.

Bubbles vary in size: volume is better.

Plateau suggests another limiting factor.
