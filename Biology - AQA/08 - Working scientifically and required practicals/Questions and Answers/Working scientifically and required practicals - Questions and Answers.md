# Working scientifically and required practicals questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** What is the independent variable?

**Answer:** The factor changed or selected by the investigator.

**2.** What is the dependent variable?

**Answer:** The quantity measured.

**3.** What does accuracy mean?

**Answer:** Closeness to the true value.

**4.** What does repeatability mean?

**Answer:** Similar results from repeats by the same investigator under the same conditions.

**5.** Which reagent tests for starch?

**Answer:** Iodine solution.

**6.** What is a positive Biuret result?

**Answer:** Lilac or purple.

**7.** At what temperature are school agar cultures normally incubated?

**Answer:** About 25°C.

**8.** Which buffer-controlled variable is changed in the amylase practical?

**Answer:** pH.

**9.** Name the acidic products causing pH to fall in the lipase milk experiment.

**Answer:** Fatty acids.

**10.** What type of sampling uses quadrats at regular intervals along a line?

**Answer:** Systematic sampling.

**11.** Which is a control variable when testing the effect of pH on amylase activity? Choose one: A the pH chosen; B the time for starch to disappear; C the temperature; D the calculated reciprocal time.

**Answer:** C: the temperature.

## 2 marks

**12.** Why keep temperature constant when investigating amylase activity at different pH values?

**Answer:** - Temperature also affects **enzyme activity**.
- Keeping it constant helps attribute differences to **pH**, rather than temperature.

**13.** Explain why repeating measurements reduces random error but not a systematic zero error.

**Answer:** - Repeats and a mean reduce the effect of **unpredictable scatter**.
- A consistent zero offset affects every reading, so it must be corrected or recalibrated.

**14.** Distinguish precision from accuracy.

**Answer:** - Precision means repeated measurements **cluster closely**.
- Accuracy means closeness to the **true value**; clustered readings can all be biased.

**15.** Why should an unusual result not automatically be discarded?

**Answer:** - It may be a genuine biological difference rather than an error.
- Check the method and repeat; exclude only with a **justified cause**.

**16.** Give two features of a good biological drawing.

**Answer:** - Clear single outlines in proportion, with no invented structures.
- Accurate labels with straight non-crossing lines, plus a suitable scale or magnification.

**17.** Why incubate agar plates inverted and below body temperature?

**Answer:** - Inversion reduces condensation dripping onto and spreading the culture.
- About 25°C reduces the risk of growing harmful human pathogens compared with body temperature.

**18.** Why include a solvent-only disc in an inhibition-zone investigation?

**Answer:** - It checks whether the solvent or disc itself affects growth.
- The comparison helps identify an effect of the **active agent**.

**19.** Why blot plant tissue before final weighing?

**Answer:** - Surface solution would add mass unrelated to osmosis.
- Consistent gentle blotting removes it without squeezing water from the tissue.

**20.** Why use percentage rather than absolute mass change for potato pieces?

**Answer:** - Pieces may have different initial masses.
- Percentage change relates the gain/loss to starting mass, giving a fairer comparison.

**21.** Why sample onto fresh iodine wells rather than adding iodine into the amylase reaction?

**Answer:** - Fresh wells test successive samples without changing the main reaction mixture.
- Adding iodine to the reaction could alter conditions and the result.

**22.** Give one limitation of bubble counting and a specific improvement.

**Answer:** - Bubbles differ in volume, so equal counts need not mean equal oxygen production.
- Collect **oxygen volume per unit time** instead.

**23.** Why allow pondweed to equilibrate after moving the lamp?

**Answer:** - Photosynthesis needs time to respond to the new conditions.
- Measuring after equilibration makes rates more comparable at each setting.

**24.** Why use an unpredictable ruler release in a reaction-time test?

**Answer:** - It reduces **anticipation** of the release.
- The measurement then better reflects response to seeing the fall.

**25.** Why investigate gravity responses in darkness?

**Answer:** - Light can produce a separate directional growth response.
- Darkness reduces this competing influence, helping isolate gravity.

**26.** Why measure an abiotic factor at each transect position?

**Answer:** - It links abundance to the actual conditions experienced at that site.
- Assuming distance alone represents light or moisture could misidentify the cause.

**27.** Why is a milk-decay endpoint comparison valid only with the same starting conditions and endpoint?

**Answer:** - Different starting pH or endpoints require different amounts of fatty acid.
- Time differences could then reflect the amount of change required rather than enzyme activity.

## 3 marks

**28.** Describe a positive reducing-sugar test and its main safety precaution.

**Answer:** - Add **Benedict’s solution** to the sample.
- Warm in a **hot water bath**, looking for a green/yellow/orange/brick-red precipitate.
- Use eye protection and handle hot tubes safely, pointing them away from people.

**29.** Describe a lipid test using ethanol.

**Answer:** - Mix the sample with ethanol so lipid can dissolve.
- Add water; a **milky emulsion** indicates lipid.
- Keep ethanol away from flames because it is flammable.

**30.** A plant-tissue sample increases from 2.00 g to 2.30 g. Calculate percentage change and explain it.

**Answer:** - Change = 2.30 −2.00 = **0.30 g**.
- Percentage = 0.30/2.00 ×100 = **15% increase**.
- Net water entered tissue by **osmosis** under these conditions.

**31.** An inhibition-zone diameter is 12 mm. Calculate its circular area, including the disc.

**Answer:** - Radius = 12/2 = **6 mm**.
- Area = π ×6².
- Area ≈ **113 mm²**, to three significant figures.

**32.** Explain how to determine a reaction-rate proxy from amylase endpoint times.

**Answer:** - Use the time for the **same amount of starch** to disappear.
- Calculate **1/time**, with s⁻¹ if time is seconds.
- A shorter endpoint time gives a larger rate proxy.

**33.** Explain why a lamp-distance experiment can be confounded by heating.

**Answer:** - Moving the lamp can change water temperature as well as light intensity.
- Temperature affects photosynthesis through enzyme activity.
- Use a monitored bath/heat shield and keep temperature comparable.

**34.** Three endpoint times are 50, 54 and 52 s. Calculate mean time, range and reciprocal mean time.

**Answer:** - Mean = (50+54+52)/3 = **52 s**.
- Range = 54 −50 = **4 s**.
- Reciprocal mean = 1/52 ≈ **0.0192 s⁻¹**; keep the rate-comparison method consistent.

**35.** Explain how peer review and replication strengthen a biological claim.

**Answer:** - Peer reviewers assess whether methods, analysis and conclusions are justified.
- Independent replication tests whether the effect can be obtained again.
- Together they increase confidence, while neither guarantees a claim is correct.

## 4 marks

**36.** Describe preparation and observation of an onion-cell slide.

**Answer:** - Place a thin tissue layer on a slide with a suitable stain.
- Lower a coverslip at an angle to limit air bubbles.
- Start at low power, locate and focus, then increase magnification with careful fine focus.
- Draw observed structures clearly, label them and include appropriate scale/magnification.

**37.** Explain how to improve a ruler-drop study that always tests the distracting condition second.

**Answer:** - Practice may make later attempts faster; fatigue may make them slower.
- Balance or randomise condition order across participants.
- Keep ruler position, hand and instructions constant and repeat both conditions.
- Compare mean times and spread using consenting participants and a safe distraction.

**38.** Plan a controlled comparison of seedling response to one-sided light.

**Answer:** - Use several seedlings of the same species, age and similar size.
- Compare one-sided with appropriate all-round light over the same time.
- Keep water, temperature and growing medium consistent.
- Record lengths and accurate labelled drawings of root/shoot directions, then repeat and compare.

**39.** A study finds taller plants where light is brighter. Explain what can and cannot be concluded.

**Answer:** - The data show a correlation in the sampled conditions.
- Light could affect photosynthesis and growth.
- Soil moisture, minerals, species or plant age could also differ.
- A controlled investigation and broader sampling are needed to isolate causes; the correlation alone does not prove light caused every difference.

## 5 marks

**40.** A bacterial inhibition zone has an overall diameter of 16 mm, including a paper disc of diameter 6 mm. Calculate the clear agar area excluding the disc, using π = 3.14. Give two variables to control when comparing two antiseptics and explain why a larger zone alone does not establish the better treatment for a patient.

**Answer:** - Zone radius = 16 ÷ 2 = **8 mm**. Disc radius = 6 ÷ 2 = **3 mm**.
- Clear agar area = **π × 8² − π × 3²** = 3.14 × (64 − 9) = **172.7 mm²**, or **173 mm²** to three significant figures.
- Keep, for example, **disc size and the volume of antiseptic per disc** the same. Also keep bacterial strain, agar and incubation conditions comparable.
- A larger clear zone supports greater growth inhibition **under those plate conditions**.
- Different diffusion through agar affects zone size. A patient treatment also depends on safety, effective dose and the infection, so this result alone cannot establish the best clinical treatment.

## 6 marks

**41.** Plan a valid agar-plate comparison of two antiseptics.

**Answer:** - Inoculate sterile agar consistently using aseptic technique and a suitable harmless culture.
- Apply equal volumes/concentrations to equal-sized spaced discs, adding a solvent-only control.
- Keep agar depth, bacterial starting amount, temperature and incubation time the same.
- Tape lids at a few points, incubate inverted at about 25°C and keep cultures closed afterwards.
- Repeat plates and measure comparable inhibition-zone diameters/areas.
- Compare results and variation, recognising diffusion can affect zone size; arrange approved disposal.

**42.** Plan an investigation of solution concentration and potato mass change.

**Answer:** - Prepare equal-dimension pieces from comparable tissue and measure initial masses.
- Use a suitable range of known concentrations, equal solution volumes and a water comparison.
- Keep time and temperature constant, using several pieces at each concentration.
- Blot consistently and record final masses.
- Calculate percentage changes and means; plot against concentration.
- Estimate the zero-change concentration and evaluate scatter, cutting differences and surface-water errors, using sharp tools safely.

**43.** Plan an investigation of pH and amylase activity.

**Answer:** - Prepare a pH range using buffers with equal starch/enzyme volumes and concentrations.
- Equilibrate solutions in a controlled water bath.
- Mix and start timing, sampling onto fresh iodine drops every 30 seconds.
- Record the first sample with no blue-black colour, keeping endpoint rules consistent.
- Repeat and compare mean times or reciprocal-time rates, with a no-enzyme control.
- Evaluate timing/sampling precision and use appropriate reagent and hot-water precautions.

**44.** Plan an investigation of light intensity and pondweed photosynthesis.

**Answer:** - Place comparable pondweed in a solution supplying consistent carbon dioxide.
- Use several measured lamp distances or measured intensities.
- Equilibrate and collect oxygen volume for a fixed interval at each setting.
- Monitor/control temperature and keep plant length, solution and timing consistent.
- Repeat and compare mean oxygen production rates, using the inverse-square relationship only where suitable.
- Evaluate gas leaks, lamp heating and plateau-causing factors; keep electrics dry and avoid overheating.
