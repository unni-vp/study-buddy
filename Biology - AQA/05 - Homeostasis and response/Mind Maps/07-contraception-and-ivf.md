# Contraception and fertility

## Hormonal contraception

Oral hormones inhibit FSH/egg maturation and ovulation.

Implants, injections and patches provide hormonal delivery.

Compare duration, correct use and side effects.

Hormonal methods do not protect against STIs.

## Barriers and devices

Condom/diaphragm prevents sperm reaching egg. Condoms reduce STI transmission.

Copper IUD interferes with fertilisation and can prevent implantation.

Hormonal intrauterine systems release progestogen.

Method and effectiveness depend on correct use.

## Other methods

Spermicides kill or disable sperm.

Avoid intercourse during fertile period, but ovulation can be hard to predict.

Sterilisation blocks gamete transport and is intended permanent.

Evaluate reversibility, evidence and personal/ethical priorities.

## Fertility treatment

FSH and LH help maturation and ovulation; pregnancy may occur through intercourse.

IVF adds fertilisation outside the body.

Hormones support maturation of several eggs.

Eggs are collected and fertilised with sperm in laboratory.

## Embryo transfer

Fertilised eggs divide to form embryos.

One, or sometimes two, transferred to uterus for possible implantation.

Transfer is not fertilisation.

More transferred embryos can increase multiple-pregnancy risks.

## Benefits and data

Chance of a child, but success uncertain, cost and physical/emotional stress.

**Success %:** successful outcomes/attempts ×100.

Compare pregnancy versus live-birth definitions and similar age groups.

Assess safety alongside chances of success.
