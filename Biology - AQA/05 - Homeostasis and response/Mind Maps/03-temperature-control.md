# Temperature control

## Why regulate

Enzymes and cells need suitable temperature.

Homeostasis adjusts internal conditions despite external changes.

Negative feedback opposes the original deviation.

Optimum is a suitable range, not absolute unchanging temperature.

## Monitoring

Brain thermoregulatory centre detects blood temperature.

Skin temperature receptors send nervous impulses.

Centre coordinates responses of vessels, sweat glands and muscles.

These are mechanisms, not simply “feeling hot”.

## Too hot: blood flow

**Vasodilation:** vessels supplying skin capillaries widen.

More blood flows near surface.

More energy transfers to surroundings.

Vessels change diameter, not depth or position.

## Too hot: sweat

Sweat glands release sweat.

**Evaporation** transfers energy from skin and cools it.

Liquid sweat without evaporation has less cooling effect.

Link the energy transfer to falling temperature.

## Too cold

**Vasoconstriction:** less near-surface blood flow, reducing energy loss.

Sweating reduces or stops.

**Shivering:** rapid muscle contractions use energy from respiration and release heat.

Energy is transferred, not created.

## Feedback and interpretation

As temperature returns towards suitable level, corrective response reduces.

Conserving energy differs from producing heat.

Use the stimulus, coordinator, effector and effect in an explanation.

Read temperature data with time and conditions.
