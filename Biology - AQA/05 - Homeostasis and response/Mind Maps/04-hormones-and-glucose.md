# Hormones and blood glucose

## Endocrine messages

Glands secrete hormones directly into blood. Target cells have suitable receptors.

Generally slower and longer lasting than nervous signals.

Pituitary can stimulate other glands.

It does not make every hormone.

## Gland locations

**Pituitary:** base of brain. **Thyroid:** neck.

**Adrenals:** above kidneys. **Pancreas:** abdomen.

**Ovaries:** pelvis. **Testes:** scrotum.

Hormone source must match its role.

## Insulin response

Pancreas monitors high glucose.

Insulin supports cell uptake and liver/muscle glycogen storage.

## Glucagon and feedback

Low glucose stimulates pancreas to release glucagon.

Liver glycogen breaks down

glucose enters blood.

Levels return towards normal and response reduces.

Glucose is sugar, glycogen storage, glucagon hormone.

## Diabetes comparison

**Type 1:** insufficient insulin

insulin replacement usually needed.

**Type 2:** insulin resistance

diet/exercise and sometimes medicines.

Obesity increases risk, not certainty.

Use glucose and insulin reference data in comparisons.

## Stress and metabolism

**Adrenaline:** fight/flight, faster heart rate and greater glucose/oxygen delivery.

**Thyroxine:** basal metabolic rate, growth and development.

Low thyroxine increases pituitary stimulation

high level reduces it.
