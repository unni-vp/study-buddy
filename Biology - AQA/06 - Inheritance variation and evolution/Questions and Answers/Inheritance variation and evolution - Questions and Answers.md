# Inheritance variation and evolution questions & answers

## 1 mark

**1.** What is a genome?

**Answer:** An organism’s entire genetic material.

**2.** Which division produces gametes?

**Answer:** Meiosis.

**3.** How many chromosomes are usually present in a human gamete?

**Answer:** 23.

**4.** What is an allele?

**Answer:** A version of a gene.

**5.** What does heterozygous mean?

**Answer:** Having two different alleles for a gene.

**6.** What is the phenotype?

**Answer:** The expressed characteristics of an organism.

**7.** Which base pairs with adenine in DNA?

**Answer:** Thymine.

**8.** Name the cell structure where proteins are synthesised.

**Answer:** Ribosome.

**9.** What is a mutation?

**Answer:** A change in DNA.

**10.** What is a clone?

**Answer:** An organism genetically identical to its source organism.

**11.** Which two classification levels form a binomial name?

**Answer:** Genus and species.

**12.** Who developed the three-domain system?

**Answer:** Carl Woese.

**13.** Which statement about a dominant allele is correct? Choose one: A it must be the most common allele; B it must improve survival; C it is expressed in a heterozygote in the simple single-gene model; D it can only be passed on by one sex.

**Answer:** C: it is expressed in a heterozygote in the simple single-gene model.

## 2 marks

**14.** Compare sexual and asexual reproduction.

**Answer:** - Sexual reproduction involves **fusion of gametes** and mixes genetic information.
- Asexual reproduction uses **one parent without gamete fusion**, producing genetically identical offspring apart from mutations.

**15.** Why can asexual reproduction be advantageous in stable conditions?

**Answer:** - There is **no need to find a mate**, so reproduction can be rapid.
- Successful genetic characteristics are preserved, allowing many offspring when conditions remain suitable.

**16.** Give one advantage and one disadvantage of sexual reproduction.

**Answer:** - Genetic **variation** increases the chance some offspring survive environmental change.
- Finding a mate and producing gametes can cost time and energy.

**17.** Describe how strawberries and daffodils reproduce asexually.

**Answer:** - Strawberries produce **runners** that grow into new plants.
- Daffodils produce **bulbs** that develop into new plants.

**18.** How do malaria parasites demonstrate both reproductive methods?

**Answer:** - They reproduce **asexually in the human host**.
- Their **sexual stage occurs in the mosquito**.

**19.** Explain how a gene differs from a chromosome.

**Answer:** - A gene is a **section of DNA coding for an amino-acid sequence** used in a protein.
- A chromosome is a long DNA molecule carrying **many genes**.

**20.** Describe the components of a DNA nucleotide.

**Answer:** - A nucleotide contains a **sugar and phosphate**.
- It also contains **one of four bases**.

**21.** Explain why amino-acid order matters in a protein.

**Answer:** - The order affects how the protein **folds into a particular shape**.
- Shape determines function, for example whether an enzyme’s **active site** fits its substrate.

**22.** Give two ways studying the human genome can be useful.

**Answer:** - Identify genes linked to **inherited disorders**, supporting understanding or treatment.
- Compare genetic information to trace **human migration** and relationships.

**23.** Distinguish genotype from phenotype using a simple allele example.

**Answer:** - **Genotype** is the allele combination, for example Aa.
- **Phenotype** is the expressed characteristic; Aa expresses the dominant characteristic in this simple model.

**24.** Why can unaffected parents have a child with a recessive disorder?

**Answer:** - Both parents can be **heterozygous carriers**.
- The child can inherit the **recessive allele from each parent**, becoming homozygous recessive.

**25.** Give a genetic cause and an environmental cause of variation.

**Answer:** - Genetic: different **alleles inherited from parents**, or a mutation.
- Environmental: different nutrition, training or exposure to sunlight.

**26.** Explain why height is not a simple one-gene characteristic.

**Answer:** - Many **genes** influence height.
- **Environmental factors**, such as childhood nutrition, also affect the phenotype.

**27.** Explain one risk of inbreeding during selective breeding.

**Answer:** - Close relatives are more likely to share harmful **recessive alleles**.
- Offspring are more likely to inherit two copies and express the disorder.

**28.** Why are antibiotics ineffective against viruses?

**Answer:** - Antibiotics act on bacterial structures or processes.
- Viruses reproduce inside host cells and do not have the same antibiotic targets.

**29.** Give two reasons the fossil record is incomplete.

**Answer:** - Many early organisms were **soft-bodied**, leaving few preserved remains.
- Geological activity destroyed or altered many remains, and many fossils have not been found.

**30.** Give two possible causes of extinction.

**Answer:** - Rapid environmental change or a new disease can prevent survival/reproduction.
- New predators or competitors can reduce food or survival until no individuals remain.

**31.** In a tree, A and B share a more recent ancestor than A and C. Explain which are more closely related.

**Answer:** - **A and B** are more closely related.
- They share the **more recent common ancestor**, not merely neighbouring names on the diagram.

## 3 marks

**32.** Explain how meiosis and fertilisation maintain chromosome number across generations.

**Answer:** - Meiosis **halves the chromosome number**, producing gametes with 23 chromosomes in humans.
- Fertilisation joins two gamete nuclei.
- The fertilised egg has **46 chromosomes**, restoring the usual body-cell number.

**33.** Describe chromosome changes during meiosis without naming its phases.

**Answer:** - DNA is **copied once**, giving joined copies of each chromosome.
- The cell **divides twice**, producing four gametes with half the original chromosome number.
- The gametes are **genetically different** because information from the two parents is reshuffled.

**34.** Explain how a coding mutation can change enzyme activity.

**Answer:** - A changed base sequence can change the **amino-acid sequence**.
- This can change folding and **active-site shape**.
- Substrate may bind less effectively, changing the reaction rate; not every mutation has this effect.

**35.** Explain how a non-coding DNA variant can affect a phenotype.

**Answer:** - Some non-coding DNA helps control **gene expression**.
- A variant can alter how much or when a gene is expressed.
- A changed amount of a protein can alter a characteristic without changing its amino-acid sequence.

**36.** Two cystic-fibrosis carriers have genotypes Cc. Determine the probability their child is affected.

**Answer:** - Each parent produces gametes **C or c**.
- The combinations are **CC, Cc, Cc, cc**.
- Only cc is affected: **1/4 = 25%** for each pregnancy.

**37.** A heterozygous parent has polydactyly, Pp. The other parent is pp. Determine the chance of an affected child.

**Answer:** - Parent gametes are **P or p**, and **p only**.
- Offspring combinations are **Pp, Pp, pp, pp**.
- P is dominant, so **2/4 = 50%** are predicted to be affected.

**38.** Use the usual XX/XY model to explain equal probabilities of female and male offspring.

**Answer:** - Egg gametes carry **X**; sperm carry **X or Y**.
- Fusion produces **XX or XY** with equal chances in the model.
- Each outcome has probability **1/2**, not a guarantee of equal numbers in a family.

**39.** In 200 children from carrier-by-carrier crosses, calculate the expected number of affected children and explain why observed numbers might differ.

**Answer:** - Recessive affected probability = **1/4**.
- Expected number = 200 ×1/4 = **50**.
- Inheritance is **random**, so observed counts can differ from the expectation.

**40.** Describe three different ways fossils can form.

**Answer:** - Hard parts can be **replaced by minerals**.
- Remains can persist where **decay is prevented**, such as amber or ice.
- Traces such as footprints or burrows can be preserved.

**41.** Why was Darwin’s theory initially difficult for some people to accept?

**Answer:** - It challenged the belief that species were **created unchanged**.
- There was **insufficient evidence** available at first.
- The mechanism of **inheritance and variation** was not yet understood.

**42.** Explain why Mendel’s work was not immediately recognised.

**Answer:** - His pea-plant results suggested inherited **units**, before genes were known.
- Chromosomes and their role in inheritance were not yet understood.
- Later discoveries provided a mechanism and evidence supporting his explanation.

**43.** State the three domains and give the distinguishing cell-type relationship.

**Answer:** - **Archaea:** a distinct prokaryotic domain, including many organisms from extreme environments.
- **Bacteria:** the bacterial prokaryotic domain.
- **Eukaryota:** organisms with eukaryotic cells, including animals, plants, fungi and protists.

## 4 marks

**44.** Describe the main steps linking a DNA base sequence to a functional protein.

**Answer:** - A base sequence provides the **code for amino-acid order**; three bases code for an amino acid.
- A template reaches a **ribosome**.
- Carrier molecules bring amino acids, which join in the specified order.
- The chain **folds into a particular shape**, enabling its function.

**45.** Explain how natural selection can make a camouflaged insect colour more common.

**Answer:** - A population contains **inherited variation** in colour.
- Better-camouflaged insects are less likely to be eaten.
- They are more likely to **survive and reproduce**.
- Their offspring inherit the alleles, so these alleles become **more common over generations**.

**46.** Explain how isolation can lead to a new species.

**Answer:** - Populations become **isolated**, preventing breeding between them.
- Different conditions and mutations produce different variation and selection pressures.
- Over many generations different alleles accumulate.
- Eventually the populations cannot interbreed to produce **fertile offspring**, so they are different species.

**47.** Describe selective breeding to produce wheat with high yield and disease resistance.

**Answer:** - Choose parents showing the **desired characteristics**.
- Breed them together.
- Select offspring with the best combination and breed those offspring.
- Repeat selection and breeding over **many generations**, rather than simply reusing the original parents.

**48.** Describe how bacteria can be genetically engineered to produce human insulin.

**Answer:** - Use enzymes to **isolate the human insulin gene**.
- Cut a **plasmid vector** and insert the gene.
- Transfer the modified plasmid into bacterial cells.
- Grow the bacteria so they express the gene and produce insulin, which is collected and purified.

**49.** Describe adult-cell cloning and identify the source of the clone’s nuclear DNA.

**Answer:** - Remove the nucleus from an **unfertilised egg**.
- Insert an **adult body-cell nucleus**.
- Use an electric stimulus to start division, then transfer the embryo to a host uterus.
- The clone’s **nuclear DNA comes from the adult nucleus donor**.

**50.** Compare plant cuttings, tissue culture and embryo splitting.

**Answer:** - A cutting grows into a plant from a portion of a parent plant.
- Tissue culture uses **small groups of cells**, nutrients and hormones to grow many plants.
- Embryo splitting separates cells from an early animal embryo for transfer into host mothers.
- Each makes genetically identical offspring from its source, but uses a different starting material.

**51.** Explain how antibiotic-resistant bacteria become more common during treatment.

**Answer:** - A **random mutation** can make some bacteria resistant.
- Antibiotic kills susceptible bacteria while resistant ones survive.
- Survivors reproduce rapidly and pass on resistance.
- Resistant bacteria form a **larger proportion** of the population; antibiotic does not deliberately create a useful mutation.

**52.** Describe how evidence helped classification develop beyond physical resemblance alone.

**Answer:** - Linnaeus grouped organisms by observable characteristics.
- Improved microscopes revealed more detail about cell structures.
- Biochemical and genetic evidence revealed deeper relationships.
- Woese’s **three-domain system** separates Archaea, Bacteria and Eukaryota using such evidence.

## 5 marks

**53.** A recessive disorder is caused by allele a. Two unaffected parents have an affected child. Deduce both parents' genotypes, using A for the usual allele. Draw a Punnett square and determine the probability that their next child is affected. Explain whether the first child's condition changes this probability.

**Answer:** Both parents must be **Aa**. The affected child is **aa**, so each parent supplied a. Because the parents are unaffected, each must also have A.

Each parent produces gametes carrying **A or a**.

| Egg / sperm | A | a |
|---|---|---|
| A | AA | Aa |
| a | Aa | aa |

One of the four equally likely combinations is **aa**, giving an affected-child probability of **1/4 = 25%**. The previous child does not change this probability: each conception is an independent event in this model.

## 6 marks

**54.** Evaluate using embryo screening when both parents carry a serious recessive disorder.

**Answer:** - A carrier cross can give a **25% affected probability** for each child, so screening may help identify embryos at risk.
- It can reduce the chance of a serious inherited condition and inform parental decisions.
- Testing and IVF can be costly, stressful and uncertain, and screening does not guarantee a child free from every health problem.
- Some object to selecting or discarding embryos, while others prioritise preventing suffering.
- Consider the condition’s severity, reliability of the test and informed choice. A justified judgement balances these issues rather than claiming screening is always right or wrong.

**55.** Evaluate replacing a local crop with an insect-resistant genetically modified crop.

**Answer:** - Resistance may reduce crop losses and raise yield, improving food supply and farmer income.
- Reduced insecticide use could lower chemical exposure and environmental damage.
- Possible effects on non-target organisms and transfer of genes to wild relatives must be assessed.
- Cost, seed access and the development of resistant pests affect long-term usefulness.
- Compare evidence from suitable field trials, including yield and ecological effects, with the local alternative.
- A reasoned judgement depends on measured benefits and risks in that setting, not on assuming all GM crops are identical.

**56.** Explain how fossils and genetic evidence support evolution, and why gaps do not make the evidence useless.

**Answer:** - Fossils show organisms lived in the past and that forms changed or became extinct over time.
- Their age and features help reconstruct relationships and sequences of change.
- The record is incomplete because preservation is rare and geological processes destroy remains.
- Genetic comparisons reveal similarities consistent with **shared ancestors**.
- Inherited variation, selection and reproductive isolation explain how populations and species can change.
- Independent evidence supports the explanation even while individual relationships are revised as new fossils or genetic data appear.

**57.** Evaluate cloning a small population of an endangered animal.

**Answer:** - Cloning can increase numbers and preserve valuable genetic material.
- Adult-cell cloning can copy an existing individual’s nuclear genome.
- It does not introduce much genetic variation, so disease or environmental change may threaten many clones similarly.
- Low success, animal welfare, host availability and cost limit its use.
- Increasing numbers will not solve habitat loss or lack of suitable food.
- It may support a conservation programme, but habitat protection and breeding that preserves diversity remain essential.
