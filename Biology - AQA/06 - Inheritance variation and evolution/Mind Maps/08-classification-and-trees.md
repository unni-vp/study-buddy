# Classification and evolutionary relationships

## Linnaean hierarchy

Kingdom, phylum, class, order.

Family, genus, species.

Groups become narrower down hierarchy.

Use characteristics to identify relationships.

## Binomial names

Genus plus species identifies an organism.

Genus capitalised, species lower case.

Example: Homo sapiens.

Scientific names avoid common-name ambiguity.

## New evidence

Microscopes reveal cell differences.

Biochemical/genetic evidence compares organisms.

Classification changes when evidence improves.

Similar appearance alone can mislead.

## Three domains

Archaea: distinct prokaryotes, many extreme-habitat examples.

Bacteria: bacterial prokaryotes.

Eukaryota: protists, fungi, plants, animals.

Domains are not simply three kingdoms.

## Read branching joins

Joins represent shared ancestors.

## Interpret carefully

More recent shared ancestor: closer relationship.

Do not judge by tip spacing alone.

Modern neighbouring species need not be ancestors.

Trees use living-organism and fossil evidence.
