# Selective breeding and genetic engineering

## Selective breeding

Choose parents with desired features.

Breed, select best offspring, breed again.

Repeat over many generations.

Humans select, rather than environmental survival alone.

## Useful outcomes

Disease-resistant crops, higher yields.

Animals producing more meat or milk.

Dogs with gentle behaviour.

Flowers with desirable colours or size.

## Inbreeding risk

Reduced diversity from related parents.

Harmful recessive alleles more likely paired.

Population may share disease vulnerability.

Compare benefit with welfare and diversity.

## Gene transfer

Enzymes isolate gene and cut vector.

## Applications

Bacteria express human insulin gene.

Crops resist pests, disease or herbicides.

Modified animals may yield useful substances.

Gene therapy seeks to address inherited disorders.

## Evaluate evidence

Possible yield and reduced pesticide benefits.

Non-target species and gene transfer concerns.

Consider cost, access and long-term resistance.

Use trials and local alternatives, not blanket claims.
