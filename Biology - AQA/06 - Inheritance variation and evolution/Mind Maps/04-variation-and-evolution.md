# Variation, selection and speciation

## Sources of variation

Genes inherited from parents.

Mutations create new variants.

Environment: diet, light, training.

Some traits, such as height, involve both.

## Random changes

Mutations occur continuously.

Most have little or no effect.

Occasionally a variant gives an advantage.

Organisms do not mutate because they need to.

## Natural selection

Population has inherited variation.

Competition/predators/environment impose selection.

Better-suited individuals survive and reproduce.

Offspring inherit advantageous alleles.

## Evolution over time

Advantageous alleles become more common.

Populations change over generations.

Individuals do not evolve during their lifetime.

Survival without reproduction is incomplete reasoning.

## Reproductive isolation

Barrier separates populations.

Different pressures favour different alleles.

Differences accumulate over generations.

Cannot interbreed to produce fertile offspring: new species.

## Antibiotic resistance

Mutation makes some bacteria resistant.

Antibiotic kills susceptible cells.

Survivors reproduce and pass on resistance.

Avoid unnecessary prescribing and follow treatment instructions.
