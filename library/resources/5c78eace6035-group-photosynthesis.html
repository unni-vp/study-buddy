Photosynthesis & limiting factors

Photosynthesis

Photosynthesis lets a plant use light to make glucose from simple substances. Think of a leaf as a solar-powered food factory: light supplies energy, while carbon dioxide and water supply the raw materials.

Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen

6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂

Light energy: chlorophyll in chloroplasts absorbs light. Photosynthesis is endothermic because energy transfers from the environment into the chloroplasts. Light supplies energy; it is not a material reactant.

Raw materials: carbon dioxide enters through stomata, while water travels from the roots through xylem. Photosynthesis makes glucose, which stores chemical energy, and releases oxygen.

Two processes can happen together: plants respire all the time, including during daylight. They photosynthesise only when there is enough light.

Where the glucose goes

UseWhy the plant needs it
RespirationTransfers energy for living processes.
StarchInsoluble storage carbohydrate. It does not dissolve and draw water into cells in the way a concentrated sugar solution would.
Fats and oilsEnergy stores, particularly in seeds.
CelluloseStrengthens cell walls.
Amino acids → proteinsBuilds enzymes and other cell materials. The plant also needs nitrate ions from the soil.

Glucose is both a fuel and a building material. A plant needs minerals as well as light, carbon dioxide and water to grow normally.

Where new plant material comes from

Much of the carbon in a plant's new organic material comes from carbon dioxide in the air. Water supplies hydrogen, while mineral ions supply elements needed in smaller amounts. Soil is not the main source of the carbon making up a growing tree.

Light supplies energy, not atoms for glucose. Chlorophyll absorbs light and helps transfer that energy into chemical processes; it does not turn sunlight into matter. Plants also need to respire to make energy available for their cells.
