Coral reefs and Andros

Conditions and distribution

  • Warm-water reefs: generally clear, sunlit tropical seas. Light supports photosynthetic partners.
  • Major examples include Andros, Florida, Mesoamerica, Red Sea, New Caledonia and Great Barrier reefs.

The living partnership

  • Coral animals: shelter algae and supply nutrients. Algae: photosynthesise and pass food to coral.
  • Nutrients circulate through organisms, waste and decomposition. Corals also capture food.

Andros: linked habitats

  • The Bahamas: the barrier reef, seagrass and mangroves provide feeding and nursery habitats.
  • Fish populations, coastal protection, fishing and tourism connect ecology to livelihoods.

Threats reinforce one another

  • Heat stress: bleaching may recover, but prolonged stress can kill coral.
  • Pollution, sediment, anchors, fishing pressure and habitat loss weaken the ecosystem.

Named protected areas

  • North and South Marine Parks: reef-related protection. West Side: mangrove creeks and tidal flats.
  • Crab Replenishment Reserve: land-crab habitat and stocks. These protect different parts of Andros.

Evaluate the management

  • Rules and monitoring: need enforcement, cooperation and protection beyond boundaries.
  • Local action improves resilience. It cannot stop global warming on its own.
