QuestionExplain and compare

Describe the main energy changes when a ball rises after being thrown, including air resistance.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Its kinetic store decreases while its gravitational potential store increases.
  • Air resistance transfers some energy to thermal stores of the ball and surroundings.

QuestionExplain and compare

Why is energy described as dissipated rather than destroyed when a moving car brakes?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Energy transfers from the car’s kinetic store to thermal stores.
  • It spreads into the surroundings and becomes less useful, but total energy is conserved.

QuestionExplain and compare

Explain why a thicker wall of the same material usually reduces cooling, for the same area and temperature difference.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • It increases the distance over which energy must transfer by conduction.
  • The rate of energy transfer decreases, so the building cools more slowly.

QuestionApply and calculate

A 0.80 kg sample warms from 18°C to 43°C. Its specific heat capacity is 900 J/kg°C. Calculate the thermal energy increase.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Δθ = 43 − 18 = 25°C.
  • ΔE = mcΔθ = 0.80 × 900 × 25.
  • 18,000 J.
QuestionApply and calculate

Explain why lubricating moving machine parts can increase efficiency.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Lubrication reduces friction.
  • Less input energy transfers to unwanted thermal stores.
  • A greater fraction of input becomes the useful output.
QuestionApply and calculate

A heater transfers 9,000 J to a 0.50 kg block. Its temperature rises by 20°C. Calculate the specific heat capacity, assuming all energy heats the block.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • c = E/(mΔθ).
  • c = 9,000/(0.50 × 20).
  • 900 J/kg°C.
QuestionApply and calculate

A metal-block heating experiment loses energy to the room. Explain the effect on the calculated specific heat capacity if all electrical input is treated as energy gained by the block.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The measured temperature rise is smaller than if all input heated the block.
  • The calculation uses c = E/(mΔθ) with an input E that includes losses.
  • The calculated c is therefore too high. Insulation reduces this error.
QuestionMulti-step application

A motor of efficiency 0.70 lifts a 20 kg load through 3.0 m in 5.0 s. Use g = 10 N/kg. Calculate the useful power and the total electrical input power.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Useful energy = mgΔh = 20 × 10 × 3.0 = 600 J.
  • Useful power = E/t = 600/5.0 = 120 W.
  • Efficiency = useful power / input power.
  • Input power = 120/0.70 = 171 W (about 170 W).
QuestionMulti-step application

Two equal-mass samples receive equal energy from identical heaters. A warms by 12°C and B by 30°C. Which has the larger specific heat capacity, and by what factor?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • c = E/(mΔθ).
  • With equal E and m, c is inversely proportional to temperature rise.
  • A has the larger c because it warms less.
  • cA/cB = 30/12 = 2.5.
QuestionMulti-step application

A 2.0 kg object falls 5.0 m from rest. Air resistance transfers 18 J to thermal stores. Use g = 9.8 N/kg. Calculate its final speed.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Gravitational energy lost = 2.0 × 9.8 × 5.0 = 98 J.
  • Kinetic energy gained = 98 − 18 = 80 J.
  • v = √(2Eₖ/m) = √(160/2).
  • v = 8.94 m/s.
