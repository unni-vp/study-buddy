QuestionMulti-step application

Analyse the simile solitary as an oyster.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The simile “solitary as an oyster” makes Scrooge's isolation seem like a hard protective shell. It suggests that he resists emotional contact, not simply that he happens to live alone. The potential pearl inside can also support an interpretation of hidden value later revealed by his transformation. The image therefore allows both criticism of his closure and hope for change.
QuestionMulti-step application

Analyse Belle's statement that another idol has displaced her.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The religious metaphor “Another idol has displaced me” presents wealth as an object of worship. The verb suggests that Scrooge's devotion to money has pushed love out of its previous place. Dickens makes the change about his deepest priorities, not just his bank balance. Belle's departure turns that abstract choice into a visible personal loss.
QuestionMulti-step application

Analyse how Ignorance is presented.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The boy is an allegorical figure, embodying lack of education and understanding. His frail appearance turns a social problem into a vulnerable child whom Scrooge must face. The spirit's warning enlarges the harm beyond one person: neglect threatens the wider community. Dickens therefore makes education a shared duty rather than an optional act of kindness.
QuestionMulti-step application

Analyse the image of a crutch without an owner.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The phrase “a crutch without an owner” represents a child's possible death through an ordinary object left behind. Its symbolic absence invites the reader to imagine the missing person and the family's grief. It also challenges Scrooge's earlier impersonal view of the poor, because one valued child's loss now feels specific and painful.
QuestionMulti-step application

Analyse the contrast between coldness and warmth.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Scrooge's cold office and chilly description connect material restriction with emotional hostility. Shared fires, meals and gatherings offer warmth alongside affection. This recurring contrast makes generosity tangible, while showing that wealth alone does not create belonging. In the ending, Scrooge's changed participation reverses the earlier pattern of shutting others out.
QuestionMulti-step application

Analyse the simile as light as a feather near the end.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • “as light as a feather” suggests release from the emotional weight of Scrooge's former life. The simile contrasts with Marley's heavy chain, linking joy with freedom from selfish priorities. Its place after the frightening future vision also marks a tonal shift towards possibility. The subsequent actions confirm that this feeling leads to practical change.
