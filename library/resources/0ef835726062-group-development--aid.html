Benefits & limitations of aid

Aid is assistance intended to meet needs or support development. Different labels describe different features of the same project.

TypeMeaning and exampleBenefit and possible limitation
Emergency aidFood, shelter or medical support following a crisis.It can save lives quickly, but does not by itself remove the causes of vulnerability.
Development aidLonger-term support, such as training, water systems or livelihood projects.It can strengthen future independence, but needs maintenance, local skills and continuing access.
Bilateral aidOne government provides assistance to another country.It can fund large programmes, but donor political or commercial interests may influence priorities.
Multilateral aidCountries contribute through an international organisation.It pools expertise and funding, although coordination and administration can be complex.
NGO supportA non-governmental organisation works with communities or local partners.Local participation can make projects appropriate, but funding and geographical reach may be limited.

Top-down projects are mainly planned by large organisations or authorities. They can provide major infrastructure but may overlook local needs. Bottom-up projects involve communities in identifying needs and making decisions. They can fit local conditions, but may be too small to provide national networks.

A grant does not need repayment; a loan does. Tied aid imposes conditions, such as buying goods from the donor. These are separate distinctions from emergency versus development aid. Assess who benefits, whether skills are transferred, and whether benefits continue after outside funding ends.
