Develop & organise your talk

Develop points rather than list them

Build each main point through claim, reason, example and significance. The example makes the idea concrete; the final explanation shows why it matters to this audience.

Claim: a quiet space could give pupils a choice. Reason: the same crowded setting does not suit everyone every day. Example: someone preparing for an afternoon presentation may want ten calm minutes. Significance: offering an option supports participation without demanding that everyone spend lunchtime in silence.

This is an illustrative situation, not a statistic about all pupils. If you use research, check its source, date, sample and relevance. Say *a survey of our class* rather than *students everywhere* when that is the limit of the evidence. Never invent a percentage to sound convincing.

Include a meaningful counterargument. Rooms and supervision cost time. Answer the actual difficulty with a workable trial, not with the claim that anyone who disagrees is selfish. A qualified answer can show a more developed understanding than absolute certainty.

Organise a talk the audience can follow

A useful route is focus → reasons and examples → challenge → response → conclusion. Adapt it to the purpose instead of forcing every talk into the same mould.

  1. Opening: establish a concrete situation and the central question.
  2. Development: explore a few connected points, with enough explanation to make each worthwhile.
  3. Qualification: acknowledge a real limitation or alternative view.
  4. Conclusion: return to the purpose and give a clear final thought or action.

Signpost a change with natural phrases: *The practical problem is…*, *That brings me to…*, or *The strongest objection is…*. A listener needs these connections more than a written reader does.

An ending should complete the argument, not introduce a new major issue. For the quiet-space proposal: “We do not need one perfect lunchtime. We need room for different ones.” The contrast returns to choice and makes the recommendation memorable.
