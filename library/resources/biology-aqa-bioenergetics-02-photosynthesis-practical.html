Photosynthesis practical

Choose the variables

  • Independent: lamp distance/light intensity. Dependent: oxygen production rate.
  • Pondweed in sodium hydrogencarbonate solution; keep CO₂ supply, volume, plant and orientation constant.

Control temperature

  • Use a water bath/heat shield and check with a thermometer. The lamp can heat the water.
  • Keep background light steady. Use the same measurement period; avoid spills near electrics and hot-lamp burns.

Collect the data

  • Measure source-to-plant distance; allow the plant to acclimatise.
  • Collect oxygen volume over a set time using an inverted water-filled tube, or count bubbles.
  • Repeat at several distances; repeat each condition and calculate a mean. Investigate anomalies.

Calculate rate

  • Rate = gas volume/time. 3.6 cm³ in 4 min = 0.90 cm³/min.
  • Bubble estimate: 48 in 2 min = 24 bubbles/min.
  • Keep time/volume units consistent. More gas in a longer time is not necessarily a faster rate.

Plot & explain

  • Plot rate on y-axis; intensity or 1/distance² on x-axis. Label units and use a suitable scale/best-fit curve.
  • Closer lamp → greater intensity; rate rises while light limits it. Another limiting factor can cause a plateau.

Limits & improvements

  • Unequal bubble sizes → count is approximate; measure gas volume instead.
  • Some oxygen dissolves or is used in respiration; check collector for gas leaks.
  • Repeats reduce random uncertainty; they do not fix heating, leaks or another uncontrolled variable.
