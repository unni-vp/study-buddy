Atomic structure overview

Elements, compounds, mixtures

  • Element: one proton number. Compound: elements chemically joined in fixed proportions. Mixture: not chemically joined.
  • Balance equations with coefficients, never by changing formulae.

Separate physically

  • Insoluble solid → filtration. Dissolved solid → crystallisation. Solvent → simple distillation.
  • Miscible liquids → fractional distillation. Dyes → chromatography; pencil baseline above solvent.

Particles & isotopes

  • Proton +1, neutron 0, electron −1. Atomic number = protons; mass number = protons + neutrons.
  • Neutral atoms: protons = electrons. Ions gain/lose electrons. Isotopes differ in neutrons.
  • Aᵣ = sum(mass × % abundance)/100.

Electrons & models

  • First 20: shells fill 2,8,8,2. Same main group → same outer electrons; period → occupied shells.
  • Scattering evidence → tiny positive nucleus, mostly empty space. Bohr: shells; Chadwick: neutrons.

Group patterns

  • Group 0: full outer shell, unreactive; boiling point rises down group.
  • Group 1: loses 1 electron; reactivity rises down group. Group 7: gains 1; reactivity falls.
  • More reactive halogen displaces less reactive halide.

Table & transition metals

  • Mendeleev left gaps and predicted properties; modern order is proton number.
  • Transition metals: generally denser, stronger, harder, higher melting and less reactive than Group 1.
  • Many form coloured compounds, variable-charge ions and catalysts.
