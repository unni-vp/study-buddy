Inheritance and probability

Inherited conditions and family trees

Polydactyly means extra fingers or toes. In the simple inheritance model it is caused by a dominant allele.

With P as the dominant allele:

  • PP: expresses the condition.
  • Pp: expresses the condition.
  • pp: does not express the condition.

For a Pp × pp cross:

  • 50% Pp.
  • 50% pp.

Cystic fibrosis is a disorder affecting cell membranes caused by a recessive allele. Using C for the usual allele and c for the disease allele:

  • CC: does not have the condition and does not carry c.
  • Cc: a carrier, usually without the condition.
  • cc: has the condition.
  • Two carriers, Cc × Cc, have a 25% probability of an affected child and a 50% probability of a carrier per conception.

For a pedigree, read its key and generation links. In a simple recessive model, two unaffected parents can have an affected child if both are carriers. “The condition skipped a generation” alone is not enough: identify the actual parent–child evidence. Work out possible genotypes before assuming every unaffected person is homozygous.

Embryo screening can identify particular alleles and help select an embryo without a specific inherited disorder.

Potential benefits include reduced suffering and a lower treatment burden.

Issues to evaluate include cost, access, decisions about embryos, test accuracy and which traits are selected. Use the information supplied. A test for one disorder does not check every possible future illness.

Reasoning from a pedigree

In a simple recessive model, two unaffected parents with an affected child must both carry the recessive allele. By contrast, an unaffected person need not be a carrier merely because a relative is affected: work through the possible parental combinations.

For a dominant condition, two affected heterozygous parents can have an unaffected child. A dominant phenotype therefore does not identify whether a person is homozygous or heterozygous. Use family evidence or a specified cross to decide.

If a question asks about two independent conceptions, multiply their probabilities. For two carrier parents, the probability of two affected children in succession is 1/4 × 1/4 = 1/16. This does not change the probability for the next individual conception.
