Magnets and fields

Poles and materials

Like: repel. Unlike: attract. Strongest near poles.

Iron, steel, cobalt and nickel are magnetic. Not every metal is.

Unmagnetised magnetic material is attracted by induction.

Field direction

Outside: north to south. A north-seeking compass end gives direction.

Closer field lines mean stronger field. Lines do not cross.

Permanent and induced

Permanent: retains magnetism.

Induced: becomes magnetic in a field and usually loses it afterwards.

Attraction alone does not prove permanent magnetism.

Earth’s field affects a compass far from other magnets.

Plot the pattern

Trace magnet on paper. Use compass, mark needle ends, move and repeat.

Join marks with curves and add direction arrows. Start at several points.

Iron filings show pattern, not direction.

Keep other magnetic objects away.

Straight wire

Current gives circular field lines around wire.

Right-hand grip: thumb follows conventional current, fingers field.

More current strengthens field. Greater distance weakens it.

Reverse current, reverse field.

Solenoid and control

Inside: strong, approximately uniform field. Outside: bar-magnet pattern.

More turns per length, more current or iron core strengthen field.

Electromagnet can switch off.

Relay: coil attracts armature to close a separate contact.
