Particle model of matter questions & answers

1 mark2 marks3 marks4 marks6 marks

1 mark

1. Write the equation linking density, mass and volume.

Answer:

ρ = m/V.

2. Name the change from solid directly to gas.

Answer:

Sublimation.

3. What is the unit of specific latent heat?

Answer:

J/kg.

4. What happens to the temperature of a pure substance while it melts at constant pressure?

Answer:

It stays constant.

2 marks

5. Compare particle movement in a solid and a gas.

Answer:
  • In a solid particles vibrate about fixed positions.
  • In a gas particles move rapidly and randomly in all directions.

6. Define internal energy.

Answer:

The total kinetic energy and potential energy of all the particles in a system.

7. A block has mass 540 g and volume 200 cm³. Calculate its density in g/cm³.

Answer:

ρ = m/V = 540/200 = 2.7 g/cm³.

8. Why does a gas usually have much lower density than a liquid of the same substance?

Answer:
  • Gas particles are much farther apart.
  • The same mass occupies a much larger volume, so mass per unit volume is lower.

9. An empty cylinder has mass 60 g. With 80 cm³ of liquid it has mass 124 g. Calculate the liquid’s density.

Answer:

Liquid mass = 124 − 60 = 64 g. Density = 64/80 = 0.80 g/cm³.

10. Explain why mass can decrease in an open beaker when water evaporates without violating conservation of mass.

Answer:
  • Water particles escape into the surroundings as vapour.
  • Total mass including the escaped vapour is conserved; particles are not destroyed.

11. Why must the starting water volume be subtracted when measuring an irregular object’s volume?

Answer:
  • The final reading includes both the original water and the displaced volume.
  • Final minus initial volume gives the volume of the submerged object alone.

3 marks

12. A 0.25 kg sample has specific heat capacity 800 J/kg°C. Calculate the energy needed to warm it from 20°C to 50°C.

Answer:
  • Δθ = 50 − 20 = 30°C.
  • ΔE = mcΔθ = 0.25 × 800 × 30.
  • 6,000 J.

13. Calculate the energy required to melt 0.15 kg of ice at 0°C. Its specific latent heat of fusion is 334,000 J/kg.

Answer:
  • E = mL.
  • E = 0.15 × 334,000.
  • 50,100 J.

14. A sealed gas occupies 40 cm³ at 120 kPa. It expands to 60 cm³ at constant temperature. Calculate its new pressure.

Answer:
  • p₁V₁ = p₂V₂.
  • p₂ = 120 × 40/60.
  • 80 kPa.

15. Explain why heating a fixed mass of gas in a rigid sealed container increases its pressure.

Answer:
  • Average kinetic energy increases, so particles move faster.
  • Wall collisions occur more frequently and involve larger momentum changes.
  • The force per unit area increases, so pressure rises.

16. A bicycle pump becomes warm during rapid compression. Explain why.

Answer:
  • A force moves the piston, doing work on the gas.
  • Energy transfers into the gas’s internal energy.
  • With little time for energy to escape, average particle kinetic energy and temperature increase.

17. Explain why internal energy increases while ice melts even though its temperature is constant.

Answer:
  • Energy is transferred into the ice by heating.
  • It increases particle potential energy as the arrangement and interactions change.
  • Average kinetic energy does not increase, so temperature stays constant.

4 marks

18. A cuboid measures 4.0 cm × 3.0 cm × 2.0 cm and has mass 192 g. Calculate its density in g/cm³ and kg/m³.

Answer:
  • V = 4.0 × 3.0 × 2.0 = 24 cm³.
  • ρ = 192/24 = 8.0 g/cm³.
  • 1 g/cm³ = 1,000 kg/m³.
  • Density = 8,000 kg/m³.

19. A gas is compressed slowly at constant temperature from 100 cm³ to 25 cm³. Its initial pressure is 90 kPa. Calculate the new pressure and explain the increase using particles.

Answer:
  • p₂ = p₁V₁/V₂ = 90 × 100/25 = 360 kPa.
  • Average particle kinetic energy stays the same because temperature is constant.
  • Particles collide with the walls more frequently per unit area in the smaller volume.
  • Greater force per unit area means greater pressure.

20. A 200 W heater melts 0.20 kg of ice at 0°C and warms the water to 10°C. Use L = 334,000 J/kg and c = 4,200 J/kg°C. Calculate the minimum heating time.

Answer:
  • Melting energy = mL = 0.20 × 334,000 = 66,800 J.
  • Warming energy = mcΔθ = 0.20 × 4,200 × 10 = 8,400 J.
  • Total energy = 75,200 J.
  • t = E/P = 75,200/200 = 376 s; real heat losses increase the time.

6 marks

21. Describe a method to determine the density of an irregular stone which does not dissolve in water. Include ways to obtain reliable measurements.

Answer:
  • Zero a balance and measure the dry stone’s mass.
  • Record the initial water volume in a measuring cylinder large enough for the stone.
  • Lower the stone gently until fully submerged, avoiding splashes and trapped air bubbles. Read the final volume at eye level.
  • Calculate stone volume from final minus initial water volume. A displacement can with collected water is an alternative.
  • Calculate ρ = m/V with consistent units.
  • Repeat and compare values; use suitably fine instrument scales and investigate anomalies. Repeats reduce random variation but do not correct a systematic zero error.

22. Explain how the energy of particles changes as a pure solid is heated, melts and then warms as a liquid. Refer to this heating curve.

Answer:
  • Initially the solid’s particles vibrate more rapidly as their kinetic energy increases, so temperature rises.
  • During melting the supplied energy changes the particle arrangement and increases potential energy.
  • Average kinetic energy stays constant during this stage, giving a flat temperature section even though internal energy increases.
  • Once all the solid has melted, further heating increases the average kinetic energy of the liquid’s particles, so temperature rises again.
  • Internal energy is the total kinetic plus potential energy of all particles. Use mcΔθ on warming sections and mL for the state change.
  • The particles themselves do not melt or expand into different particles; the material changes state.
