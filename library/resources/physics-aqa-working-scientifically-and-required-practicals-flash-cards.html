Why does taking a mean of repeats help with random error but not with an unchanged zero offset?

Answer
  • Random fluctuations can partly cancel, reducing their effect on the mean.
  • A zero offset shifts all readings systematically, so the mean retains the offset unless corrected.

QuestionApply and calculate

Three repeated times are 1.8, 2.0 and 1.9 s. Calculate the mean, range and half-range uncertainty estimate.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Mean = (1.8 + 2.0 + 1.9)/3 = 1.9 s.
  • Range = 2.0 − 1.8 = 0.2 s.
  • Half-range estimate = ±0.1 s, with other sources of uncertainty considered separately.
QuestionApply and calculate

An empty cylinder has mass 80 g. With 50 cm³ of liquid it has mass 120 g. Calculate liquid density.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Liquid mass = 120 − 80 = 40 g.
  • ρ = m/V = 40/50 = 0.80 g/cm³.
  • This equals 800 kg/m³.
QuestionApply and calculate

A spring measures 12.0 cm unloaded and 15.0 cm under a 6.0 N load. Calculate extension and spring constant within the proportional region.

QuestionApply and calculate

A heater supplies 8,000 J to a 0.40 kg block, raising its temperature by 20°C. Calculate specific heat capacity using the measured input.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • c = E/(mΔθ).
  • c = 8,000/(0.40 × 20).
  • 1,000 J/kg°C, assuming the input energy heats the block.
QuestionApply and calculate

Explain why heat loss to the room generally makes a metal-block specific-heat result too high when all electrical input is used in the calculation.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Not all supplied energy increases the block’s thermal store.
  • The measured temperature rise is smaller than the ideal rise.
  • In c = E/(mΔθ), using all input E with that smaller Δθ overestimates c. Insulation reduces the error.

QuestionApply and calculate

Why does a filament lamp’s I–V graph curve even when its circuit is connected correctly?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer

  • Higher temperature increases resistance.
  • Current then rises less rapidly with voltage, so the I–V gradient decreases.

QuestionMulti-step application

Describe how to measure the density of an irregular solid that sinks and does not dissolve in water.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Measure mass with a balance, checking its zero.
  • Record an initial water volume in a measuring cylinder or use a displacement can.
  • Fully submerge the solid, removing trapped bubbles, and measure displaced volume.
  • Calculate ρ = m/V in matching units; read the meniscus at eye level and repeat suitably.
QuestionMulti-step application

Describe how to investigate the I–V characteristic of a resistor.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Connect ammeter in series, voltmeter across the resistor and an adjustable low-voltage supply.
  • Change voltage through a suitable range, recording paired V and I readings.
  • Obtain negative readings by reversing polarity where appropriate and repeat. Limit heating to keep temperature approximately constant.
  • Plot I against V and assess the best-fit line; a constant-temperature ohmic resistor gives a straight line through the origin.
QuestionMulti-step application

Explain how to measure wave speed on a stretched string using a stationary pattern.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Use a vibration generator and signal generator with fixed string type and tension.
  • Adjust frequency until a clear stationary pattern forms; record f.
  • Measure several loop lengths. Each loop is λ/2, so find λ from the total distance.
  • Calculate v = fλ, repeat and keep hanging loads secure.
QuestionMulti-step application

A student says, “My readings repeat closely, so they must be accurate.” Evaluate this claim and suggest a check.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Closely clustered readings show precision and repeatability under the same conditions.
  • They could all contain the same systematic error, such as a zero offset.
  • Check zero and calibration against a reference, or use an independent appropriate instrument.
  • Compare with the expected or reference value and its uncertainty before concluding accuracy.
