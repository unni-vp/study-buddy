Grammar

Nouns and articles

Grammar is the set of connections that makes a sentence work. Think of a sentence as a team: subject, verb and description must agree.

Learn a noun with its gender: le livre, la maison, l'école (f). Gender belongs to the French word, not to the object itself.

Before a vowel or silent h, le/la become l'. Use mon amie, not ma amie. Possessives agree with the thing owned: son livre can mean his book or her book.

Du, de la, de l', des express an unspecified amount: je bois de l'eau. After quantity and many negative expressions use de / d': beaucoup de livres; je n'ai pas de vélo. An identification with être keeps the article: ce n'est pas un problème.

Contractions: à + le = au, à + les = aux; de + le = du, de + les = des. No contraction with la or l'.

General likes use a definite article: j'aime la musique. Days with le can be habitual: le lundi, on Mondays. Languages: je parle français, but le français est intéressant. A person from England is un Anglais / une Anglaise; the adjective anglais has no capital.

Changing nouns

  • Most plurals add -s; masculine -(e)au / -eu commonly add -x: un jeu, des jeux.
  • Words already ending -s or -x do not add another ending.
  • Person nouns may add -e, change -eur to -euse/-rice, or -en to -enne: un ami / une amie; un acteur / une actrice.
  • Some keep the same word and change the article: un/une artiste. Learn irregulars such as monsieur / messieurs.
  • An infinitive can act as a noun: lire est agréable — reading is pleasant.
  • Chaque takes a singular noun; plusieurs is plural. Tout changes to toute/tous/toutes to match its noun.
  • Aucun / aucune means no/not any: je n'ai aucune idée.

Adjectives and comparisons

An adjective describes a noun and agrees with it: un ami intéressant; une amie intéressante; des amies intéressantes.

Plural usually adds -s; masculine -s/-x stays unchanged. Some masculine -al forms become -aux: local / locaux. Learn beau / belle / beaux, nouveau / nouvelle / nouveaux and other irregulars as complete forms.

Most adjectives follow the noun: une voiture rouge. Common adjectives including petit, grand, bon and nouveau often precede it. Learn the position with the phrase.

  • Plus / moins / aussi ... que: more / less / as ... as.
  • Plus de / moins de + noun: more / fewer or less of something.
  • Meilleur describes something better: un meilleur film.
  • Mieux describes doing something better: elle chante mieux.
  • Le plus intéressant / la plus intéressante: the most interesting.
  • Le meilleur / la meilleure / les meilleurs / les meilleures: the best.
  • Le pire / la pire / les pires: the worst; le mieux, best as an adverb.

Frequency words commonly sit after the simple verb: je mange souvent ici. In a compound tense, short adverbs often go between the auxiliary and participle: j'ai bien mangé. A time phrase can frame the sentence: hier, j'ai travaillé.

Present verbs and verb families

Je, tu, il/elle/on, nous, vous, ils/elles identify who acts. Vous can address one person politely or several people. On takes the il verb form, even when it means we.

For regular -er verbs remove -er, then add -e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent: je parle, tu parles, il parle, nous parlons, vous parlez, ils parlent. The present can mean I speak or I am speaking.

Avoir also expresses age and sensations: j'ai quinze ans; j'ai faim/soif/froid/chaud/peur.

Spelling preserves sounds: nous mangeons, nous commençons, j'achète, je jette. Other frequent irregulars include je bois, je cours, je crois, je reçois, je ris, je suis (suivre), je vois. Learn each with its infinitive and past participle, not an invented regular ending.

Modals and reflexives

Modals add can, must, want or know how. The next verb is an infinitive: je peux aider.

Je voudrais means I would like; j'ai dû partir means I had to leave.

Reflexives use me, te, se, nous, vous, se before the verb: je me lave; nous nous levons. With a following infinitive: je vais me lever. Some plural uses are reciprocal: nous nous parlons — we talk to each other.

Many reflexive perfect forms use être and subject agreement: elle s'est levée. When the direct object follows, agreement works differently: elle s'est lavé les mains. Do not add -e automatically to every reflexive participle.

Past and future

Perfect participles include parlé, choisi, entendu, pris, fait, eu, été. Most verbs use avoir; movement verbs such as aller/partir/venir use être. Être participles normally agree: allée, allés, allées. With avoir there is no subject agreement; a direct object before the verb can cause agreement: les photos que j'ai prises.

Imperfect endings: -ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient. Être uses ét-: j'étais, nous étions. Je jouais = I used to play / was playing. J'ai joué = I played / have played.

Future endings: -ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont. Conditional endings are the imperfect set. Je regarderai = I will watch; je regarderais = I would watch. Singular irregular anchors include aurai, ferai, irai, serai and aurais, ferais, irais, serais. Learn the irregular singular forms rather than assuming the whole verb is regular.

Depuis + present: j'habite ici depuis deux ans — I have lived here for two years and still do. Venir de + infinitive: je viens de finir — I have just finished. Être en train de + infinitive: je suis en train de lire — I am in the process of reading.

Choose a tense from the meaning

Read the intended message before choosing an ending. Yesterday does not always require only the perfect: an account can combine a background state with a completed event.

Il pleuvait quand nous sommes arrivés. — It was raining when we arrived.

  • Pleuvait gives the background weather.
  • Sommes arrivés gives the completed arrival. An all-female group writes arrivées.

Je regardais un film quand mon frère est entré. — I was watching a film when my brother came in. The imperfect frames the ongoing activity; the perfect introduces the event.

Intended meaningFormExample
A repeated past actionImperfectNous jouions dans le parc.
A completed actionPerfectNous avons joué samedi.
A planAller + infinitiveNous allons jouer demain.
What we will doSimple future of a regular -er verbNous jouerons demain.
What we would like to doA wish or conditional formNous aimerions jouer.

For regular -er future forms, add the future ending to the infinitive. For the conditional, add the imperfect endings to the same infinitive. Nous jouerons means we will play; nous jouerions means we would play. The extra i changes the meaning.

Do not assume every verb of movement uses être. J'ai marché and j'ai voyagé use avoir. Learn the auxiliary with the particular verb and construction.

Pronouns and connected clauses

Pronouns replace repetition, like a label on a box you have already identified.

  • Direct object: je regarde le film; je le regarde. Me/te/le/la/nous/vous/les go before the verb.
  • Indirect object: je parle à ma sœur; je lui parle. Use lui for to him/her and leur for to them; me/te/nous/vous can also be indirect objects.
  • Y: j'y vais — I go there. En: j'en veux — I want some. Use before the verb; il y en a means there is/are some.
  • Before a vowel: m', t', l', s'. With a modal: je vais le regarder.
  • After a preposition use moi, toi, lui, elle, nous, vous, eux, elles: avec lui, pour elles.
  • Qui replaces the subject: une amie qui aime lire.
  • Que replaces the object: le film que je regarde.
  • Où links a place/time: le parc où je joue.

Passive present: le livre est écrit par une journaliste — the book is written by a journalist. The thing acted on is the subject; the participle agrees with it.

Replace the right noun, in the right position

Starting sentenceReplacementReason

Je vais visiter le musée.Je vais le visiter.The object pronoun belongs before its infinitive.
Je ne regarde pas les photos.Je ne les regarde pas.The pronoun stays before the verb inside the negative.

For a relative clause, ask which word does the action. Une amie qui travaille means a friend who works: qui is the subject. Le livre que je lis means the book that I read: je is the subject, and que replaces the object.

Y commonly replaces a place introduced with à: Je vais à Paris. J'y vais. En can replace a quantity phrase: Je veux des pommes. J'en veux deux. Keep the number when it carries information.

Negatives, questions and instructions

Wrap the conjugated verb in the negative: je ne mange pas; je n'ai pas mangé.

Personne ne vient = nobody comes; rien ne change = nothing changes. Do not add pas to these expressions.

Questions can use intonation (Tu aimes lire ?), est-ce que (Est-ce que tu aimes lire ?) or inversion (Aimes-tu lire ?). Add a question word: où, quand, pourquoi, comment, combien, qui, que.

Commands omit the subject: Regarde ! Regardez ! Regular -er tu commands omit final -s. Plural nous commands: Parlons ! — Let's speak. Learn Va ! Allez ! Fais ! Faites ! Sois ! Soyez !

Put the negative around the conjugated verb

Je ne travaille plus. — I no longer work.

Je n'ai pas travaillé. — I did not work.

Je ne vais pas travailler. — I am not going to work.

In the last two examples, the negative surrounds the auxiliary or conjugated aller. It does not surround the participle or final infinitive instead. Keep the message precise: ne...que means only; ne...plus means no longer.

Useful linking patterns

  • Il faut partir: it is necessary to leave.
  • Il est difficile de choisir: it is difficult to choose.
  • Il vaut mieux attendre: it is better to wait.
  • Cela vaut la peine de visiter: it is worth visiting.
  • Il manque un livre: a book is missing.
  • Il y a / il y avait / il y aura: there is/are; there was/were; there will be.
  • Il fait beau / il pleut / il neige: the weather is nice / it rains / it snows. Il est huit heures: it is eight o'clock.
  • Pour / sans / avant de + infinitive: pour apprendre; sans parler; avant de partir.
  • Après avoir + participle: après avoir mangé — after eating.
  • En + present participle: en parlant — while/by speaking. Remove -ons from nous present and add -ant. Irregulars: étant, ayant, faisant.
  • Dans le train, inside the train; en train, by train. Prepositions change meaning, so learn the whole phrase.

For reading, recognise in-/im- (opposite), -able/-eable, -ion/-ation, -ment/-amment/-emment, -ième, and agent -eur/-ateur. Rapide becomes rapidement; deux becomes deuxième; consommer becomes consommateur. Use context as well as the ending.

Sentence check

Mes amies sont allées au cinéma parce qu'elles aiment les films français.

The subject is plural and feminine. Sont agrees with it; allées agrees after être; françaises would be wrong because films is masculine plural.

Check who, when, verb form, noun gender/number, pronoun reference and negative meaning. Adding a complicated clause helps only when it communicates the right idea.
