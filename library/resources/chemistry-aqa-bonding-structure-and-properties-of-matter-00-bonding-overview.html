Bonding & properties

Three strong bonds

  • Ionic: attraction between opposite ions after electron transfer. Covalent: shared electron pairs attract both nuclei.
  • Metallic: positive ions attract delocalised electrons. Identify the charged particles in explanations.

Structure decides properties

  • Ionic lattices: high melting; conduct only molten or dissolved, when ions move.
  • Small molecules: low melting/boiling; weak forces between molecules. No mobile charged particles.
  • Giant covalent: many strong bonds → high melting.

States & models

  • Solid: particles vibrate in fixed positions. Liquid: close particles move past each other. Gas: particles far apart, moving randomly.
  • At a change of state, energy changes attractions/spacing, not particle identity. Model drawings simplify size, forces and movement.

Metals & polymers

  • Metals conduct through mobile delocalised electrons; layers can slide. Different-sized alloy atoms hinder sliding.
  • Polymers: long molecules, strong bonds along each chain, forces between chains; most are solid at room temperature.

Carbon structures

  • Diamond: four bonds per carbon, rigid and hard, no free electrons. Graphite: three bonds; weak forces between layers let them slide.
  • Graphene: one strong, conducting layer. Fullerenes: hollow cages; nanotubes: strong conducting cylinders.

Nanoparticles

  • 1–100 nm: large surface area : volume ratio. Small amounts can be effective catalysts or coatings.
  • Uses include medicine, electronics, sunscreen and antibacterial surfaces. Properties differ from bulk material; assess exposure and uncertain risks.
