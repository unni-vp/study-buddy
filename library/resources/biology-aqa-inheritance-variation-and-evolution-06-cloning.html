Cloning

Text version

Cloning methods and evaluation

Plant cuttings

Take part of a parent plant.

Grow roots and shoots under suitable conditions.

Rapid, low-cost copies of useful plants.

Clones share genotype, not necessarily phenotype.

Tissue culture

Small groups of plant cells.

Nutrients, suitable hormones and sterile conditions.

Grow many genetically identical plants.

Useful for valuable or rare varieties.

Embryo splitting

Split cells from an early animal embryo.

Transfer resulting embryos into host mothers.

Offspring identical to embryo source.

Not the same as adult nucleus transfer.

Adult nucleus transfer

Remove unfertilised egg nucleus.

Uses and limits

Preserve valuable nuclear genetic information.

Increase numbers of useful or threatened organisms.

Success can be low. Consider welfare and costs.

Cloning does not solve habitat loss.

Diversity and accuracy

Little new genetic variation among clones.

Shared disease/environmental vulnerability.

Adult donor supplies nuclear DNA.

Egg donor and host have different roles.
