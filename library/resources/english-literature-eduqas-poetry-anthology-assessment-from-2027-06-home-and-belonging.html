Home & belonging

I Shall Return: the idea

  • McKay’s speaker promises a return to cherished landscape, music and community. Precious colours make the remembered homeland valuable.
  • Final pain explains the need for belonging. Repetition expresses resolve and possible self-reassurance.

McKay: the method

  • A Shakespearean sonnet: three quatrains and a couplet. Nature → community → emotional explanation.
  • Jamaican migration and the Harlem Renaissance illuminate cultural attachment. The hoped-for return is not shown happening.

Compare Wordsworth

  • Both connect nature with emotional relief. Wordsworth’s remembered flowers already change his present mood.
  • McKay longs to be physically home. Compare fulfilled recollection with future promise, using each ending.

Compare Drummer Hodge

  • Both place identity in relation to a landscape. Hodge’s unfamiliar sky contrasts with McKay’s cherished colours and music.
  • Hodge cannot return. The landscape absorbs him after death; McKay imagines belonging through living participation.

Context changes meaning

  • Wordsworth: Romantic faith in imagination. McKay: separation from a homeland and culture. Hardy: an ordinary life displaced by imperial war.
  • Use these contexts to explain specific choices, not as detached biography.

Write a connected argument

  • Choose the pair that fits the question. Track time: remembered past → present effect → hoped-for or permanent future.
  • Compare language, form and structure as well as content. A shared natural setting does not prove shared emotions.
