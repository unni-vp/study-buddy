Boundaries and sound

Reflection

Draw a normal perpendicular to the boundary.

i = r, measured from the normal.

Smooth: specular reflection.

Rough: diffuse scattering.

Each patch still obeys i = r.

Refraction

Oblique wavefront reaches glass at different times. One part slows first, turning the wavefront.

Air to glass: towards normal.

Normal incidence: no direction change.

Ray practical

Trace block or mirror position. Mark narrow rays at two points.

Remove apparatus. Join marks and draw normal.

Measure angles. Vary incidence and material.

Use fine marks and consistent eye position. Do not claim 0.1° with a 1° protractor.

Sound and hearing

Sound needs a medium. Air vibrations are longitudinal.

Eardrum and other parts vibrate over a limited frequency range.

Normal hearing: 20 Hz–20 kHz.

Pitch: frequency. Loudness: amplitude.

Ultrasound and echoes

Ultrasound: above 20 kHz.

Partial reflection at different-material boundaries gives echoes.

Medical imaging, flaw detection and echo sounding.

Distance = vt/2.

Both journeys are timed.

Seismic evidence

P-waves: longitudinal, solids and liquids.

S-waves: transverse, not liquids.

Missing S-waves across the core support a liquid outer core.

Paths and arrival times reveal boundaries and core size.
