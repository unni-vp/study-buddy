QuestionApply and calculate

Explain the formation of a V-shaped valley.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • A river uses vertical erosion to cut down into its bed.
  • Weathering and mass movement weaken and lower the valley sides.
  • The river removes fallen material, leaving steep sides shaped like a V.
QuestionApply and calculate

Explain how a waterfall retreats to form a gorge.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Softer rock beneath resistant rock is eroded faster and a plunge pool forms.
  • The resistant overhang collapses because it is unsupported.
  • Repetition moves the waterfall upstream, leaving a narrow, steep-sided gorge.
QuestionApply and calculate

Explain how an oxbow lake forms.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Erosion narrows the neck of a meander.
  • During high flow, the river cuts through the neck and takes a straighter route.
  • Deposition seals the ends of the old bend, leaving an oxbow lake.
QuestionApply and calculate

Explain how a spit forms.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Longshore drift transports sediment along the coast.
  • Where the coastline changes direction, material continues into open water and is deposited as energy falls.
  • The ridge grows outward; its end may curve if wave direction changes.
QuestionMulti-step application

Explain how climate and geology can influence a UK river landscape.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Heavy or frequent rainfall raises discharge, giving a river more power to erode and transport material.
  • Storms can also increase flooding and floodplain deposition.
  • Resistant rock erodes slowly and can produce steeper features.
  • Softer or less resistant rock erodes more quickly, helping valleys widen.
QuestionMulti-step application

Explain how erosion and deposition are connected along a coast.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Waves erode cliffs and release sediment.
  • Longshore drift transports that sediment along the shoreline.
  • Where wave energy falls, the sediment is deposited.
  • This can build beaches or spits, so erosion in one place supplies landforms elsewhere.
QuestionMulti-step application

Explain how natural processes and people combine to shape the River Wye landscape.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The Wye rises on Plynlimon, where high rainfall and steep slopes produce rapid runoff and energetic erosion.
  • Downstream, tributaries raise discharge and gentler relief allows meanders, floodplains and deposition.
  • Carboniferous Limestone creates a steep lower gorge around Symonds Yat, showing that geology modifies the usual downstream pattern.
  • Farming and building alter runoff, while natural flood-management work with more than 140 landowners slows and stores water upstream.
QuestionMulti-step application

Explain why managing one part of the North Norfolk coast can affect another part.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Soft cliffs at places such as Happisburgh supply sediment to the coastal system.
  • Longshore drift moves this material towards beaches and depositional features such as Blakeney Point.
  • Groynes at a defended settlement such as Cromer retain beach material locally.
  • This may reduce the sediment reaching another place, narrowing its beach and increasing exposure to erosion.
