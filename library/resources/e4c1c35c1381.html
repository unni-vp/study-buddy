Chemistry of the atmosphere questions & answers

1 mark2 marks3 marks4 marks6 marks

1 mark

1. What gas makes up most of today’s atmosphere?

Answer:

Nitrogen.

2. Approximately what percentage of dry air is oxygen?

Answer:

21%.

3. What process increased atmospheric oxygen?

Answer:

Photosynthesis.

4. Name a greenhouse gas other than carbon dioxide or methane.

Answer:

Water vapour.

5. Which pollutant is toxic, colourless and odourless?

Answer:

Carbon monoxide.

6. Which kind of radiation is emitted by Earth’s warm surface?

Answer:

Long-wavelength infrared.

7. What is global dimming?

Answer:

A reduction in sunlight reaching Earth’s surface caused by atmospheric particulates.

2 marks

8. Describe two likely features of the early atmosphere.

Answer:
  • Much carbon dioxide.
  • Little or no oxygen.

9. How did Earth’s oceans form in the volcanic-outgassing model?

Answer:
  • Volcanoes released water vapour.
  • As Earth cooled, vapour condensed into liquid water.

10. Give two processes that reduced atmospheric carbon dioxide over long timescales.

Answer:
  • Photosynthesis incorporated carbon into organisms.
  • Dissolution/carbonate sediment formation and fossil-fuel formation stored carbon outside the atmosphere.

11. Give two human activities that increase methane emissions.

Answer:
  • Cattle farming or flooded rice cultivation.
  • Anaerobic decomposition of organic waste in landfill.

12. Explain how burning sulfur-containing fuel can harm the environment.

Answer:
  • Sulfur burns to form sulfur dioxide.
  • This can cause acid rain, damaging ecosystems/buildings.

13. Why can nitrogen oxides form from a nitrogen-free fuel?

Answer:
  • Air contains nitrogen and oxygen.
  • They react at the high combustion temperature.

14. Explain why increasing oxygen supply reduces carbon monoxide formation.

Answer:
  • Carbon monoxide forms during incomplete combustion.
  • More oxygen supports complete oxidation to carbon dioxide.

15. Why does deforestation increase atmospheric carbon dioxide?

Answer:
  • Burning/decay releases carbon dioxide from biomass.
  • Fewer trees remove it through photosynthesis.

16. Give two possible effects of climate change.

Answer:
  • Sea-level rise can increase coastal flood risk.
  • Habitat/rainfall changes can affect species or food production.

17. Why is one cold winter insufficient evidence against a long-term warming trend?

Answer:
  • Weather varies by location and year.
  • Climate trends need long-term, broad measurements, not one local event.

18. Define the carbon footprint of a product.

Answer:
  • Its total carbon dioxide and other greenhouse-gas emissions.
  • Across the full life cycle, including production, use and disposal.

19. A product’s emissions are 30, 12, 60 and 8 kg CO₂e at four life-cycle stages. Calculate total footprint.

Answer:
  • Total = 30+12+60+8.
  • 110 kg CO₂e.

3 marks

20. Explain the greenhouse effect using radiation wavelengths.

Answer:
  • Short-wavelength sunlight passes through and warms Earth’s surface.
  • The surface emits long-wavelength infrared.
  • Greenhouse gases absorb and re-emit some infrared, including downwards, reducing net energy loss at a given temperature.

21. Explain how crude oil and natural gas deposits formed.

Answer:
  • Ancient marine biomass such as plankton died and was buried in sediment.
  • Oxygen-poor conditions limited complete decay.
  • Heat and pressure changed it over millions of years into fossil-fuel deposits.

22. Explain how limestone formation can store carbon.

Answer:
  • Carbon dioxide dissolves in oceans and contributes to carbonate formation.
  • Carbonate sediments and shells/skeletons accumulate.
  • They compact into limestone, storing carbon outside the atmosphere.

23. Explain the formation of coal.

Answer:
  • Dead vegetation accumulates in conditions limiting decay.
  • It becomes buried under sediments.
  • Heat and pressure over long timescales transform it into a carbon-rich fuel.

24. Why is evidence for the early atmosphere uncertain?

Answer:
  • Events occurred billions of years ago, with no direct measurements.
  • Ancient rocks/materials have been altered or destroyed.
  • Scientists compare surviving evidence and models, revising explanations as new data arise.

25. Explain how a growing population can raise carbon-dioxide emissions.

Answer:
  • Greater demand for energy and transport increases fossil-fuel burning.
  • Burning carbon-containing fuels releases carbon dioxide.
  • Extra farmland/development can also cause deforestation and reduced carbon uptake.

26. A total footprint is 135 kg CO₂e, including 80 kg from use. Use emissions fall by 25%. Calculate new footprint and whole-footprint reduction.

Answer:
  • Saving = 0.25 ×80 = 20 kg.
  • New footprint = 135−20 = 115 kg CO₂e.
  • Whole reduction = 20/135 ×100 ≈ 14.8%.

27. Explain why carbon monoxide is dangerous.

Answer:
  • It is colourless and odourless, making exposure difficult to notice.
  • It binds strongly to haemoglobin.
  • This reduces blood’s oxygen-carrying ability.

28. Explain how particulates affect sunlight and human health.

Answer:
  • Particles scatter/reflect incoming sunlight.
  • Less sunlight reaches the surface, causing global dimming.
  • Inhaled particles can damage respiratory health.

4 marks

29. Compare global warming with acid rain.

Answer:
  • Greenhouse gases absorb/re-emit outgoing infrared, enhancing warming.
  • This affects average temperature and climate.
  • Sulfur dioxide/nitrogen oxides form acidic substances in atmospheric moisture.
  • Acid rain damages plants, aquatic systems and buildings; it is a different mechanism.

30. Give four potential consequences of climate change with a brief link to each.

Answer:
  • Warmer oceans and melting land ice raise sea level, increasing coastal flooding.
  • Changed rainfall can increase drought and limit freshwater.
  • Habitat conditions shift, changing species distributions.
  • Heat/rainfall changes can alter crop yields and food security.

31. Explain why full-life-cycle evidence is needed to compare two transport technologies.

Answer:
  • Manufacturing and raw materials can emit greenhouse gases before either is used.
  • Fuel/electricity production affects use-related emissions.
  • Disposal/recycling and lifetime change the total.
  • Compare equal transport service, not just exhaust emissions from one trip.

6 marks

32. Evaluate a news claim about climate change based on a short temperature record from one city.

Answer:
  • A short local record may reflect weather variability and local factors rather than global climate.
  • Examine measurement quality, coverage, missing data and whether start/end dates were selectively chosen.
  • Compare longer records from many locations and independent methods.
  • Consider physical greenhouse mechanisms and other influences, not correlation alone.
  • Peer-reviewed analysis and independently tested models increase confidence, while exact local predictions remain uncertain.
  • A justified conclusion states what these limited data show and why they cannot alone establish or overturn a global trend.

33. Evaluate a factory plan to reduce sulfur dioxide and its carbon footprint.

Answer:
  • Removing sulfur from fuel or capturing sulfur dioxide can reduce acid rain and respiratory damage.
  • Efficiency improvements reduce fuel use and associated carbon dioxide.
  • Lower-carbon energy may further reduce emissions, but manufacturing/supply impacts must be included.
  • Compare costs, reliability, removal efficiency and maintenance using measured data.
  • A sulfur-dioxide treatment alone does not necessarily reduce carbon dioxide; extra treatment energy can add emissions.
  • Choose a justified combination based on full-life-cycle greenhouse emissions and local pollutant benefits.
