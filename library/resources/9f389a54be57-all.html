Geographical skills questions & answers

1 mark

1. In which order are grid references read?

Answer:

Eastings, then northings.

2. What does a contour line join?

Answer:

Points of equal height.

3. Which graph is most suitable for showing a relationship between two variables?

Answer:

Scatter graph.

4. What is the range of 4, 7, 9 and 15?

Answer:

11.

5. What is interpolation?

Answer:

Estimating a value within the known data range.

6. Name the sampling method that collects data at regular intervals.

Answer:

Systematic sampling.

2 marks

7. A distance is 8 cm on a 1:50,000 map. Calculate the real distance in kilometres.

Answer:
  • 1 cm represents 0.5 km.
  • 8 × 0.5 = 4 km.

8. Explain how contour spacing shows gradient.

Answer:
  • Closely spaced contours show height changing rapidly over a short distance, so the slope is steep.
  • Widely spaced contours show a gentle slope.

9. Give two features required on a clear graph.

Answer:
  • Labelled axes with units.
  • An even and suitable scale.

10. Explain one difference between a bar chart and a histogram.

Answer:
  • A bar chart represents separate categories and usually has gaps.
  • A histogram represents continuous grouped data, so its bars touch.

11. Calculate the percentage increase from 40 to 50.

Answer:
  • Increase = 10.
  • 10 ÷ 40 × 100 = 25%.

12. Explain the difference between reliability and validity.

Answer:
  • Reliability is whether repetition produces similar results.
  • Validity is whether the method measures what the enquiry needs.

13. A chart shows renewables at 50.4% and fossil fuels at 31.8% of electricity generation. Calculate the difference and give the correct unit.

Answer:

50.4 − 31.8 = 18.6 percentage points.

3 marks

14. The values are 2, 4, 4, 5 and 10. Calculate the mean and median, and identify which better represents a typical value.

Answer:
  • Mean = 25 ÷ 5 = 5.
  • Median = 4.
  • The median may be more representative because the high value 10 pulls up the mean.

15. Explain why extrapolation is less reliable than interpolation.

Answer:
  • Extrapolation predicts beyond the measured data range.
  • The existing trend may not continue there.
  • No observations are available to check the estimate directly.

16. Explain one advantage and one limitation of a choropleth map.

Answer:
  • It makes broad spatial patterns and regional comparisons easy to see.
  • Values are averaged across each area, so local variation may be hidden.

17. Explain how GIS could help investigate flood risk.

Answer:
  • Layers for relief, rivers, rainfall, land use and settlements can be stored.
  • The layers can be overlaid to identify places where hazards and people coincide.
  • Data can be updated and different management options compared.

18. A chart is labelled “UK electricity generation, 2024”. Explain why a claim about all UK energy use in 2027 would go beyond it.

Answer:
  • It measures electricity generation, not all energy used in transport, heating and industry.
  • Its data refer to 2024, so they do not directly establish the 2027 mix.
  • A conclusion must keep the same measure and time period unless further evidence is supplied.

4 marks

19. Explain how to construct a six-figure grid reference for a point.

Answer:
  • Identify the easting line immediately to the left of the point.
  • Estimate tenths across that grid square to obtain the third easting digit.
  • Identify the northing line immediately below the point.
  • Estimate tenths up the square, then write all three eastings before all three northings.

20. Explain how to analyse a scatter graph effectively.

Answer:
  • State whether correlation is positive, negative or absent and comment on its strength.
  • Quote paired values or refer to the line of best fit as evidence.
  • Identify any anomalies.
  • Explain that correlation alone does not prove one variable caused the other.

21. Explain why systematic sampling may be suitable along an urban transect.

Answer:
  • Regular intervals give coverage from one end of the route to the other.
  • The rule is simple and repeatable, improving reliability.
  • It reduces the researcher's freedom to choose only convenient sites.
  • However, a repeating pattern could coincide with the interval and bias the sample.

22. Explain how a geographer could improve the accuracy and reliability of river velocity measurements.

Answer:
  • Measure a fixed distance carefully and use the same method at every site.
  • Repeat readings and calculate a mean to reduce the influence of unusual results.
  • Use calibrated equipment and record units consistently.
  • Keep the position in the channel or release method consistent where possible.

23. A map counts drought-related disasters by country over 1974–2003. Explain why it cannot alone show which country has the greatest drought risk today.

Answer:

The period is historical, so climate, population and management may have changed. Countries also differ in exposure and vulnerability, affecting whether dry weather becomes a recorded disaster. Reporting quality and the definition of an event can vary. The map shows recorded events in its stated period; assessing current risk needs comparable recent evidence and information about who or what is exposed.

6 marks

24. Evaluate the usefulness of a choropleth map for showing population data.

Answer:

A choropleth map makes regional patterns easy to see because a consistent colour scale allows rapid comparison. It works well for rates or percentages, such as population density, because differently sized areas remain comparable. However, each area receives one shade, so variation within it is hidden. Results also depend on class intervals: broad or uneven classes can exaggerate or conceal patterns. Large rural areas may dominate visually despite containing few people. It is useful for an overview, but should be combined with finer-scale data or another map when local detail matters.

25. Assess the value of using mean, median and range together.

Answer:

The mean uses every value, so it gives a useful overall average, but extreme results can pull it away from a typical value. The median is resistant to extremes and may describe skewed geographical data better. The range shows total spread, helping the reader see whether values are consistent, though it depends only on the two extremes. Used together, they show centre and variation and reveal when the mean may mislead. The interquartile range can strengthen the analysis because it describes the middle half without depending on extremes.

26. Evaluate a fieldwork data set that contains a small opportunity sample.

Answer:

An opportunity sample is quick and practical, so it may allow data to be collected within limited time. However, people or sites that are easiest to reach may differ from the wider population. A small sample makes unusual responses more influential and weakens confidence in the conclusion. Repeating the survey at different times, increasing the sample and using random, systematic or stratified selection could make it more representative. The data may still suggest a pattern, but the conclusion should be cautious and limited to the sampled place and time.

27. Explain how a geographer should reach and evaluate a conclusion.

Answer:

The conclusion should answer the enquiry directly and use processed evidence, such as values, averages, graph trends and mapped patterns. It should identify exceptions and avoid claiming causation from correlation alone. Evaluation then considers sample size and method, measurement accuracy, reliability, representativeness and source bias. Improvements must be specific, such as repeating measurements under comparable conditions or adding sites through stratified sampling, and should explain how they improve the evidence. This creates a conclusion whose strength matches the quality of the data.
