Radiation and half-life

Radioactive decay and types of radiation

Some nuclei are unstable. Radioactive decay is a random nuclear change that emits radiation as the nucleus becomes more stable. You cannot predict which nucleus decays next. Large samples nevertheless show a predictable overall pattern.

Activity is the number of decays per second, measured in becquerels (Bq). Count rate is what a detector records per second or minute. It is usually lower than activity because a detector does not detect every emission.

RadiationWhat it isPenetration and rangeIonising power
Alpha, α2 protons + 2 neutrons; helium nucleusStopped by paper or outer skin; a few cm in airHigh
Beta-minus, βFast electron emitted from nucleus as neutron becomes protonStopped by a few mm of aluminium; metres in airMedium
Gamma, γElectromagnetic radiation from nucleusReduced by thick lead/concrete; long rangeLow compared with alpha/beta
NeutronUncharged particle from nucleusCan penetrate matter; important in fissionNo charge; can cause effects through interactions with nuclei

Ionisation means removing electrons from atoms. Radiation can damage living cells and DNA. Gamma is not completely “stopped” by a thin barrier; shielding reduces the intensity.

Beta electrons come from a nuclear change, not the electron shells. Alpha emission changes the nucleus; losing an ordinary outer electron does not.

Activity, count rate and dose answer different questions

QuantityWhat it describesImportant limitation
Activity, in BqHow many nuclei decay per second in the sourceIt does not say how much radiation reaches a person.
Count rate, in counts/s or counts/minWhat the detector recordsDistance, shielding, efficiency and background affect it.
Dose, in Sv or mSv when suppliedA measure used to assess biological effects of exposureRadiation type, absorption and exposed tissue matter.

Moving the detector farther away reduces its count rate without changing the source's activity. Removing shielding can increase exposure without making the source decay faster.
