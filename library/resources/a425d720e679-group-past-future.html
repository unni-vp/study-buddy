Past & future time frames

Past and future

Time frameBuild itExample
completed past: perfectavoir/être in present + participlej'ai parlé; elle est partie

Perfect participles include parlé, choisi, entendu, pris, fait, eu, été. Most verbs use avoir; movement verbs such as aller/partir/venir use être. Être participles normally agree: allée, allés, allées. With avoir there is no subject agreement; a direct object before the verb can cause agreement: les photos que j'ai prises.

Imperfect endings: -ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient. Être uses ét-: j'étais, nous étions. Je jouais = I used to play / was playing. J'ai joué = I played / have played.

Future endings: -ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont. Conditional endings are the imperfect set. Je regarderai = I will watch; je regarderais = I would watch. Singular irregular anchors include aurai, ferai, irai, serai and aurais, ferais, irais, serais. Learn the irregular singular forms rather than assuming the whole verb is regular.

Depuis + present: j'habite ici depuis deux ans — I have lived here for two years and still do. Venir de + infinitive: je viens de finir — I have just finished. Être en train de + infinitive: je suis en train de lire — I am in the process of reading.

Choose a tense from the meaning

Read the intended message before choosing an ending. Yesterday does not always require only the perfect: an account can combine a background state with a completed event.

Il pleuvait quand nous sommes arrivés. — It was raining when we arrived.

  • Pleuvait gives the background weather.
  • Sommes arrivés gives the completed arrival. An all-female group writes arrivées.

Je regardais un film quand mon frère est entré. — I was watching a film when my brother came in. The imperfect frames the ongoing activity; the perfect introduces the event.

Intended meaningFormExample
A repeated past actionImperfectNous jouions dans le parc.
A completed actionPerfectNous avons joué samedi.
A planAller + infinitiveNous allons jouer demain.
What we will doSimple future of a regular -er verbNous jouerons demain.
What we would like to doA wish or conditional formNous aimerions jouer.

For regular -er future forms, add the future ending to the infinitive. For the conditional, add the imperfect endings to the same infinitive. Nous jouerons means we will play; nous jouerions means we would play. The extra i changes the meaning.

Do not assume every verb of movement uses être. J'ai marché and j'ai voyagé use avoir. Learn the auxiliary with the particular verb and construction.
