Cells

Cell types and structures

A cell is a tiny working unit of a living organism. Its smaller parts each do a job.

A cell is like a factory: instructions guide the work, workers make products, and a boundary controls deliveries. This is a memory aid; cells do not think or make decisions.

StructureMust-know functionFactory memory aid
NucleusContains genetic material; controls cell activities through its genes.Instruction office
CytoplasmWhere many chemical reactions occur; enzymes control these reactions.Working space

MitochondriaSite of aerobic respiration, which transfers energy for cell processes.Energy supply

ChloroplastsSite of photosynthesis; chlorophyll absorbs light.Solar-powered production

Animal, plant and bacterial cells

FeatureAnimal cellPlant cellBacterial cell
Genetic materialDNA is enclosed in a nucleus.DNA is enclosed in a nucleus.The main DNA is a circular loop in the cytoplasm; some cells also have plasmids.
Cell boundaryA membrane controls exchange. There is no cell wall.A membrane controls exchange. A cellulose wall provides support.A membrane controls exchange. A wall made from a different material provides support.
Respiration and protein productionMitochondria carry out aerobic respiration; ribosomes make proteins.Mitochondria carry out aerobic respiration; ribosomes make proteins.There are ribosomes but no mitochondria. Respiration does not require a nucleus.
Other structuresMost cells have cytoplasm and the structures listed above.Many have a large permanent vacuole. Photosynthetic cells have chloroplasts.Bacteria are usually much smaller and lack chloroplasts.

Plant and animal cells are eukaryotic because their genetic material is enclosed in a nucleus. Bacteria are prokaryotic. A plasmid is an additional small ring of DNA; it is not a bacterial nucleus.

A diagram shows a representative cell, not every exception. For example, underground root hair cells normally lack chloroplasts, while mature mammalian red blood cells lack a nucleus. Identify a cell using several features together, rather than assuming every green-looking structure must be a chloroplast.

The wall supports the cell; the membrane controls exchange. They are separate structures.
