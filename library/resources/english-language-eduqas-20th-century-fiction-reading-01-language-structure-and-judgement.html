Language, structure and judgement

Word to meaning

  • “Swallowed” time: personification makes another minute seem irretrievably consumed.
  • Name the method only when useful. Explain the particular meaning rather than saying a word is effective.

An image develops

  • The repaired glove: practical care becomes a “crooked bridge”.
  • The simile suggests an imperfect continuing connection, not that every disagreement has disappeared.

Narrative focus

  • Close viewpoint: Ada expects confrontation before the father’s purpose is known.
  • Follow changes in what the reader knows and how the character interprets them.

Shows the selected practice passage moving from isolation through interruption to connection.

Control the pace

  • “She turned.” The isolated movement delays the revelation at a decisive moment.
  • Longer description keeps us waiting earlier. Sentence length has no single automatic effect.

Build the paragraph

  • Claim → evidence → explanation: connect a precise choice to the question.
  • Impression: father seems threatening. Evidence: forceful footsteps. Explain Ada’s anxious interpretation.

Evaluate with nuance

  • Determined, not simply impulsive: months of saving qualify the secret departure.
  • The final letter suggests connection. Reach a judgement that accounts for both the hurt and the ambition.
