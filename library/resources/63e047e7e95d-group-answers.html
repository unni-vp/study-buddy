Quotations & analytical vocabulary

Useful vocabulary for a precise argument

Use a term when it expresses the idea clearly. The explanation and evidence matter more than an impressive label.

TermPlain meaningUseful Macbeth connection
TyrantA cruel, oppressive rulerMacbeth protects himself by harming the people he should serve.
HubrisExcessive pride or overconfidenceHe treats the apparitions' ambiguous promises as guarantees of safety.
HamartiaA tragic error or flaw contributing to downfallAmbition overriding conscience is one useful reading; his repeated decisions matter too.
TransgressionCrossing a moral or accepted boundaryKilling his king, relative and guest breaks several duties.
CatalystSomething that starts or speeds up eventsThe prophecies encourage a desire that Macbeth chooses to act on.
DuplicitousDeceptive through concealed intentionsThe couple perform loyal hospitality while planning murder.
Integrity / restraintKeeping principles / controlling desiresBanquo is tempted, but does not murder to achieve the promised future.
PatriarchalOrganised mainly around male authorityLady Macbeth uses expectations of masculine courage to pressure her husband.
EmasculationUndermining someone's sense of masculinityHer conditional manhood challenge turns refusal into shame.
Sacrilege / blasphemyViolating something sacred / showing disrespect towards God or sacred beliefsMacduff calls the killing sacrilegious; regicide names the act more precisely than treating the terms as synonyms.

Learn what each phrase means in its moment, then use it to develop an argument. A precise reference to an event can also support a point; do not invent quotation wording.
