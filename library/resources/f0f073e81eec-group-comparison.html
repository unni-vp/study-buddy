Comparison and exam approach

Comparing two unseen poems

The second task asks you to compare the two printed poems. Read the second fully before choosing comparison points. Do not assume they communicate the same message because their subjects are similar.

Use shared ideas as paragraph topics:

  • How each speaker views the situation.
  • A significant image and its effect in each poem.
  • A shift or ending that develops the idea differently.

Both poems explore ... but ... is a useful opening. The paragraph must then prove both sides with evidence and analysis.

Bring the poems together throughout. Two separate mini-essays followed by one comparison sentence do not sustain a comparison.

A contrasting poem and comparison

Departure

I rinse the cup and turn the key.
The morning opens down the street.
A bus lifts sunlight from the road;
I choose the window for my seat.

The house grows smaller as we turn.
Its darkened panes no longer stare.
I carry nothing from the shelf,
but find the space I needed there.

Overview: the speaker leaves a familiar place and experiences absence as an opportunity for freedom.

Worked comparison

Both poems connect a domestic cup with separation, but the speakers respond differently. In Waiting, the cup remains warm and a second place is repeatedly prepared, suggesting attachment to an absent person. In Departure, rinsing the cup and turning the key complete a routine before moving away. These practical actions make departure seem chosen rather than endured.

The outside world also has a different role. Waiting describes windows carrying “other lives”, a metaphor that places companionship beyond the speaker's reach. Departure's morning “opens”, using personification to make the street seem welcoming. Movement offers possibility instead of emphasising exclusion.

The endings reinforce this contrast. Waiting places readiness beside non-arrival in two short statements, leaving the relationship unresolved. Departure ends with finding needed space, presenting the loss of familiar objects as emotional room to grow. Both use absence, but one makes it painful and the other liberating.

Managing the two tasks

The single-poem task is worth 15 marks and the comparison 25 marks. The paper advises about 20 minutes and 40 minutes respectively. Include reading and brief planning within those periods; leave time to check each answer.

Single poem

  • Read the task and poem carefully.
  • Establish an overview and trace the main development.
  • Select a few strong details from different parts.
  • Explain language, structure or form where they shape meaning.

Comparison

  • Read the second poem and identify its distinct attitude.
  • Choose linked comparison points before writing.
  • Use evidence and analysis from both poems in each developed comparison.
  • Give the comparison enough time; it is worth more marks.

Final checks

Have you answered the actual task? Is the speaker distinct from the poet? Are your claims supported? Have you explained the ending? In the comparison, have you developed similarities and relevant differences?

There is no need to invent historical context for unseen poems. The task assesses understanding and analysis of the texts in front of you.
