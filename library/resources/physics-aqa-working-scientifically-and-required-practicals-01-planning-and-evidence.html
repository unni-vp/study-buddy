Planning and scientific evidence

Hypothesis and variables

Hypothesis: testable explanation or prediction.

Independent: deliberately changed. Dependent: measured response.

Controls prevent confounding.

Repeating an unfair comparison does not make it valid.

Method design

Choose apparatus, range and spacing. Measure systematically.

Repeat measurements and preserve raw data.

Representative sample avoids convenience bias.

Describe setup, measurement, processing and comparison.

Risk and precautions

Hazard: source of harm. Risk: likelihood and seriousness.

Hot objects: heatproof surface, careful handling.

Electric circuits: low voltage and limited current.

Secure loads and moving equipment. Keep electrics dry.

Models and predictions

Models simplify reality to explain and predict.

State useful limits, such as proportional spring behaviour.

Compare prediction with observations.

New reliable evidence can require a model to change.

Claims and causation

Use data, units and uncertainty to support conclusions.

Correlation alone does not prove causation.

Consider uncontrolled variables and sampling.

Distinguish observation from interpretation.

Review and choices

Peer review checks methods and reasoning. Independent reproduction adds evidence.

Review is not a guarantee. Media can oversimplify claims.

Evaluate benefits, risks, cost, environment and ethical effects.

Perceived risk may differ from measured risk.
