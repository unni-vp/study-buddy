QuestionExplain and compare

Distinguish a permanent magnet from an induced magnet.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • A permanent magnet retains its magnetism and produces its own field.
  • An induced magnet becomes magnetic in an external field and usually loses most or all magnetism when the field is removed.

Why can a voltmeter show induced potential difference even when the coil does not carry a significant current?

QuestionExplain and compare

Explain why a transformer normally needs AC rather than steady DC.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer

  • This induces secondary potential difference. Steady DC does not provide the sustained field change needed.

QuestionApply and calculate

Explain how an induced current opposes a magnet being pushed into a coil.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Movement changes the magnetic field linked with the coil and induces current.
  • The current produces a field that opposes the original change.
  • An opposing magnetic force means work must be done to continue pushing, supplying the electrical energy.

QuestionApply and calculate

For the same transmitted power, a grid voltage is doubled. State the new current and heating loss as fractions of their original values, with resistance fixed.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer

  • Heating loss becomes (1/2)² = one quarter.
QuestionMulti-step application

Describe a compass method for plotting the field around a bar magnet.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Trace the magnet on paper and keep it fixed.
  • Place a small compass nearby and mark both ends of its needle.
  • Move it to continue from a previous mark, repeating to trace a line. Join marks smoothly.
  • Add arrows using the north-seeking end and repeat from other starting points. Keep other magnetic objects away.
QuestionMulti-step application

An 80% efficient transformer supplies 12 V at 3.0 A from a 240 V source. Calculate input power and input current.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Output power = 12 × 3.0 = 36 W.
  • Efficiency = output/input, so input power = 36/0.80 = 45 W.
  • Iₚ = Pₚ/Vₚ = 45/240 = 0.1875 A, about 0.19 A.
  • Input power exceeds output because some energy is dissipated.
QuestionMulti-step application

Explain how opposite forces on the two sides of a motor coil can cause rotation rather than canceling the turning effect.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The sides carry current in opposite directions within the same field.
  • They experience opposite motor-effect forces.
  • The forces act at different positions and create moments in the same rotational sense.
  • In a simple DC motor, a split-ring commutator reverses coil current each half-turn to maintain rotation.
