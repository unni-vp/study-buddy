QuestionApply and calculate

Explain why gravity is not balanced by a forward force in a satellite’s circular orbit.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Gravity provides an inward resultant force.
  • This changes the direction of motion rather than the speed in an ideal circular orbit.
  • The satellite continually accelerates towards the centre; no forward balancing force is needed to maintain motion.

QuestionApply and calculate

A circular orbit has radius 8.0 × 10⁶ m and period 8,000 s. Calculate average orbital speed using distance/time.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • One orbit is 2πr = 2π × 8.0 × 10⁶ m.
  • v = 2πr/T = (2π × 8.0 × 10⁶)/8,000.
  • v ≈ 6.3 × 10³ m/s.
QuestionApply and calculate

A satellite is 600 km above a planet with radius 6,400 km. Its period is 6,000 s. Calculate its circular orbital speed.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Orbital radius = 6,400 + 600 = 7,000 km = 7.0 × 10⁶ m.
  • v = 2πr/T = (2π × 7.0 × 10⁶)/6,000.
  • v ≈ 7.3 × 10³ m/s.
QuestionApply and calculate

Explain why red-shift supports an expanding universe.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Matching lines from most distant galaxies have longer wavelengths, showing recession.
  • More distant galaxies generally recede faster.
  • This distance–speed pattern supports expansion of space itself.
QuestionMulti-step application

Explain how a nebula develops into a main-sequence star.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Gravity pulls together gas and dust.
  • Contraction raises temperature and pressure, forming a protostar.
  • When the core is hot enough, hydrogen fusion begins, producing helium and releasing energy.
  • Fusion maintains outward pressure effects that balance gravity, giving a stable main-sequence stage.
QuestionMulti-step application

Explain why scientists may revise a cosmological model when new observations are made.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • A model should explain existing evidence and give testable predictions.
  • Scientists compare new observations with those predictions, considering measurement uncertainty.
  • Reliable evidence that does not fit may require the model to be modified or replaced.
  • For example, accelerating expansion led to further questions about dark energy rather than a claim that expansion was already completely explained.
