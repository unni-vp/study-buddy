Design and evidence

Text version

Designing investigations and evaluating evidence

Testable question

Define a hypothesis with a biological reason.

Choose range, intervals and repeats.

Measure a named dependent quantity.

Models simplify and can be tested.

Control the comparison

Keep other influential factors consistent.

Explain how each could affect results.

Use control experiment where appropriate.

Representative sampling limits selection bias.

Measurement quality

Suitable range/resolution and correct units.

Check zero, calibrate, read at eye level.

Precise does not automatically mean accurate.

Resolution and spread limit uncertainty.

Random versus systematic

Random: unpredictable variation. Mean reduces effect.

Systematic: consistent bias. Recalibrate/correct cause.

Investigate anomalies before discarding.

Repeats cannot remove a constant offset.

Conclusions and graphs

Use suitable bars, scatter or line graph.

Best fit reflects pattern, not every point.

Support trends with data and units.

Compare hypothesis, spread and alternatives.

Scientific judgement

Peer review checks reasoning/methods.

Replication tests reproducibility.

Risk combines likelihood and consequence.

Evaluate ethical, social, economic and environmental issues.
