QuestionApply and calculate

Explain how deforestation can change the rainforest water cycle.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Fewer leaves intercept rainfall.
  • Less water returns to the atmosphere through transpiration.
  • Surface runoff rises and local rainfall recycling may fall.
QuestionApply and calculate

  • Descending air creates high pressure and prevents cloud formation.
  • Rainfall is therefore very low, producing sparse vegetation.

QuestionFocused recall

What is the difference between a nutrient store and a transfer?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • A store holds nutrients, such as living biomass. A transfer moves nutrients between stores, such as leaf fall moving nutrients from biomass to litter.
QuestionFocused recall

Why can lush rainforest grow on soils with a small available nutrient store?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Warm, moist conditions support rapid decomposition, and roots quickly absorb the released nutrients. Much of the stock is retained in living biomass rather than accumulating in soil.
QuestionFocused recall

How does leaching differ from soil erosion?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Leaching carries dissolved nutrients through soil in water. Erosion physically removes soil particles.
QuestionFocused recall

Why can burning forest create only a short-lived improvement in soil nutrients?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Ash can release nutrients briefly, but clearance removes biomass and litter inputs. Exposed soil then loses nutrients through leaching and erosion.
QuestionFocused recall

Do nutrient minerals evaporate from soil in the same way as water?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • No. Important losses include leaching of dissolved nutrients, removal in harvested biomass and physical erosion of soil.
QuestionFocused recall

Does an epiphyte normally feed directly on its supporting tree?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • No. It uses the tree as support to reach light and obtains water and nutrients from its surroundings, such as rain and trapped debris.
QuestionFocused recall

What does canopy interception do to rainfall?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Leaves hold and delay some rainfall. This can slow the delivery of water to the ground and reduce direct raindrop impact.
QuestionFocused recall

Why does clearing trees affect both runoff and atmospheric moisture?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Reduced canopy and root effects can increase rapid runoff, while less transpiration returns less water vapour to the atmosphere.

QuestionFocused recall

Are nutrients and energy both recycled in exactly the same way?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • No. Nutrients can cycle between organisms and the environment. Energy passes through feeding relationships and is eventually dissipated as heat.
QuestionFocused recall

Why are warm-water reef builders usually found in clear, shallow seas?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The algae living in their tissues need light for photosynthesis, so light must penetrate the water. Warmth and suitable salinity also matter.
QuestionFocused recall

Why is “warmer water always helps coral” incorrect?

Answer

How can a productive reef exist in nutrient-poor surrounding water?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Nutrients are retained and recycled efficiently among organisms. Exchanges within the coral–algae partnership and rapid reuse limit losses.
QuestionFocused recall

Why can removing grazing fish damage coral indirectly?
