Menstrual cycle

Text version

Menstrual-cycle hormones

Puberty

Reproductive hormones cause secondary sexual characteristics.

Oestrogen: mainly ovaries.

Testosterone: testes; stimulates sperm production.

Cycle length varies; 28 days is an example.

FSH

Pituitary produces follicle-stimulating hormone.

It promotes follicle/egg maturation and oestrogen production.

Oestrogen inhibits further FSH release.

FSH matures the egg; it does not trigger its release.

Oestrogen

Ovary produces it as follicle develops.

It rebuilds the uterine lining.

Inhibits FSH, but high mid-cycle levels stimulate LH release.

Hormones do not all peak together.

LH and ovulation

Pituitary releases luteinising hormone.

An LH surge triggers release of the egg.

This is ovulation, not fertilisation.

Look for peak near ovulation on a graph.

Progesterone

Ovary produces it after ovulation.

It maintains the uterine lining.

Inhibits FSH and LH.

If pregnancy does not occur, falling levels allow menstruation.

Follow the cycle

Day 1: menstruation starts. Follicle then matures.

Oestrogen rises, LH peaks, ovulation follows.

Progesterone rises after ovulation.

Interpret the supplied graph rather than assuming exact dates.
