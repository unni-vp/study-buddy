Motion & graphs

Describing motion

Average speed = total distance ÷ total time

At constant speed:

s = vt

  • s: distance, in m.
  • v: speed, in m/s.
  • t: time, in s.

Average velocity = displacement ÷ time

Velocity includes direction.

A runner travels 200 m in 40 s: average speed = 5.0 m/s. If the runner returns to the starting point, average velocity for the full journey is zero even though average speed is not.

Useful approximate speeds

These are estimates, not fixed properties. Terrain, fitness, traffic and conditions matter.

a = (v − u)/t

  • a: acceleration, in m/s².
  • u: initial velocity, in m/s.
  • v: final velocity, in m/s.
  • t: time, in s.

Example: a car increases its velocity from 5 to 17 m/s in 4 s.

a = (17 − 5) ÷ 4 = 3 m/s²

Slowing gives acceleration opposite to the motion.

For constant acceleration:

v² − u² = 2as

Example: an object starts from rest and accelerates at 2.0 m/s² over 25 m.

v = √(2 × 2.0 × 25) = 10 m/s

This equation is not for changing acceleration.

Moving in a circle at constant speed still changes velocity, because direction changes. A resultant force towards the centre is needed.

Average speed is not usually the average of two speeds

A cyclist travels 60 m at 3 m/s, then 60 m at 6 m/s.

  1. First time = 60 ÷ 3 = 20 s.
  2. Second time = 60 ÷ 6 = 10 s.
  3. Total distance = 120 m; total time = 30 s.
  4. Average speed = 120 ÷ 30 = 4 m/s, rather than 4.5 m/s.

The slower stage takes longer. A simple average of speeds works only in appropriate circumstances, such as equal time spent at each speed.

Reading motion graphs

GraphGradientArea
Distance–timeSpeedNot the distance travelled
Velocity–timeAccelerationSigned displacement
  • A flat distance–time line means stationary; a straight sloping line means constant speed.
  • On a curved distance–time graph, draw a tangent at the time and calculate its gradient to estimate instantaneous speed.
  • A flat velocity–time line means constant velocity, not necessarily rest.
  • For a velocity–time graph, find areas using rectangles and triangles or count squares. Area below the time axis is negative displacement. Add the magnitudes of areas for total distance.

Negative acceleration can mean speeding up

Choose right as positive. A trolley changes from −2 m/s to −6 m/s in 2 s.

a = (−6 − (−2)) ÷ 2 = −2 m/s²

It is moving left and becoming faster. Both velocity and acceleration point left. Negative acceleration therefore does not always mean slowing down. An object slows when acceleration points opposite to its velocity.

A displacement–time line can slope down as an object moves back towards the chosen origin. A graph of total distance travelled cannot decrease. Check exactly what “distance” labels in a question describe.
