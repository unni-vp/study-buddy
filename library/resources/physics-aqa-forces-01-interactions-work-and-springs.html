Interactions, work & springs

Contact or non-contact

  • Contact: friction, drag, tension, normal force. Non-contact: gravity, electrostatic, magnetic.
  • Scalar: speed, mass, energy. Vector: force, velocity, momentum. Arrow length and direction represent a vector.

Weight & components

  • W = mg, N = kg × N/kg; weight acts at centre of mass. Mass stays the same if g changes.
  • For angled forces, draw tip-to-tail at a stated scale. Diagonal = resultant; perpendicular components have the same combined effect.

Work done

  • W = Fs, distance along force. 40 N × 3 m = 120 J. Work against friction warms surfaces.
  • A stationary object needs more than one force to change shape: pulls at opposite ends, for example.

Hooke’s law

  • F = ke up to proportional limit. 6 N / 0.030 m = 200 N/m.
  • Elastic = recovers shape; inelastic = permanent. Losing proportionality need not immediately mean permanent deformation.

Spring practical

  • Record unloaded length; add known loads; let settle; read length at eye level.
  • Extension = loaded − original. F = mg includes hanger. Repeat; secure stand, avoid overload and falling masses.

Graph & stored energy

  • F vertical vs e horizontal: straight-line gradient = k. Unload to test elastic recovery.
  • Eₑ = ½ke², triangular area under proportional F–e graph. Do not use final force × extension.
