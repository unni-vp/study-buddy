Reaction energy & bond calculations

Bond energies

Breaking bonds requires energy. Energy must be supplied to overcome the attraction holding atoms together.

Making bonds releases energy. When atoms form new bonds in the products, energy transfers out.

Both happen during a reaction. Compare the total energy taken in with the total released to decide whether the overall reaction is exothermic or endothermic.

Overall energy change = energy to break reactant bonds − energy released forming product bonds.

Calculation resultMeaning
NegativeMore energy released than taken in: exothermic
PositiveMore energy taken in than released: endothermic

Worked example

H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl

Supplied bond energies, in kJ/mol of bonds:

  • H–H: 436.
  • Cl–Cl: 243.
  • H–Cl: 431.
  1. Break one H–H and one Cl–Cl: 436 + 243 = 679 kJ for the molar quantities in the equation.
  2. Form two H–Cl bonds: 2 × 431 = 862 kJ released.
  3. Overall change = 679 − 862 = −183 kJ for the equation as written.
  4. It is exothermic, because forming product bonds releases 183 kJ more than breaking reactant bonds takes in.

Use the values supplied in the question, even if they differ from another data table. Count every bond and include the equation coefficients. A molecule of methane, CH₄, contains four C–H bonds; two water molecules contain four O–H bonds altogether.

Find an unknown bond energy

For the same reaction, suppose overall change is −183 kJ and H–Cl bond energy is x:

  1. The reactant bonds require 679 kJ to break.
  2. Two H–Cl bonds form, releasing 2x.
  3. Use energy taken in minus energy released: 679 − 2x = −183.
  4. Rearrange to give 2x = 679 + 183 = 862.
  5. Divide by two: x = 431 kJ/mol.

The chemical bonds themselves do not release energy when broken. The release happens when the new bonds form.

Count energy, not just the number of bonds

Breaking two weak bonds can require less energy than breaking one very strong bond. Whether a reaction is exothermic depends on the total bond energies, not merely whether more bonds are formed than broken.

First balance the equation, then count bonds in the displayed molecules. For CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O, the reactants contain four C–H bonds and two O=O bonds. The products contain two C=O bonds and four O–H bonds. The coefficients multiply complete molecules and therefore their complete bond counts.
