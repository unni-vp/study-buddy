People and lifestyle

Introducing yourself and other people

This theme lets you explain who you are, how you live and what you want to do. Build a sentence like a train: the subject and verb form the engine, then details, reasons and contrasts make the journey longer.

Start with accurate short sentences. Add complexity when you can control it.

FrenchEnglish
Je m'appelle Shreya.My name is Shreya.
J'ai seize ans.I am sixteen.
J'habite en Angleterre.I live in England.
Je suis plutôt calme.I am fairly calm.
Ma sœur est très drôle.My sister is very funny.
Mon ami est gentil.My friend is kind.

Age uses avoir: j'ai seize ans, not je suis seize ans. Nationality and most descriptions use être.

Adjectives match the person described: gentil → gentille, sportif → sportive, patient → patiente. Plural descriptions usually add -s. An adjective ending in a silent -e often keeps the same feminine spelling.

Relationships and personality

Learn a small number of useful patterns, then change the people and reasons.

  • Je m'entends bien avec ... — I get on well with ...
  • Je me dispute avec ... — I argue with ...
  • Nous nous aidons. — We help one another.
  • Elle m'écoute quand j'ai un problème. — She listens to me when I have a problem.
  • Il est parfois impatient. — He is sometimes impatient.
  • On passe du temps ensemble. — We spend time together.

Reflexive verbs use a small extra pronoun: je me dispute, tu te disputes, elle se dispute. Before a vowel, me becomes m': je m'entends.

Possessives agree with the noun, not with the owner: mon frère, ma sœur, mes parents. Use mon before a feminine singular noun beginning with a vowel: mon amie.

Developing a relationship answer

Je m'entends bien avec ma sœur parce qu'elle m'écoute. Cependant, elle est parfois impatiente.

I get on well with my sister because she listens to me. However, she is sometimes impatient.

The reason explains the opinion; the contrast makes the relationship less one-dimensional. Do not describe every person with only sympa.

Opinions and changes over time

Useful opinion phrases:

  • À mon avis ... — In my opinion ...
  • Je trouve que ... — I find that ...
  • Je préfère ... — I prefer ...
  • Ce qui est important pour moi, c'est ... — What matters to me is ...

For a shorter controlled relative clause, use qui as the subject: J'ai une amie qui m'aide. — I have a friend who helps me.

Follow the time frame

TimeFrench exampleMeaning
NowJe passe du temps avec mes amis.I spend time with my friends.
Completed pastHier, j'ai aidé mon frère.Yesterday, I helped my brother.
Past habitQuand j'étais jeune, je jouais avec ma sœur.When I was young, I used to play with my sister.
Planned futureDemain, je vais rendre visite à ma grand-mère.Tomorrow, I am going to visit my grandmother.

A time phrase helps, but the verb must also fit. Hier je joue is not the same as hier j'ai joué.

Je mange des légumes tous les jours parce que c'est bon pour la santé.

I eat vegetables every day because it is good for your health.

Partitive articles describe an unspecified amount: du pain, de la soupe, de l'eau. After an amount, use de: beaucoup de légumes, trop de sucre.

After a negative, these usually become de/d': Je ne mange pas de viande. — I do not eat meat. Do not change the definite article used for a general preference: Je n'aime pas le café.

Habits, problems and improvements

  • Je fais du sport deux fois par semaine. — I exercise twice a week.
  • Je ne fume pas. — I do not smoke.
  • Je dors moins quand j'ai beaucoup de devoirs. — I sleep less when I have a lot of homework.
  • Il faut boire de l'eau. — You must drink water.
  • Je voudrais changer mes habitudes. — I would like to change my habits.

Il faut + infinitive states what is necessary. Pour + infinitive explains a purpose: je marche pour rester en forme — I walk to stay fit.

A developed sequence

Avant, je mangeais trop de sucre. La semaine dernière, j'ai commencé à manger plus de fruits. À l'avenir, je vais faire davantage d'exercice.

Before, I used to eat too much sugar. Last week, I started eating more fruit. In future, I am going to exercise more.

The imperfect describes a past habit; the perfect gives a completed action; aller + infinitive gives a future plan. Keep each change relevant to the question.

School life and opinions

Describe your school, subjects, routine and rules. French-speaking school systems do not always use the same names as British ones.

  • mon collège / mon lycée — my school; these name different stages in France.
  • une matière — a school subject.
  • les devoirs — homework.
  • un examen — an examination.
  • les règles — rules.
  • un professeur — a teacher.

Ma matière préférée, c'est la biologie parce qu'elle est intéressante.

My favourite subject is biology because it is interesting.

Je préfère les sciences aux langues. — I prefer sciences to languages.

Les cours commencent à neuf heures. — Lessons start at nine o'clock. The plural subject requires commencent, not commence.

Rules and a balanced view

Il faut arriver à l'heure. Je trouve cette règle raisonnable parce qu'elle nous aide à travailler. Cependant, certains élèves la trouvent difficile.

You have to arrive on time. I find this rule reasonable because it helps us work. However, some pupils find it difficult.

La replaces the feminine noun règle. A reason is more useful than a long list of unrelated rules.

Give a reason that belongs to the subject

J'aime les langues parce que je peux parler avec des personnes d'autres pays.

I like languages because I can speak with people from other countries.

This reason explains what makes languages useful. C'est intéressant is correct, but a more specific reason gives you something to develop. Keep the agreement clear: les langues sont utiles, while le français est utile.

For rules, distinguish a requirement from an opinion:

MeaningFrenchWhat the grammar does
We must wear a uniform.Nous devons porter un uniforme.Devons agrees with nous; porter stays in the infinitive.
I find the uniform practical.Je trouve l'uniforme pratique.The sentence evaluates the requirement.
I would prefer to wear my own clothes.Je préférerais porter mes vêtements.The conditional expresses a preference rather than a current fact.

A balanced answer can explain why a rule is useful and why you dislike it. Those are different ideas, so connect them with mais or cependant.

Work, ambitions and future plans

  • un emploi / un travail — a job / work.
  • travailler à l'étranger — to work abroad.
  • gagner de l'argent — to earn money.
  • aider les autres — to help others.
  • continuer mes études — to continue my studies.
  • apprendre un métier — to learn a trade or profession.

Je voudrais travailler dans un hôpital parce que j'aime aider les autres.

I would like to work in a hospital because I like helping others.

Après mes examens, je vais continuer mes études. Plus tard, je travaillerai à l'étranger.

After my exams, I am going to continue my studies. Later, I will work abroad.

Travaillerai is the simple future: travailler + -ai. Je voudrais expresses a wish; je vais travailler expresses a plan. Choose the meaning you intend.

A job title after être or devenir normally has no indefinite article: Elle est médecin. — She is a doctor. Follow any noun/adjective agreement needed in the phrase you choose.

Connect an ambition to a quality

Je voudrais travailler avec des enfants parce que je suis patient(e).

I would like to work with children because I am patient.

Je préfère travailler dehors, mais je voudrais aussi un emploi stable.

I prefer working outdoors, but I would also like a stable job.

The quality or condition gives the ambition a reason. You can then describe a next step: Après mes examens, je vais continuer mes études. A wish, a definite plan and a completed experience have different meanings; choose the form that answers the question.

Turning ideas into a developed response

A useful pattern is detail + reason + example or contrast. Think of adding branches to an idea, rather than stacking unrelated sentences.

J'aime mon collège parce que les professeurs sont patients. Par exemple, mon professeur de français m'aide quand je ne comprends pas. Cependant, les devoirs sont parfois difficiles.

I like my school because the teachers are patient. For example, my French teacher helps me when I do not understand. However, homework is sometimes difficult.

Useful connections

  • parce que — because.
  • mais / cependant — but / however.
  • par exemple — for example.
  • quand — when.
  • donc — therefore / so.
  • à l'avenir — in future.

Check the essentials

  • Answer the requested person, topic and time frame.
  • Include the verb, not only nouns and adjectives.
  • Match adjectives and articles to their nouns.
  • Keep both parts of written negatives: ne ... pas, ne ... jamais.
  • Use j'ai + past participle for completed past actions where avoir is required.
  • After a modal or aller in the near future, use the infinitive: je veux travailler, je vais étudier.

For a longer answer, develop the required points. Do not force a memorised health or school paragraph into a question about a different aspect of your life.
