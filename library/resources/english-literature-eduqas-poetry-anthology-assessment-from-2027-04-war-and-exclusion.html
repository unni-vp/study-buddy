War & exclusion

Drummer Hodge: the idea

  • Hardy names a young drummer hurriedly buried without a coffin. He will become part of land he never understood.
  • Local landscape terms and strange stars separate him from Wessex. Natural continuity does not cancel loss.

Hodge: the method

  • Three six-line stanzas; alternating rhyme. Present burial → past home → permanent future.
  • Second Boer War, southern Africa. Ordinary loss challenges glamorous imperial war; it is not a First World War setting.

Disabled: the idea

  • Owen contrasts a veteran’s present dependence with sport, attraction and recruitment enthusiasm.
  • Colourful memory meets grey isolation. The public that celebrated enlistment offers little meaningful welcome.

Disabled: the method

  • Past/present shifts deny a simple heroic journey. Repeated final questioning leaves the man waiting.
  • First World War context exposes recruitment glamour and inadequate care. Analyse society’s treatment, not a claim about every disabled life.

Kamikaze: the idea

  • Garland’s returning pilot survives a suicide mission but is shunned. Sea life and childhood memories offer possible reasons for returning.
  • His daughter reconstructs his thoughts; the pilot never gives us his own account.

Kamikaze: the method

  • Seven six-line stanzas. Layered voices and fluid enjambment give way to learned silence at home.
  • Second World War military honour conflicts with family affection. Compare social rejection with Disabled and irreversible loss with Hodge.
