Understand and infer

Reading for the question

Reading is like looking through a map: first locate the relevant area, then check the precise detail. Do not translate a whole passage before looking at its questions.

The paper lasts one hour and has 50 marks: comprehension is 40 and French-to-English translation is 10. Comprehension answers are in English or use the requested non-verbal response. Dictionaries are not allowed.

  1. Read the question and identify who, what, where, when or why.
  2. Find the matching part of the text.
  3. Check the subject, verb, time phrase, negative and qualifying words.
  4. Answer the exact request, including both details if it asks for two.

Léa aime le cinéma, mais son frère préfère le sport. — Léa likes cinema, but her brother prefers sport. A question about the brother needs his preference, not Léa's.

Opinions, changes and reference

Avant, je trouvais les maths difficiles. Maintenant, j'aime cette matière parce que le professeur explique bien.

Before, I found maths difficult. Now, I like this subject because the teacher explains well. The opinion changes: difficult is the earlier view; a positive view and its reason are current.

Track pronouns: Ma sœur aime la lecture. Elle lit souvent. Elle refers to the sister. Les livres sont chers, mais je les achète. Les replaces the books.

Distinguish fact from opinion: il y a trois magasins states a number; les magasins sont agréables expresses a judgement. For a mixed view, preserve both sides.

Unfamiliar words and inference

Use several clues rather than the first guess:

  • Context: what situation is being described?
  • Grammar: is the word a person, action, description or manner?
  • Word family: does its base or ending help?
  • Logic: does the proposed meaning fit the complete sentence?

Cette solution est impossible. Knowing possible and the opposite prefix im- helps you infer impossible.

Elle répond rapidement. Rapide means quick; -ment gives the adverb quickly.

Les consommateurs achètent moins de produits. Consommer means consume; consommateur refers to a consumer. The surrounding achats help check the inference.

A cognate looks like an English word and shares its meaning, such as important. A false friend looks familiar but differs: actuellement means currently; une librairie is a bookshop. Familiar spelling is a clue, not proof.

Use any glossary supplied beside a text. If one word remains unclear, continue with the information you can establish; do not invent a whole interpretation from it.
