Calculations & statistics

Numerical and statistical skills

Averages and spread

  • Mean: total of all values ÷ number of values.
  • Median: middle value after ordering the data.
  • Mode: most frequent value.
  • Modal class: group containing the greatest frequency.
  • Range: highest − lowest.
  • Interquartile range: upper quartile − lower quartile; it measures the spread of the middle 50%.

Worked example: 2, 3, 3, 6, 11

  • Mean = 25 ÷ 5 = 5
  • Median = 3
  • Mode = 3
  • Range = 11 − 2 = 9

The mean is raised by the extreme value 11, so the median may better represent a typical value.

Percentages

Percentage = part ÷ whole × 100

Percentage change = change ÷ original value × 100

Example: traffic rises from 80 to 100 vehicles. Change = 20. Percentage increase = 20 ÷ 80 × 100 = 25%.

Interpolation and extrapolation

  • Interpolation estimates within the known data range; it is usually safer.
  • Extrapolation estimates beyond the known range; it is less reliable because the trend may change.

A calculation in clear steps

Suppose a village's population rises from 800 to 1,000.

  1. Find the increase: 1,000 − 800 = 200 people.
  2. Divide by the starting population: 200 ÷ 800 = 0.25.
  3. Multiply by 100: 0.25 × 100 = 25% increase.

Dividing by the final value would answer a different question. If a share rises from 20% to 30%, it rises by 10 percentage points, but the percentage increase is 50%.

Using a sample and handling unusual values

A sample estimates a wider population. A large sample is not automatically representative: interviewing many people at only one shopping centre may still miss other groups.

Check an unusual reading against the recording sheet and conditions. Correct a proven transcription mistake, but do not delete a genuine anomaly merely because it weakens the expected trend. A median is less affected by extreme values than a mean, while the range is strongly affected by them.
