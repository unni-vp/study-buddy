QuestionEnzymes

How does the lock-and-key model explain enzyme action?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • A substrate with a complementary shape binds to the enzyme's active site.
  • Products form and leave; the enzyme is unchanged and can be reused.
QuestionEnzymes

How does temperature affect enzyme activity?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • At low temperature, particles have less kinetic energy, so there are fewer successful collisions.
  • Rate rises towards an optimum.
  • Above the optimum, the active site changes shape and the enzyme becomes denatured.
QuestionEnzymes

How does pH affect enzyme activity?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Each enzyme has an optimum pH.
  • A pH too far from the optimum can change the active site's shape, so the substrate no longer fits as well.

QuestionEnzymes

How does bile help lipid digestion?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Bile is alkaline, so it neutralises stomach acid entering the small intestine.
  • It emulsifies fat into small droplets, increasing the surface area available to lipase.
  • Bile is not an enzyme.

What are two tests for lipids?

Answer

QuestionFood tests and practical

Why use separate samples and positive and negative controls in food tests?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Separate samples prevent one reagent affecting another test.
  • A known positive shows the test can produce the expected result.
  • Distilled water provides a negative comparison.
QuestionFood tests and practical

Outline the amylase and pH required practical.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Mix measured starch and buffer, then add amylase and start timing.
  • Every 30 seconds, place a fresh sample onto a fresh iodine drop.
  • Record when iodine stays orange-brown, showing no starch is detected.
  • Repeat at several pH values and calculate means.
QuestionFood tests and practical

What variables should be controlled in the amylase and pH practical?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Temperature, enzyme and starch concentrations, solution volumes and sampling interval.
  • Only pH should be changed.
QuestionFood tests and practical

How is relative rate calculated in the amylase practical?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • For the same starting amount of starch, relative rate = 1 ÷ time.
  • A shorter time means a faster reaction.

QuestionHeart and circulation

What do atria, ventricles, valves and the septum do?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Atria receive blood; ventricles pump blood out.
  • Valves prevent backflow.
  • The septum separates the oxygenated and deoxygenated sides.
QuestionHeart and circulation

Why is the left ventricle wall thicker than the right ventricle wall?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The left ventricle pumps blood around the whole body at higher pressure.
  • The right ventricle only pumps blood to the nearby lungs.
QuestionHeart and circulation

What do the coronary arteries and natural pacemaker do?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Coronary arteries supply the heart muscle with oxygen and glucose.
  • The natural pacemaker in the right atrium controls the heartbeat; an artificial pacemaker can correct an irregular rhythm.
QuestionHeart and circulation

What is the cardiac output equation?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Cardiac output = heart rate × stroke volume.
  • Stroke volume is the amount of blood pumped by the heart in each beat.
QuestionBlood vessels and lungs

How are arteries, veins and capillaries adapted to their functions?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Arteries have thick muscular and elastic walls for high pressure.
  • Veins have a wide lumen and valves for low-pressure return to the heart.
  • Capillaries have one-cell-thick walls and large networks for rapid exchange.
QuestionBlood vessels and lungs

Why do artery and vein not always mean oxygenated and deoxygenated?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Arteries carry blood away from the heart; veins carry blood towards it.
  • The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood and the pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood.
QuestionBlood vessels and lungs

How is a red blood cell adapted to carry oxygen?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Haemoglobin binds oxygen.
  • Its biconcave shape gives a large surface area.
  • It has no nucleus, leaving more space for haemoglobin.

QuestionBlood vessels and lungs

How are alveoli adapted for gas exchange?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Many alveoli give a large surface area.
  • Moist, thin walls give a short diffusion distance.
  • Ventilation and blood flow maintain steep concentration gradients.
  • Oxygen diffuses into blood; carbon dioxide diffuses into alveoli.
QuestionHeart disease and health

How does coronary heart disease affect the heart?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Fatty deposits narrow coronary arteries.
  • Less oxygen reaches heart muscle, reducing aerobic respiration.
  • A complete blockage can cause a heart attack.
QuestionHeart disease and health

Compare stents and statins as treatments for coronary heart disease.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • A stent rapidly holds an artery open but has procedure and clot risks.
  • A statin lowers cholesterol and slows deposit formation but needs long-term use and may cause side effects.
QuestionHeart disease and health

What are important benefits and risks of replacement valves, donor hearts and artificial hearts?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Replacement valves restore one-way flow but require surgery and may wear out or need anticoagulants.
  • Donor hearts can restore pumping but are scarce and risk rejection.
  • Artificial hearts support circulation but carry infection, clot and equipment risks.

QuestionPlant tissues and transport

What do xylem and phloem transport?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Xylem carries water and mineral ions from roots towards leaves.
  • Phloem translocates dissolved sugars from sources such as leaves to growing or storage tissues.
QuestionPlant tissues and transport

What is transpiration?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Water evaporates from moist mesophyll surfaces and water vapour diffuses out through stomata.
  • This water loss helps pull a continuous stream of water up the xylem.
QuestionPlant tissues and transport

How do temperature, air movement, humidity and light usually affect transpiration?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Higher temperature and more air movement usually increase transpiration.
  • Higher humidity decreases it by reducing the water-vapour gradient.
  • Greater light often increases it because stomata open for gas exchange.
QuestionPlant tissues and transport

What does a potometer measure, and how is bubble movement rate calculated?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • A potometer measures water uptake, which estimates transpiration.
  • Bubble movement rate = distance ÷ time.
  • For volume uptake, use capillary volume πr² × distance, then divide by time.
QuestionPlant tissues and transport

Why must a potometer be airtight, and why is the shoot cut under water?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Leaks would make bubble movement unrelated to water uptake.
  • Cutting under water prevents air entering and blocking the xylem.
