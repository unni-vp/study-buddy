QuestionExplain and compare

Why does a gas usually have much lower density than a liquid of the same substance?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Gas particles are much farther apart.
  • The same mass occupies a much larger volume, so mass per unit volume is lower.

QuestionExplain and compare

Explain why mass can decrease in an open beaker when water evaporates without violating conservation of mass.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Water particles escape into the surroundings as vapour.
  • Total mass including the escaped vapour is conserved; particles are not destroyed.

QuestionApply and calculate

A 0.25 kg sample has specific heat capacity 800 J/kg°C. Calculate the energy needed to warm it from 20°C to 50°C.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Δθ = 50 − 20 = 30°C.
  • ΔE = mcΔθ = 0.25 × 800 × 30.
  • 6,000 J.

QuestionApply and calculate

A sealed gas occupies 40 cm³ at 120 kPa. It expands to 60 cm³ at constant temperature. Calculate its new pressure.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • p₁V₁ = p₂V₂.
  • p₂ = 120 × 40/60.
  • 80 kPa.
QuestionApply and calculate

Explain why heating a fixed mass of gas in a rigid sealed container increases its pressure.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Average kinetic energy increases, so particles move faster.
  • Wall collisions occur more frequently and involve larger momentum changes.
  • The force per unit area increases, so pressure rises.
QuestionApply and calculate

A bicycle pump becomes warm during rapid compression. Explain why.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • A force moves the piston, doing work on the gas.
  • Energy transfers into the gas’s internal energy.
  • With little time for energy to escape, average particle kinetic energy and temperature increase.
QuestionApply and calculate

Explain why internal energy increases while ice melts even though its temperature is constant.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Energy is transferred into the ice by heating.
  • It increases particle potential energy as the arrangement and interactions change.
  • Average kinetic energy does not increase, so temperature stays constant.

QuestionMulti-step application

A gas is compressed slowly at constant temperature from 100 cm³ to 25 cm³. Its initial pressure is 90 kPa. Calculate the new pressure and explain the increase using particles.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • p₂ = p₁V₁/V₂ = 90 × 100/25 = 360 kPa.
  • Average particle kinetic energy stays the same because temperature is constant.
  • Particles collide with the walls more frequently per unit area in the smaller volume.
  • Greater force per unit area means greater pressure.
QuestionMulti-step application

A 200 W heater melts 0.20 kg of ice at 0°C and warms the water to 10°C. Use L = 334,000 J/kg and c = 4,200 J/kg°C. Calculate the minimum heating time.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Melting energy = mL = 0.20 × 334,000 = 66,800 J.
  • Warming energy = mcΔθ = 0.20 × 4,200 × 10 = 8,400 J.
  • Total energy = 75,200 J.
  • t = E/P = 75,200/200 = 376 s; real heat losses increase the time.
