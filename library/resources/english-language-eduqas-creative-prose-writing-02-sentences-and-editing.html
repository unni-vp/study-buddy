Sentences, punctuation and editing

Choose precise words

  • Specific nouns and verbs: searched a pocket twice gives more than very worried.
  • Use vocabulary accurately. More adjectives and longer words do not automatically improve a sentence.

Vary for meaning

  • Short emphasis: isolate a deadline or discovery. Longer sentence: connect action, thought and consequence.
  • Choose the effect in context; do not force every sentence to be short or elaborate.

Secure the boundaries

  • Comma splice: two complete clauses joined only by a comma. Use a full stop, conjunction or suitable semicolon.
  • Colon: after a complete clause, introduce an explanation or detail.

Control the dialogue

  • New speaker, new paragraph. Put the question mark or comma where the spoken words require it.
  • Keep dialogue tags and surrounding narrative grammatically connected.

Keep the viewpoint steady

  • Tense and reference: check who is acting and when. Signal memories and return to the main timeline.
  • Check agreement, pronouns, apostrophes, its/it’s and common spellings.

Read twice to edit

  • Meaning pass: prompt, development, sequence, viewpoint and ending.
  • Accuracy pass: sentences, punctuation and spelling. Remove repetition rather than adding decoration.
