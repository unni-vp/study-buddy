Cells & fuel cells

Simple cells

  • Two different metals in electrolyte, connected externally. Chemical reaction supplies electrical energy.
  • Voltage depends on electrodes and electrolyte. Keep electrolyte/concentration and temperature fixed when comparing metals.
  • Use lead polarity and reference convention to interpret reactivity data.

Batteries & recharge

  • Series voltages add: 3 × 1.5 V = 4.5 V. Battery = two or more cells.
  • Non-rechargeable, e.g. alkaline: stops when reactant is used up.
  • Rechargeable: external current reverses chemical reactions. Compare mass, capacity, lifetime cost and recharge time.

Continuous fuel supply

  • External hydrogen and oxygen/air feed the cell. 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O. Hydrogen oxidised; oxygen reduced.
  • Electrons travel through external circuit/load; ions through electrolyte.
  • Continues while supplied with reactants; refill fuel rather than reverse reaction to recharge.

Acidic half-equations

  • Hydrogen: 2H₂ → 4H⁺ + 4e⁻.
  • Oxygen: O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O.
  • Add and cancel H⁺/electrons. Balance atoms and charge. Fuel cell generates current; electrolysis requires a power supply.

If electrolyte is alkaline

  • Hydrogen: 2H₂ + 4OH⁻ → 4H₂O + 4e⁻.
  • Oxygen: O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻.
  • Sum gives same overall reaction. Use the electrolyte specified; do not mix acidic and alkaline half-equations.

Fuel cell vs rechargeable

  • Fuel: water during use, quick refuelling possible; hydrogen storage, flammability and infrastructure matter.
  • Battery: recharge from grid, no exhaust; mass, charge time, finite life and materials matter.
  • Check hydrogen production/electricity sources, efficiency and manufacture. Neither is automatically zero-carbon overall.
