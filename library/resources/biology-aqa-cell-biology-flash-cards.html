QuestionCell structures

What is the difference between a eukaryotic cell and a prokaryotic cell?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • A eukaryotic cell has its genetic material enclosed inside a nucleus.
  • A prokaryotic cell is smaller and has no nucleus; its main DNA loop is free in the cytoplasm.
QuestionCell structures

What are the functions of the nucleus, cytoplasm and cell membrane?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Nucleus: contains genetic material and controls the cell's activities.
  • Cytoplasm: where many enzyme-controlled chemical reactions occur.
  • Cell membrane: controls movement of substances into and out of the cell.
QuestionCell structures

What are the functions of mitochondria and ribosomes?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Mitochondria are the site of aerobic respiration, which transfers energy for cell processes.
  • Ribosomes are the site of protein synthesis.
QuestionCell structures

Which structures are found in plant cells but not animal cells?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • A cellulose cell wall and a permanent vacuole.
  • Photosynthetic plant cells also contain chloroplasts.
  • Root hair cells normally have no chloroplasts because they are underground and do not photosynthesise.
QuestionCell structures

What are the functions of the cell wall, chloroplasts and permanent vacuole?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Cell wall: strengthens and supports the plant cell.
  • Chloroplasts: contain chlorophyll; they are the site of photosynthesis.
  • Permanent vacuole: contains cell sap and helps keep the cell firm when filled with water.

QuestionMicroscopy

Why can an electron microscope reveal more detail than a light microscope?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • It has greater magnification and greater resolving power.
  • This allows much smaller structures to be distinguished.
QuestionMicroscopy

What is the magnification equation?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Magnification = image size ÷ actual size.
  • Image size and actual size must be in the same units.
  • Magnification has no unit.
QuestionMicroscopy

How do you convert between millimetres, micrometres and nanometres?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • mm → µm: multiply by 1,000.
  • µm → nm: multiply by 1,000.
  • Reverse either conversion by dividing by 1,000.
QuestionMicroscopy

What are the key steps when using a light microscope?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Start with the lowest-power objective and centre the specimen.
  • Use coarse focus, then fine focus.
  • Increase magnification and use fine focus again.
  • Total microscope magnification = eyepiece × objective.

  • A suitable stain improves contrast, making structures easier to see.

QuestionCulturing microorganisms

How do bacteria reproduce, and what shape does their population growth follow?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Bacteria reproduce by binary fission: one cell divides into two.
  • Repeated doubling gives exponential growth, such as 1 → 2 → 4 → 8.
QuestionCulturing microorganisms

What equation calculates a bacterial population after repeated doubling?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Final population = starting population × 2ⁿ.
  • n = total time ÷ division time.
  • Convert both times to the same unit first.

QuestionCulturing microorganisms

Give four aseptic actions and their reasons.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Sterilise equipment and agar to remove unwanted microorganisms.
  • Cool a sterilised inoculating loop so it does not kill the transferred bacteria.

QuestionCell division and stem cells

What happens during the three main stages of the cell cycle?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • 1. The cell grows, increases sub-cellular structures and copies its DNA.
  • 2. During mitosis, chromosome copies separate and the nucleus divides.
  • 3. The cytoplasm and membrane divide to form two cells.
QuestionCell division and stem cells

What does mitosis produce, and why is it useful?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Two genetically identical daughter cells with the same chromosome number as the parent cell.
  • Mitosis supports growth, repair and replacement of cells.
QuestionCell division and stem cells

What happens to chromosomes before and during mitosis?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • DNA is copied before mitosis, creating two joined copies of each chromosome.
  • During mitosis, the joined copies separate and one complete set moves to each end of the cell.
  • The chromosome number is maintained, not halved.

  • Adult bone-marrow stem cells form a more limited range, including blood cells.
  • Plant meristem cells can form any type of plant cell throughout the plant's life.

Give an exam-ready definition of diffusion.

Answer
  • The net movement of particles from an area of higher concentration to an area of lower concentration, down a concentration gradient.
QuestionTransport

Which four factors can increase the rate of diffusion?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • A steeper concentration gradient.
  • A higher temperature.
  • A larger surface area.
  • A shorter diffusion distance.
QuestionTransport

Give an exam-ready definition of osmosis.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The net movement of water molecules from a dilute solution to a more concentrated solution, through a partially permeable membrane.
QuestionTransport

What happens to plant and animal cells in very dilute solutions?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Water enters a plant cell by osmosis; it becomes turgid, and the cell wall prevents bursting.
  • An animal cell may swell and burst because it has no cell wall.
QuestionTransport

What happens to plant and animal cells in concentrated solutions?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Water leaves both cells by osmosis.
  • A plant cell becomes flaccid and may become plasmolysed.
  • An animal cell shrinks.
QuestionTransport

Give an exam-ready definition of active transport.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The movement of substances from lower to higher concentration, against the concentration gradient, using energy transferred by respiration.
QuestionTransport

Give two examples of active transport.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Root hair cells absorb mineral ions from dilute soil into the cell.
  • Small-intestine cells absorb sugars into the blood when the concentration in the gut is lower than in the blood.
QuestionTransport

Compare diffusion, osmosis and active transport.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Diffusion: particles move down a concentration gradient; no respiration energy required.
  • Osmosis: water moves through a partially permeable membrane from dilute to more concentrated solution; no respiration energy required.
  • Active transport: substances move against a concentration gradient using energy from respiration.
QuestionExchange surfaces

Why do large multicellular organisms need specialised exchange surfaces and transport systems?

Answer

  • Exchange through the outer surface alone would be too slow to meet the cells' needs.
QuestionExchange surfaces

What features make an exchange surface efficient?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • A large surface area.
  • A thin surface for a short diffusion distance.
  • A good blood supply and ventilation to maintain steep concentration gradients.
QuestionExchange surfaces

How do you calculate the surface-area-to-volume ratio of a cube?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Surface area = 6a² and volume = a³, where a is side length.
  • Write the ratio as surface area : volume and simplify it.
  • As a cube gets larger, its surface-area-to-volume ratio decreases.

QuestionOsmosis practical

Outline the required practical investigating osmosis in plant tissue.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Measure equal-size potato cylinders and record each initial mass.
  • Place them in equal volumes of different solution concentrations for the same time and temperature.
  • Blot consistently, measure final mass, calculate percentage mass change, repeat and calculate means.
QuestionOsmosis practical

What is the percentage mass change equation?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Percentage mass change = (final mass − initial mass) ÷ initial mass × 100.
  • Keep the sign: a mass loss gives a negative result.
QuestionOsmosis practical

What do positive, negative and zero percentage mass changes mean?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Positive: net water gain; the surrounding solution was more dilute.
  • Negative: net water loss; the surrounding solution was more concentrated.
  • Zero: no net movement of water, although water molecules still move both ways.
QuestionOsmosis practical

How can a graph estimate the concentration inside potato cells?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Plot solution concentration against mean percentage mass change.
  • Read the x-value where the best-fit line crosses 0% mass change.
  • At this concentration there is no net movement of water.
QuestionOsmosis practical

Why are percentage change, blotting and repeats important in the osmosis practical?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Percentage change allows fair comparison when starting masses differ.
  • Blotting removes surface solution that would falsely increase mass.
  • Repeats help identify anomalies and allow a mean to be calculated.
