States & particle models

States and explaining properties

StateParticle arrangement and movement
SolidClose together; vibrate around fixed positions
LiquidClose together but can move past one another
GasFar apart; move rapidly and randomly

Heating transfers energy to particles. Melting and boiling require enough energy to overcome the forces holding particles in place or close together. Freezing and condensing transfer energy to the surroundings.

At the stated pressure:

  • Below the melting point: the substance is solid.
  • Between melting and boiling points: it is liquid.
  • Above the boiling point: it is gas.
  • During a state change, two states can coexist. The particles do not themselves melt, grow or become liquid particles.
  • State symbols: (s) solid, (l) liquid, (g) gas, (aq) dissolved in water. Aqueous is not the same as molten.
  • Sphere models leave out forces, internal structure and the real shapes of particles. They cannot fully explain why different substances change state at different temperatures.

Structure → force → energy or movement

StructureMelting and boilingElectrical conduction
Giant ionicHigh: many strong attractions between opposite ions need much energy to overcomeSolid: no, ions fixed. Molten or dissolved: yes, ions can move and carry charge
Small moleculesUsually low: weak intermolecular forces need little energy to overcomeUsually no mobile charged particles
Giant covalentVery high: many strong covalent bonds must be brokenUsually no free charged particles; graphite/graphene are exceptions
MetallicUsually high: strong attraction between positive ions and delocalised electronsDelocalised electrons move through the structure and carry charge

For small molecular substances, melting or boiling overcomes intermolecular forces, not covalent bonds inside the molecules. Larger molecules generally have stronger intermolecular forces and higher boiling points.

Identify the moving charge carrier

SampleCan it conduct?Reason
Solid sodium chlorideNo.Its charged ions cannot move freely through the lattice.
Molten sodium chlorideYes.Its ions can move and carry charge.
A piece of copperYes.Delocalised electrons move while the metal ions remain in the structure.
Liquid water in the simplified molecular modelVery poorly when pure.Water molecules have no overall charge and the pure liquid contains very few mobile ions.
GraphiteYes.Delocalised electrons move through its layered structure.

“The particles can move” is not enough: name the charged particles. Molecules in many liquids and gases move freely but do not therefore conduct well.
