Electromagnetic waves

Choosing electromagnetic waves

WaveUsesWhy it is suitable
RadioRadio and television broadcastsCarries information over long distances; can be generated and detected by electrical circuits
MicrowaveSatellite communication; cookingSuitable frequencies pass through the atmosphere; food absorbs energy and heats up
InfraredElectric heaters, cooking, thermal camerasAbsorbed radiation transfers energy to thermal stores; warm objects emit IR that a detector can measure
VisibleFibre-optic communicationLight carries information along fibres; suitable fibres limit energy loss
UltravioletEnergy-efficient fluorescent lamps; sun tanningCan make a phosphor emit visible light; affects skin pigment, with health risks
X-rayMedical imagingSoft tissue transmits more than dense bone, creating contrast
GammaImaging with suitable tracers; cancer treatmentPenetrating radiation can be detected outside a body; carefully targeted doses damage cancer cells

Choose the wave using absorption, transmission, penetration and risk, not its name alone. Different wavelengths interact differently with the same substance.

Radio waves and circuits: alternating electrical currents produce radio waves. Absorption by a receiving circuit can induce an alternating current at the wave's frequency. Changes in atoms can emit or absorb electromagnetic radiation. Gamma rays originate from changes in atomic nuclei.
