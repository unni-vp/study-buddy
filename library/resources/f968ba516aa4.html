Physical and human fieldwork questions & answers

1 mark2 marks3 marks4 marks6 marks

1 mark

1. State the Liverpool fieldwork enquiry question.

Answer:

How far does environmental quality change across Liverpool town centre?

2. State the River Alyn fieldwork enquiry question.

Answer:

How do river characteristics change downstream along the River Alyn?

3. Which sampling method collected data at regular points along a route?

Answer:

Systematic sampling.

4. Name one River Alyn characteristic that was measured.

Answer:

Channel width.

5. Name one indicator used in Liverpool's environmental-quality survey.

Answer:

Litter.

6. What is reliability?

Answer:

Whether repeating a method gives similar results.

2 marks

7. Give two types of data collected in Liverpool.

Answer:
  • Environmental-quality scores.
  • Pedestrian counts.

8. Explain why pedestrian counts were completed for a set time.

Answer:
  • A consistent duration makes results from different sites comparable.
  • It avoids a site appearing busier simply because it was observed for longer.

9. Give two reported River Alyn downstream changes.

Answer:
  • The channel generally became wider and deeper.
  • Bedload became smaller and rounder.

10. Explain why several depth readings are taken across a channel.

Answer:
  • River beds are uneven, so one reading may be unrepresentative.
  • Several readings allow a mean depth to be calculated.

11. Explain the difference between quantitative and qualitative data.

Answer:
  • Quantitative data is numerical, such as a pedestrian count.
  • Qualitative data is descriptive, such as an annotated field sketch.

12. Give one strength and one limitation of an environmental-quality survey.

Answer:
  • Strength: it turns several observations into comparable site scores.
  • Limitation: scores may be subjective and vary between observers.

3 marks

13. Explain how the Liverpool environmental-quality data was collected.

Answer:
  • At each site, factors including litter, graffiti, noise, traffic, building condition and attractiveness were assessed.
  • Each factor was given a score using the survey scale.
  • Scores were added to produce a total that could be compared between sites.

14. Explain why field sketches and land-use maps were useful in Liverpool.

Answer:
  • They recorded the location of shops, services and other buildings.
  • They showed the character and function of each site.
  • This helped relate environmental quality and pedestrian flow to land use.

15. Explain why bedload may become smaller and rounder downstream.

Answer:
  • Stones are transported for longer distances downstream.
  • They collide with each other and the river bed.
  • Attrition chips edges, making them smaller and rounder.

16. Explain why River Alyn velocity tended to increase downstream.

Answer:
  • Tributaries and runoff add water, increasing discharge.
  • The channel becomes larger and usually more efficient.
  • Proportionally less water is slowed by contact with the bed and banks.

4 marks

17. Explain the main conclusion from the Liverpool investigation.

Answer:
  • Environmental quality varied across the sampled parts of the town centre.
  • Central shopping areas generally had the highest quality scores.
  • These areas also had high pedestrian flow and concentrated retail and services.
  • Quieter streets away from the centre generally had lower scores and fewer pedestrians.

18. Explain the main conclusion from the River Alyn investigation.

Answer:
  • Width and depth generally increased downstream.
  • Velocity also tended to increase as the channel became more efficient.
  • Bedload became smaller and rounder through transport and attrition.
  • The overall pattern matched river theory, although local conditions may have affected individual sites.

19. Explain how the reliability of pedestrian-flow data could be improved.

Answer:
  • Repeat counts at each site rather than relying on one result.
  • Use the same count duration and clear counting rules.
  • Repeat at comparable times or days to reduce temporary effects.
  • Calculate a mean so one unusual count has less influence.

20. Explain how the River Alyn investigation could improve its bedload sample.

Answer:
  • Use the same systematic selection rule at every site.
  • Measure a larger number of stones.
  • Use the same equipment and roundness chart consistently.
  • Calculate a mean size and compare the distribution rather than one stone.

6 marks

21. Evaluate the Liverpool fieldwork methods.

Answer:

Systematic sites along a route gave coverage and made busier and quieter areas comparable. Pedestrian counts produced numerical data, while land-use maps and field sketches added context. The environmental-quality survey compared several indicators with a single total. However, quality scoring can be subjective, and a count at one time may not represent other days. The supplied account does not include the interval, number of sites, repeats or raw results, so confidence must be cautious. Clear scoring guidance, trained observers, repeated timed counts and more representative site coverage would strengthen reliability and validity.

22. Evaluate the River Alyn fieldwork methods.

Answer:

Measuring width, mean depth, velocity and bedload at several downstream sites allowed river theory to be tested using varied evidence. Consistent sites and methods support comparison. However, channel shape is irregular, velocity changes across a channel and one bedload sample may be unrepresentative. The exact velocity equipment, sampling interval, repeats and raw data are not supplied, so these should not be assumed. More depth points, repeated velocity readings, a larger systematic bedload sample and a recorded fixed procedure would reduce random variation and make the conclusion more dependable.

23. Compare the Liverpool and River Alyn enquiries.

Answer:

Both investigations used several sites and systematic sampling to look for spatial change. Both combined numerical data with observations and needed consistent methods for fair comparison. Liverpool investigated a human environment across a town centre using quality scores, pedestrian flow and land use. River Alyn investigated a physical downstream pattern using width, depth, velocity and bedload. Liverpool's quality scores risk observer subjectivity, while river measurements risk natural short-term variation and equipment error. In both, repetition and a clear sampling rule would strengthen the evidence.

24. Assess how far the River Alyn results support downstream river theory.

Answer:

The reported results broadly support theory: width and depth generally increased, velocity tended to rise, and bedload became smaller and rounder downstream. Greater discharge and a more efficient channel can explain the size and velocity changes, while attrition and continued transport explain bedload change. However, the account provides no numerical values, statistical test or named anomalies. Local geology, obstacles, recent rainfall or human modification may affect individual sites. The evidence supports the overall model qualitatively, but raw data and method details are needed to judge the strength of that support precisely.
