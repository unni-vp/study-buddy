Network types and connections

Networks, benefits and risks

A computer network is two or more computers or devices connected so they can communicate and share resources.

Think of a network as a transport system: devices are destinations, links are roads and protocols are agreed traffic rules.

AspectBenefitPossible disadvantage
Shared resourcesUsers can share files, printers and an internet connection.Setup, equipment and maintenance cost money.
CommunicationUsers can collaborate and access shared services from different devices.Congestion can reduce performance.
Central managementAccounts, updates and backups can be managed centrally.Users may depend on network equipment and services remaining available.
Connected devicesInformation is easier to exchange.Malware or faults can spread; security and privacy need protection.

PAN, LAN and WAN

TypeScale and ownershipExample
PANVery short range around one person; Bluetooth is required herePhone connected to wireless earbuds
LANSmall geographical area, often owned and managed by one person or organisationSchool or home network
WANWide geographical area, often using third-party infrastructure and distributed ownershipThe Internet

Classify a network using geographical scale, how devices connect and who owns or manages the infrastructure.

Wired and wireless networks

FeatureWired networkWireless network
ConnectionA cable connects each device.Radio signals connect devices without a cable to each one.
MobilityMovement is restricted by cables.Users can move around within coverage.
InstallationCabling can cost money and disrupt a building.Convenient where cables are difficult; mobile devices are easier to add.
PerformanceUsually stable, with low latency and less radio interference.Distance, obstacles, interference and congestion can reduce signal quality.
SecurityPhysical access makes interception harder, but a cable does not guarantee security.Signals may extend beyond a building, so authentication and encryption are important.
Suitable useFixed devices needing a stable connection.Phones and tablets needing mobility.

Copper and fibre-optic cable

FeatureCopper cableFibre-optic cable
SignalCarries electrical signals.Carries light.
Suitable useCommon, relatively inexpensive shorter links.High-bandwidth links over long distances.
Trade-offCan be affected by electromagnetic interference.Resists electromagnetic interference, but installation can cost more.

Choose a connection for the stated situation. Do not assume one option is always faster or more secure in every installation.
