Dictation and sound practice

How dictation works

Dictation means writing the French you hear, rather than translating it. Each sentence is played three times:

  1. The complete sentence: understand the overall message.
  2. Short sections: write the words and their order.
  3. The complete sentence again: check missing words and agreement.

The Higher task contains at least 30 words, including two words outside the vocabulary list. Use sound–spelling patterns to attempt those words rather than leaving gaps immediately.

The response is marked across the extracts as a whole: 5 marks for communicating the meaning and 5 for transcription and grammatical accuracy. It is not automatically one mark per word or one fixed deduction per error.

Recovering silent letters

Sound and grammar work together like two clues to the same answer.

Mes amies sont arrivées hier. — My female friends arrived yesterday.

  • Mes and amies signal a plural noun.
  • Sont is the plural auxiliary.
  • Arrivées takes feminine plural agreement after être.
  • Hier identifies the finished past event.

J'ai regardé un film. The heard /e/ ending could have several written spellings, but after j'ai a past participle is needed: regardé, not regarder.

Je vais regarder un film. After vais, the next verb is an infinitive: regarder, not regardé.

Les enfants jouent. The -s of enfants and -ent of jouent may be silent; the plural subject still determines the spelling.

Final checks

  • a / à, ou / où, et / est: use the sentence meaning.
  • -er / -é / -ez: check the verb's job and subject.
  • le / la / les, un / une: check the noun.
  • Apostrophes: j'ai, l'école, d'eau.
  • Negatives: both parts where required.
  • Plural and feminine endings, including after être.
  • Word order and any omitted small words.

Practice with real audio

The AQA sound bank provides recorded vocabulary for sound–spelling practice. The assessment resources provide listening papers and available recordings. Select the matching recording and paper; keep the transcript hidden until after attempting an item.

The sample question paper and answer scheme are saved locally. The audio links require internet access.

After a difficult item, identify the missed clue: sound, word meaning, negative, time frame or speaker. Replay the phrase and connect it to its spelling. Reading a transcript helps explain a mistake, but hearing the phrase is what trains listening.
