Mathematical requirements and periodic table questions & answers

1 mark

1. Convert 30 cm³ into dm³.

Answer:

0.030 dm³.

2. Write 0.0006 in standard form.

Answer:

6 ×10⁻⁴.

3. How many significant figures are in 0.00480?

Answer:

Three.

4. What does an element’s atomic number count?

Answer:

Protons.

5. What is the chemical symbol for potassium?

Answer:

K.

6. What is the chemical symbol for iron?

Answer:

Fe.

7. What unit is used for the quantity of substance?

Answer:

Mole, mol.

8. What volume does one mole of gas occupy at room temperature and pressure in the course approximation?

Answer:

24 dm³.

9. What does ∝ mean?

Answer:

Proportional to.

10. Which graph feature gives instantaneous reaction rate?

Answer:

Tangent gradient.

11. What does the small 2 in MgCl₂ indicate? Choose one: A two magnesium ions; B two chloride ions for every magnesium ion; C a charge of 2− on every chloride ion; D two moles in every sample.

Answer:

B: two chloride ions for every magnesium ion.

2 marks

12. Convert 0.0025 kg to grams and 500 cm³ to dm³.

Answer:
  • 0.0025 ×1000 = 2.5 g.
  • 500/1000 = 0.500 dm³.

13. Calculate (8 ×10⁴)/(2 ×10²).

Answer:
  • 8/2 =4; exponent =4−2 =2.
  • 4 ×10².

14. Round 0.04682 to three significant figures.

Answer:
  • Count from the first non-zero digit, 4.
  • 0.0468.

15. A quantity rises from 80 to 100. Calculate percentage increase.

Answer:
  • (100−80)/80 ×100.
  • 25%.

16. Determine protons and electrons in Mg²⁺, atomic number 12.

Answer:
  • 12 protons.
  • 10 electrons, after losing two.

17. An isotope has atomic number 17 and mass number 37. Find neutron count and explain why decimal Ar is not used.

Answer:
  • Neutrons =37−17 = 20.
  • Ar is a weighted isotope mean, not this individual isotope’s mass number.

18. Write the formula formed by Al³⁺ and O²⁻.

Answer:
  • Equal charges require two aluminium ions (+6) and three oxide ions (−6).
  • Al₂O₃.

19. Write the formula formed by Ca²⁺ and NO₃⁻.

Answer:
  • Two nitrate ions balance one calcium ion.
  • Ca(NO₃)₂, retaining the nitrate group.

20. Calculate Mr of Mg(OH)₂ using Mg=24, O=16 and H=1.

Answer:
  • 24+2×(16+1).
  • 58, with no unit on Mr.

21. Calculate moles in 7.2 g magnesium, Ar=24.

Answer:
  • n =7.2/24.
  • 0.30 mol.

22. Calculate mass of 0.15 mol carbon dioxide, Mr=44.

Answer:
  • Mass =0.15 ×44.
  • 6.6 g.

23. Calculate moles in 240 cm³ gas at room temperature and pressure.

Answer:
  • n =240/24 000.
  • 0.010 mol.

24. Calculate mass concentration for 3.0 g solute in 200 cm³ solution.

Answer:
  • Volume = 0.200 dm³.
  • Concentration =3.0/0.200 = 15 g dm⁻³.

25. Calculate percentage yield from actual 6.0 g and theoretical 8.0 g.

Answer:
  • 6.0/8.0 ×100.
  • 75%.

26. Calculate Rf from spot distance 18 mm and front distance 30 mm.

Answer:
  • 18/30.
  • 0.60, no unit.

27. Rearrange c=n/V to make V the subject.

Answer:
  • Multiply by V: cV=n.
  • Divide by c: V=n/c.

3 marks

28. Calculate weighted Ar for 80% isotope mass 10 and 20% isotope mass 11.

Answer:
  • Weighted total =80×10 +20×11 = 1020.
  • Divide by100.
  • Ar =10.2; individual atoms still have integer mass numbers.

29. Calculate nitrogen’s mass percentage in NH₄NO₃, using N=14, H=1, O=16.

Answer:
  • Mr =2×14 +4×1 +3×16 = 80.
  • Nitrogen contribution = 28.
  • Percentage =28/80 ×100 = 35%.

30. Calculate the number of molecules in 0.20 mol H₂ using 6.02 ×10²³ mol⁻¹, and the number of H atoms.

Answer:
  • Molecules =0.20 ×6.02 ×10²³ = 1.204 ×10²³.
  • Each molecule contains two atoms.
  • H atoms = 2.408 ×10²³ before final requested rounding.

31. For 2Mg+O₂ →2MgO, find oxygen moles and MgO mass from 0.10 mol Mg; MgO Mr=40.

Answer:
  • Oxygen needed =0.10/2 = 0.050 mol.
  • MgO formed = 0.10 mol.
  • Mass =0.10 ×40 = 4.0 g, if reaction completes with enough oxygen.

32. Find empirical formula from 2.4 g carbon and 0.6 g hydrogen; Ar values 12 and 1.

Answer:
  • Carbon moles =2.4/12 = 0.20. Hydrogen moles =0.6/1 = 0.60.
  • Divide by0.20: ratio 1:3.
  • Empirical formula CH₃.

33. An empirical formula is CH₂ and molecular Mr is 56. Determine molecular formula.

Answer:
  • Empirical mass =12+2 = 14.
  • Multiplier =56/14 = 4.
  • Molecular formula C₄H₈.

34. Calculate molar concentration of 2.0 g NaOH in 250 cm³; Mr=40.

Answer:
  • Moles =2.0/40 = 0.050 mol.
  • Volume = 0.250 dm³.
  • c =0.050/0.250 = 0.200 mol dm⁻³ before rounding to requested precision.

35. Convert 0.25 mol dm⁻³ sodium carbonate to g dm⁻³, using Na=23, C=12 and O=16.

Answer:
  • Mr(Na₂CO₃)=46+12+48 = 106.
  • Mass concentration =0.25 ×106.
  • 26.5 g dm⁻³, before final requested rounding.

36. Calculate atom economy for CaCO₃ →CaO+CO₂ when CaO is desired; masses 100, 56 and 44.

Answer:
  • Desired contribution = 56.
  • Total product contribution =56+44 = 100.
  • Atom economy =56/100 ×100 = 56%.

37. A tangent has points (10 s, 8 cm³) and (50 s, 32 cm³). Calculate instantaneous rate.

Answer:
  • ΔV =32−8 = 24 cm³.
  • Δt =50−10 = 40 s.
  • Rate =24/40 = 0.60 cm³ s⁻¹.

38. A line passes through (2, 7) and (8, 19). Find gradient and intercept.

Answer:
  • m =(19−7)/(8−2)= 2.
  • 7=2×2+c, so c=3.
  • y= 2x+3.

39. Measurements 18.9, 19.0 and 19.1 cm³ are concordant. Calculate their mean and range.

Answer:
  • Mean =(18.9+19.0+19.1)/3 = 19.0 cm³.
  • Range =19.1−18.9 = 0.2 cm³.
  • Report precision consistently with the measurement resolution.

40. Counts 1, 2 and 3 have frequencies 2, 5 and 3. Calculate the mean count.

Answer:
  • Weighted total =1×2 +2×5 +3×3 = 21.
  • Total frequency = 10.
  • Mean =21/10 = 2.1.

41. Calculate surface area and volume of a cube with side 2 cm.

Answer:
  • Surface =6×2² = 24 cm².
  • Volume =2³ = 8 cm³.
  • SA:V =24:8 = 3:1.

4 marks

42. For 2Mg+O₂ →2MgO, a mixture has 0.30 mol Mg and 0.10 mol O₂. Identify limiting reagent and amounts left/formed.

Answer:
  • Compare moles/coefficient: Mg 0.30/2=0.15; oxygen 0.10/1=0.10.
  • Oxygen limits.
  • It uses 0.20 mol Mg, leaving 0.10 mol Mg.
  • Forms 0.20 mol MgO.

43. A 25.0 cm³ NaOH sample requires 15.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ H₂SO₄. Calculate NaOH concentration.

Answer:
  • Acid moles =0.100×0.0150 = 0.00150 mol.
  • H₂SO₄+2NaOH →Na₂SO₄+2H₂O gives 1:2 ratio.
  • NaOH moles = 0.00300 mol.
  • c =0.00300/0.0250 = 0.120 mol dm⁻³.

44. Use H–H436, Cl–Cl243 and H–Cl431 kJ mol⁻¹ to calculate energy change for H₂+Cl₂ →2HCl.

Answer:
  • Broken bonds:436+243 = 679 kJ mol⁻¹.
  • Formed bonds:2×431 = 862 kJ mol⁻¹.
  • Change =679−862 = −183 kJ mol⁻¹.
  • Negative means exothermic, for the reaction quantities shown.

45. A 2 cm cube is cut into eight 1 cm cubes. Compare total volume and exposed surface area.

Answer:
  • Original volume = 8 cm³ and surface = 24 cm².
  • New total volume =8×1 = 8 cm³, unchanged.
  • New total surface =8×6 = 48 cm², doubled.
  • More exposed area can increase solid reaction rate without adding substance.

46. Explain how to extract a rate from a curved chemical graph without confusing it with yield.

Answer:
  • Draw tangent at the specified time.
  • Calculate Δquantity/Δtime on a large triangle using actual scales.
  • Give correct rate units.
  • Final curve height measures total amount, not instantaneous speed.

47. Using the same temperature and pressure, determine oxygen needed for 120 cm³ hydrogen in 2H₂+O₂ →2H₂O, and hydrogen moles at room conditions.

Answer:
  • Gas-volume ratio H₂:O₂ = 2:1.
  • Oxygen required =120/2 = 60 cm³.
  • Hydrogen moles =120/24 000.
  • 0.0050 mol.

48. A 10 g 18-carat gold alloy is recycled with 90% of its gold recovered. Calculate gold content and recovery.

Answer:
  • Gold fraction =18/24 = 0.75.
  • Original gold =10×0.75 = 7.5 g.
  • Recovery =7.5×0.90.
  • 6.75 g gold, before rounding to requested precision.

49. A gas-production rate rises linearly from 0 to 8 cm³ s⁻¹ over 5 seconds. Calculate gas produced from the rate-time graph.

Answer:
  • Total amount is the area under the rate-time graph.
  • Triangle area =½×5×8.
  • Result = 20.
  • Units s×cm³ s⁻¹ give 20 cm³.

5 marks

50. A reaction produces 0, 12, 24 and 36 cm³ of gas at times 0, 10, 20 and 30 s. Plot gas volume against time for this interval, draw a suitable line and calculate its gradient with units. Can these results alone establish the final gas yield? Explain.

Answer:
  • Put time (s) on the horizontal axis and gas volume (cm³) on the vertical axis. Choose regular scales covering the supplied range.
  • Plot (0,0), (10,12), (20,24) and (30,36). They lie on a straight line through the origin.
  • Gradient = (36 − 0) ÷ (30 − 0) = 1.2 cm³/s, the constant measured rate over this interval.
  • These results do not establish the final yield because no plateau has been observed. Continue measuring until no more gas is produced.

6 marks

51. Zinc reacts with hydrochloric acid: Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂. A student uses 6.50 g Zn and 80.0 cm³ of 2.00 mol/dm³ HCl. Aᵣ(Zn) = 65 and one mole of gas occupies 24.0 dm³ at RTP. Identify the limiting reactant and calculate the hydrogen volume collected if the percentage yield is 90.0%.

Answer:
  • Zinc amount = 6.50 ÷ 65 = 0.100 mol.
  • Acid volume = 0.0800 dm³, so HCl amount = 2.00 × 0.0800 = 0.160 mol.
  • The equation needs two moles HCl per mole Zn. This acid can react with only 0.160 ÷ 2 = 0.0800 mol Zn, so HCl is limiting.
  • Theoretical H₂ amount = 0.0800 mol.
  • Theoretical gas volume = 0.0800 × 24.0 = 1.92 dm³.
  • Collected volume = 1.92 × 0.900 = 1.728 dm³, or 1.73 dm³ to three significant figures.
