Substances & separation

What is the substance?

  • Element: atoms of one element. Compound: different elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio.
  • Mixture: substances retain chemical properties and can be separated physically. Compounds need chemical reactions.

Symbols & equations

  • H₂O = 2H and 1O; Ca(OH)₂ = 1Ca, 2O, 2H. Use correct upper/lower case in symbols.
  • 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO. Count each element on both sides; change coefficients only.
  • Binary names often end -ide; -ate names usually include oxygen.

Filtration

  • Separates insoluble solid from liquid. Residue stays on filter; filtrate passes through.
  • Dissolved salt passes through too. Salt + sand: dissolve salt, filter sand, crystallise filtrate.

Crystallisation

  • Partly evaporate solvent → concentrated solution → cool → crystals form.
  • Filter crystals and dry them. Avoid heating hydrated salts strongly to complete dryness.
  • Wear goggles; handle hot glassware carefully.

Distillation

  • Simple: boil liquid, cool vapour in condenser, collect liquid distillate. Recover water from salt solution.
  • Fractional: column gives repeated evaporation/condensation to separate miscible liquids by boiling point.
  • Lower-boiling fraction usually comes first; cooling water enters condenser at bottom.

Chromatography

  • Components have different attractions to solvent and stationary phase, so travel different distances.
  • Pencil baseline above solvent; small spots; mark solvent front. Ink could dissolve; submerged spots wash off.
  • No new substances form in these physical separations.
