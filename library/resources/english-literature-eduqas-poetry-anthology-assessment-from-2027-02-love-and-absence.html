Love & absence

Sonnet 29: the idea

  • Barrett Browning’s thoughts resemble vines growing around the beloved tree. They express desire but can hide the real person.
  • The speaker wants actual presence. Commands make her an active voice in love.

Sonnet 29: the method

  • A Petrarchan sonnet: 14 lines. Early turn with Yet starts rejecting thought as a substitute. Ending reverses the opening.
  • Natural growth becomes release as greenery falls. Context: Sonnets from the Portuguese and love for Robert Browning.

Dusting the Phone: the idea

  • Kay’s speaker fears disaster whether the telephone rings or stays silent. Every outcome feeds uncertainty.
  • Polishing and dressing for the personified phone suggest service to an object that controls the day.

Dusting: the method

  • Free verse and tercets lead to an isolated last line. Fragments and questions interrupt attempts at control.
  • Modern domestic communication makes dependence concrete. The lover’s gender is not specified; the speaker is not automatically the poet.

A shared problem

  • Absence makes imagination powerful. Browning uses a growing plant; Kay uses a household device.
  • Both speakers address a beloved and want more than imagined contact. Compare how desire is voiced.

A different ending

  • Browning imagines closeness freeing her from thought. Kay’s attempted ultimatum collapses into uncertainty.
  • Build an argument about control, then connect metaphor, changing tone and ending. Do not give two separate summaries.
