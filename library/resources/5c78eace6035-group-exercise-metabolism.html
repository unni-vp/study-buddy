Exercise & metabolism

Exercise and oxygen debt

Muscles need more energy during exercise because they contract more frequently or forcefully.

  1. Heart rate increases, moving more oxygenated blood to the muscles.
  2. Breathing rate and breath volume increase, bringing more oxygen into the lungs and removing more carbon dioxide.
  3. Increased oxygen and glucose delivery supports a higher rate of aerobic respiration.
  4. If oxygen supply cannot meet demand, muscles also use more anaerobic respiration.
  5. Glucose is incompletely oxidised, lactic acid builds up, and muscles become fatigued and contract less efficiently during prolonged vigorous activity.

After exercise

Oxygen debt is the extra oxygen needed after exercise to deal with accumulated lactic acid.

Breathing and heart rate remain raised for a while. This supplies extra oxygen and keeps substances moving between tissues.

Blood carries lactic acid from the muscles to the liver, where it is converted back into glucose. The lungs supply oxygen; they do not perform this conversion. Respiration continues after exercise has stopped.

Investigating exercise

To compare different activities, measure resting pulse, perform each activity for the same duration at a defined intensity, then measure pulse immediately afterwards. Monitor recovery too.

  • Use the same participants for each activity where possible, with full recovery between trials. Randomise activity order to reduce order effects.
  • Control duration and define pace; fitness, age, medication and health can affect results. Repeat and compare means or changes from resting pulse.
  • A pulse counted for 15 seconds is multiplied by 4 to estimate beats/minute. Delays after exercise underestimate the immediate response because recovery has begun.
  • Choose safe, moderate activities and account for participants' health; stop if someone feels unwell.

Interpreting recovery data

A raised pulse after activity shows that the body has not immediately returned to its resting state. Compare recovery using the same measurement method and starting point. A person with a higher resting pulse is not necessarily using more anaerobic respiration: intensity, fitness and other factors affect the readings.

The course links lactic acid accumulation with fatigue during vigorous exercise. It should not be used as a simple explanation for every cramp or for soreness several days later. Keep the measured pulse result separate from an inference about what is happening inside muscle cells.

Metabolism

Metabolism is the sum of all chemical reactions in a cell or the body. It includes both building molecules and breaking them down.

Think of a factory with assembly lines and recycling lines. Enzymes control the reactions, and energy transferred by respiration supports the building work.

ReactionPurpose
Glucose → starchStorage in plants.
Glucose → glycogenStorage in animals, especially liver and muscles.
Glucose → cellulosePlant cell walls.
Glycerol + three fatty acids → lipidMakes lipid molecules for storage and cell structures.
Glucose + nitrate-derived nitrogen → amino acids → proteinsPlants build proteins, including enzymes, for growth and repair.
RespirationBreaks down glucose and transfers energy.
Breakdown of excess proteins/amino acids → ureaRemoves excess nitrogen; urea is excreted in urine.

Digestion and metabolism are connected: digestion supplies small molecules that cells absorb and use to build their own materials. Respiration is one part of metabolism, not another name for the whole of metabolism.

Linking the building and breakdown reactions

Animals can store glucose as glycogen, then break that store down when glucose is needed. Plants can make starch for storage and cellulose for cell walls. These substances are different products made from glucose units, rather than alternative names for glucose.

Excess amino acids cannot simply be stored as an unlimited amino-acid reserve. Their breakdown in the liver produces urea, which travels in blood to the kidneys for excretion. The liver makes the urea; the kidneys remove it from blood.
