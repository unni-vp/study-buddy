Conversation & recovery

Developing the conversation

After the photos, the unprepared conversation lasts about 4½–5½ minutes and can use any of the three topics within the photo card's theme. The description has 5 marks; the conversation has 15 for communication and 5 for language.

Answer the actual question, then develop it:

J'aime mon quartier parce qu'il est calme. Il y a un parc où je retrouve mes amis. Cependant, il n'y a pas assez de bus. L'année prochaine, j'aimerais participer à un projet pour améliorer le quartier.

I like my neighbourhood because it is quiet. There is a park where I meet my friends. However, there are not enough buses. Next year, I would like to join a project to improve the neighbourhood.

The reason, relative clause, contrast and future wish add meaningful detail. They are not a checklist to force into every short answer.

Useful time frames:

  • Normalement, je... — normally, I...
  • La semaine dernière, j'ai... — last week, I...
  • Quand j'étais petit(e), je... — when I was young, I...
  • Demain, je vais... — tomorrow, I am going to...
  • À l'avenir, j'aimerais... — in future, I would like...

Listening and recovering

Question clues: Qu'est-ce que tu as fait ? asks what you did; Qu'est-ce que tu vas faire ? asks what you are going to do; Pourquoi ? needs a reason.

Vous pouvez répéter, s'il vous plaît ? — Could you repeat, please? A brief request can help when you miss a question. The role-play examiner must follow its task script, so do not depend on being coached towards an answer.

If a word disappears from memory, simplify: c'est utile, je peux aider, j'aime travailler avec les autres. Keep communicating rather than switching to a long unrelated paragraph.

Common mistakes

  • Preparing only one theme or assuming the conversation questions are preselected for you.
  • Describing only one of the two photos.
  • Giving an answer when instructed to ask a question.
  • Using yesterday with a present-tense account throughout.
  • Reading the text aloud during the supervised preparation period.
  • Speaking so quickly that otherwise correct sounds become unclear.
