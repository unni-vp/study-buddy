Story overview & Act 1

The story in five acts

Macbeth is a successful soldier who murders his king to gain the crown. Once he has power, he becomes afraid of losing it and commits more violence. His attempt to control the future destroys his security, relationships and life.

Imagine cheating to win a competition, then having to tell a new lie every time someone gets close to the truth. Macbeth's first crime starts a chain of concealment and fear. The consequences grow far beyond him, damaging Scotland itself.

ActMain eventsWhy it matters
1: temptation and choiceWitches predict Macbeth's rise; he becomes Thane of Cawdor. Duncan names Malcolm his heir. Lady Macbeth urges Macbeth to kill Duncan.The prophecy tempts Macbeth, but he recognises the moral objections and chooses murder.
2: murder and disorderMacbeth kills Duncan offstage. Lady Macbeth returns the daggers. Macduff discovers the body. Malcolm and Donalbain flee; Macbeth becomes king.The crown is gained through betrayal. Blood, sleeplessness and unnatural events reveal the cost.
3: fear and isolationMacbeth arranges Banquo and Fleance's murders. Banquo dies; Fleance escapes. Macbeth sees Banquo's ghost at a banquet.Killing Duncan does not bring safety. Private guilt disrupts public kingship.
4: false confidence and crueltyApparitions give misleading assurances. Macbeth orders an attack on Macduff's family. In England, Malcolm tests Macduff before joining him.Macbeth moves from fearful hesitation to impulsive tyranny. The victims' suffering becomes visible.
5: collapse and resistanceLady Macbeth sleepwalks and later dies. Soldiers carry branches from Birnam Wood. Macduff reveals his unusual birth and kills Macbeth. Malcolm takes power.The apparent guarantees fail. Order returns, but the human losses remain.

Key distinction: the witches predict; Macbeth decides. They do not command him to murder Duncan.

Act 1 — Temptation and choice

What happens

  • The witches introduce a world of confusing appearances. Duncan hears that Macbeth has fought bravely for Scotland.
  • They greet Macbeth as Thane of Glamis, Thane of Cawdor and future king. They predict that Banquo's descendants will be kings.
  • Macbeth receives the title of Cawdor. One prediction coming true makes the promise of kingship seem believable. Duncan names Malcolm his heir, creating an obstacle to Macbeth's ambition.
  • Lady Macbeth reads her husband's letter and plans Duncan's murder during his visit. Macbeth objects, but she challenges his courage and persuades him to agree.

Why this act matters

Shakespeare puts temptation beside conscious choice. Macbeth knows that killing Duncan would betray his king, relative and guest. His soliloquy exposes these objections before he commits himself, so the audience sees his responsibility rather than a man simply obeying fate.

Duncan trusts Macbeth just after being betrayed by the previous Cawdor. This creates dramatic irony: the audience knows the danger that Duncan cannot see. Public praise and private plotting pull in opposite directions.

Important quotations

QuotationWho and whereWhat to analyse
“Fair is foul, and foul is fair”Witches, 1.1Language — paradox and reversed word order: The paradox mixes good and evil. It introduces deception before Macbeth appears.
“brave Macbeth”Captain, 1.2Language / structure — approving adjective establishing the tragic reversal: This admiring description establishes the honour Macbeth will lose. His violence initially serves Scotland.
“Stars, hide your fires”Macbeth, 1.4Language / imagery — imperative and light-dark imagery: The imperative asks for concealment. Light suggests exposure; he recognises that his desires are shameful.
“unsex me here”Lady Macbeth, 1.5Language / form — imperative in an appeal to spirits: Her command to the spirits rejects the tenderness she associates with femininity. Needing to ask for cruelty also suggests effort and vulnerability.
“innocent flower” / “serpent”Lady Macbeth, 1.5Imagery — contrasting flower and serpent images: The contrasting images turn hospitality into a disguise for danger. The serpent can also suggest temptation and betrayal.
“vaulting ambition”Macbeth, 1.7Imagery / form — riding metaphor within a soliloquy: The metaphor of leaping too far suggests ambition that causes its own fall. Macbeth identifies his dangerous motive himself.
“The instruments of darkness tell us truths”Banquo, 1.3Imagery / structure — metaphor of evil agents and a warning before the crime: The warning separates truth from good intentions. Banquo recognises the danger Macbeth increasingly ignores.
“To find the mind's construction in the face”Duncan, 1.4Structure — dramatic irony through the timing of Macbeth's entrance: A face cannot reveal intention. Macbeth's entrance after this admission makes Duncan's renewed trust dramatically ironic.
“When you durst do it, then you were a man”Lady Macbeth, 1.7Language — conditional claim and manipulation of identity: She makes masculinity conditional on murder, turning Macbeth's moral doubts into a supposed failure of courage.

Turning point: Macbeth agrees to murder Duncan. A possible future becomes a deliberate plan.
