Defence & vaccination

Skin & nose

  • Skin: physical barrier; antimicrobial substances and clotting at cuts help protect.
  • Nose: hairs and mucus trap pathogens and particles.
  • These barriers are non-specific: they act against many pathogens.

Airways & stomach

  • Trachea/bronchi: mucus traps pathogens; cilia move mucus to the throat for swallowing.
  • Stomach hydrochloric acid kills many swallowed pathogens.

White blood cells

  • Phagocytosis: engulf and digest pathogens.
  • Antibodies: bind specific pathogen antigens and help destroy the pathogen.
  • Antitoxins: neutralise bacterial toxins. Different white cells have different roles.

Specificity

  • An antibody’s binding site has a shape complementary to its antigen.
  • Antibodies target antigens; antitoxins neutralise toxins. They are not interchangeable terms.

Vaccination sequence

  • Dead/inactive pathogen or antigens → white cells make specific antibodies → memory cells remain.
  • Later exposure → faster, larger antibody response, destroying pathogens before significant illness.

Population protection

  • High vaccine uptake leaves fewer susceptible hosts, reducing transmission and helping protect those unable to be vaccinated.
  • Evaluate disease reduction against reliable risk evidence, cost, access and uptake. Protection is not identical for every person.
