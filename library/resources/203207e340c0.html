River Alyn physical fieldwork: school account

Course: OCR A J383 | Exam year: 2027 | Specification: Geographical Skills, fieldwork Source: Student account supplied on 4 October 2026. Findings are reported observations; numerical data has not been supplied.

Enquiry question

How do river characteristics change downstream along the River Alyn?

In plain English: does the river become wider, deeper and faster as it travels towards its mouth, and do its stones change?

An everyday analogy: stones carried along a river can knock together like objects tumbling in a washing machine. Repeated collisions can chip their edges. The analogy illustrates collisions, not every process in a river.

Measurements

  • Width: measured across the channel.
  • Depth: measured at intervals and used to calculate an average depth.
  • Velocity: measured using a float or flow meter over a measured distance. The exact method used is unconfirmed.
  • Bedload: size and shape recorded to investigate whether stones became smaller and rounder downstream.
  • Channel shape and valley features: observed at each site.

Sampling

The supplied account describes systematic sampling, with sites from upstream to downstream. This allowed downstream comparisons.

For an exam answer, confirm how sites were selected. Systematic sampling requires a regular interval or another fixed selection rule; simply using several sites along a river does not establish that rule. Site spacing has not yet been supplied.

Reported findings

  • Channel width and depth generally increased downstream.
  • Velocity tended to increase downstream.
  • Bedload became smaller and rounder downstream.

Explanation given in the account

  • The account links higher velocity to a larger, more efficient channel.
  • It links smaller, rounder bedload to attrition and transport over time.

Treat these as explanations of the reported pattern, rather than proof from measurements alone. Local conditions can change the trend at individual sites.

Conclusion

The reported pattern broadly matched river theory. Individual sites may have differed because of local conditions. Specific anomalies have not been supplied.

Details still needed for precise exam answers

  • Site names/locations, number of sites and the sampling rule or interval.
  • Actual width, depth, velocity and bedload measurements.
  • Whether a float or a flow meter was used and the exact procedure.
  • Measurement repeats, conditions and actual limitations or improvements.
