QuestionApply and calculate

Describe the sequence from a hot-surface stimulus to withdrawing a hand in a spinal reflex.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • A receptor detects the stimulus and a sensory neurone carries an impulse to the spinal cord.
  • A relay neurone passes the signal to a motor neurone through synapses.
  • The motor neurone activates a muscle, which contracts to withdraw the hand.

QuestionApply and calculate

Explain how excess amino acids are prepared for safe excretion.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer

  • Urea travels in blood to the kidneys and is excreted in urine.

QuestionApply and calculate

Explain why dialysis fluid contains appropriate glucose and ion concentrations but little or no urea.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Suitable useful-substance concentrations prevent unwanted net loss of glucose and required ions.
  • Low urea concentration maintains a concentration gradient from blood to dialysis fluid.
  • Urea therefore diffuses out across the partially permeable membrane.
QuestionApply and calculate

Explain how thyroxine levels are controlled by negative feedback.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer

  • A sufficiently high level reduces further stimulation, opposing the original deviation.

QuestionMulti-step application

Describe a valid ruler-drop comparison of reaction time with and without a distraction.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Start with ruler zero at the same thumb/finger position and release at an unpredictable time.
  • Record catch distances in both conditions and convert to times using a suitable table.
  • Repeat, keep ruler, hand, release setup and posture consistent, and reduce anticipation/practice effects.
  • Compare mean times, considering variation and using a safe, defined distraction.
QuestionMulti-step application

Explain how a low body-water level produces a smaller volume of concentrated urine.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Concentrated blood leads to more ADH release from the pituitary.
  • ADH increases kidney-tubule water permeability.
  • More water is reabsorbed into blood.
  • Less water remains in urine, so urine volume falls and concentration rises.
QuestionMulti-step application

Explain how FSH, oestrogen and LH interact to lead to ovulation.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • FSH stimulates follicle/egg maturation and oestrogen production.
  • Oestrogen rebuilds the uterine lining and inhibits further FSH release.
  • A sufficiently high oestrogen level stimulates an LH surge around mid-cycle.
  • LH triggers release of the egg, or ovulation.

QuestionMulti-step application

Describe how to investigate phototropism using newly germinated seedlings.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Use same-species seedlings of similar age and size, with one-sided light and a suitable all-round-light control.
  • Keep water, temperature and other growing conditions comparable; use several seedlings.
  • Record starting and later lengths plus labelled biological drawings of growth direction over the same interval.
  • Repeat and compare bending/elongation, using controls to distinguish directional light from other influences.
QuestionMulti-step application

Evaluate hormonal contraception and condoms for preventing pregnancy and reducing infection transmission.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Hormonal methods can suppress maturation/ovulation and may offer long-lasting protection, depending on the method.
  • They may have side effects or require correct scheduled use, and do not protect against STIs.
  • Condoms prevent sperm reaching an egg and reduce STI transmission, but correct use is important.
  • Compare evidence on effectiveness, duration and individual priorities; there is no single biological measurement that decides every personal choice.
