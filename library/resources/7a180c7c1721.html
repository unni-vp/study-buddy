Mathematical requirements and equations questions & answers

1 mark2 marks3 marks4 marks6 marks

1 mark

1. Convert 250 mA to amperes.

Answer:

0.250 A.

2. Convert 3.0 minutes to seconds.

Answer:

180 s.

3. Write 72,000 in standard form.

Answer:

7.2 × 10⁴.

4. How many significant figures are in 0.00620?

Answer:

Three.

5. What unit is obtained by dividing energy in joules by time in seconds?

Answer:

Watt (W).

6. What does the symbol ∝ mean?

Answer:

Proportional to.

7. What quantity is given by the gradient of a distance–time graph?

Answer:

Speed.

8. What quantity is given by the signed area under a velocity–time graph?

Answer:

Displacement.

2 marks

9. Convert 8.0 cm² to m².

Answer:

1 cm² = 10⁻⁴ m², so 8.0 cm² = 8.0 × 10⁻⁴ m².

10. Convert 25 cm³ to m³.

Answer:

1 cm³ = 10⁻⁶ m³, so 25 cm³ = 2.5 × 10⁻⁵ m³.

11. A potential difference of 9.0 V causes a current of 0.30 A. Calculate resistance.

Answer:

R = V/I = 9.0/0.30 = 30 Ω.

12. A speed is 72 km/h. Convert it to m/s.

Answer:

72/3.6 = 20 m/s.

13. A resistor’s I–V graph has current on y and voltage on x. What does its straight-line gradient equal?

Answer:
  • Gradient = I/V = 1/R.
  • Reversing the axes to V against I would give R.

14. Explain why a straight-line graph with a non-zero y-intercept does not show direct proportionality between y and x.

Answer:
  • Direct proportion has y = kx.
  • It requires a line through the origin; a non-zero intercept adds a constant term.

15. A power output rises from 80 W to 100 W. Calculate the percentage increase.

Answer:

Percentage increase = (100 − 80)/80 × 100 = 25%.

16. At fixed mass, what happens to kinetic energy if speed triples? Explain using the relationship.

Answer:
  • Eₖ ∝ v².
  • Tripling speed multiplies kinetic energy by 3² = 9.

17. An echo returns after 0.10 s in water where wave speed is 1,500 m/s. Calculate one-way distance.

Answer:

Distance = vt/2 = 1,500 × 0.10/2 = 75 m.

18. A 0.050 m length has estimated uncertainty ±0.001 m. Calculate percentage uncertainty.

Answer:

0.001/0.050 × 100 = 2%.

19. A histogram bar covers a width of 5 units and represents 20 readings. Calculate its frequency density.

Answer:

Frequency density = frequency/interval width = 20/5 = 4 readings per unit.

20. Round 0.004867 to three significant figures.

Answer:

0.00487. Leading zeros are not significant; retain 4, 8 and rounded 7.

3 marks

21. Calculate speed from kinetic energy 36 J and mass 2.0 kg.

Answer:
  • Eₖ = ½mv² gives v = √(2Eₖ/m).
  • v = √(2 × 36/2.0) = √36.
  • v = 6.0 m/s.

22. A gas at 100 kPa occupies 60 cm³. It is compressed to 40 cm³ without changing temperature or gas mass. Calculate final pressure.

Answer:
  • p₁V₁ = p₂V₂.
  • p₂ = 100 × 60/40.
  • p₂ = 150 kPa. Matching volume units are used on both sides.

23. A force–extension best-fit line passes through (0.020 m, 4 N) and (0.050 m, 10 N). Calculate gradient and identify its physical meaning.

Answer:
  • Gradient = (10 − 4)/(0.050 − 0.020).
  • 200 N/m.
  • It is the spring constant k in the proportional region.

24. A tangent to a distance–time graph rises by 18 m over 3.0 s. Calculate the speed and explain why the tangent is used.

Answer:
  • Gradient = 18/3.0 = 6.0 m/s.
  • A tangent gives the rate of change at the chosen point.
  • It estimates instantaneous speed, unlike a chord’s interval-average rate.

25. Find the mean, median and mode of 20, 21, 21, 22 and 26.

Answer:
  • Mean = 110/5 = 22.
  • Median = middle sorted value = 21.
  • Mode = most frequent value = 21.

26. A frequency table records value 2 three times, value 3 four times and value 5 once. Calculate the mean.

Answer:
  • Weighted sum = 2 × 3 + 3 × 4 + 5 × 1 = 23.
  • Total frequency = 3 + 4 + 1 = 8.
  • Mean = 23/8 = 2.875, about 2.9 at two significant figures.

27. A cube has side length 3.0 cm. Calculate its volume in cm³ and m³, and its total surface area in cm².

Answer:
  • Volume = 3.0³ = 27 cm³.
  • Volume = 27 × 10⁻⁶ = 2.7 × 10⁻⁵ m³.
  • Surface area = 6 × 3.0² = 54 cm².

28. A 400 N force acts normally on a 20 cm² surface. Calculate pressure in Pa.

Answer:
  • Area = 20 × 10⁻⁴ = 0.0020 m².
  • p = F/A = 400/0.0020.
  • p = 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa.

29. An electromagnetic wave has wavelength 6.0 cm in vacuum. Calculate frequency using c = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s.

Answer:
  • λ = 0.060 m.
  • f = c/λ = (3.0 × 10⁸)/0.060.
  • f = 5.0 × 10⁹ Hz.

30. A 2.0 A current flows for 5.0 minutes through a 6.0 V component. Calculate charge and energy transferred.

Answer:
  • t = 300 s, so Q = It = 2.0 × 300 = 600 C.
  • E = QV = 600 × 6.0.
  • E = 3,600 J.

31. A 0.20 T field acts on a perpendicular 8.0 cm wire carrying 1.5 A. Calculate force.

Answer:
  • l = 0.080 m.
  • F = BIl = 0.20 × 1.5 × 0.080.
  • F = 0.024 N.

32. The velocity–time area above zero is 30 m and the area below zero has magnitude 10 m. Calculate displacement and total distance.

Answer:
  • Signed displacement = 30 − 10 = 20 m in the chosen positive direction.
  • Total distance = 30 + 10 = 40 m.
  • Opposite-direction travel reduces displacement but still adds to distance.

33. A force of 600 N acts perpendicular to a lever, 0.050 m from the pivot. Calculate the moment and state why total lever length is not always used.

Answer:
  • M = Fd = 600 × 0.050 = 30 Nm.
  • d is the perpendicular distance to the force’s line of action.
  • It need not equal the full length of the lever.

4 marks

34. An object accelerates uniformly from 3.0 m/s to 7.0 m/s over 10 m. Calculate acceleration using v² − u² = 2as.

Answer:
  • Rearrange: a = (v² − u²)/(2s).
  • v² − u² = 7.0² − 3.0² = 40 m²/s².
  • a = 40/(2 × 10).
  • a = 2.0 m/s², using constant acceleration.

35. A 50 kg load is lifted 2.0 m in 5.0 s by a motor with efficiency 0.80. Use g = 10 N/kg. Calculate input power.

Answer:
  • Useful energy = mgh = 50 × 10 × 2.0 = 1,000 J.
  • Useful power = 1,000/5.0 = 200 W.
  • Input power = useful power/efficiency = 200/0.80.
  • 250 W.

36. Velocity rises linearly from 0 to 6.0 m/s in 4.0 s, then stays at 6.0 m/s for another 5.0 s. Calculate total displacement.

Answer:
  • Accelerating section: triangle area = ½ × 4.0 × 6.0 = 12 m.
  • Steady section: rectangle area = 5.0 × 6.0 = 30 m.
  • Total = 12 + 30 = 42 m.
  • All velocities are positive, so distance also equals 42 m.

37. An ideal transformer has a 240 V primary with 1,200 turns and a secondary with 60 turns. Secondary current is 2.0 A. Calculate secondary voltage and primary current.

Answer:
  • Vₛ = 240 × 60/1,200 = 12 V.
  • Secondary power = 12 × 2.0 = 24 W.
  • Ideal primary power also equals 24 W.
  • Iₚ = 24/240 = 0.10 A.

38. A student calculates a walking speed as 1.2 × 10⁴ m/s. Explain how to check and improve the calculation.

Answer:
  • Estimate expected walking speed as order 1 m/s, so the result is implausible.
  • Check distance and time conversions, especially cm to m and minutes to seconds.
  • Check that distance was divided by time rather than multiplied.
  • Recalculate with labelled quantities and units, keeping sensible significant figures.

6 marks

39. A heater warms 0.50 kg of ice from −10°C to 0°C and then melts it completely. Use c_ice = 2,100 J/kg°C and L = 334,000 J/kg. Heater power is 500 W and 80% of its input reaches the ice. Calculate the required time.

Answer:
  • First stage is warming: ΔE₁ = mcΔθ = 0.50 × 2,100 × 10 = 10,500 J.
  • Second stage is melting at constant temperature: E₂ = mL = 0.50 × 334,000 = 167,000 J.
  • Total energy reaching the ice = 177,500 J.
  • Efficiency = useful/input, so required input = 177,500/0.80 = 221,875 J.
  • Time = E_input/P = 221,875/500 = 443.75 s, about 440 s to two significant figures.
  • The calculation separates warming from the state change and assumes constant power and the stated efficiency throughout.
