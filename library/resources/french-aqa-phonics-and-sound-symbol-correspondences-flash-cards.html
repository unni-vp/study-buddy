QuestionMeaning and grammar

Explain why a single s differs in maison and chanson.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • In maison it lies between vowel sounds and is usually /z/. In chanson it is followed by a consonant and is /s/.
QuestionMeaning and grammar

Explain the difference between bon and bonne.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Bon has a nasal vowel; bonne has a pronounced n and a non-nasal vowel. The doubled consonant changes the sound pattern.
QuestionMeaning and grammar

Explain why et amis should not be read with a linking t.

Answer

QuestionMeaning and grammar

What changes between les hommes and les héros?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Les hommes allows /z/ liaison. Héros has an h aspiré, which blocks liaison; neither h is pronounced like English h.

QuestionApply the language

Explain three pronunciation checks for Les petits enfants jouent.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • No liaison between les and petits, because petits starts with a consonant.
  • Petits enfants can link with /z/.
  • The verb ending -ent in jouent is silent.
QuestionApply the language

Why can je parle, tu parles and ils parlent sound similar, but require different spellings?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Regular -e/-es/-ent endings can be silent.
  • The subject identifies the person and number.
  • Written agreement is still required even when it is not audible.
QuestionDevelop a response

A learner says every final consonant is silent and every vowel-starting next word causes liaison. Correct both claims with examples.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Many final consonants are silent, but avec has a final /k/.
  • Some final r sounds are heard, as in partir.
  • Liaison depends on the word combination: les amis allows it.
  • Et amis has no liaison after et.

Answer
