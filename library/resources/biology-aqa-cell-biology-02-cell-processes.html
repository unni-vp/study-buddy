Cell processes

Chromosomes & genes

  • The nucleus contains chromosomes made of DNA.
  • Each chromosome carries many genes.
  • Body-cell chromosomes normally occur in pairs.
  • DNA copying produces two joined identical copies of each chromosome.
  • A copied X shape is one chromosome with two sister chromatids.

1. grow & copy dna

  • Before mitosis, the cell grows.
  • The number of structures, such as ribosomes and mitochondria, increases.
  • DNA replicates to make two copies of each chromosome.
  • DNA amount doubles; chromosome number has not doubled.
  • The joined copies will separate during mitosis.

2. mitosis

  • The joined chromosome copies separate.
  • One complete set is pulled to each end of the cell.
  • The nucleus divides.
  • DNA copying happened before this stage.
  • Mitosis maintains chromosome number; it does not halve it.

3. cell division

  • The cytoplasm and membrane divide.
  • Two genetically identical daughter cells form, with the same chromosome number as the parent.
  • Needed for growth, development, repair and replacement: for example, healing skin.
  • Bacteria divide by binary fission, not mitosis.

Human stem cells

  • An undifferentiated cell can divide and produce cells that differentiate.
  • Embryonic stem cells can form most types of human cell.
  • Adult bone marrow stem cells form a more limited range, including blood cells.
  • The source affects which cells can be produced.

Medical uses

  • Potentially replace damaged cells, such as insulin-producing cells in diabetes or nerve cells in paralysis.
  • Therapeutic cloning: an embryo is produced with the patient's genes.
  • Its stem cells can provide genetically matched tissue, which the patient's immune system does not reject.
  • Potential treatments are not guaranteed cures.

Benefits, risks & ethics

  • Benefit → replacement cells may repair damaged tissue.
  • Risks include transfer of viral infection and uncontrolled division.
  • Some people have ethical or religious objections, including to embryo destruction.
  • Others consider treatment benefits more important.
  • Evaluate the source, benefits and risks, then give a reasoned judgement.

Plant meristems

  • Meristems produce cells that can differentiate into any type of plant cell throughout life.
  • They can produce plant clones quickly and economically.
  • Clone rare plants → help prevent extinction.
  • Clone useful crop plants → many identical plants with features such as disease resistance.
  • Plant meristems produce plant cells; human stem cells produce human cells.
