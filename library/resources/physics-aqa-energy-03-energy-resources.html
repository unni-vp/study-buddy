Energy resources

Fossil & nuclear fuels

  • Coal, oil, gas and nuclear fuels are finite. Fossil fuels support heating, electricity and transport.
  • Burning fossil fuels releases CO₂ and pollutants. Nuclear: reliable electricity, low operational CO₂, radioactive waste.

Costs beyond fuel

  • Extraction damages habitats. Nuclear plants need expensive construction/decommissioning.
  • Biofuel can regrow, but uses land and releases CO₂ when burned. Carbon balance depends on regrowth and production.

Wind, Sun & waves

  • No fuel combustion during operation, but output varies with weather. Solar also varies with daylight.
  • Wind: visual/noise/wildlife concerns. Waves: harsh conditions and maintenance. Solar: area and siting matter.

Water & Earth

  • Hydro reservoirs can supply on demand but flood land; drought reduces supply.
  • Tides are predictable, not continuous; estuary impacts. Geothermal is steady at suitable sites, with drilling costs.

Match supply to use

  • Heating, transport and electricity have different needs. Use a mix, storage and flexible backup.
  • Renewable means replenished as used; it does not mean always available or impact-free.

Use evidence

  • Compare power, reliability, cost, emissions, waste and habitats. Justify which matters most.
  • Read totals and percentage shares carefully. Scientific evidence cannot alone settle political or economic choices.
