Rates, curves and collision theory

Curves, tangents and final yield

A product–time graph starts low, rises and levels when no more product forms. The gradient measures rate:

  • Steeper slope: faster reaction.
  • Decreasing slope: reactants are being used, reducing collision frequency.
  • Horizontal plateau: no further measured change, often because a limiting reactant has been used up.
  • Final height: total product amount, not reaction rate.

For rate at a particular time, draw a tangent at that point and use a large triangle on the tangent: change in product quantity/change in time.

A tangent rise of 24 cm³ over 40 s gives 0.60 cm³ s⁻¹.

A chord between two curve points gives an interval mean, not an instantaneous rate.

Compare speed and amount separately

Change in a gas-producing reactionInitial rateFinal gas amount
Use smaller solid pieces, keeping their mass and all other conditions the same.It usually increases because more surface is exposed.It is unchanged if the same reactant still limits the reaction.
Add a suitable catalyst to the same starting mixture.It increases.It is unchanged for a completed reaction with the same limiting amount.
Increase acid concentration at the same solution volume.It usually increases.It may also increase if the acid was limiting, because more acid is now present.

To compare a curve fairly, read both its early gradient and its final height. A faster curve may finish at the same level, a higher level or a lower level depending on the starting amounts and conditions.
