Nervous control

Control system

Stimulus is a change. Receptors detect it.

Coordination centres receive/process signals.

Effectors are muscles or glands.

CNS is brain and spinal cord.

Reflex route

Withdrawal is rapid and automatic, without a conscious decision.

Sensory, relay and motor neurones connect receptor to muscle.

Synapses

Impulse travels electrically along a neurone.

Chemical transmitter is released and diffuses across the gap.

It binds to the next cell’s receptors, starting a new impulse.

Do not say electricity jumps the synaptic gap.

Brain regions

Cerebral cortex: conscious thought, memory and language.

Cerebellum: balance and movement coordination.

Medulla: breathing and heart rate.

Functions are interconnected, not completely isolated.

Investigating brain

Study damage, electrical stimulation and MRI scans.

Link observations with function, using several kinds of evidence.

Delicate tissue and complex connections make treatment difficult.

Evaluate benefit against damage risk.

Reaction-time practical

Ruler zero at fingers. Drop unpredictably; record catch distance.

Convert using a suitable table. Repeat, compare mean times.

Match ruler, posture, hand and release method.

Control anticipation, practice and unsafe distractions.
