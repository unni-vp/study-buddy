Exchange surfaces

Why exchange systems?

  • A single-celled organism has a large surface area to volume ratio → exchange across its outer surface can meet its needs.
  • Large multicellular organisms have lower SA:V and long diffusion distances.
  • They need specialised exchange surfaces and transport systems to deliver and remove enough substances.

Surface area to volume

  • For a cube of side a: surface area = 6a²; volume = a³.
  • 1 cm cube: 6 cm² ÷ 1 cm³ → 6:1.
  • 3 cm cube: 54 cm² ÷ 27 cm³ → 2:1.
  • The larger cube has more total surface area, but less surface per unit volume.
  • Splitting a solid into small pieces exposes more area without changing its total volume.

Lung alveoli

  • Many tiny air sacs → large surface area.
  • Thin alveolar and capillary walls → short diffusion path.
  • Good blood supply and ventilation maintain steep gradients.
  • Oxygen: alveolar air → blood. Carbon dioxide: blood → alveolar air.
  • An alveolus is an air sac, not a whole lung.

Small intestine

  • Villi and microvilli → large absorption surface area.
  • Thin lining → short diffusion distance.
  • Good blood supply carries absorbed substances away → maintains gradients.
  • Sugars can enter the blood by active transport even against a concentration gradient.
  • Connect each adaptation to its effect.

Fish gills

  • Many filaments and lamellae → large area.
  • Thin surfaces → short diffusion distance.
  • Good blood supply carries substances to and from the surface.
  • Water flow maintains gradients for gas exchange.
  • Oxygen diffuses from water into blood; carbon dioxide moves out.

Plant roots

  • Root hairs give a large contact area with the soil.
  • Water enters by osmosis, across a partially permeable membrane.
  • Mineral ions can enter by active transport against their concentration gradient.
  • Respiration supplies the energy for active transport.

Plant leaves

  • Broad → large area for exchange.
  • Thin → short diffusion distance.
  • Stomata allow gases to enter and leave.
  • Internal air spaces shorten gas diffusion paths.
  • Carbon dioxide enters for photosynthesis; oxygen can diffuse out.
  • State the substance and its direction in context.

The four useful adaptations

  • Large area → more particles cross at once.
  • Thin barrier → shorter diffusion path.
  • Efficient blood supply → brings or removes substances to maintain gradients.
  • Ventilation → refreshes air or water at gas-exchange surfaces.
  • Use adaptation → effect → faster exchange in explanations.
