Population & migration

Ageing population

A population ages when older people make up a greater proportion of the total. Longer life expectancy, lower birth rates and large generations reaching retirement all contribute.

EffectWhy it mattersPossible response
More demand for health and social careA larger older population can increase the need for treatment, carers and accessible services. Needs vary between individuals.Train care workers, coordinate services and adapt housing so people can live independently.
Pressure on pensions and public spendingMore pensioners relative to workers can make funding harder.Encourage saving, review pension arrangements and support people who want to work longer.
Demand for workersSome sectors may face shortages as experienced staff retire.Training, flexible employment and working-age migration can help fill gaps.
Economic and social contributionsOlder people spend money, volunteer, provide childcare and may remain in paid work.Design accessible services and avoid treating every older person as dependent.

A response has limits. Later retirement may be difficult for someone with ill health or a physically demanding job. Migration can support the workforce, but migrants also need housing and services and will themselves age. A combination of measures is more realistic than one permanent solution.
