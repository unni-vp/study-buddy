UK environmental challenges and energy

Climate creates hazards

  • Air masses, Atlantic influence and continentality: explain contrasting UK weather.
  • Relief lifts moist air. A succession of storms can saturate a catchment.

Somerset: causes and effects

  • 2013–14: prolonged rain, saturated land, slow lowland drainage and high tides combined.
  • Flooding damaged homes, isolated Muchelney and disrupted farms, roads and businesses.

Manage at several scales

  • Immediate: rescue, supplies, warnings and pumping. Longer term: dredging, raised roads and storage.
  • The Flood Action Plan combines organisations. Maintenance and exceptional rainfall limit every scheme.

Compare the resources

  • Finite: coal, oil, gas and uranium. Renewable: wind, solar, water and sustainably replenished biomass.
  • Low carbon is different from renewable. Reliability, costs and impacts must also be compared.

Hinkley Point C

  • Under construction: intended large, dependable low-carbon electricity output.
  • Jobs and energy security come with high cost, delay, disruption and long-term waste responsibilities.

Dryholme and future choices

  • About 1 MW: organic feedstock produces biogas, heat, electricity and useful digestate.
  • Prevent leakage and pollution. Use demand reduction and a diverse supply mix at local and national scales.
