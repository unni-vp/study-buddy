QuestionMulti-step application

Analyse the Inspector's image of society as one body.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The metaphor of “one body” presents people as connected parts of a whole. Harm to one part affects the wider community, challenging Arthur's individualism. The image enlarges responsibility beyond the family and makes the Inspector's message a social argument rather than simply a demand for one confession.
QuestionMulti-step application

Analyse how Sybil's reference to class exposes her prejudice.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Her distancing phrase “that class” groups vulnerable people together as socially inferior. The demonstrative places them outside her own circle, while the category replaces attention to an individual's needs. Her charity role therefore becomes a source of exclusion, revealing a gap between respectable position and compassionate conduct.
QuestionMulti-step application

Analyse the change in lighting when the Inspector arrives.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The opening lighting supports the family's comfortable celebration. Its later harshness makes the atmosphere feel exposed and less forgiving. As a stage direction, this changes what an audience sees as well as what it hears. The visual contrast supports the investigation's movement beneath respectable appearances.
QuestionMulti-step application

Analyse why the ending can be described as cyclical.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The final telephone call returns the family to the prospect of a young woman's death being investigated. The structural return follows a brief period of relief, so it reverses their belief that consequences have disappeared. It also leaves the audience to ask whether the lesson will now be accepted, rather than offering a comfortable final resolution.
QuestionMulti-step application

Analyse Eric's indirect description of his drunken conduct.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The phrase “in that state” avoids a direct account of what he did. This evasive language can reveal discomfort and shame, but also limits his willingness to name the harm clearly. His later admission matters, yet Priestley does not allow drunkenness or attempted financial help to excuse his coercive behaviour.
QuestionMulti-step application

Analyse the function of the single dining-room setting.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The confined setting keeps the family under pressure as disclosures accumulate. Eva never enters their protected room, but the Inspector brings her experience into it. The contrast between their material comfort and the insecurity described outside prevents the celebration staying separate from its social consequences.
