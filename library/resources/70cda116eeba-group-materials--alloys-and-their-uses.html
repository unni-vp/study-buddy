Alloys and their uses

An alloy is a mixture containing a metal with other elements. Different-sized atoms distort layers and make sliding harder, so alloys are often harder than the pure metal.

AlloyComposition/propertySuitable use
BronzeCopper + tin; hard and wear-resistantStatues, bearings or medals
BrassCopper + zinc; workable and corrosion-resistantMusical instruments or fittings
Gold jewellery alloyGold with silver/copper/zinc; harder than pure goldJewellery
High-carbon steelIron with more carbon; strong but brittleCutting tools
Low-carbon steelSofter, easier to shapeCar body panels
Stainless steelContains chromium/nickel; hard and corrosion-resistantCutlery or kitchen equipment
Aluminium alloyLow density with useful strengthAircraft structures

Gold purity

  • 24 carat: pure gold.
  • 18 carat: 18/24 = 75% gold.

Example: a 12 g sample of 18-carat alloy contains:

12 × 0.75 = 9 g gold
