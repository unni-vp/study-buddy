QuestionApply and calculate

Explain how a falling object reaches terminal velocity.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Initially weight exceeds drag, so it accelerates downwards.
  • Increasing speed increases drag, reducing the resultant force.
  • When drag equals weight, acceleration is zero and velocity becomes constant.

A 3.0 kg trolley moving right at 4.0 m/s sticks to a 1.0 kg trolley moving left at 2.0 m/s. Calculate their shared final velocity, assuming no resultant external force.

QuestionMulti-step application

A 0.50 kg ball moving right at 6.0 m/s rebounds left at 4.0 m/s in 0.10 s. Find the average force on the ball.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Right positive: u = +6.0, v = −4.0 m/s.
  • Δp = m(v − u) = 0.50 × (−4.0 − 6.0) = −5.0 kg m/s.
  • F = Δp/Δt = −5.0/0.10 = −50 N.
  • The force is 50 N left.

QuestionMulti-step application

Explain the effect of doubling a car’s speed on thinking distance and braking distance, assuming fixed reaction time, mass and braking force.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Thinking distance = vt, so it doubles.
  • Kinetic energy = ½mv², so it quadruples.
  • Braking work Fs removes that energy; with fixed F, braking distance quadruples.
  • Total stopping distance is the sum, so its increase depends on the original two distances; it does not simply double.
