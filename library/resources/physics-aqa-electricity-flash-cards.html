QuestionApply and calculate

Explain why a filament lamp’s I–V graph becomes less steep at larger positive potential differences.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Increasing current heats the filament.
  • Ions vibrate more, increasing opposition to electron flow, so resistance increases.
  • Current then increases less rapidly with pd; the graph flattens.

QuestionApply and calculate

Why does adding a resistor in parallel reduce total resistance when the supply pd is fixed?

QuestionApply and calculate

Explain why transmitting a fixed power at high potential difference reduces losses in National Grid cables.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • P = VI means higher V gives lower I for the same power.
  • Cable heating power is I²R.
  • Lower current therefore reduces unwanted heating and improves transmission efficiency.
QuestionApply and calculate

Explain how an earth wire and fuse protect someone if a live wire touches a metal appliance case.

Answer

  • A large fault current flows.
  • The fuse melts and breaks the live circuit, disconnecting the supply.
QuestionApply and calculate

Explain how a spark can form between a charged object and a nearby conductor.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The charge creates a strong electric field in the gap.
  • The field ionises air, allowing it to conduct.
  • Charge flows through the air as a spark, reducing the charge imbalance.
QuestionMulti-step application

A cable has resistance 0.80 Ω and carries 50 A. Calculate its heating power and the heating power if current is halved. State the factor change.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • P = I²R.
  • Initial P = 50² × 0.80 = 2,000 W.
  • New P = 25² × 0.80 = 500 W.
  • Heating falls to one quarter, because current is squared.
QuestionMulti-step application

A component gives these readings: 2.0 V at 0.40 A, and 6.0 V at 0.80 A. Calculate both resistances and explain whether it is ohmic across this range.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • First R = 2.0/0.40 = 5.0 Ω.

  • It is not ohmic across this range; a heating filament is one possible explanation.
