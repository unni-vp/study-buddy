Concentrations and solution volumes

Concentration tells you how much solute is packed into a given volume of solution, rather like how strong a squash drink is.

1 dm³ = 1,000 cm³. Divide cm³ by 1,000 before using a concentration per dm³.

Concentration unitFormula
g/dm³c = mass ÷ volume
mol/dm³c = moles ÷ volume

Rearrangements for mass concentration

  • Mass = c × V.
  • V = mass ÷ c.

Rearrangements for molar concentration

  • n = c × V.
  • V = n ÷ c.

Here n is moles and V is solution volume in dm³. Use c in the matching concentration unit.

Example: mass concentration

A solution contains 5.0 g of NaCl in a final volume of 250 cm³.

  1. Convert volume: V = 250 ÷ 1,000 = 0.250 dm³.
  2. Calculate concentration: c = 5.0 ÷ 0.250 = 20 g/dm³.

Worked example: find the mass of NaOH in 150 cm³ of a 0.200 mol/dm³ solution.

  1. Convert the volume: 150 ÷ 1000 = 0.150 dm³.
  2. Calculate moles: n = cV = 0.200 × 0.150 = 0.0300 mol.
  3. Use the molar mass, 40 g/mol: mass = 0.0300 × 40 = 1.20 g.
  • Convert mol/dm³ to g/dm³ by multiplying by molar mass; reverse by dividing.
  • At fixed volume, doubling the solute doubles concentration.
  • At fixed solute amount, doubling the final solution volume halves concentration. Dilution adds solvent; it does not remove solute.

Dilution uses the final solution volume

Suppose 50 cm³ of a 0.400 mol/dm³ solution is diluted to a final volume of 200 cm³.

  1. Initial volume = 0.050 dm³.
  2. Solute amount = 0.400 × 0.050 = 0.0200 mol.
  3. Final volume = 0.200 dm³.
  4. New concentration = 0.0200 ÷ 0.200 = 0.100 mol/dm³.

The amount of solute stays the same while its concentration falls. “Dilute to 200 cm³” means a final solution volume of 200 cm³, not adding 200 cm³ of water.
