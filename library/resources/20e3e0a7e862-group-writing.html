Essay writing

Building an answer

The source-based task asks you to discuss the printed extract and the play as a whole. Read the wording carefully. An answer focused only on the extract misses the wider development; a memorised essay may miss the task.

  1. Give a direct argument about the named character or theme.
  2. Explain an important moment in the extract, with short evidence.
  3. Analyse a precise word, stage direction, contrast or structural choice.
  4. Connect it to a relevant moment elsewhere in the play.
  5. Show what develops or remains unchanged.

Example of a developed argument

Priestley makes Sheila's willingness to admit wrongdoing a source of hope. Her dismissal of Eva began in jealousy, so her later insight does not make her innocent. Instead, the contrast between that impulsive action and her questioning of Gerald shows that she is learning to examine power. Her parents' relief after the investigation appears doubtful makes her continuing concern more significant. The ending therefore tests moral growth through changed attitudes, rather than through the avoidance of punishment.

Common mistakes

  • Retelling every event without linking it to the question.
  • Treating all five people's actions as identical or equally motivated.
  • Saying the final call proves a single explanation of Goole.
  • Discussing language without explaining its meaning or stage effect.
  • Excusing Eric's conduct because he later regrets it.
  • Adding historical facts without using them to clarify the play.
