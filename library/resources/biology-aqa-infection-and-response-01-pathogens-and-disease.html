Pathogens & disease

Four pathogen groups

  • Bacteria: cells multiply and may release toxins. Viruses: reproduce inside living cells, damaging them.
  • Fungi and protists can cause disease too. Communicable diseases pass between organisms.

Break the transmission route

  • Contact → hygiene/isolation. Contaminated water/food → sanitation, clean preparation and cooking.
  • Airborne droplets → reduce exposure/isolate infected people. Vectors → prevent bites and breeding.
  • Vaccination reduces susceptible hosts; match prevention to the pathogen’s route.

Measles

  • Virus spread by inhaling droplets from coughs/sneezes.
  • Fever and red rash; complications can be fatal.
  • Vaccination in childhood reduces illness and spread.

HIV → immune damage

  • Spread by sexual contact or exchanged body fluids, including shared needles. Initially flu-like illness.
  • Attacks immune cells; AIDS is severe late-stage damage, with vulnerability to infections/cancers.
  • Antiretroviral drugs control multiplication. Condoms and avoiding needle sharing reduce spread.

Bacterial infections

  • Salmonella: contaminated food; bacteria/toxins cause fever, cramps, vomiting and diarrhoea. Hygiene/cooking and poultry vaccination help.
  • Gonorrhoea: sexual contact; yellow/green discharge and painful urination. Condoms and suitable antibiotics help; resistant strains limit treatment.

Malaria

  • A protist causes recurrent fever and can be fatal. The mosquito is the vector, not the pathogen.
  • Life cycle includes mosquitoes. Nets prevent bites; controlling breeding reduces vectors.
  • Plant virus/fungus examples: TMV and rose black spot; see plant-health map.
