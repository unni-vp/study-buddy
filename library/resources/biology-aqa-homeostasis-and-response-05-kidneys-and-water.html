Kidneys and water balance

Water and losses

Too-concentrated fluids draw water from cells by osmosis.

Water leaves lungs

sweat loses water, ions and urea.

Kidneys regulate urine composition.

Water gain/loss must keep cells functioning efficiently.

Urea production

Protein digestion yields amino acids.

Excess is deaminated in liver, producing toxic ammonia.

Ammonia is converted into urea.

Blood carries urea to kidneys for excretion in urine.

Filter and reabsorb

Small water, ions, glucose and urea enter filtrate.

Cells and large proteins remain in blood.

Normally all glucose and suitable water/ions are reabsorbed.

Filtration alone does not select only harmful substances.

ADH response

More-concentrated blood stimulates increased ADH release.

Less ADH gives larger, more dilute urine.

Dialysis

Blood and suitable fluid separated by partially permeable membrane.

Urea diffuses down its concentration gradient.

Matched glucose/ion levels limit unwanted loss. Cells/proteins cannot cross.

Repeated sessions burden time and daily life.

Transplant

Donor kidney can restore more normal regulation.

Requires donor availability, surgery and immune suppression.

Rejection and increased infection risk are limitations.

Compare suitability and likely outcome with dialysis.
