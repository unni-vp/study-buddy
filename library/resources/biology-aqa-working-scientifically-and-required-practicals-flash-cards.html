Explain why repeating measurements reduces random error but not a systematic zero error.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Repeats and a mean reduce the effect of unpredictable scatter.
  • A consistent zero offset affects every reading, so it must be corrected or recalibrated.
QuestionExplain and compare

Distinguish precision from accuracy.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Precision means repeated measurements cluster closely.
  • Accuracy means closeness to the true value; clustered readings can all be biased.

Explain how to determine a reaction-rate proxy from amylase endpoint times.

Answer

QuestionApply and calculate

Explain why a lamp-distance experiment can be confounded by heating.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Moving the lamp can change water temperature as well as light intensity.
  • Temperature affects photosynthesis through enzyme activity.
  • Use a monitored bath/heat shield and keep temperature comparable.
QuestionApply and calculate

Three endpoint times are 50, 54 and 52 s. Calculate mean time, range and reciprocal mean time.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Mean = (50+54+52)/3 = 52 s.
  • Range = 54 −50 = 4 s.
  • Reciprocal mean = 1/52 ≈ 0.0192 s⁻¹; keep the rate-comparison method consistent.
QuestionApply and calculate

Explain how peer review and replication strengthen a biological claim.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Peer reviewers assess whether methods, analysis and conclusions are justified.
  • Independent replication tests whether the effect can be obtained again.
  • Together they increase confidence, while neither guarantees a claim is correct.

QuestionMulti-step application

Explain how to improve a ruler-drop study that always tests the distracting condition second.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Practice may make later attempts faster; fatigue may make them slower.
  • Balance or randomise condition order across participants.
  • Keep ruler position, hand and instructions constant and repeat both conditions.
  • Compare mean times and spread using consenting participants and a safe distraction.
QuestionMulti-step application

Plan a controlled comparison of seedling response to one-sided light.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Use several seedlings of the same species, age and similar size.
  • Compare one-sided with appropriate all-round light over the same time.
  • Keep water, temperature and growing medium consistent.
  • Record lengths and accurate labelled drawings of root/shoot directions, then repeat and compare.
QuestionMulti-step application

A study finds taller plants where light is brighter. Explain what can and cannot be concluded.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The data show a correlation in the sampled conditions.
  • Light could affect photosynthesis and growth.
  • Soil moisture, minerals, species or plant age could also differ.
  • A controlled investigation and broader sampling are needed to isolate causes; the correlation alone does not prove light caused every difference.
