Resources and water

Potable water and freshwater treatment

Potable water is safe to drink, with sufficiently low microbes and dissolved salts. It is usually not chemically pure, because dissolved substances remain.

For suitable freshwater sources:

  1. Select an appropriate source, such as ground water, a river or reservoir.
  2. Filter through suitable beds to remove suspended solids.
  3. Sterilise using chlorine, ozone or ultraviolet light to kill microorganisms.

Filtration alone does not reliably remove all dissolved salts or kill all microbes. Groundwater may need less suspended-solid removal than muddy surface water, but source quality still determines treatment. Boiling can kill many microbes but does not remove dissolved salt.

Match each treatment to the problem

Problem in the sampleSuitable processWhat that step does not establish
Suspended soil particlesFiltration separates insoluble particles.Clear water may still contain dissolved chemicals and microbes.
Harmful microorganismsSterilisation reduces harmful microbes.It does not remove dissolved salt.
Excess dissolved saltDistillation or reverse osmosis removes much of the salt.Collected water still needs appropriate handling and checks.

A positive sodium or chloride test identifies an ion, not its concentration or whether a drinking-water limit has been exceeded. Likewise, a negative result in one test does not establish that all possible contaminants are absent.
