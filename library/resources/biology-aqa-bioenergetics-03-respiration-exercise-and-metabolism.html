Respiration, exercise & metabolism

Aerobic respiration

  • Glucose + oxygen → CO₂ + water. Mostly in mitochondria; complete oxidation.
  • Exothermic, continuous in living cells; energy for movement, keeping warm and building molecules.
  • Respiration is not breathing; energy is transferred, not created.

Anaerobic & fermentation

  • Muscles: glucose → lactic acid. Yeast/plants: glucose → ethanol + CO₂. No oxygen required.
  • Incomplete breakdown transfers much less energy per glucose than aerobic respiration.
  • Yeast fermentation: CO₂ raises bread dough; ethanol makes alcoholic drinks.

During exercise

  • Heart rate and breathing rate/volume increase, delivering more oxygenated blood to muscles.
  • If oxygen supply cannot meet demand, anaerobic respiration increases → lactic acid builds up.
  • Prolonged vigorous activity → fatigue and less efficient contraction.

Recovery & investigation

  • Oxygen debt: extra oxygen needed after exercise to deal with accumulated lactic acid. Blood carries it to liver → glucose.
  • Compare resting/post-exercise pulse; standardise time/pace, allow recovery, repeat and use safe activities.
  • 15-second pulse count × 4 = beats/min.

Building molecules

  • Metabolism = all reactions in cells/body. Enzymes control reactions; respiration supplies energy.
  • Glucose → starch (plants), glycogen (animals), cellulose (plant walls).
  • 1 glycerol + 3 fatty acids → lipid.

Protein & waste

  • Plants: glucose + nitrate-derived nitrogen → amino acids → proteins.
  • Excess proteins/amino acids are broken down, producing urea for excretion.
  • Digestion supplies small molecules; cells rebuild their own materials. Respiration is one part of metabolism.
