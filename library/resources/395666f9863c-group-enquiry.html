Enquiry & evaluation

Enquiry and data quality

Sampling

  • Random: every item has an equal chance; reduces selection bias.
  • Systematic: data is collected at regular intervals; easy to repeat and gives coverage.
  • Stratified: sample proportions match important groups in the population.
  • Opportunity: use whoever or whatever is available; quick but more biased.

Reliability, accuracy and validity

  • Accuracy: how close a measurement is to the true value.
  • Reliability: whether repeating the method gives similar results.
  • Validity: whether the method actually answers the enquiry question.

Improve data by using clear instructions, calibrated equipment, repeated readings, a larger representative sample and consistent conditions where possible.

Sources and bias

For every source, ask:

  • Who produced it and why?
  • When was it collected?
  • Is the sample or area representative?
  • Does the scale hide local differences?
  • Has the presentation selected or omitted information?

Reaching a conclusion

A strong conclusion:

  • answers the enquiry directly;
  • supports each claim with processed evidence;
  • recognises anomalies and limits;
  • avoids claiming that correlation proves cause;
  • suggests realistic improvements and explains how they would help.

High-yield checklist

  • Show every calculation and include units.
  • Choose a graph or map because it suits the data.
  • Quote evidence when describing a pattern.
  • Separate description (what) from explanation (why).
  • Evaluate the method, data and conclusion rather than saying only “get more data”.
