QuestionPhotosynthesis

How do carbon dioxide and water reach a leaf's photosynthesising cells?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Carbon dioxide enters through stomata and diffuses through leaf air spaces.
  • Water is absorbed by roots and transported to leaves in the xylem.

Answer
  • Glucose is used in respiration to transfer energy.
  • It is converted into insoluble starch for storage and into fats or oils, especially in seeds.
QuestionPhotosynthesis

How is glucose used to build plant structures?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • It is converted into cellulose for cell walls.
  • With nitrate-derived nitrogen, it helps make amino acids and then proteins.
QuestionPhotosynthesis

When do plants photosynthesise and respire?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Plants photosynthesise only when enough light is available.
  • They respire continuously, during both day and night.
QuestionLimiting factors

What is a limiting factor?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The factor currently preventing the rate of a process from increasing further.
  • Improving a non-limiting factor will not increase the rate.
QuestionLimiting factors

Which four factors can limit photosynthesis?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, temperature and the amount of chlorophyll.

How does light intensity affect photosynthesis rate?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Increasing light raises the rate while light is limiting.
  • The rate eventually plateaus when another factor becomes limiting.
QuestionLimiting factors

How does carbon dioxide concentration affect photosynthesis rate?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • More carbon dioxide provides more reactant, increasing rate while carbon dioxide is limiting.
  • The rate plateaus when another factor limits it.
QuestionLimiting factors

How does temperature affect photosynthesis rate?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Warming towards the optimum increases enzyme activity.
  • Above the optimum, enzymes can denature, so the rate falls.
QuestionLimiting factors

How can chlorophyll amount limit photosynthesis?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Less chlorophyll means less light is absorbed.
  • Disease or magnesium deficiency can reduce chlorophyll amount.
QuestionLimiting factors

What does a rising section and a plateau show on a rate-versus-light graph?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • A rising section shows that extra light increases rate, so light is limiting.
  • A plateau shows that more light alone will not help; another factor is limiting.
QuestionLimiting factors

Why can you not identify a limiting factor from a flat line alone?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • A plateau only shows that the x-axis factor is no longer limiting.
  • You need the conditions or a comparison curve to identify which other factor limits the rate.
QuestionLimiting factors

How are light intensity and distance from a point-like lamp related?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Light intensity ∝ 1 ÷ distance².
  • Doubling distance gives one-quarter intensity; halving distance gives four times the intensity.

QuestionLimiting factors

How should a grower decide whether to add light, heat or carbon dioxide?

QuestionRespiration

What is cellular respiration?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer

  • Breathing is ventilation, not respiration.

QuestionRespiration

What is the word equation for anaerobic respiration in muscle cells?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Glucose → lactic acid.
  • No oxygen is required, and much less energy is transferred than in aerobic respiration.

Answer
  • Glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide.
  • This incomplete breakdown transfers less energy than aerobic respiration.

  • Carbon dioxide makes bread dough rise.
  • Ethanol is the useful product in alcoholic drink production.

QuestionExercise

Why do heart rate and breathing increase during exercise?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Working muscles need more energy.
  • Higher heart rate delivers more oxygen and glucose; faster and deeper breathing brings in more oxygen and removes more carbon dioxide.
  • This supports a higher rate of aerobic respiration.
QuestionExercise

Why does lactic acid build up during vigorous exercise?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Oxygen supply cannot meet demand, so muscles use more anaerobic respiration.
  • Glucose is incompletely oxidised and lactic acid accumulates.
QuestionExercise

How can lactic acid affect muscle performance?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Its accumulation is associated with fatigue, so muscles contract less efficiently during prolonged vigorous activity.
QuestionExercise

What is oxygen debt?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The extra oxygen needed after exercise to deal with accumulated lactic acid.
  • Breathing and heart rate remain raised while this extra oxygen is supplied.
QuestionExercise

What happens to lactic acid after exercise?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Blood carries it from muscles to the liver.
  • The liver converts it back into glucose.
QuestionExercise

How can an investigation compare pulse responses to different activities fairly?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Use the same participants, activity duration and defined intensity, with full recovery between trials.
  • Randomise activity order, repeat measurements and compare mean changes from resting pulse.
  • Measure immediately because recovery begins quickly.

  • Multiply the 15-second count by 4.

How do plants make amino acids and proteins?

Answer

QuestionMetabolism

How is urea formed?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Excess amino acids cannot be stored.
  • Their nitrogen-containing part is removed during breakdown, forming urea, which is excreted in urine.
QuestionMetabolism

How are digestion, respiration and metabolism related?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Digestion supplies small soluble molecules that cells absorb and use.
  • Respiration is one energy-transferring reaction within metabolism.
  • Metabolism includes respiration plus many other building and breakdown reactions.
