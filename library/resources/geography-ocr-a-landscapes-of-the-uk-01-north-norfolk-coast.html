Coastal landscapes and North Norfolk

Erosion and weak cliffs

  • Hydraulic action and abrasion: attack the cliff foot. Saturation can trigger slumping.
  • Happisburgh: weak deposits and continued retreat threaten homes and roads.

Move and deposit sediment

  • Longshore drift: diagonal swash and straight backwash move sediment along the shore.
  • Blakeney Point: shingle deposition builds a spit, sheltering mudflats and salt marsh.

Other coastal landforms

  • Headlands and bays: contrasting rock resistance gives contrasting retreat.
  • Cave → arch → stack: erosion enlarges weaknesses and roof collapse isolates rock.

Protect Cromer

  • Sea wall: resists waves. Groynes: retain sediment so a beach absorbs wave energy.
  • Defence protects settlement and tourism, but costs money and may affect sediment supply elsewhere.

Conserve Holkham dunes

  • Marram and roots: trap and bind sand. Dunes provide habitat and a natural buffer.
  • Boardwalks: reduce trampling while allowing visitors. They cannot prevent every storm impact.

Compare the choices

  • Cromer: protect. Happisburgh: adapt to retreat. Holkham: support natural processes.
  • Judge effectiveness, cost, fairness and effects elsewhere. A storm surge floods land; erosion removes it.
