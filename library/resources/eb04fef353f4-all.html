Popular culture

Free-time activities

This theme is about what you enjoy, how you celebrate and how fame affects people. A strong answer works like a short review: describe the activity, explain your opinion and add a specific example.

FrenchEnglish
écouter de la musiqueto listen to music
regarder un filmto watch a film
lire un livreto read a book

Jouer à is used with games and sports: jouer au tennis, jouer aux échecs. Jouer de is used with musical instruments: jouer du piano, jouer de la guitare.

Faire de gives faire du sport and faire de la natation. Keep the article with the activity as part of the phrase.

Preferences, reasons and frequency

  • J'aime / j'adore / je préfère ... — I like / love / prefer ...
  • Je n'aime pas / je déteste ... — I dislike / hate ...
  • C'est amusant / passionnant / relaxant. — It is fun / exciting / relaxing.
  • C'est ennuyeux / trop cher. — It is boring / too expensive.
  • tous les jours — every day.
  • une fois par semaine — once a week.
  • souvent / rarement — often / rarely.
  • le week-end — at weekends.

Je préfère lire parce que cela me permet de me détendre. Cependant, j'aime aussi sortir avec mes amis.

I prefer reading because it lets me relax. However, I also like going out with my friends.

After a preference verb, a following verb stays in the infinitive: j'aime lire, je préfère regarder. Do not change it to another present-tense form.

Be precise about time

Le samedi often describes a Saturday habit. Samedi dernier means last Saturday. Samedi prochain means next Saturday.

A time expression and its verb must work together:

  • Normalement, je regarde des films. — Normally, I watch films.
  • Samedi dernier, j'ai regardé un film. — Last Saturday, I watched a film.
  • Samedi prochain, je vais regarder un film. — Next Saturday, I am going to watch a film.

Describing a recent experience

The perfect tense gives completed events. Think of it as choosing a finished scene from a photo album.

Le week-end dernier, j'ai joué au tennis avec mon ami. Nous avons gagné notre match. Après, nous avons mangé au restaurant. C'était une journée agréable.

Last weekend, I played tennis with my friend. We won our match. Afterwards, we ate at a restaurant. It was a pleasant day.

  • J'ai joué / nous avons gagné: completed actions.
  • C'était: a past description, using the imperfect.
  • Après / puis / ensuite: after / then / next, ordering the account.

For aller, use être in the perfect: je suis allé for a male speaker; je suis allée for a female speaker. The extra -e changes the written agreement, not the basic meaning.

Add a reaction and a reason, rather than a list of activities: J'ai aimé le concert parce que les chansons étaient intéressantes.

Tell an experience in three connected stages

Avant le concert, j'étais impatient(e). Pendant le concert, j'ai chanté avec mes amis. Après, nous avons parlé de nos chansons préférées.

Before the concert, I was excited. During the concert, I sang with my friends. Afterwards, we talked about our favourite songs.

The imperfect sets the background feeling. The perfect tense gives completed events. Time links organise the account, so the reader can follow the experience instead of receiving a list of tense examples.

Customs, festivals and celebrations

Useful vocabulary:

  • un anniversaire — a birthday.
  • une fête — a party or festival.
  • un cadeau — a present.
  • un gâteau — a cake.
  • un repas — a meal.
  • inviter des amis — to invite friends.
  • célébrer en famille — to celebrate with family.
  • les traditions — traditions.

Pour mon anniversaire, j'invite mes amis et nous partageons un repas.

For my birthday, I invite my friends and we share a meal.

Two French examples

La Fête de la musique a lieu le 21 juin. — The music celebration takes place on 21 June. It includes participation by amateur and professional musicians.

Le 14 juillet est la fête nationale en France. — 14 July is France's national day. Celebrations can include public events and fireworks.

Different people and communities celebrate differently. Describe a particular tradition or your own experience; do not assume everyone in a French-speaking country has the same customs.

Useful development

J'aime cette fête parce qu'elle rassemble les gens. L'année dernière, j'ai écouté de la musique avec ma famille.

I like this celebration because it brings people together. Last year, I listened to music with my family.

Comparing celebrations and planning

D'habitude describes what normally happens; cette année introduces the particular year.

D'habitude, je fête mon anniversaire à la maison. Cette année, je vais organiser un pique-nique, car c'est moins cher qu'un restaurant.

Usually, I celebrate my birthday at home. This year, I am going to organise a picnic because it is cheaper than a restaurant.

Useful comparisons:

  • plus intéressant que ... — more interesting than ...
  • moins cher que ... — cheaper than ...
  • aussi amusant que ... — as fun as ...
  • la meilleure partie — the best part.

Je vais organiser is a plan; j'organiserai means I will organise; j'aimerais organiser means I would like to organise. The last form uses the conditional ending on a regular -er verb.

Describing a celebrity

Give a reasoned opinion about a singer, actor, sportsperson or other public figure. A short, accurate description is more useful than memorising a long biography.

J'admire cette chanteuse parce qu'elle a du talent et qu'elle travaille beaucoup. Ses chansons me donnent envie d'apprendre une langue.

I admire this singer because she has talent and works hard. Her songs make me want to learn a language.

Ses chansons means his or her songs. The possessive agrees with the plural noun chansons, not the singer's gender.

Fame: advantages and difficulties

Develop both sides when the question calls for a balanced view.

Advantages

  • On peut gagner beaucoup d'argent. — You can earn a lot of money.
  • On peut voyager et rencontrer des gens. — You can travel and meet people.
  • On peut aider les autres. — You can help others.

Difficulties

  • On a moins de vie privée. — You have less privacy.
  • Les commentaires peuvent être négatifs. — Comments can be negative.
  • Il y a beaucoup de pression. — There is a lot of pressure.

Être célèbre peut être intéressant, mais je préfère une vie plus calme.

Being famous can be interesting, but I prefer a quieter life.

On peut + infinitive explains a possibility. A celebrity is not automatically a good role model: explain the behaviour you admire or question.

Explain the consequence of an advantage

Une personne célèbre peut aider une association parce que beaucoup de gens l'écoutent. Cependant, elle doit faire attention à sa vie privée.

A famous person can help an organisation because many people listen to them. However, they must take care of their private life.

The reason explains how fame can be useful, and the contrast adds a limitation. Peut and doit each take an infinitive. Do not make a specific claim about a real celebrity's actions unless you know it is accurate; a general example can express the idea clearly.

Building a developed cultural answer

Choose detail, evidence and reaction, like a short review rather than a publicity poster.

La semaine dernière, j'ai regardé un concert en ligne. J'ai trouvé la chanteuse impressionnante parce que sa voix était puissante. Cependant, je préfère les petits concerts où l'ambiance est plus calme. À l'avenir, j'aimerais écouter davantage de musique française.

Last week, I watched a concert online. I found the singer impressive because her voice was powerful. However, I prefer small concerts where the atmosphere is calmer. In future, I would like to listen to more French music.

The relative clause with où adds information about the setting. The reason and contrast develop the opinion. The past event and future wish fit a coherent account.

Common mistakes

  • Mixing up jouer au football and jouer du piano.
  • Writing j'aime regarde instead of j'aime regarder.
  • Using a future time phrase with a past-tense verb.
  • Forgetting agreement after être: elle est allée, ils sont allés.
  • Translating an English idiom word for word when a simpler French phrase would communicate the idea.
  • Repeating c'est bien without explaining what is good and why.

Keep your response tied to the requested activity, celebration or person. A flexible bank of accurate phrases adapts better than one memorised paragraph.
