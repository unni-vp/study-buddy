QuestionApply and calculate

Explain how prolonged rainfall can cause river flooding.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Rain fills soil stores until the ground becomes saturated.
  • More water then flows over the surface or rapidly through the soil into rivers.
  • Discharge rises above channel capacity and water spills onto the floodplain.
QuestionApply and calculate

Explain why the Somerset Levels are vulnerable to flooding.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • The landscape is very low and flat; about 18% of Somerset lies below average high-tide level.
  • Water drains slowly through rivers and artificial channels, especially when the ground is saturated.
  • High tides can restrict river outflow, so water spreads widely across the Levels.
QuestionApply and calculate

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Large fertiliser inputs can be washed into rivers.
  • Nutrients encourage excessive algal growth.
  • Decomposition then reduces oxygen, harming aquatic life.
QuestionApply and calculate

Explain how anaerobic digestion produces useful energy.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Microorganisms break down organic material in a sealed tank without oxygen.
  • This releases methane-rich biogas.
  • The gas is burned in suitable equipment to produce heat or electricity.
QuestionMulti-step application

Explain how air masses and continentality create contrasting UK weather.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Tropical maritime air crosses the Atlantic and brings mild, moist conditions.
  • Polar continental air can bring cold, dry conditions from eastern Europe.
  • Coastal places are moderated by the sea because water heats and cools slowly.
  • Inland areas have a greater temperature range because land changes temperature faster.
QuestionMulti-step application

Explain how two long-term strategies reduce flood risk on the Somerset Levels.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Dredging selected sections of the Parrett and Tone increases channel capacity; programmes in 2014, 2016 and 2018 removed about 270,500 m³ of silt.
  • Raising the Muchelney–Drayton road maintains community access during floods, reducing isolation even when surrounding land stores water.
QuestionMulti-step application

Explain why the UK's energy mix has changed since 1950.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Domestic coal declined as mines closed and pollution and carbon concerns grew.
  • Oil and gas became important for transport, heating and electricity.
  • Nuclear provided dependable low-operational-carbon electricity.
  • Falling costs, technology and climate policy encouraged growth in renewables.
