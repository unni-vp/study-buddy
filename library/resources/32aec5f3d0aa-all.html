Vocabulary

Words as useful phrases

A word is easier to remember when it has a job in a sentence. Learn la gare → à la gare → Je vais à la gare: station, at/to the station, I go to the station.

For nouns, keep article + noun + meaning together. For verbs, learn the infinitive, a useful form and the following preposition: aider quelqu'un, help someone; parler à quelqu'un, speak to someone.

An infinitive stays unchanged after a modal: je peux lire. Learn the spelling and sound together, not just an English translation.

These small words often decide the meaning of a whole answer.

Je n'aime plus ce sport means I no longer like this sport. Je n'aime pas ce sport means I do not like it. The tiny word plus changes the message.

Useful paired opinions: amusant / ennuyeux (fun/boring), utile / inutile (useful/useless), facile / difficile (easy/difficult), cher / bon marché (expensive/cheap).

Numbers, dates and practical details

Learn 0–20 as individual forms, then build larger numbers. Pay special attention to the French patterns:

  • soixante-dix: 70; soixante et onze: 71.
  • quatre-vingts: 80; quatre-vingt-un: 81.
  • quatre-vingt-dix: 90; quatre-vingt-onze: 91.
  • cent / mille: hundred / thousand.

Quinze (15) and cinquante (50), treize (13) and trente (30) are easy to confuse in listening. Check the full number, not its first sound.

Dates: le premier juin, 1 June; le dix juin, 10 June. Time: huit heures et demie, 8:30; neuf heures moins le quart, 8:45. Prices: douze euros cinquante, €12.50.

Months: janvier, février, mars, avril, mai, juin, juillet, août, septembre, octobre, novembre, décembre. Days: lundi, mardi, mercredi, jeudi, vendredi, samedi, dimanche.

Similar-looking words and word families

Some words resemble English and share a meaning: important, possible, information. Others are traps:

FrenchMeaningAvoid confusing with
actuellementcurrentlyactually
attendrewait forattend
une librairiebookshoplibrary: une bibliothèque
demanderaskdemand
resterstayrest: se reposer
une journéea day as a durationa journey: un voyage

Context selects the right meaning. Une histoire can be a story or history. Une pièce can be a room, a piece or a play. Le temps can be time or weather.

Recognise reading families:

  • Possible → impossible: opposite meaning.
  • Rapide → rapidement: adjective becomes adverb.
  • Préparer → préparation: verb becomes noun.
  • Deux → deuxième: number becomes ordinal.
  • Consommer → consommateur: action becomes the person who does it.

Do not assume every similar spelling has the same meaning, or that an ending works on every word. Check the sentence.

For a word you keep forgetting, connect four pieces:

la chambre — room → une petite chambre — a small room → J'ai réservé une chambre — I booked a room → the sound of chambre.

Alternate recognising French and producing French. If you only recognise the word, you may still struggle to write or say it.

Common mistakes

  • Learning maison without its feminine article.
  • Removing accents: a means has; à is a preposition.
  • Treating ou (or) and où (where) as interchangeable.
  • Guessing that a familiar-looking word is an English cognate.
  • Memorising one meaning while ignoring other listed meanings.
  • Ignoring negatives and time markers because the topic nouns are easier to recognise.
