Nouns and articles

Grammar is the set of connections that makes a sentence work. Think of a sentence as a team: subject, verb and description must agree.

Learn a noun with its gender: le livre, la maison, l'école (f). Gender belongs to the French word, not to the object itself.

Before a vowel or silent h, le/la become l'. Use mon amie, not ma amie. Possessives agree with the thing owned: son livre can mean his book or her book.

Du, de la, de l', des express an unspecified amount: je bois de l'eau. After quantity and many negative expressions use de / d': beaucoup de livres; je n'ai pas de vélo. An identification with être keeps the article: ce n'est pas un problème.

Contractions: à + le = au, à + les = aux; de + le = du, de + les = des. No contraction with la or l'.

General likes use a definite article: j'aime la musique. Days with le can be habitual: le lundi, on Mondays. Languages: je parle français, but le français est intéressant. A person from England is un Anglais / une Anglaise; the adjective anglais has no capital.

Changing nouns

  • Most plurals add -s; masculine -(e)au / -eu commonly add -x: un jeu, des jeux.
  • Words already ending -s or -x do not add another ending.
  • Person nouns may add -e, change -eur to -euse/-rice, or -en to -enne: un ami / une amie; un acteur / une actrice.
  • Some keep the same word and change the article: un/une artiste. Learn irregulars such as monsieur / messieurs.
  • An infinitive can act as a noun: lire est agréable — reading is pleasant.
  • Chaque takes a singular noun; plusieurs is plural. Tout changes to toute/tous/toutes to match its noun.
  • Aucun / aucune means no/not any: je n'ai aucune idée.
