Present-tense verbs

Present verbs and verb families

Je, tu, il/elle/on, nous, vous, ils/elles identify who acts. Vous can address one person politely or several people. On takes the il verb form, even when it means we.

For regular -er verbs remove -er, then add -e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent: je parle, tu parles, il parle, nous parlons, vous parlez, ils parlent. The present can mean I speak or I am speaking.

These are different families: do not use the choisir pattern for every -ir verb. For the first three singular persons, entendre/prendre/traduire/connaître/écrire use -s, -s, -t (entendre and prendre have no added -t after their existing -d); partir uses pars/pars/part; venir viens/viens/vient; ouvrir follows -er present endings. Vous forms end -ez except dites for dire and faites for faire among common irregulars. Learn related irregular forms with the complete word list.

Avoir also expresses age and sensations: j'ai quinze ans; j'ai faim/soif/froid/chaud/peur.

Spelling preserves sounds: nous mangeons, nous commençons, j'achète, je jette. Other frequent irregulars include je bois, je cours, je crois, je reçois, je ris, je suis (suivre), je vois. Learn each with its infinitive and past participle, not an invented regular ending.
