Heating & practicals

Specific heat capacity

  • Energy to warm 1 kg by 1°C without a state change. Units: J/kg°C.
  • ΔE = mcΔθ. Higher c → less warming for the same mass and input.

Worked example

  • 0.50 kg water, c = 4,200 J/kg°C, warmed 20 → 40°C.
  • Δθ = 20°C; ΔE = 0.50 × 4,200 × 20 = 42,000 J. Convert grams to kg first.

Measure c

  • Balance → mass; thermometer → rise; joulemeter or Pt → energy.
  • Insulate a block; improve heater/probe contact. Record initial temperature, heat, record rise.
  • c = E/(mΔθ). Repeat; keep electrical parts dry; avoid hot surfaces.

Explain error

  • Energy also heats equipment and surroundings. Using all input for the sample makes c too high.
  • Insulation reduces losses. Good thermal contact reduces lag. Energy vs temperature-rise gradient = mc ideally.

Insulators

  • Thicker material or lower conductivity → slower conduction under matching conditions.
  • Trapped air reduces transfer; draught-proofing limits escaping warm air.
  • Payback time = cost / yearly saving, assuming constant savings.

Cooling investigation

  • Compare equal thicknesses; same containers, lids, water mass, starting temperature and room.
  • Measure temperature at equal intervals; repeat and average. Smaller drop in same time → better insulation.
  • For thickness: keep material fixed. Handle hot water safely.
