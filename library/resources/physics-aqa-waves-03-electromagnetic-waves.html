Electromagnetic waves

Shared properties

All are transverse and travel in a vacuum.

Transfer energy from source to absorber.

c = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s in vacuum.

Different materials absorb and transmit wavelengths differently.

Spectrum order

Increasing frequency: radio, microwave, IR, visible, UV, X-ray, gamma.

Wavelength decreases in the same order.

Visible: red to violet. Eyes detect only this part.

f = c/λ. Convert wavelength first.

Communication

Radio: radio and TV. Oscillating circuits generate waves.

A receiver can develop AC at the same frequency.

Microwave: suitable waves pass through atmosphere for satellite signals.

Visible: signals along optical fibres.

Heating and detection

Microwaves: absorbed by food, increasing thermal energy.

IR: heaters, cooking and thermal cameras.

Warm objects emit detectable IR.

UV: tanning and fluorescent lamps, with skin risks.

Medical applications

X-ray: bone absorbs more than soft tissue, giving contrast.

Gamma: suitable tracers allow imaging. Targeted radiation can damage cancer cells.

Match penetration and dose to the purpose.

Gamma originates from nuclear changes.

Hazards and dose

UV: skin ageing and skin-cancer risk.

X-rays and gamma: ionisation, cell damage, mutations and cancer risk.

Risk depends on type and dose. Minimise unnecessary exposure and use shielding.

1 Sv = 1,000 mSv. Compare matching units.
