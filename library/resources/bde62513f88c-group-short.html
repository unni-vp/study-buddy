Role-play and reading aloud

The speaking tasks

Speaking is a conversation, not a recital. Build answers like a small bridge: answer the question, add a reason, then give a relevant detail.

The test lasts 10–12 minutes, with 15 minutes of supervised preparation. The three tasks total 50 marks:

TaskWhat you doMarks
Role-playComplete five short tasks, including asking a question10
Reading aloudRead a prepared short text, then answer four unprepared questions15
Photo cardDescribe two photos, then take part in an unprepared conversation25

In preparation, you can make written notes and use them during the test. Do not read the text aloud during preparation. Dictionaries are not allowed. Prepare for all three themes; the questions in the unprepared parts are not given in advance.

Role-play: communicate exactly

Each role-play task is worth 2 marks. Follow its English instruction and give the requested information. A clear, accurate sentence usually works better than an unrelated memorised speech.

NeedUseful French
requestJe voudrais un billet pour Paris.
explain a problemIl y a un problème avec ma chambre.
give a preferenceJe préfère le train parce que c'est confortable.
ask a priceCombien coûte le billet ?
ask a timeÀ quelle heure part le train ?
ask a locationOù est la gare ?

Question words: où = where; quand = when; pourquoi = why; comment = how; combien = how much/how many; quel/quelle = which/what.

If asked about yesterday, use a past form. If asked to request something, je voudrais + noun/infinitive is useful. Include all details in the prompt: a room for two people needs pour deux personnes.

Reading aloud with meaning

The Higher text has at least 50 words. Pronunciation is worth 5 marks. The following four compulsory questions form a conversation marked as a whole for 10 marks, rather than separate identical marks per reply.

During silent preparation:

  • Break the text into meaningful phrases at punctuation.
  • Identify u/ou, nasal vowels, silent endings and liaison.
  • Notice infinitive -er and participle -é, normally pronounced /e/.
  • Work out the meaning, so your phrasing supports it.

Read at a steady pace. If you stumble, correct the word briefly and continue. Do not race to hide a difficult sound.

Short reading practice

Dans ma ville, il y a un grand parc où je retrouve mes amis après les cours. Nous aimons jouer au football, mais parfois nous préférons discuter. La semaine dernière, nous avons organisé un pique-nique. C'était une journée agréable parce qu'il faisait beau. À l'avenir, j'aimerais participer à un projet pour protéger les espaces verts près de chez moi.

Notice où, the silent plural endings in amis/cours, j'aimerais, and the vowel groups in journée/beau. Follow the punctuation; the text moves from normal activities to a past event and a future wish.

The unprepared questions ask for your responses, not just a translation of the paragraph. Listen to the question's verb and time phrase.
