Radiation uses & nuclear energy

Exposure routes

  • Irradiation: source exposes object; remove/shield source → exposure ends.
  • Contamination: unwanted radioactive material remains and decays. Alpha is especially harmful internally; gamma can irradiate from outside.

Dose & precautions

  • Reduce time, increase distance, use suitable shielding and prevent inhaling/swallowing material.
  • Background: rocks/radon, cosmic rays, living things; fallout adds human-made sources. Location/work affect dose. 1 Sv = 1,000 mSv.

Useful emissions

  • Gamma tracer escapes body; choose half-life long enough to measure, short enough to limit dose.
  • Target radiation to destroy unwanted tissue while limiting healthy-tissue damage. Beta can monitor thin-sheet thickness; gamma sterilises equipment.

Judge risks

  • Same nuclei count: shorter half-life → greater initial activity, faster decline. Long-lived contamination persists.
  • Consider type, dose, position and time with benefit and alternatives. Peer review checks research claims.

Fission

  • Large unstable nucleus usually absorbs a neutron and splits into two smaller nuclei.
  • Releases 2–3 neutrons, gamma and energy; products have kinetic energy. Neutrons can trigger more fissions; reactor controls the chain.

Fusion

  • Two light nuclei join into a heavier nucleus; some mass becomes radiation energy.
  • High temperatures help overcome repulsion; powers stars. Not the same as melting. Both fission and fusion change nuclei.
