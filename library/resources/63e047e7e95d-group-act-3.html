Act 3: fear & isolation

Act 3 — Fear and isolation

What happens

  • Banquo suspects Macbeth. Macbeth fears the prediction about Banquo's descendants and hires murderers to kill Banquo and Fleance.
  • Macbeth and Lady Macbeth are unhappy despite gaining the crown. He conceals the details of his new murder plot from her.
  • Banquo is killed, but Fleance escapes. At the royal banquet Macbeth reacts to Banquo's ghost, which the guests cannot see. Lady Macbeth tries to cover for him, then dismisses the guests.
  • Macbeth decides to return to the witches. Hecate plans to exploit his confidence, while Scottish nobles increasingly oppose his rule and Macduff seeks help in England.

Why this act matters

Macbeth's motive shifts from gaining power to trying to secure it. Each attempt at safety produces another threat. Fleance's escape exposes the limits of his control, while the banquet turns private fear into a public failure of leadership.

The ghost can be read as supernatural punishment, a visible expression of guilt, or both. Whatever the production emphasises, the guests see a king who cannot command himself. The marriage also changes: Macbeth now organises violence independently, leaving Lady Macbeth to protect a public image she can no longer manage.

Important quotations

QuotationWho and whereWhat to analyse
“To be thus is nothing”Macbeth, 3.1Language — absolute negative judgement: Being king feels worthless without security. The absolute “nothing” reveals how little satisfaction success brings.
“fruitless crown”Macbeth, 3.1Imagery — metaphor of infertility: The metaphor makes his kingship seem unable to produce a lasting dynasty. He fears that his crimes will benefit Banquo's family.
“Naught's had, all's spent”Lady Macbeth, 3.2Language / imagery — antithesis and the language of expenditure: The balanced contrast between nothing gained and everything spent exposes the emptiness of their achievement.
“full of scorpions is my mind”Macbeth, 3.2Imagery — metaphor of poisonous creatures: The animal imagery makes anxiety seem painful and poisonous. His thoughts torment him and encourage further violence.
“Thou canst not say I did it”Macbeth to the ghost, 3.4Language / dramatic form — denial addressed to the visible ghost: His denial points towards the guilt he tries to escape. Hiring killers does not free him from responsibility.
“I am in blood”Macbeth, 3.4Imagery — extended metaphor of wading through blood: The image of wading through blood presents violence as a condition he is trapped in. He uses this feeling to justify continuing rather than stopping.
“Thou played'st most foully for 't”Banquo, 3.1Language / dramatic form — moral judgement in a private soliloquy: His private suspicion creates tension beneath public politeness. It is suspicion rather than certain proof.
“Be innocent of the knowledge, dearest chuck”Macbeth, 3.2Language / structure — imperative and changed marital power: The command excludes Lady Macbeth from his new plot. Their earlier partnership becomes unequal knowledge and control.
“blood will have blood”Macbeth, 3.4Language / imagery — repetition within the blood motif: Repetition suggests bloodshed producing retaliation and exposure. Macbeth recognises consequences but continues the violence.

Turning point: Macbeth becomes an isolated ruler whose attempts to protect his crown expose his instability.
