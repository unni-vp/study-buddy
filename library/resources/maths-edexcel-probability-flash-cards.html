QuestionFair outcomes

A fair six-sided die is rolled. Find the probability of a prime number.

Answer
  • The primes are 2, 3 and 5. Probability = 3/6 = 1/2.
QuestionRelative frequency

A spinner lands on blue 36 times in 120 spins. Estimate the probability of blue and the expected number of blues in another 500 spins under the same conditions.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Estimated probability = 36/120 = 0.3.
  • Expected number = 500 × 0.3 = 150. This is a prediction, not a guaranteed count.
QuestionExhaustive outcomes

A biased spinner has four outcomes with probabilities 0.15, 0.28, 0.32 and x. Find x.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Probabilities of these exhaustive, disjoint outcomes sum to 1.
  • x = 1 − (0.15 + 0.28 + 0.32) = 0.25.
QuestionSample space

A fair coin and a fair six-sided die are used independently. Find the probability of a head and a number greater than 4.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • There are 2 × 6 = 12 equally likely pairs.
  • H5 and H6 are favourable, so probability = 2/12 = 1/6.
QuestionIndependent events

P(A) = 0.4 and P(B) = 0.3. A and B are independent. Find P(A and B).

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • For independent events, multiply their probabilities.
  • P(A and B) = 0.4 × 0.3 = 0.12.
QuestionUnion

P(A) = 0.6, P(B) = 0.5 and P(A and B) = 0.2. Find P(A or B).

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B).
  • 0.6 + 0.5 − 0.2 = 0.9. The overlap must not be counted twice.
QuestionLarge samples

A fair coin gives 7 heads in 10 tosses. Explain why this does not establish that the coin is biased and how a larger experiment would help.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Small random samples can differ substantially from the theoretical probability 1/2.
  • A much larger unbiased sample tends to give a more stable relative frequency. It still does not guarantee exactly half heads.
QuestionVenn condition

In a group of 20 people, 6 are in A only, 4 are in both A and B, 8 are in B only, and 2 are in neither. Find P(A | B) and P(A′).

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • There are 4 + 8 = 12 people in B. Of them, 4 are also in A.
  • P(A | B) = 4/12 = 1/3.
  • There are 10 people not in A, so P(A′) = 10/20 = 1/2.
QuestionAt least one

Two independent components each fail with probability 0.1. Find the probability that at least one fails.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Use the complement: neither fails means both work.
  • P(both work) = 0.9² = 0.81.
  • P(at least one fails) = 1 − 0.81 = 0.19.
QuestionUnordered selection

A teacher chooses 2 students from 12. How many different pairs are possible? Explain why 12 × 11 is too large.

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • There are 12 × 11 ordered choices.
  • Each pair is counted twice: choosing A then B is the same pair as B then A.
  • The number of pairs is (12 × 11)/2 = 66.
QuestionProbability methods

How do mutually exclusive events differ from independent events?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • Mutually exclusive events cannot occur together. Independence means one event does not change the probability of the other.
QuestionProbability methods

Can two mutually exclusive events with positive probabilities be independent?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • No. Their intersection probability is zero, but the product of their positive probabilities is not zero.

Why is an expected frequency not a guaranteed result?

QuestionProbability methods

How do you find P(A | B) when P(B) > 0?

Select the card or press Space to reveal the answer.

Answer
  • P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B)/P(B). The denominator represents the restricted group.
QuestionProbability methods

What assumption allows probabilities to be multiplied as P(A)P(B)?
