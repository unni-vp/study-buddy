Climate through time

Evidence of past climate

A climate record is like a collection of clues. Some are direct measurements; others are proxies, which record a response to climate rather than temperature itself.

EvidenceWhat it can showLimitation
Thermometer recordsInstrumental observations show recent temperature change. Long records allow averages and trends to be compared.Coverage is shorter than geological history, and instruments, sites and methods need checking.
Ice coresTrapped bubbles preserve samples of past air, including greenhouse gases. Isotopes in the ice help reconstruct past temperature.Dating and interpretation introduce uncertainty. A polar record is not a direct global thermometer.
Tree ringsRing width and density can indicate growing conditions; overlapping records extend the sequence.Growth also depends on rainfall, species, soil, pests and competition. A wide ring does not always mean a warmer year.
Diaries and harvest recordsAccounts may identify frost, floods or growing seasons at a known place and time.They are local, incomplete and influenced by what observers chose to record.
PaintingsThey can support evidence of particular conditions, such as a frozen river.Artistic choices and unusual events may be overrepresented. One painting does not establish a long-term trend.

Look for agreement between independent sources and distinguish local change from global averages. The Medieval Warm Period and Little Ice Age describe regional patterns that were not identical everywhere.
