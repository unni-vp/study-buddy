Radiation & half-life

Alpha, beta, gamma

  • Alpha: helium nucleus, highly ionising, stopped by paper/skin; cm in air.
  • Beta: fast nuclear electron; medium ionisation, stopped by mm of aluminium; metres in air.
  • Gamma: EM radiation; less ionising, very penetrating; thick lead/concrete reduces intensity.

Random decay

  • Cannot predict when one nucleus will decay. A large sample follows a predictable pattern.
  • Activity = decays/s (Bq). Count rate is detector events per unit time; not all emissions are detected.

Half-life

  • Same time interval halves the remaining undecayed nuclei or activity.
  • 640 → 320 → 160 → 80 Bq: three half-lives. With 2 h half-life, elapsed time = 6 h.

Remaining vs decayed

  • After n half-lives, remaining fraction = (½)ⁿ.
  • Three half-lives: remaining 1/8, decline 7/8. Remaining : initial = 1:8; decline : initial = 7:8.

Background correction

  • Measure background without source; subtract from total using matching time units.
  • 84 total − 4 background = 80 counts/s source. One half-life later: 40 + 4 = 44 total.

Graph & measurement

  • Read time between a source count and half that count; check other starting values.
  • Keep geometry/shielding fixed. Longer counting and repeats reduce random fluctuations; they do not control decay.
