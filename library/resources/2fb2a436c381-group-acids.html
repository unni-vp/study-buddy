Acids, alkalis & salts

Acids, alkalis and pH

An acid forms H⁺ ions in aqueous solution. An alkali is a soluble base and supplies OH⁻ ions in water.

  • pH below 7: acidic. pH 7: neutral. pH above 7: alkaline, on the usual 0–14 scale.
  • Universal indicator gives an approximate pH by colour comparison. A calibrated pH probe provides a numerical measurement.
  • Acid + alkali → salt + water.
  • The key ionic equation is H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l).

Neutralisation is like matching pairs: hydrogen ions and hydroxide ions combine. The other ions remain in solution as the salt. Equal volumes do not necessarily neutralise each other: concentration and equation ratio matter.

Strong is different from concentrated

TermMeaning
Strong acidCompletely ionised in water; hydrochloric, nitric and sulfuric acids
Weak acidPartially ionised in water; ethanoic, citric and carbonic acids
ConcentratedLarge amount of dissolved acid per unit volume of solution
DiluteSmall amount per unit volume

Strength asks what fraction of the acid ionises. A strong acid ionises completely in water. A weak acid only partly ionises.

Concentration asks how much acid is dissolved in each unit volume. A strong acid can therefore be dilute, and a weak acid can be concentrated. The two words describe different things.

For comparable solutions at the same concentration, a stronger acid produces more H⁺ ions and has a lower pH. Each decrease of one pH unit means 10 times greater H⁺ concentration.

Going from pH 4 to pH 2 is a decrease of two units, so the H⁺ concentration is 10 × 10 = 100 times greater.

Strength, concentration and acid amount

A solution can have low pH because it contains many hydrogen ions, but pH alone does not tell you the total amount of acid in the container. Volume matters too. A small volume of concentrated solution can contain fewer moles than a much larger dilute sample.

Neutralisation of an acid by an alkali involves H⁺ and OH⁻ forming water. It is not automatically a redox reaction: no electron gain or loss is shown in H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O.
