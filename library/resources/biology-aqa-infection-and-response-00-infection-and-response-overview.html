Infection and response overview

Pathogens cause disease

  • Bacteria may release toxins; viruses reproduce inside cells. Fungi and protists also cause disease.
  • Spread: contact, water, air, food or vectors. Hygiene, isolation, vaccination and vector control break routes.

Named human infections

  • Measles: droplets, fever/rash. HIV: body fluids, damages immune cells; antiretrovirals control it.
  • Salmonella: food, gut illness. Gonorrhoea: sexual contact, discharge/pain; antibiotic resistance matters.
  • Malaria: protist carried by mosquitoes; recurrent fever. Nets and breeding control reduce spread.

Defence & vaccination

  • Skin, mucus/cilia and stomach acid form barriers. White cells engulf pathogens, make antibodies and antitoxins.
  • Vaccination → specific antibodies/memory → faster later response. High uptake reduces transmission.

Medicines & targeting

  • Antibiotics treat bacteria, not viruses. Painkillers treat symptoms. Trials check toxicity, efficacy and dose.
  • Monoclonals: lymphocyte + tumour cell → hybridoma → clone. Specific binding enables tests and targeted treatments; side effects remain.

Plant problems

  • TMV: mosaic leaves; black spot: fungal spots/leaf loss. Both reduce photosynthesis. Aphids feed on sap.
  • Nitrate shortage → less protein/growth; magnesium shortage → less chlorophyll/chlorosis.
  • Identify using symptoms, references, labs or antibody tests.

Plant protection

  • Cell walls, waxy cuticle and shedding bark resist entry.
  • Antibacterial chemicals attack bacteria; poisons, thorns and hairs deter feeding.
  • Drooping/curling leaves and mimicry discourage herbivores.
