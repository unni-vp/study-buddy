Measurements and data

Measurement quality and errors

TermMeaningUseful action
AccuracyClose to the true valueCalibrate, correct zero error and use a valid method
PrecisionRepeated values cluster closelyReduce random variation and improve measurement technique
ResolutionSmallest change an instrument can distinguishChoose a suitable scale or sensor
RepeatabilitySame investigator and conditions give similar resultsRepeat the measurement consistently
ReproducibilityDifferent investigators or equipment give similar resultsCompare independent investigations

Random error makes results vary unpredictably. Repeat measurements and use a mean to reduce its effect. Systematic error consistently shifts results, such as a balance with an uncorrected zero offset. More repeats do not remove that offset.

Zero error: the instrument does not read zero when it should. Check it before use, then reset it or correct the readings. Repeated readings can be close together yet still be inaccurate if they all contain the same offset.

Parallax: a scale is viewed from an unsuitable angle. Read at eye level and, where appropriate, keep a pointer close to the scale.

Anomalies: a result that does not fit the pattern needs investigation. Repeat the measurement and record what happened. Exclude it from a mean only with a reason, such as a documented measurement failure. Do not discard a result just because it is inconvenient.

Calibration checks the scale, not just zero

A thermometer may read 0°C correctly but read 48°C when a reference is 50°C. Resetting its zero alone cannot fix that scale error. Compare with suitable reference measurements over the range to be used.

For two length readings made on a ruler with the same fixed zero offset, subtracting them can cancel that offset. Other errors, such as parallax and an uncertain moving endpoint, still remain. Identify how a quantity was measured before claiming that every zero error changes the final difference.
