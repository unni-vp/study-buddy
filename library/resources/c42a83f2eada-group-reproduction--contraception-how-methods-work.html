Reproduction and fertility

Contraception: how methods work

Contraception reduces the chance of pregnancy through different mechanisms. Separate hormone effects, barriers and other methods.

MethodMechanism and comparison point
Oral hormonal contraceptiveHormones inhibit FSH and egg maturation/ovulation; needs correct regular use
Hormonal implant, injection or patchHormonal methods can suppress egg maturation/release; longer-lasting delivery reduces dependence on a daily pill
Condom or diaphragmBarrier prevents sperm reaching an egg; correct use matters. Condoms also reduce STI transmission
Copper IUDA device in the uterus; copper interferes with sperm and prevents fertilisation. It can also prevent implantation
Hormonal intrauterine systemReleases progestogen, altering cervical mucus and uterine lining; a hormone-releasing device
SpermicideKills or disables sperm; effectiveness depends on method and use
Avoiding intercourse during the fertile periodAvoids sperm meeting an available egg, but predicting ovulation can be difficult
SterilisationSurgery blocks sperm or egg transport; intended as permanent

Evaluate using effectiveness, side effects, duration, reversibility, use requirements and STI protection, plus the evidence given. Do not claim hormone methods protect against STIs. Personal and ethical choices cannot be decided by biological measurements alone.
