# Themes, context and methods

## Poverty and responsibility

- Poor people: individuals with dignity, not surplus numbers. Cratchit happiness does not erase deprivation.
- Marley and children: neglected duties have personal and social consequences.
- Ending: charity plus better wages makes change practical.

## Family and Christmas

- Shared rooms, meals and invitations: warmth and belonging. Scrooge: cold isolation.
- Christmas: concentrates generosity, forgiveness and community.
- Promise: keep its values throughout the year, not only one day.

## Redemption and time

- Past: causes and missed kindness. Present: duties. Future: possible consequences.
- Future: conditional warning leaves room for choice.
- Actions: the final stave confirms a lasting change.

## Context linked to evidence

- 1843: industrial growth alongside deprivation. Dickens’s childhood work informs concern for vulnerability.
- Poor Law and workhouses: harsh relief contrasts with personal compassion.
- Population arguments: abstract numbers are challenged through Tiny Tim.

## Form and narrative voice

- Novella: concentrated moral journey. Ghost story: fear opens a route to change.
- Staves: musical title suggests shared celebration. Ending revisits the opening world differently.
- Narrator: humour, direct address and emotion shape judgement.

## Imagery and essay writing

- Cold/warmth: emotional contrast. Light/dark: insight and fear. Chains: consequences. Food: sharing and need.
- Evidence: connect a short anchor with method and meaning. Track development.
- Task: printed extract and wider novella, with relevant context woven into analysis.
