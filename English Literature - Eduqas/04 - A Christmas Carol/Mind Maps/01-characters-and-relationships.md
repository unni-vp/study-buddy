# Characters and relationships

## Scrooge’s recovery

- Start: money, fear and emotional enclosure. Past pain explains but does not excuse cruelty.
- Change: defends Fezziwig, regrets Belle, cares about Tim and faces his grave.
- End: salary, charity and family. “Light as a feather” suggests release from a burden.

## Marley: a warning double

- Similarity: another businessman who neglected human duties.
- Chain: choices forge consequences. “Mankind was my business” redefines work through metaphor.
- Difference: Scrooge still has time to change.

## Christmas Past

- Light: truth and painful memory. Changing age combines youth with recollection.
- Scenes: kindness and loneliness reveal values Scrooge has forgotten.
- Resistance: trying to extinguish the light suggests rejection of self-knowledge.

## Christmas Present

- Warmth and abundance: generous alternative to hoarding.
- Cratchits: love has worth without wealth. Poverty still harms.
- Ignorance and Want: social deprivation threatens the wider community.

## Christmas Yet to Come

- Silence: Scrooge must interpret the scenes. Darkness and solemn movement create dread.
- Contrast: indifference to the rich man versus grief for Tim.
- Question: can different choices change what may happen?

## Family and lost kindness

- Bob and Mrs Cratchit: care and anger reveal the costs of low wages. Tiny Tim: a valued individual.
- Fred and Fan: invitations and affection offer belonging.
- Fezziwig: humane employer. Belle: love displaced by wealth.
