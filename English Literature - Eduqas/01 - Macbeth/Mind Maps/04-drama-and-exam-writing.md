# Drama & exam writing

## What an audience knows

- Soliloquy: private thoughts. Aside: concealed from other characters. Dramatic irony: audience knows what a character does not.
- Track contrasts between public behaviour and private speech, rather than treating the play as a novel.

## How the action is staged

- Duncan’s murder is offstage: anticipation and aftermath dominate. Macduff’s family faces violence onstage.
- Knocking interrupts the murder aftermath. Banquet shifts from ceremony to chaos.
- Explain why the order and staging matter.

## Sound, form & imagery

- Much speech is blank verse; rhythmic patterns can vary. Witches’ rhyme can sound ritual-like.
- Lady Macbeth’s fragmented sleepwalking prose contrasts with earlier control.
- Blood, sleep, darkness and clothing develop repeated ideas; explain effects, not just technique names.

## 15-mark extract

- About 20 minutes. Give a clear overview, then follow changes across the printed passage.
- Short evidence → precise word/action analysis → meaning → possible audience response.
- Keep focus on this passage; avoid retelling the whole play.

## 25-mark essay

- About 40 minutes. 20 literary marks + 5 for writing accuracy. Select relevant themes and three/four developing moments.
- Link beginning, middle and end; show how Shakespeare presents the idea.
- Claim → evidence → method → meaning/effect → question. Develop connected paragraphs, rather than listing every theme.

## Flexible evidence

- “Vaulting ambition”: riding **metaphor**. “Fruitless crown”: infertility **metaphor**. “Little water” to “damned spot”: **structural reversal**.
- “also feel it as a man” (4.3): inclusive **adverb**. “Walking shadow”: insubstantial-life **metaphor**.
- Explain evidence in its moment, then link it to the argument.
