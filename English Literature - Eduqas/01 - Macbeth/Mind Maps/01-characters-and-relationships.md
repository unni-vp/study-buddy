# Characters & relationships

## Macbeth’s transformation

- “Brave Macbeth” (1.2) → secret desire → hesitation → murder → independent tyranny.
- “Fruitless crown” (3.1): power without secure inheritance. “Walking shadow” (5.5): achievement feels empty.
- Pity for wasted potential can coexist with condemnation.

## Lady Macbeth’s change

- “Unsex me here” (1.5): she tries to suppress compassion. Challenges manhood; controls the plan.
- “A little water” (2.2) minimises guilt; sleepwalking and “damned spot” (5.1) expose its persistence.
- Her requests for cruelty can reveal vulnerability, not effortless evil.

## Marriage: power shifts

- Act 1: “partner of greatness”; shared ambition. Act 2: she returns daggers and covers panic.
- Act 3: he plans Banquo’s death without her; she tries to protect his image at the banquet.
- Act 5: emotional isolation. He is not controlled by her throughout.

## Banquo & Macduff

- Banquo is a foil: shares curiosity about prophecy but warns of deception; Fleance’s escape keeps Macbeth insecure.
- Macduff resists tyranny and grieves: “I must also feel it as a man” (4.3).
- His duty to Scotland conflicts painfully with family safety.

## Duncan & Malcolm

- Duncan rewards service but misjudges loyalty. His trust makes Macbeth’s betrayal more disturbing.
- Malcolm tests Macduff before trusting him; responsible kingship contrasts with self-protection.
- Ending restores political order, without undoing suffering.

## Witches & innocents

- Witches offer tempting predictions and double meanings; they never tell Macbeth to kill Duncan.
- Lady Macduff and her son make the cost of tyranny visible through domestic dialogue and stage violence.
- Fleance escapes; the play does not show his future kingship.
