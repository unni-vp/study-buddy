# Macbeth

## Five-act journey

- 1: prophecy and choice. 2: Duncan murdered; Macbeth crowned. 3: Banquo killed, Fleance escapes; ghost at banquet.
- 4: misleading assurances; Macduff’s family killed. 5: Lady Macbeth dies; resistance defeats Macbeth; Malcolm restores rule.

## Ambition → insecurity

- A praised defender becomes a king who attacks his own people. He gains power but loses peace.
- The witches tempt; Macbeth chooses. “Vaulting ambition” (1.7) shows he understands the danger.
- New crimes try to protect the first crime; violence escalates.

## The marriage changes

- Lady Macbeth initially directs the plot and challenges his manhood. After Duncan’s murder she manages his panic.
- Macbeth later excludes her from plans. Her sleepwalking reveals guilt; his power leaves him isolated.
- Shared ambition → divided knowledge → loneliness.

## Contrasts & victims

- Banquo hears prophecy but warns of “instruments of darkness” (1.3). Macduff combines courage with grief.
- Duncan’s trust exposes betrayal; Lady Macduff and her son reveal tyranny’s human cost.
- Malcolm offers a different model of leadership.

## Motifs & stagecraft

- Blood: bravery → murder → guilt. Sleep: rest → lost innocence. Washing cannot erase responsibility.
- Asides/soliloquies expose hidden thoughts. Dramatic irony makes trust dangerous. Public banquet exposes private fear.
