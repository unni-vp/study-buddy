# Power, violence & guilt

## Ambition with awareness

- Macbeth knows duties as relative, subject and host. “Vaulting ambition” (1.7) pictures overreaching and a fall.
- His hesitation establishes responsibility: he understands objections before choosing the crime.

## Violence escalates

- Defends Scotland → murders a sleeping guest → hires Banquo’s killers → attacks Macduff’s family.
- Violence changes from public service to private security and then cruelty.
- Fear does not excuse the decisions it helps explain.

## Blood changes meaning

- Early blood proves bravery. After Duncan’s murder it becomes evidence and moral contamination.
- “Neptune’s ocean” (2.2): hyperbole makes guilt seem impossible to cleanse. Macbeth imagines staining the sea.

## Sleep & washing

- Murdering sleep suggests destroying innocence and peace. Lady Macbeth’s confident “little water” fails in the long term.
- Repeated hand-rubbing in 5.1 makes inner guilt visible. Her command cannot remove an imagined stain.
- Link the early claim to its later reversal.

## Kingship vs tyranny

- Duncan rewards others; Macbeth protects himself by attacking them. “Fruitless crown” (3.1) shows insecurity.
- Scotland suffers beyond the palace. Malcolm’s return contrasts national recovery with Macbeth’s isolation.

## A tragic ending

- “Life’s but a walking shadow” (5.5) empties life of substance; power has not brought meaning.
- Macbeth still fights, but courage cannot cancel murder. Lady Macbeth’s reported death limits what the audience knows.
- An audience can feel horror, pity and relief together.
