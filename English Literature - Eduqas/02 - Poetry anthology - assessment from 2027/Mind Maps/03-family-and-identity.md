# Family & identity

## Cousin Kate: the idea

- Rossetti’s abandoned speaker addresses the cousin who married the lord. The son gives her a final source of pride and power.
- Glove simile exposes disposability. Direct address makes her account angry and persuasive.

## Cousin Kate: the method

- Six eight-line stanzas delay the revelation of the child. Public shame contrasts with maternal affection.
- Victorian class, inheritance and sexual double standards explain unequal judgement. Her claims about Kate remain one speaker’s view.

## Catrin: the idea

- Clarke connects childbirth with a later argument over skating. The cord becomes a metaphor for continuing attachment.
- Love and conflict coexist: mother and daughter seek separate identities without losing their bond.

## Catrin: the method

- Two unequal stanzas link past and present. Free verse and enjambment carry complicated feeling across boundaries.
- Clinical squares contrast with emotional circles. Motherhood is voiced from lived experience; neither side simply wins.

## Origin Story: the idea

- Ewing recounts her parents’ meeting through a comic book, then uses fragile paper to explore their love.
- A comic may be preserved or worn through use. An ended relationship can still leave value through the child.

## Origin Story: the method

- Two free-verse stanzas move from family narrative to reflection. Lists contrast protection with lived enjoyment.
- Chicago and Black family experience give personal history significance. Compare Cousin Kate’s defiance with this affectionate reassessment.
