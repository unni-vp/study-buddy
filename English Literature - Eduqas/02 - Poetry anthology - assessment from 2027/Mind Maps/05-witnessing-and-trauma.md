# Witnessing & trauma

## Remains: the idea

- Armitage’s soldier recalls a shared shooting, then cannot escape it at home. Uncertainty about the man’s weapon persists.
- The mark on the road becomes a mental presence. War imagery relocates the conflict inside his head.

## Remains: the method

- Colloquial dramatic monologue; enjambment drives the account. Shared pronouns narrow towards isolated responsibility; final couplet concentrates it.
- Veterans’ testimony informed The Not Dead. The poetic speaker is not Armitage himself.

## War Photographer: the idea

- Duffy’s photographer develops film while painful memories return. A few published images receive a brief response.
- Religious imagery makes his task solemn. Domestic safety contrasts with suffering he witnessed.

## War Photographer: the method

- Four sestets with ABBCDD rhyme impose order beside emotional disturbance. The ending resumes the work cycle.
- Film darkroom and newspaper culture shape the ethical problem. Professional composure can coexist with distress.

## Decomposition: the idea

- Ghose’s speaker re-examines a photograph of a sleeping beggar in Bombay. Earlier pride becomes shame about turning hardship into art.
- Stone imagery seems to erase individuality; the poem then challenges that way of seeing.

## Decomposition: the method

- Five quatrains in free verse. Past confidence changes to present self-criticism. The beggar is asleep, not confirmed dead.
- Compare photographer, subject and viewer responsibilities. Duffy questions the audience; Ghose also questions the image-maker.
