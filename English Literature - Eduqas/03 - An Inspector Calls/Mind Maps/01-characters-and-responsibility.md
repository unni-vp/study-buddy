# Characters and responsibility

## Arthur Birling

- Role: manufacturer seeking business and status. Dismisses Eva after a strike.
- Language: commercial priorities frame people through cost and profit.
- Irony: wrong predictions weaken authority. Ending: welcomes possible escape.

## Sybil Birling

- Role: socially confident charity leader. Rejects Eva through prejudice.
- Language: distancing class labels replace attention to individual need.
- Irony: condemns the father before learning he is Eric. Status does not guarantee care.

## Sheila Birling

- Start: jealousy plus customer influence costs Eva a job.
- Development: admits harm, questions Gerald and challenges comfortable appearances.
- Ending: responsibility survives doubts about Goole. Growth does not erase guilt.

## Eric Birling

- Conduct: drinking, coercion and stolen money create serious harm.
- Language: evasive descriptions reveal discomfort. Financial help does not excuse earlier conduct.
- Ending: admits responsibility. He is flawed, not a simple moral hero.

## Gerald Croft

- Position: socially desirable fiancé. Helps Daisy but holds more power in the affair.
- Development: confesses, then investigates Goole and shares the family’s relief.
- Interpretation: recognise kindness and self-interest together.

## Goole and Eva

- Goole: directs questions, challenges evasions and broadens the moral message. Identity stays unresolved.
- Eva: never appears. Others control the telling of her life.
- Agency: seeks fair pay and refuses stolen money. Represents vulnerable ordinary people.
