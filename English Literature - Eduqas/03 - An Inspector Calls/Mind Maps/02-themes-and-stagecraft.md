# Themes and stagecraft

## Responsibility and guilt

- Pattern: separate choices combine to narrow Eva’s options.
- Contrast: genuine acceptance versus fear of scandal. Remorse matters if values change.
- Ending: technical doubts do not undo admitted conduct.

## Class, money and gender

- Power: Arthur controls work. Sheila influences a shop. Sybil controls aid.
- Gerald and Eric: greater social and economic power affects relationships.
- Contrast: secure dining room versus Eva’s insecurity outside it.

## Generations and appearances

- Sheila and Eric become more willing to learn. Their original faults remain significant.
- Gerald complicates a simple age divide. Sybil also shows women can defend hierarchy.
- Respectability: public roles conceal harmful behaviour.

## Irony and revelation

- 1912 setting: audience knows Arthur’s confident predictions are wrong.
- Sybil: condemns the unknown father before identifying her own son.
- Ordered disclosures: suspense turns a mystery into a moral enquiry.

## What the stage shows

- Lighting: comfortable atmosphere becomes exposed. Single setting: pressure accumulates.
- Doorbell: interrupts self-interest. Act endings: recognition and anticipated confession create suspense.
- Goole: detective and morality-play features, without a proven supernatural identity.
