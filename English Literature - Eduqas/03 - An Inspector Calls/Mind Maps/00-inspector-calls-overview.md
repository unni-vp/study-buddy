# An Inspector Calls overview

## Act 1: power exposed

- Setting: Brumley, 1912. Engagement celebration is interrupted by Goole.
- Arthur: factory dismissal after a pay strike. Sheila: shop dismissal caused by jealousy.
- Meaning: comfortable lives can depend on unequal power.

## Act 2: appearances break

- Gerald: protection and shelter coexist with an unequal affair. Sheila returns the ring.
- Sybil: class prejudice blocks charitable help. She condemns the father before identifying Eric.
- Method: disclosure and dramatic irony increase pressure.

## Act 3: lesson tested

- Eric: coercive conduct, pregnancy and stolen money. Eva refuses stolen funds.
- Inspector: connects harm and social duties. Identity later becomes doubtful.
- Sheila and Eric retain guilt. Others welcome relief. Final call renews investigation.

## Characters and choices

- Arthur and Sybil: status, confidence and resistance. Sheila and Eric: faults, remorse and learning.
- Gerald: helpful actions and unequal control. Goole: moral investigation. Eva: absent, but morally active.
- Judge each motive. Do not assume all harms are identical.

## Shared responsibility

- Class: employers, customers and charities control Eva’s options. Gender: economic and sexual vulnerability.
- Generations: changed attitudes contrast with restored complacency. Reputation: respectability conceals harm.
- Central question: is avoiding punishment the same as acting responsibly?

## The ending reopens the case

- Cycle: apparent escape is overturned. The lesson still concerns admitted conduct.
- Audience: Goole’s identity remains uncertain. The ambiguity does not erase responsibility.
